# Disagreement map

Events where the arms split, and what the split was actually about. Kept as the
experiment runs rather than assembled afterwards, so the reasoning is recorded before
the outcome colours it.

The unit of interest is not who won. It is **what the arms disagreed about**, because
that names the thing a research method has to get right.

---

## PWR — Quanta Services, 2026-07-30 bmo

| arm | call | size | realised |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A naive | **Lean Up** (+20) | 7.5% | |
| B plan-first | **Lean Down** (−18) | 8.0% | |
| C skill | *pending* | | **+17.26%** close-to-close, +13.8% to the open |

Both arms cited **the same two facts**:

- an option-implied move of **11.96%** (Benzinga Pro, 7/26) against a 3.85% historical median
- the same week of comparable names being **sold on beats** — Corning −15.7%, Sanmina
  −18.9%, Amkor −24.5%, Comfort Systems −5.3%

And drew opposite conclusions from them.

**A read the washout as the signal.** −12.75% over five sessions and −28.5% from the
52-week high with *no company-specific news in the window* — the de-rating was the
AI-power complex unwinding and a hawkish Fed, not deteriorating fundamentals. So
expectations arriving into the print were far lower than a month earlier, while backlog
was at a record $48.5bn with the 12-month component +45.4%.

**B read the regime as the signal.** Four comparable names beat and were sold hard in the
same tape. That is a reaction function, and Quanta was reporting into it at 40x forward
with Earnings ESP at zero, consensus unrevised, and JPMorgan already stepped aside.

Both are good arguments. Both are supported by the corpus. They are not reconcilable, and
the difference was worth **±14%** on a single event.

### What this case actually shows

1. **The disagreement is not about evidence, it is about which frame dominates.**
   Positioning-washout versus sector-reaction-function. Neither arm lacked information;
   they weighted the same information oppositely. More research would not have resolved
   it.
2. **Both got the size roughly right in rank terms and badly wrong in level.** 7.5% and
   8.0% against a realised 17.26%. Both comfortably beat the 3.85% proxy — they knew this
   was a big one — but both landed at less than half the actual move. If that
   underestimation is systematic it matters more than the direction call, because it is
   correctable.
3. **B's method actively argued against itself here.** Its stated rule discounts "legible
   narrative … as already priced" and weights "the observed sector reaction function"
   heavily. The washout *was* the illegible, non-consensus read, and the rule pushed B
   away from it.
4. **The implied move was in the corpus after all.** Both arms recovered 11.96% from a
   news body, despite `anchors.json` marking implied move unavailable. Worth checking how
   often that happens across the sample — if news articles routinely carry the implied
   move, the anchor gap is smaller than assumed.

## BKNG — Booking Holdings, 2026-08-04 amc

Three-way split, outcome pending in this note.

| arm | call | size | basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A naive | Lean Up | 4.2% | travel-peer read-across; Airbnb raised FY guidance |
| B plan-first | Lean Down | 4.5% | Estimize sat above the sell side, so the effective bar was higher than published |
| C skill | Neutral | 4.5% | both cases concrete, so the honest label is no edge |

The interesting feature: **C reached Neutral not by splitting the difference but by finding
both cases strong.** That is a different object from the panel's Neutral in `LEDGER.md`,
which came from averaging seven persona scores toward the middle. Worth tracking whether
this kind of Neutral behaves better.
