The S&P 500 rose 1.4% to 7,707.81, a record, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average advanced 870 points, or 1.6%, to 54,048.41, also an all-time high.
The Nasdaq 100 outpaced the tape with a 2.5% jump to 29,502.39. The Russell 2000 climbed 1.5% to 3,027.36.
West Texas Intermediate crude sank 5.3% to $76.06 a barrel, while Brent slid 5.2% to $79.42.
Rate traders trimmed hawkish bets accordingly. The implied probability of a 25-basis-point Federal Reserve rate hike in September dropped to roughly 57% from about 65% a session earlier, with softer domestic data reinforcing the move: June job openings fell to 7.36 million against a 7.45 million forecast, factory orders unexpectedly declined 0.3% for a second straight month, and the trade deficit narrowed to $73.3 billion.
The retreat in energy costs rippled straight into rates. The 10-year Treasury yield eased five basis points to 4.63%, pulling back from Monday’s 18-month high of 4.75%. The 2-year fell five basis points to 4.20% and the 30-year slipped four basis points to 5.19%.

Sterling Infrastructure Inc. (NASDAQ:STRL) dropped 11.7% on a margin-mix problem: revenue rose 90% to $1.17 billion and the revenue guidance midpoint was lifted 8.7%, but the adjusted EBITDA midpoint went up only 5.3%.
