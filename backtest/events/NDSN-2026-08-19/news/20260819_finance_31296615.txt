Nordson stock has delivered a strong 41.7% return over the past year, yet both the Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) intrinsic value estimate and market multiples currently point to the shares trading at a premium rather than at a clear bargain.
Over the last 12 months, Nordson has returned 41.7%, which puts extra focus on whether the current price already embeds optimistic expectations.
Expectations for continued cash generation and solid margins can support the current valuation, while any setback in execution or weaker cash flows could weigh heavily on an already full price.
Nordson passes just 1 of 6 valuation checks, which suggests the stock leans expensive on the broader set of metrics rather than offering obvious value.
The issue now is whether Nordson's premium pricing still leaves enough room for an attractive long term return from here.
The Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) model values Nordson by projecting future cash generation and discounting it back to today. On this view, Nordson's latest twelve month free cash flow sits at about $702 million, and the model assumes these cash flows continue to grow from this base rather than shrinking or spiking unusually.
Using those cash flow projections, the DCF model points to an estimated intrinsic value of about $264 per share. With the current share price sitting above that level, the model implies Nordson trades at roughly a 15.1% premium to its own cash flow estimate.
On the DCF numbers alone, Nordson stock currently screens as overvalued relative to its projected cash flows.
The P/E ratio suits Nordson because earnings are a key driver of how investors usually think about the stock. Nordson currently trades on a P/E of about 32.1x, which is above the Machinery industry average of 27.4x and also above the peer group average of 34.7x. That places the stock in the higher priced part of its sector on earnings.
The fair P/E ratio from the model is lower at around 23.7x. This is the level that would typically align with Nordson's profile once factors such as margins, size and risk are taken into account. The gap between the current 32.1x and this fair level indicates that investors are paying a premium for each dollar of Nordson's earnings relative to what the model suggests.
On the P/E multiple, Nordson stock currently screens as overvalued compared with the level implied by the tailored fair ratio.
