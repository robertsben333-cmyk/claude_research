Investing.com -- Europe could contribute heavily to another coordinated strategic oil stock release by September if flows through the Strait of Hormuz or regional energy infrastructure remain materially disrupted, Citi analysts said.
Any European contribution would probably focus on refined products, led by diesel. This would differ from the current International Energy Agency-led release, which has largely relied on supplies from the United States and Japan.
A second intervention is not guaranteed. European governments could instead preserve their remaining emergency inventories, subsidise fuel prices or allow higher energy costs to pressure Washington into resuming negotiations with Iran.
Oil product prices have risen sharply following the collapse of the Iran-U.S. memorandum of understanding and renewed military confrontation over the past two weeks. Prices are now only 10% to 15% below their late-March 2026 highs.
The IEA's coordinated release of 400 million barrels of crude oil and refined products is nearing completion and should conclude within the next one to two months. The additional supply has eased pressure in physical markets and reduced concerns that disruption around the Strait of Hormuz could produce outright shortages.
IEA members held about 1.8 billion barrels of strategic inventories when the programme was announced in March. That total included 1.2 billion barrels of government-controlled stocks and another 600 million barrels held by companies under government obligations.
The programme called for the release of 280 million barrels from public reserves and 119 million barrels from industry-held inventories. Stocks in the latter category were kept at commercial storage facilities but previously could not be accessed freely.
Government-controlled inventories had recorded a physical draw of roughly 170 million barrels by July. Releases averaged about 1.1 million barrels per day and peaked above 2.7 million barrels per day in April.
With the existing programme nearly exhausted, the condition of Hormuz shipping routes and regional energy facilities will determine whether consuming nations need to draw further on emergency reserves.
