Okta stock has delivered a strong 88.0% gain over the past three years, yet its valuation checks send a mixed message as the Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) estimate suggests the shares are roughly in line with intrinsic value while market based multiples point to an expensive profile.
Okta's 88.0% return over three years highlights how much expectations have shifted and makes the current share price more sensitive to any change in sentiment.
Recent optimism around demand for Okta's identity products and new integrations, such as the Lightbits enterprise security link up, can support growth expectations, while any disappointment versus bullish revenue forecasts remains a key risk for the current valuation.
With Okta screening as undervalued in only 1 of 6 valuation checks, the stock currently leans expensive rather than a clear bargain on the broader measures.
For investors, the debate is whether Okta's recent share price strength already reflects these growth expectations or if there is still room for upside without stretching valuation too far.
The Discounted Cash Flow model uses projected free cash flows to estimate what Okta stock could be worth today. For Okta, the latest twelve month Free Cash Flow is about $895.4 million, and the model assumes growing cash flows over time rather than a sharp decline or recovery phase.
Based on these inputs, the Discounted Cash Flow model points to an estimated intrinsic value of about $145 per share, which is only slightly above the current share price and indicates that Okta is roughly fairly priced on a cash flow basis. Oppenheimer's expectation of stronger near term revenue, supported by demand across Okta's identity products, helps explain why the market price is close to this intrinsic value estimate.
Overall, Okta appears roughly fairly valued on a cash flow basis, with the current market price broadly aligned with the level suggested by the Discounted Cash Flow model.

P/E is a useful yardstick for Okta because it already reports positive earnings that can anchor the comparison. On this measure, Okta trades on a P/E of about 94.4x, which is far higher than the wider IT industry average of roughly 17.3x and also well above the peer group average of around 38.6x. That places the stock at a clear premium to many other software and IT companies.
The fair P/E ratio implied by Simply Wall St's model is about 38.0x. This is the level that would typically line up with Okta's profile when taking into account factors such as growth outlook, profitability and risk. The gap from this fair ratio to the current 94.4x suggests investors are paying a substantial extra premium for Okta's earnings today.
On the P/E multiple, Okta stock appears overvalued, with its earnings priced far above both industry norms and the level suggested by the fair ratio model.
