Okta (NASDAQ:OKTA) shares rose 1.9% in premarket trading Monday after Wells Fargo upgraded the cybersecurity company to Overweight from Equal-Weight and lifted its price target to $180 from $150, arguing that demand trends, execution and competitive positioning are improving enough to support a stronger growth outlook.

Okta is a cloud-based identity and access-management company. Its software helps businesses securely manage employee and customer logins, authentication and permissions, with revenue generated largely through subscriptions to products including workforce identity, identity governance and Auth0.
Wells Fargo analyst Richard Poland said evidence is building that Okta's core demand is recovering.
Increasing body of evidence in support of core demand improving; view low-teens growth as likely base case, rather than upside, Poland wrote.
Identity security is also climbing corporate spending priorities. Wells Fargo's latest channel work found partners ranked identity as the No. 2 spending priority behind only AI, up from No. 4 last quarter.
Just as importantly, Okta ranked No. 1 for identity market-share gains, overtaking Microsoft, while channel results improved substantially quarter over quarter.
Poland said Okta's push toward large enterprises, increased sales capacity and partner expansion are beginning to translate into bigger opportunities. Cross-selling identity governance and Auth0 is also gaining traction.
Our approach has been see-to-believe, and we're starting to see it, Poland said.
Partners reported improvement in new customer wins, larger deal sizes and greenfield Auth0 opportunities, including a reported $15 million-plus total contract value replacement of Oracle technology.
Wells Fargo also sees AI becoming a potential tailwind. Its survey ranked Okta as the second-largest potential AI beneficiary in identity, tied with CyberArk and trailing only Microsoft.
Investors Takeaway
The upgrade raises the bar for Okta's next earnings reports.
Investors should focus on revenue growth, remaining performance obligations, large-enterprise deal activity and cross-sell adoption for identity governance and Auth0.
Wells Fargo's thesis strengthens if Okta can sustain low-teens growth while continuing to win share from Microsoft and legacy identity vendors.
The main risk is that stronger channel commentary fails to translate into reported bookings and durable growth. Any renewed weakness in seat expansion or enterprise IT budgets would challenge the bank's more bullish assumptions and make the new $180 target harder to justify.
