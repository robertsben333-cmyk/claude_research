Does Franklin Resources Look Undervalued on Excess Returns?
The Excess Returns model looks at how efficiently Franklin Resources turns its equity base into profits above the required return for shareholders. For Franklin Resources, the model points to a stable, fee driven business that earns more than its estimated cost of equity.
The inputs show a Book Value of $23.33 per share and a Stable EPS of $2.67 per share, compared with a Cost of Equity of $1.95 per share. That gap creates an Excess Return of $0.73 per share, supported by an Average Return on Equity of 11.24%. The Stable Book Value used in the model is $23.78 per share, based on the median book value from the past five years. Together, these assumptions feed into an intrinsic value estimate of about $39.44 per share, which implies Franklin Resources trades at roughly a 15.8% discount to this Excess Returns value.
On this model, Franklin Resources stock appears undervalued relative to its estimated intrinsic worth.
P/E is a useful cross check for Franklin Resources because earnings are a core metric for an asset manager. The stock currently trades on a P/E of about 25.5x. This sits well below the Capital Markets industry average of 39.7x and also below the peer average of 56.4x. On simple comparisons with sector and peer multiples, Franklin Resources does not look stretched.
The fair P/E ratio implied by the model is 16.5x. This reflects what investors might typically pay for Franklin Resources given its financial profile and risk inputs. The current 25.5x P/E is therefore meaningfully higher than this fair ratio. The gap suggests investors are paying a premium relative to what the model indicates as a more neutral valuation level, even if the stock screens cheaper than many peers.
On the P/E framework alone, Franklin Resources stock screens as overvalued relative to the modelled fair multiple.
