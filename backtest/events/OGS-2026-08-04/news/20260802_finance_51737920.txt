ONE Gas has delivered a 24.5% total return over the past five years, yet its Dividend Discount Model (DDM) intrinsic value estimate currently points to the stock trading at a premium, which sits awkwardly beside a broader appraisal that already leans expensive.
Over five years, ONE Gas has returned 24.5%, which suggests the stock has already rewarded patient holders and may have priced in a fair amount of optimism.
Expectations for steady utility cash flows can support today's price, while any pressure on allowed returns or higher funding needs may weigh on what investors are prepared to pay.
ONE Gas passes only 1 of 6 valuation checks, so the broader toolkit currently points to a stock that is not a clear bargain.
The issue now is whether that combination of past returns and a premium to the DDM intrinsic value leaves enough room for a fresh entry into ONE Gas at around US$77.65.
The Dividend Discount Model (DDM) looks at what you pay today for the stream of dividends you expect to collect. For ONE Gas, the model uses a recent annual dividend of about $2.81 per share, an estimated return on equity of 8.24% and a payout ratio of roughly 62%. That combination implies a dividend growth rate of about 3.1%, which lines up with a mature utility that is returning a meaningful share of earnings while still retaining some capital to reinvest.
Based on those inputs, the DDM points to an intrinsic value of about $70 per share, compared with the current price around $77.65. That gap translates into an estimated 10.7% premium to the model's value, so the share price is higher than what these dividend assumptions support. For income focused investors, the key question is whether the stability of ONE Gas and any future dividend decisions justify paying above this dividend based estimate.
On this dividend model, ONE Gas stock currently screens as overvalued relative to its estimated intrinsic value.
The P/E ratio suits a utility like ONE Gas because earnings tend to be a key anchor for how investors judge the stock. ONE Gas trades at about 17.8x earnings, compared with a Gas Utilities industry average near 14.0x and a peer group average around 14.2x. That points to a clear premium price tag relative to sector and peer benchmarks.
The modelled fair P/E ratio for ONE Gas sits at roughly 18.1x. That is very close to the current 17.8x level, so the stock lines up closely with what the model suggests investors might pay given its earnings profile and risk characteristics. For you as a potential shareholder, that means the current P/E does not stand out as a bargain or an obvious stretch based on these earnings based checks.
On this P/E yardstick, ONE Gas stock comes across as roughly fairly valued.
