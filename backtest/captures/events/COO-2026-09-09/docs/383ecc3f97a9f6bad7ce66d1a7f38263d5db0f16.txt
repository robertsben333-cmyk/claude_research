Key Elements      CooperCompanies    CooperVision    CooperSurgical    Albert White    Brian Andrews    MyDay    MiSight    Biofinity    PARAGARD    RI Witness    Asia-Pacific    Japan    China    EMEA    embryo culture media recall                                                                                           CooperCompanies delivered a quarter of sharp contrasts on Wednesday—record sales and a major strategic pivot overshadowed by a sobering outlook for Asia that forced a full-year revenue cut. 
 The contact lens and women’s health giant reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of $1.08 billion, up 8% from a year earlier, and non-GAAP earnings per share of $1.21, a 26% jump that beat consensus by $0.11. CEO Albert White called it the “tenth consecutive quarter of beating consensus earnings expectations.” 
 Yet the company lopped about $100 million off the midpoint of its annual revenue guidance—now $4.28 billion to $4.32 billion, implying organic growth of 3.5% to 4.5%—solely because of sagging consumer demand in Japan and China. The disclosure sent shares lower in after-hours trading as investors weighed the near-term headwinds against the potential of a lucrative sale of the CooperSurgical arm. 
 The Asia problem 
 CooperVision, the contact lens unit, grew 8% to $724 million, but organic growth was just 4%. The drag came entirely from Asia-Pacific, where revenue contracted 6%. White said the region’s consumer weakness was “greater than expected,” with Japanese and Chinese buyers shifting behavior in ways that hurt the company—wearing glasses more, using e-commerce channels where the company is weaker, and showing less appetite for premium products. 
 “What we’re seeing now is softness in the market itself,” White told analysts. “We’re continuing to execute on MyDay and repositioning products, but we’re doing it in a market that’s now considerably softer than when we started the process.” 
 He expects Asia-Pacific to decline again in the third quarter before stabilizing to grow roughly in line with the market by the fiscal fourth quarter. 
 The Americas (up 7%) and EMEA (up 6%) held up well, buoyed by daily silicone hydrogel lenses and the flagship MyDay franchise, which posted double-digit growth. Biofinity grew 5% organically, and myopia-control lens MiSight jumped 24% to $32 million, driven by strong early reception in Japan and the rollout of MyDay MiSight in Europe. 
 CooperSurgical: fertility rebound and a possible sale 
 CooperSurgical grew 8% to $358 million, with organic growth of 6%. Fertility revenue rebounded 10% organically to $144 million, helped by capital-equipment sales of the RI Witness tracking system and a restocking lift from Middle East distributors as airspace reopened. The medical-device unit, including PARAGARD, was flat—better than management anticipated—while surgical OBGYN devices grew 6%. 
 The most consequential news, however, came from White’s update on the strategic review. With the resolution of lawsuits stemming from a December 2023 embryo culture media recall, the company is now “actively advancing discussions with multiple parties that have submitted significant indications of interest in CooperSurgical.” 
 “The market valuation right now, I believe, is absurd,” White said. “Private investors are willing to pay a premium price over the public markets. If that is the case, then we’re going to do what’s best for our shareholders.” He expects to provide a definitive update “soon,” possibly before the next earnings call in September.

 Operating margin jumped nearly two full percentage points as back-office consolidation efforts—especially inside CooperSurgical—delivered leverage. CFO Brian Andrews noted that SG&#x26;A expenses rose just 1%, with CooperSurgical’s expenses declining year over year for the second consecutive quarter.     
 Guidance and capital allocation 
 Full-year EPS guidance was held at $4.58–$4.66, despite the second-quarter beat, because of deteriorating foreign-exchange rates in the second half, tariff costs of roughly $22 million, and the revenue reset in Asia. Gross margin is expected to dip to about 66% in Q3 from 68.1%, partly because an AI-driven inventory optimization will temporarily reduce manufacturing absorption. 
 Free cash flow is set to be the silver lining. Management raised the 2026 outlook to approximately $650 million, excluding litigation payouts, and reaffirmed a $2.2 billion cumulative free cash flow target for fiscal 2026 through 2028—inclusive of litigation payments. The company filed for up to $15 million in tariff refunds, which are not included in guidance. 
 While share buybacks were limited to $13 million in the quarter due to a self-imposed blackout during strategic review activities, Andrews said the company expects “to be significantly more aggressive moving forward.” If CooperSurgical is sold, the vast majority of proceeds would be used for buybacks, White confirmed. 
 Litigation milestone 
 The quarter included a $271.6 million net charge for the embryo culture media recall, consisting of $324.1 million in settlement accruals partly offset by $52.5 million in insurance recoveries. The charge was excluded from non-GAAP results, and White emphasized that settlements now cover more than 95% of claimants, removing a major overhang that had slowed the strategic process. 
 Analyst grilling on Asia and the breakup 
 Questions from the analyst community centered on whether the Asia-Pacific weakness is structural or temporary, and on the mechanics of a potential CooperSurgical separation. 
 Jeff Johnson of Baird pressed on the persistence of the Asia decline, with comps becoming easier. White said the market itself has fallen below expectations and that rationalization of legacy hydrogel products—about 60% complete—will continue to weigh into 2027 before the regional business grows in line with the market. 
 Robbie Marcus of JPMorgan sought details on the consumer behavior shift: “Is it that volumes are going down? Are consumers shifting to private label? Are they extending wear, trading back to glasses?” White acknowledged some wearer behaviors are changing, including a move toward e-commerce, where CooperVision is less strong, and a lack of pricing power in the region. 
 On the breakup, Jonathan Block of Stifel asked about the interest level. White replied there is “significant interest in the entire business and in pieces of the business,” but because enough parties are interested in the whole, the company is proceeding with a sale of the entire unit. He said the back-office synergies lost in a separation would not be large, since manufacturing and distribution are already separate, and that a transition-services agreement could manage the rest. 
 Marcus also inquired about free cash flow and tax implications of a standalone CooperVision. White said Capex will start declining meaningfully next year, unlocking free cash flow upside, and that the effective tax rate would likely remain similar. 
 Bottom line 
 CooperCompanies enters the back half of fiscal 2026 in a transitional moment: managing a soft Asian market, finalizing a historically large litigation settlement, and pursuing what could be a transformative sale of its surgical business. The record results and aggressive buyback posture offer a cushion, but the stock’s near-term path will hinge on how quickly Asia stabilizes and whether a deal materializes at the premium valuation management expects.
