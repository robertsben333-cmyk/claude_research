Key Elements      Broadcom    TSMC    Third Point    Duquesne Family Office    Appaloosa    Stanley Druckenmiller    Dan Loeb    David Tepper    Alphabet    AMD    MediaTek    SemiAnalysis    Bank of America                                                                                           Wall Street's top hedge funds are staging a rare asset rotation in the semiconductor space. According to the latest second-quarter 13F filings — which reflect holdings as of June 30 — multiple heavyweight funds simultaneously trimmed or entirely exited their Broadcom (AVGO) positions while pivoting capital aggressively toward foundry leader TSMC (TSM; 2330.TW), sparking broad debate over whether the capital tide in AI chip investing is shifting. 
 The most emblematic moves in this reshuffling came from Dan Loeb's Third Point and Stanley Druckenmiller's Duquesne Family Office. Third Point liquidated its entire 50,000-share Broadcom stake in the second quarter while adding 185,000 shares of TSMC, bringing its total position to 460,000 shares worth approximately $220 million. Duquesne likewise exited Broadcom entirely, selling all 195,955 shares, and rotated into TSMC by purchasing 94,400 shares, lifting its total TSMC holdings to 589,680 shares valued at roughly $282 million. 
 By contrast, David Tepper's Appaloosa took a more bifurcated approach. The fund aggressively added 322,500 shares of TSMC, bringing its total to 1.65 million shares worth approximately $788 million (about NT$25 billion), making it one of the largest buyers of TSMC during the quarter. Yet Appaloosa also initiated a new 150,000-share position in Broadcom valued at roughly $56.66 million, making Tepper one of the few fund managers to add to both Broadcom and TSMC simultaneously.

 Behind this rotation lies a stark divergence in the two companies' stock performance. TSMC has climbed roughly 41% year-to-date and 78.82% over the past year, with shares reaching as high as $426.35. Broadcom, meanwhile, has faced significant pressure recently, falling 8.13% over the past week and posting a year-to-date gain of only about 13.97%. Market chatter attributes part of Broadcom's weakness to a report from research firm SemiAnalysis suggesting that Alphabet (GOOGL) may be considering diversifying its custom chip suppliers, potentially shifting orders toward Broadcom competitors AMD (AMD) and MediaTek. The market has consequently grown concerned that Broadcom's share of Google's custom TPU chip business could be eroded. 
      
 The report offered specific figures: full-year 2026 shipment estimates for Google's TPU v7 (Ironwood) have been revised down from 3.2 million units to 2.7 million units, primarily because the ramp-up of CoWoS-S advanced packaging capacity that Broadcom handles on Google's behalf has progressed slower than expected. Broadcom's overall 2026 CoWoS wafer capacity estimate was also cut from 250,000 wafers to 215,000, and market sources indicate Google is in talks with AMD to co-develop a tenth-generation TPU project.     
 It is worth noting that Broadcom's recent share-price pressure is not attributable to a single factor. In the same week, Bank of America analysts flagged concerns about XPV — a joint venture among Broadcom, private equity firms Blackstone and Apollo that finances AI accelerator leasing. If the platform scales to 20 gigawatts (GW), senior debt could accumulate to $370 billion by mid-2029 (including roughly $150 billion in new issuance in 2027). BofA downgraded Broadcom's issuer rating outlook from "Overweight" to "Neutral," sending shares down 5.94% on the day. Additionally, a critical security vulnerability with a CVSS score of 9.8 was disclosed in VMware's vCenter software, which has already been actively exploited by hacking groups — another blow to investor confidence and a further source of recent downward pressure on the stock. 
 Yet Broadcom's fundamentals remain robust. Revenue for the second quarter of fiscal 2026 reached $22.2 billion (about NT$710 billion), up 48% year-over-year, with AI semiconductor revenue hitting $10.8 billion (about NT$340 billion), a 143% year-over-year surge. The company guided third-quarter revenue to $29.4 billion (about NT$930 billion), with AI chip revenue expected to reach $16 billion (about NT$510 billion). CEO Hock Tan went further, predicting that AI-related revenue in 2027 "will very comfortably exceed $100 billion." 
 TSMC, for its part, continues to benefit from explosive AI chip demand. Second-quarter 2026 revenue reached $40.2 billion (about NT$1.3 trillion), and the company raised its full-year dollar revenue growth outlook to "slightly above 40%." High-performance computing (HPC) now accounts for 66% of its platform revenue mix, and 2026 capital expenditure guidance was raised to between $60 billion and $64 billion (about NT$2 trillion). CEO C.C. Wei stated that AI-related demand will remain strong from now until around 2030. 
 Beyond the Broadcom-versus-TSMC dynamic, the filings also reveal a broader trend of fund managers rotating from traditional chipmakers toward semiconductor equipment and advanced-process plays. Druckenmiller exited Intel (INTC) and Micron (MU) entirely, pivoting instead into Lam Research (LRCX), AMD, and Entegris — companies tied to equipment and advanced manufacturing processes. Third Point, meanwhile, exited Lam Research, KLA, the VanEck Semiconductor ETF, and its entire NVIDIA stake, while adding to ASML. 
 It is important to note that 13F data reflects holdings only as of June 30 and is not a real-time record of trading activity, so it cannot be read as a definitive snapshot of these funds' current positions. Market analysts point out that Broadcom's recent pullback may have over-discounted concerns about TPU market-share erosion. Broadcom not only renewed a five-year partnership agreement with Google, but Google's overall spending plans over the coming years are massive — this is an expanding pie, and Broadcom will still command a substantial slice of it. 
 For investors, the message is nuanced: Wall Street's biggest funds are indeed rotating from Broadcom toward TSMC, but that does not mean Broadcom's fundamentals have deteriorated.
