(ZS) — Q3 FY2026 Results                        Elliot      May 27, 2026                   10                         Share             The quarter itself was genuinely strong — revenue of $850M beat consensus by ~$14M, ARR grew 25% with net new ARR accelerating to $166M, Z-Flex surged 60% QoQ, Zero Trust Everywhere customers jumped from 550 to 700+, and non-GAAP operating margin hit an all-time high of 23%. The business showed real momentum on every operational metric that the bull case depends on.  The stock is down ~21% after hours because management delivered two simultaneous shocks that had nothing to do with Q3 execution: (1) two sales leaders departed at the end of the quarter, prompting management to de-risk the Q4 and FY27 outlook, and (2) the early FY2027 revenue and ARR growth guide of 16-17% implies a wrenching deceleration from current 24-25% growth — roughly 5-8 percentage points below where the Street was modeling. The reaction is not about Q3. It is about the terminal velocity of the business, whether the sales disruption is structural or transitory, and what multiple a 16-17% grower with elevated CapEx deserves.  What Was New This Quarter                 1. Sales leadership departures — the event that determined the stock outcome.   Two leaders under CRO Mike Rich departed at end of Q3. Management would not confirm voluntary vs. involuntary, seniority, or the counterfactual guidance without the disruption. One has been replaced internally; the second replacement is “in late stages.” Management said it “could have impact in the short term” and is taking a “prudent approach” — which proved to mean meaningful guidance cuts.    2. FY2027 early look: 16-17% growth — the actual bomb.   Highly unusual to issue initial FY27 guidance three months before fiscal year-end. Kevin Rubin said they felt obliged to reset expectations. The 16-17% implies net new ARR growth in the low-to-mid single digits organically — roughly flat on a percentage basis vs. FY26’s 9.5% organic net new ARR growth, and well below the 22%+ the Street was likely modeling. Key drivers named: new logo weakness, sales leadership transition risk, and uncertain uptake pace for the integrated SecOps/Red Canary product.    3. CapEx inflation — a FCF margin structural headwind.   FY26 CapEx guided to high single digits of revenue (vs. prior mid-single digits) due to AI data center component shortages. FY27 CapEx will increase a further ~200bps vs. FY26. Management has passed through one price increase on Branch appliances already. FCF margin guide for FY26 was cut 350-420bps (from 26.5-27% to 22.8-23.3%) — that’s not trivial for a business where FCF yield is a primary valuation input.    4. Z-Flex inflection — massively accelerating.   Q3 Z-Flex TCV: $480M, up 60% QoQ from $290M in Q2. $1B+ delivered over the last 12 months. Average term: 4 years. Two specific deals disclosed: an 8-figure 5-year deal with a Fortune 500 finance/insurance customer (ARR +50%), and an 8-figure 3-year deal with a Global 2000 semiconductor manufacturer (ARR +60%). This signals deep customer commitment and forward visibility, though the ARR ramp on Z-Flex deals typically runs 6-12 months.    5. Zero Trust Everywhere acceleration.   >700 customers vs. >550 in Q2 — a ~27% sequential jump. This matters because Zero Trust Everywhere customers are 2-3x higher ARR than ZIA/ZPA-only customers. The installed base expansion is the primary organic ARR growth engine.    6. Metered/non-seat-based usage now >30% of new ACV.   Up from >25% in Q2. The ARR tied to metered offerings grew >100% YoY. This is structurally important — it means ZS is transitioning part of its business model toward consumption pricing (Zero Trust Branch, Cloud, AI tokens) which scales with usage rather than headcount. Underappreciated as a growth algorithm modifier.    7. AI Protect $100M+ TTM bookings.   Disclosed for first time this quarter. Still early-stage but providing concrete commercial traction. Acquisition of Symmetry Systems (access graph for AI agent identity mapping) announced May 21 — technology/talent deal, ARR immaterial, strategically forward-looking.    8. New logo weakness explicitly acknowledged.   Kevin Rubin said in the FY27 commentary: “I did take a tempered view of new logos going into 2027” and “I did acknowledge that’s one area where we haven’t been performing as well as we’d like.” Jay Chaudhry added they have limited coverage in the 2,000-10,000 user segment and are building specific programs to address it. This is the most explicit admission of new logo underperformance management has made.   Call / Filing Nuggets That Matter                   Net new ARR ex-Red Canary tells the real story.   Total net new ARR of $166M (+24%) includes Red Canary. Organic was $153M (+14%). Total organic ARR: +21%. The 24% headline ARR growth obscures the fact that the core business is growing meaningfully slower.        Z-Flex is creating a different RPO dynamic.   RPO of $6.5B (+30%) with 46% current. Z-Flex at 4-year average terms is pulling bookings forward into long-dated commitments. A 60% QoQ surge in Z-Flex TCV suggests backloaded ARR ramps over the next 6-12 months — supportive for revenue visibility but potentially obscuring near-term new logo softness.        FCF quality deserves scrutiny.   Q3 FCF margin was 16% (down from 18% YoY), with management attributing it to “timing of cash collections.” YTD FCF margin is 29% — meaning Q4 FCF will slow further as CapEx surges. CapEx jumped from $18M (2% of revenue) in Q2 to $42M (5%) in Q3, and management guided FY26 CapEx to high single digits. This is a real P&amp;L and cash flow headwind, not one-time noise.        Red Canary will disappear from disclosure in FY27.   Management explicitly stated they “will not be providing separate disclosure of Red Canary in 2027.” This makes the organic vs. inorganic tracking impossible for investors — a potential transparency concern, especially since Red Canary’s net new ARR growth is expected to be slower than the overall business.        EMEA deceleration.   EMEA grew only 16% in Q3, vs. 18% in Q2 and 31% Americas. Jay brushed it off as normal geo rotation, but the divergence between Americas (+31%) and EMEA (+16%) is widening and warrants attention. Jay acknowledged “areas of execution we need to improve.”        Largest ever branch deal.   An 8-figure upsell with a leading healthcare system for 2,000 sites — largest branch deal in ZS history. Zero Trust Branch ARR tripled YoY. But these are still large-deal lumpy wins; the 150%+ APJ deal value growth YoY is similarly lumpy.        The federal deal caveat.   Saket Kalia specifically asked how to size the 8-figure federal agency deal to understand the underlying “flow business.” Kevin confirmed the large part of the deal was renewals already in ARR — so the net new ARR impact was less dramatic than the TCV headline implied.      Q&amp;A Tension:   The sharpest exchange was Ittai Kidron (Oppenheimer) pressing Kevin on whether sales leadership is the   only   factor in the FY27 deceleration, or whether there are other structural issues. Kevin offered two factors: the leadership transition and the pace of SecOps uptake — but notably did not address new logo market share or competitive displacement risk. The Street clearly wanted a quantified counterfactual (”what would guidance have been without the leadership change?”) and never got one. Brad Zelnick asked the same question first and got essentially the same non-answer. That refusal to quantify the leadership impact is a key unresolved overhang.   What the Market May Be Missing                 1. The Z-Flex backlog creates a multi-quarter revenue and ARR bridge that isn’t visible in net new ARR.   $1B+ Z-Flex TCV over 12 months at 4-year average terms means ZS has ~$3.5-4B of uncommitted future ARR sitting in Z-Flex contracts — revenue that ramps as customers activate modules. This is structural forward visibility that cushions downside scenarios. The Street’s concern about FY27 deceleration may underweight the ARR ramp embedded in existing Z-Flex commitments.    2. The FY27 guide may be deliberately sandbagged.   Management acknowledged providing FY27 guidance “far earlier than we would normally do” — which means they’re working with less pipeline visibility than usual. Kevin Rubin explicitly said he’s using a “tempered approach” with room to improve. If the leadership transition resolves in Q1-Q2 FY27 and new logo programs show early traction, the actual FY27 outcome could clear 17-18%. A 100-200bps beat vs. initial guidance is a pattern ZS has established in FY26 — where organic net new ARR of 9.5% was the guide after being raised from 6.7%.    3. Zero Trust Everywhere at 700 enterprises and growing 27% QoQ is a different business than ZIA/ZPA alone.   The 2-3x ARR uplift when customers add Branch + Cloud creates a compounding expansion flywheel. With only 700 out of 4,500+ enterprise customers at this stage, the installed base conversion is the dominant ARR growth engine for the next 2-3 years — one that doesn’t depend on new logos at all. The Street may be underweighting installed base expansion and overweighting new logo underperformance.    4. The Mythos/frontier model AI threat cycle is a structural pull-forward for ZPA/deception tech upsells.   Jay Chaudhry laid out two specific near-term revenue opportunities driven by Mythos: ZPA expansion from partial to full-user coverage, and deception technology (decoys/honeypots). These are fast-close upsells to existing customers — exactly the motion ZS executes well. If even 10-15% of inbound inquiries convert into deals in H1 FY27, the growth trajectory looks meaningfully better than the 16-17% initial guide implies.   2nd-Order Implications                 Chain 1: FY2027 revenue guide at 16-17% → immediate Street estimate revision → multiple compression   FY27 consensus revenue was probably ~$3.95-4.0B (implying ~21% growth). Management guided $3.87-3.93B (16-17% on $3.33B). Estimates likely come down ~5-8%. EPS consensus of ~$4.58 will be revised to ~$4.15-4.40 depending on margin/CapEx assumptions. At $146.24 AH, P/FY27 EPS of ~33-35x — no longer obviously cheap for a business decelerating from 25% to 16-17%. Estimate cuts are the first domino and happen within 24-48 hours post-report.   →   Thesis implication: Short-term multiple compression is likely as targets get revised down from $210-230 range to $150-180.    Chain 2: CapEx inflation (+200bps in FY27) → FCF margin contraction → impairs the FCF yield narrative   At $184.60 pre-earnings, ZS generated ~$770M FCF in FY26 (23% margin on $3.33B). Add +200bps CapEx in FY27 on $3.88B revenue = ~$78M more CapEx = FCF margin compresses ~200bps. At $3.88B × 21% FCF = $815M FCF. FY27 FCF yield at $146.24 AH = $815M / $23.5B equity = 3.5% — not compelling for a growth stock. The FCF story that propped up ZS valuation at prior lows is temporarily impaired.   →   Thesis implication: FCF-based buyers who anchored on the FY26 26-27% margin guide will need to see FY28 normalization to re-enter. This pushes the re-rating horizon out 6-12 months.    Chain 3: Sales leader departures + new logo weakness → extended GTM rebuild → harder-to-verify recovery timeline   ZS spent most of FY25 rebuilding the sales force. The Q3 FY26 result showed real improvement — 6 straight quarters of productivity growth, record Q3 $1M+ deals. The departure of two leaders re-introduces execution uncertainty precisely when the flywheel appeared to be turning. The market has already been burned once by ZS management transitions (the $114 low in April 2026 reflected peak uncertainty). A second cycle of “we’ll rebuild again” will be met with skepticism. The key unresolved question — whether these were performance-related exits or natural attrition — was never answered on the call.   →   Thesis implication: The stock is unlikely to re-rate until the replacement leaders are in place, ramped, and Q1/Q2 FY27 net new ARR shows acceleration. That’s a 3-6 month waiting period at minimum.    Chain 4: Non-seat metered usage at 30%+ of new ACV → growth algorithm becomes harder to model → multiple discount   Investors price in a discount when the growth drivers shift from predictable seat-based ARR to consumption-based metered units (Branch traffic, cloud workloads, AI agent tokens). The upside is higher if usage inflects; the downside is more visible if macro or enterprise AI deployment slows. This is the same dynamic that created P/NTM compression in cloud companies transitioning to consumption. ZS is more advanced in this transition than the market probably appreciates — and the 100%+ ARR growth in metered units is structurally positive, but the uncertainty premium compounds with the FY27 sales disruption story.   →   Thesis implication: Investors will reduce terminal multiple assumptions on valuation uncertainty, partially independent of growth rate. This is a second-order headwind on top of the guide cut.    Estimate revision direction:   Street revenue for FY27 moves from ~$3.97B to ~$3.88-3.90B. EPS moves from ~$4.58 to ~$4.20-4.45. Price targets collapse from $210-230 range to $150-185. This happens quickly (within 3-5 trading days) and represents the most immediate stock pressure beyond the AH move.    Net thesis effect: Thesis under stress.   The long-term platform thesis (ZS as the dominant Zero Trust infrastructure layer for AI-era enterprises) remains intact. The near-term execution thesis — that the sales rebuild would generate accelerating net new ARR through FY26 and into FY27 — is fractured. The magnitude of the FY27 guide cut, combined with two leadership exits, the CapEx headwind, and the explicit new logo gap acknowledgment, constitutes a genuine step-down in conviction. The variant perception that ZS was underearning relative to its opportunity is harder to hold at current multiples.   Valuation                 Base case (2-year, from $146.24 AH):    FY28 revenue of ~$4.46B (+15% after reacceleration from 16-17%), non-GAAP FCF margin of ~24% = ~$1.07B FCF. Exit at 30x FCF (appropriate for a 15-17% grower with platform optionality and net cash balance): implied EV = $32.1B. Plus ~$2.5B net cash (continued generation): equity value ~$34.6B / 165M diluted shares =   ~$210/share  . Annualized IRR from $146.24 over 2 years:   ~19.8%.    Bull case:   FY28 FCF of $1.15B at 35x + $3B net cash = $43.3B / 165M =   ~$262/share → ~34% IRR.    Bear case:   Sales disruption extends, FY27-28 growth stays 13-15%, CapEx pressure compresses FCF margins to 21%. FY28 FCF ~$800M at 22x (re-rated) + $2B net cash = $19.6B / 165M =   ~$119/share → -9% IRR.   Note: the bear case is not about the business collapsing — it is about a re-rating to a lower-growth multiple on compressed FCF.    Reverse 10% hurdle (from $146.24, 2-year):   Required price: ~$177. Required equity value: ~$29.2B. Required EV: ~$27.4B. At 30x FCF: requires ~$913M FCF in FY28. At $4.46B revenue, that is a 20.5% FCF margin — achievable in a normalized CapEx environment. The 10% hurdle is clearable, but requires the CapEx pressure to be transient, not permanent.    Where the current multiple sits:   At $146.24 AH, ZS trades at ~5.9x FY27 revenue and ~33-34x revised FY27 EPS. For context, at the April 2026 low of $114.63, ZS was at ~4.7x FY26 revenue — the cheapest it has traded in this cycle. The AH price of $146.24 is not at that extreme but is closer to the trough valuation zone than to fair value at peak growth. The re-rating catalyst is a credible FY27 net new ARR re-acceleration signal — and that won’t come until Q1 FY27 (October 2026 earnings).    Multiple sensitivity note:   The single variable that drives this stock most is growth rate, not margin. At 20%+ ARR growth, ZS deserves 8-10x NTM revenue; at 16-17%, fair value compresses to 6-7x. The stock at $146.24 is pricing in roughly 16-17% growth with a modest execution discount. There is no automatic cheap case here unless you believe the guide is conservative.                                       Bull/Bear — Key Variable                 Single most important variable: Whether the FY2027 16-17% growth guide is a sandbagged floor that management beats, or a fair reflection of a structural growth deceleration in the core platform.   This matters more than execution on any single product line because it determines (a) whether the stock is a coiled spring at $146 or fairly priced at a lower multiple, and (b) whether investors should underwrite a 3-year re-acceleration thesis or reassess the terminal growth rate permanently.   Why it controls the outcome:   A ZS growing 20%+ deserves 8-9x NTM revenue. A ZS growing 16-17% deserves 6-7x NTM revenue. That single decision swings fair value by roughly $40-60/share. The guide delta vs. Street expectations is unusually large for a company at this stage — it’s not a 1-2pp miss, it’s a 5-8pp structural de-rating.    Bull path: The guide is a heavily sandbagged initial look.   Precedent: ZS entered FY26 with organic net new ARR guidance of 6.7% and ended at 9.5%. The current guide was issued under duress (leadership exits, early in the fiscal year, two quarters away) with Kevin Rubin explicitly noting it was “far earlier than we would normally do.” If (1) the second sales leader replacement lands quickly and the organization stabilizes by Q1 FY27, (2) the Mythos/AI security inbound demand converts into commercial wins in H1 FY27, and (3) Zero Trust Everywhere installed base expansion continues at 25-30% QoQ pace, the actual growth rate could come in at 18-20%. At 18% growth and $146.24 entry: EV/FY27 revenue re-rates from 5.9x toward 7x → stock approaches $165-175.    Bull implied price range:   $175-225 within 12-18 months   if FY27 delivery clears 18-19% and the leadership vacuum is visibly filled.    Bear path: The guide embeds a real structural deceleration that predates the sales exits.   The new logo gap (acknowledged as “limited coverage in the 2,000-10,000 user segment”), the EMEA growth slowdown (+16%), and the tempered Red Canary/SecOps uptake assumptions all point to organic growth challenges that cannot be explained away by two leadership changes. If Q1 FY27 net new ARR (November 2026 earnings) comes in below expectations again — which would happen if the replacement leaders are slow to ramp and AI security deal closures slip — the guide holds, estimates compress further, and the valuation settles at 5-6x NTM revenue. A 5.5x FY27 revenue multiple implies ~$130-135/share.    Bear path:   $110-135   if FY27 delivery confirms the guide, CapEx headwinds persist, and the growth deceleration narrative becomes entrenched. The bear case does not require the business to break — just for investors to re-rate a 15-17% grower with elevated CapEx to a lower terminal multiple.    Why it’s hard to call:   The key uncertainty is not the market opportunity (clearly large and urgency-driven) or the product (genuinely differentiated architecture). It is the repeatability and timing of the sales motion rebuild. ZS has demonstrated it can rebuild sales execution — they spent FY25 doing exactly that and showed real improvement through FY26. But the clock resets with each leadership change, and the exact senior departure details (voluntary/involuntary, territory coverage, named accounts affected) were never disclosed. Without that information, investors cannot accurately size the disruption.    Leading indicators to watch:   (1) Net new ARR in Q4 FY26 — management guided organic at 9.5%, which means the reported number should land ~$155-165M total including Red Canary; a material miss here extends the bear cycle; (2) Whether management provides more granular FY27 segmentation (new logo vs. upsell vs. Red Canary) at Q4 earnings; (3) Zenith Live conference (Las Vegas, June 2026) — first customer/partner event post-earnings, a canary for AI security commercial momentum.
