               August 29, 2026 
 Options imply a 4% move, IV rank is near 79, and call flow is lopsided. Here is what that means for the September 1 release. 
 
 
 Markets don&#8217;t need a blowout quarter from Medtronic on Tuesday. They only need the number to land inside a guidance range management has spent three quarters building credibility around. That distinction shapes everything about how to think about MDT&#8217;s options into September 1. 
 Medtronic reports financial results for its first quarter of fiscal year 2027, which ended July 31, 2026, on Tuesday, September 1. The consensus EPS estimate sits at $1.39, which would mark a 10.3% rise compared to the same quarter of the prior year. Revenue consensus is $9.47 billion, a 10.4% increase year-over-year. Management itself guided Q1 EPS to $1.38–$1.40 back in June, so the Street is essentially sitting on top of the midpoint with almost no cushion built in. 
 The Numbers Behind the Consensus 
 Full-year FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance is $5.90–$6.00, with first-quarter EPS expected in the $1.38–$1.40 range. Tariffs are modeled at roughly $250 million for the year, with approximately $75 million hitting in Q1, and are projected to reduce FY2027 gross margin by about 20 basis points. That tariff line is the key variable. If Medtronic demonstrates any pass-through or absorption improvement versus its own conservative assumptions, a guide raise becomes the base case rather than upside. 
 In Q4 FY2026, the company posted adjusted EPS of $1.55, edging past the $1.54 consensus, while revenue climbed 9.9% year-over-year to $9.81 billion, above the $9.62 billion analysts had expected. Organic revenue growth reached 6.6%, with the Cardiovascular segment leading at 13.8%. The question heading into Tuesday is whether that acceleration in the cardiovascular book, driven by pulsed-field ablation and the Affera platform, carries into the fiscal first quarter.

 On the product front, the commercial ramp of Affera pulsed-field ablation technology in electrophysiology, continued adoption of the Hugo robotic surgery platform, and integration of recently acquired assets including SPR Therapeutics and Scientia Vascular are the key growth vectors. Peers ISRG and BSX provide context: Intuitive Surgical&#8217;s robotic surgery volume growth remains a benchmark for procedural demand, while Boston Scientific&#8217;s PFA positioning puts competitive pressure directly on Affera&#8217;s ramp rate.  Options Market Analysis 
 Options data compiled by Bloomberg shows MDT shares could move approximately 4% when the company reports earnings on September 1 before the market opens. Implied moves for Medtronic earnings over the past two years have ranged between 3.0% and 4.1%. That is a narrow band for a large-cap medtech, and it sets up an important structural observation: the realized move has frequently exceeded it. The largest actual movement in the past eight quarters was an 8.8% jump in November 2025, more than double the 3.6% implied move at that time. 
 IV rank for MDT currently reads about 79%, clearing the model floor for premium selling and reading high versus the one-year range. That level reflects earnings-driven demand for options, but the realized volatility picture complicates the pure premium-selling thesis. The 4% expected move on a stock sitting near $91 equates to roughly a $3.60 band in either direction, giving a practical range of approximately $87.40–$94.60 around the event.

 Risk and Forward Outlook
