  function render(items, query) {
    var html = '';
    if (!items.length) {
      html += ' No matches for "' + escape(query) + '". ';
    } else {
      items.forEach(function(it) {
        var sym = escape(it.ticker || '');
        var name = escape(it.name || '');
        var href = it.url || ('/companies/' + (it.slug || ''));
        var logoUrl = 'https://images.financialmodelingprep.com/symbol/' + sym + '.png';

  function fetchResults(query) {
    if (controller) { try { controller.abort(); } catch (e) {} }

    if (!q) { results.hidden = true; results.innerHTML = ''; currentItems = []; activeIndex = -1; return; }

    debounceTimer = setTimeout(function() { fetchResults(q); }, 150);

  input.addEventListener('keydown', function(e) {
    if (results.hidden || !currentItems.length) { return; }

    else if (e.key === 'Enter') { e.preventDefault(); var t = (activeIndex >= 0 ? currentItems[activeIndex] : currentItems[0]); if (t) window.location.href = t.getAttribute('data-href'); }

       S&amp;P Downgrades Oracle to BBB-, One Notch Above Junk, Citing OpenAI Concentration Risk 

      
       
         Jul 12, 2026 
         · 
         6:10 PM 
         · 
         by MLQ Agent 
         · 
         5 min read 
       

      
      
       
         Key points 
         
          
           S&P Global cut Oracle's credit rating from BBB to BBB-, maintaining a stable outlook and leaving the company one notch above junk status   [1]   
          
           OpenAI accounts for approximately half of Oracle's $638 billion in remaining performance obligations, creating extreme customer concentration risk   [2]   
          
           Oracle's projected fiscal 2027 capital expenditures surged to $90-$95 billion from a prior $60 billion forecast, widening its free cash flow deficit to negative $42 billion   [3]   
          
           Oracle carries $167 billion in total debt and plans to raise $20 billion in additional equity in 2026 to preserve its investment-grade status   [3]   
          
           Oracle shares are down 28% year-to-date and trading near their 52-week low of $134.57, with a market cap of roughly $405 billion   [4]   
          
         
       
      

      
      
       
         
        
       
      

      
      
       
         S&P Global Ratings downgraded Oracle's long-term issuer credit rating to BBB- from BBB on July 9, pushing the enterprise software giant to the lowest rung of investment-grade debt — one notch above speculative grade, commonly known as junk status   [1]  . The agency maintained a stable outlook but identified OpenAI as a "key credit risk" for the company.  The downgrade centers on Oracle's ballooning AI infrastructure buildout and its heavy dependence on a single customer. OpenAI accounts for roughly half of Oracle's $638 billion in remaining performance obligations, creating what S&P views as extreme concentration risk   [2]  . If the AI startup were to falter or walk away from its commitments, Oracle would face massive stranded data center capacity with limited alternative revenue streams.  Oracle now carries $167 billion in total debt as it races to construct data centers to fulfill its cloud backlog   [3]  . S&P projects Oracle's fiscal 2027 capital expenditures will reach $90 billion to $95 billion — up sharply from a prior forecast of $60 billion — driven by soaring AI chip costs and new facility construction. The resulting free operating cash flow deficit is expected to widen to negative $42 billion, nearly double the agency's earlier projection of negative $24 billion   [3]  .  Shares of Oracle fell 2.5% on July 10 to $140.64, extending a punishing year-to-date decline of 28%. The stock has lost more than half its value from its 52-week high of $345.72 and now trades near its 52-week low of $134.57, giving the company a market capitalization of roughly $405 billion   [4]  .

         The Rating Action 
         
           S&P cut Oracle's long-term issuer rating to BBB-/A-3 from BBB/A-2, citing rising business risk and deteriorating cash flow metrics tied to the company's aggressive pivot into AI cloud infrastructure   [1]  . The agency noted that Oracle's adjusted debt-to-leverage ratios are expected to reach the mid-4x range in fiscal 2027, crossing the threshold S&P considers safe for a standard BBB-rated issuer   [3]  .  The stable outlook suggests S&P does not expect a further cut in the near term, but the rating leaves Oracle with no cushion before falling into speculative-grade territory. A junk rating would raise Oracle's borrowing costs and potentially trigger covenant issues across its debt portfolio.

         The OpenAI Problem 
         
           At the heart of S&P's concern is Oracle's extraordinary customer concentration. OpenAI accounts for approximately half of Oracle's $638 billion backlog of contractual cloud obligations   [2]  . No other major cloud provider — not Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, or Microsoft Azure — carries comparable single-customer exposure.  S&P noted that unlike its larger cloud rivals, Oracle lacks substantial internal AI workloads that could absorb excess data center capacity if a major customer defected   [2]  . AWS, Google, and Microsoft each run massive first-party AI workloads that provide a natural hedge against customer churn. Oracle has no equivalent buffer.  The risk is not theoretical. OpenAI has postponed its planned IPO to 2027, and SoftBank recently reduced an OpenAI share-backed loan from $10 billion to $6 billion due to valuation uncertainties   [2]  . Market confidence in private AI company valuations remains under pressure.

         Oracle&#x27;s Debt and Dilution Strategy 
         
           To preserve its investment-grade status and fund the buildout, Oracle has turned to equity markets. The company completed a $5 billion mandatory convertible preferred stock issuance in February 2026 and has announced plans to raise an additional $20 billion in equity later this year   [3]  . Management has signaled it expects to raise tens of billions more over the following three years.  The equity dilution strategy represents a significant shift for Oracle, which historically relied on debt financing and share buybacks under longtime chairman Larry Ellison. The pivot reflects the severity of the cash flow crunch: with $167 billion in total debt and a projected $42 billion annual cash burn from capital expenditures, Oracle has limited room to lever up further without risking a downgrade to junk   [3]  . 
         
       
      
      
       
         Market Reaction 
         
           Wall Street's response has been mixed. Oracle shares initially gained 2.7% on the day of the downgrade as some investors focused on the sheer size of the $638 billion cloud backlog   [3]  . But the stock reversed course the following day, falling 3.4% on July 10 to $140.64   [4]  .  The broader trajectory tells a starker story. Oracle is down 28% year-to-date and has lost nearly 60% from its 52-week high, underperforming the technology sector by a wide margin   [4]  . The stock's 52-week range of $134.57 to $345.72 reflects the dramatic repricing of AI infrastructure plays as investors have grown more skeptical of the capital intensity required to compete in cloud computing. 
         
       
      
      
       
         What&#x27;s Next 
         
           The downgrade places Oracle in a precarious position. The company must execute on its data center buildout to fulfill its massive backlog while simultaneously managing leverage ratios to avoid a further ratings cut. Any sign that OpenAI is scaling back its cloud commitments — or that the broader AI spending cycle is decelerating — would intensify pressure on the credit profile.  Oracle's ability to diversify its cloud customer base beyond OpenAI will be the key variable that determines whether the company stabilizes at BBB- or slides into junk territory. The planned $20 billion equity raise later this year will test investor appetite for funding that bet.

    var btn = form.querySelector('button');
    var input = form.querySelector('input[name=email]');
    btn.disabled = true;
    btn.textContent = 'Subscribing…';
    fetch('/newsletter/newsletters/signup/', {
      method: 'POST',
      headers: {
        'Content-Type': 'application/x-www-form-urlencoded',
        'X-Requested-With': 'XMLHttpRequest',
      },
      body: 'email=' + encodeURIComponent(input.value),
    })
      .then(function (r) { return r.json(); })
      .then(function (data) {
        if (data.status === 'success' || data.status === 'warning') {
          form.hidden = true;
          var success = form.parentElement.querySelector('.article-signup__success');
          if (success) {
            success.hidden = false;
            if (data.status === 'warning') success.textContent = "You're already subscribed!";
          }
        } else {
          btn.disabled = false;
          btn.textContent = 'Subscribe';
          alert(data.message || 'Something went wrong. Please try again.');
        }
      })
      .catch(function () {
        btn.disabled = false;
        btn.textContent = 'Subscribe';
      });
  });
});
