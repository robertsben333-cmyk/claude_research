Oracle (ORCL) options embed a 13% implied move for the September 10 earnings report - the largest implied swing in the company&#x27;s quarterly history.  A 2.2:1 call-to-put ratio signals net bullish positioning, with traders wagering on sustained AI cloud momentum ahead of the print.  Dell&#x27;s record $95 billion AI backlog and $25 billion full-year guidance raise, reported September 1, deliver direct validation of the enterprise AI capex cycle.     Lead 
 Options traders on Friday built the largest implied earnings swing in Oracle Corp.'s history into the near-term options chain, pricing a 13% move in either direction ahead of the  ORCL  first-quarter fiscal 2027 report scheduled for September 10. A 2.2:1 call-to-put ratio across the relevant strike range places the bulk of that positioning on the bullish side, with investors betting that Oracle's cloud infrastructure unit can sustain its 93% growth trajectory against record enterprise demand for AI computing. The setup follows Dell Technologies' (DELL) blockbuster second-quarter results - the most direct confirmation yet that enterprise AI infrastructure capex has moved from commitment to cash. 
 What Is Driving the Record Implied Move? 
 The elevated  implied volatility  reflects a convergence of  execution risk  and upside potential that is singular to this quarter. In Q4 FY2026, reported June 10,  Oracle Cloud Infrastructure  (OCI) revenue reached $5.8 billion, up 93% year-over-year, while total cloud revenues hit a record $9.9 billion, up 47%. The company's remaining performance obligations - contracted but unrecognized future revenue - stood at $638 billion, a figure that expanded $85 billion in a single quarter. OCI's 97.5% global GPU utilization rate confirms that supply continues to trail demand by a wide  margin , sustaining pricing power while also concentrating delivery risk. Against that backdrop, the 13% implied move - wider than the 12% priced before June's print - signals that options markets are assigning a broader range of outcomes to the conversion of backlog into recognized revenue. 
 Why Did Dell's Results Raise the Stakes for Oracle? 
 Dell Technologies (DELL), reporting on September 1, provided the sharpest real-world signal that enterprise AI infrastructure spending is accelerating. The company posted record quarterly revenue of $47 billion, up 58% year-over-year, with AI server orders of $60.9 billion and an AI backlog that surged to a record $95 billion. Management raised full-year revenue guidance by $25 billion to $192 billion and projected that AI server revenue would triple in fiscal 2027. The Infrastructure Solutions Group alone posted $31.8 billion in revenue, up 89%, driven by a 122% surge in traditional server and networking revenue alongside a 26% rise in storage. For Oracle watchers, the relevance is structural: Dell's customers - corporations, governments, and cloud providers investing at scale in AI infrastructure - are the same buyers who sign Oracle Cloud Infrastructure contracts. Dell's record backlog serves as a demand-side confirmation of the same capex wave that produced Oracle's $638 billion  performance obligation .      What Traders Are Watching for September 10 
 Consensus estimates place Oracle's Q1 FY2027 revenue at approximately $19.1 billion, representing roughly 20% year-over-year growth. Cloud infrastructure is the decisive variable, with analysts tracking whether OCI can sustain or extend its 93% growth rate as new data center capacity comes online. AI workloads, multicloud database deployments, and sovereign cloud contracts carry the most pricing and margin uncertainty. Oracle has signaled plans to raise $45 billion to $50 billion in financing during fiscal 2027 to fund infrastructure expansion - the pace and cost of that capital program will draw scrutiny alongside the revenue figures. A further expansion of the remaining performance obligation beyond $638 billion would represent the single most bullish data point the print could produce. 
 Invest in AI - The Broader Infrastructure Test 
 The September 10 print carries weight that extends beyond Oracle alone. Enterprise technology investors tracking where to invest in AI are using quarterly vendor results to determine whether the capex supercycle is durable or front-loaded. Oracle's $638 billion remaining performance obligation -  spread  across cloud infrastructure contracts with governments, financial institutions, and technology companies - makes the Q1 FY2027 result a high-frequency check on whether contracted AI spending is converting to recognized revenue on schedule. Dell's blowout results established the hardware-layer data point; Oracle's cloud infrastructure revenue line is the managed-compute equivalent. 
 Outlook 
 The 13% implied move embedded in ORCL options for September 10 - the largest in the company's quarterly history - marks a moment when the enterprise AI infrastructure trade faces its most consequential near-term test. Dell's record $95 billion AI backlog confirmed that AI demand is intact at the hardware layer; Oracle's cloud infrastructure print will determine whether that demand extends to managed AI compute at cloud scale. The 2.2:1 call-to-put skew reflects a market leaning toward a positive surprise, particularly if OCI revenue growth and a further expansion of the remaining performance obligation confirm that the contracted backlog is converting on schedule and that Oracle's aggressive capital raise is translating into capacity that customers can consume.
