Key Elements      Adobe    Shantanu Narayen    David Wadhwani    Anil Chakravarthy    Steven Day    Dan Durn    SEMrush    Firefly    Acrobat    Adobe Express    Creative Cloud    GenStudio    Adobe Experience Platform    NVIDIA    Cannes Lions                                                                                           Adobe is making its boldest strategic bet in a decade. The creative software giant announced on Thursday it will aggressively expand freemium offerings across its flagship products — Acrobat, Express, and Firefly — absorbing a roughly $500 million hit to second-half annualized recurring revenue in exchange for capturing what CEO Shantanu Narayen called "the next generation of Adobe loyalists." 
 "We are at a transformative moment," Narayen said on the company's Q2 fiscal 2026 earnings call. "The opportunity is to serve billions of business professionals and consumers through a comprehensive freemium funnel." 
 The numbers behind the quarter tell a story of a company performing well by traditional metrics while simultaneously upending its own playbook. Revenue hit $6.62 billion, up 11% year-over-year in both reported and constant currency terms. Non-GAAP earnings per share reached $5.96, an 18% jump from the prior year. GAAP EPS was $4.25, reflecting a $70 million non-cash goodwill impairment charge tied to the legacy Publishing and Advertising unit.

 The ARR figure includes approximately $480 million from the April acquisition of SEMrush. Without it, organic ARR growth would have been closer to 10.5%. 
  The Freemium Pivot: Why Now  
 The strategic shift centers on a fundamental change in user behavior that Adobe's leadership described in unusually candid terms. David Wadhwani, president of Adobe's Digital Media business, explained that users are increasingly arriving at Adobe through "intent-based searches" — queries like "summarize this PDF" or "generate pixel art for social media" — rather than through traditional product discovery paths. 
 "What we see is a shift in — or an emergence in — terms of LLM usage, and that's driving a lot more intent-based search," Wadhwani said. "If we just took that traffic direct to a paid flow to buy Acrobat and download Acrobat, it would not produce as much opportunity long-term for Adobe." 
 The numbers supporting this conviction are striking. Traffic to adobe.com grew over 40% year-over-year, with business professional and consumer traffic up 35% and creative and marketing professional traffic up over 50%. Monthly active users for business and consumer products surpassed 850 million, a 20% increase from a year ago. Creative Freemium MAU jumped from 50 million to 90 million — a 70% surge. 
      
 Firefly is already showing early monetization signals from the approach. Firefly ARR grew approximately 50% quarter-over-quarter, approaching $300 million exiting Q2. Creative agent trials are underway, and the product is now accessible through ChatGPT, Claude, and soon Microsoft Copilot and Google Gemini. 
  Creative Cloud Price Increases Shelved  
 In a related move, Adobe confirmed it is deferring previously planned line optimizations — effectively price increases — for Creative Cloud in the second half of the fiscal year. Narayen described the decision as a matter of focus: "Anything that comes in the way of the company aligning, and the market understanding that we are going to go after that entire creative opportunity right now, I think will detract from what is the real prize for this company." 
 He emphasized the deferral is not permanent. "That one, we can defer. It's not going away," Narayen said. The combined impact on second-half ARR is split roughly evenly between the freemium expansion and the price increase deferral. 
  Segment Performance  
 Business Professionals and Consumers subscription revenue reached $1.85 billion, growing 15% in constant currency. Acrobat AI Assistant ARR tripled year-over-year, and Express MAU grew more than 20% quarter-over-quarter. The newly launched Adobe Productivity Agent transforms Acrobat from a static document viewer into an interactive AI experience. 
 Creative and Marketing Professionals subscription revenue hit $4.54 billion, up 11% in constant currency. GenStudio ARR grew over 25% year-over-year, and Adobe Experience Platform with native apps grew over 30%. The company now processes over 70 billion profile activations per day.

  SEMrush and Brand Visibility  
 The $480 million ARR SEMrush acquisition, which closed in April, is being positioned as a cornerstone of Adobe's brand visibility offering. Anil Chakravarthy, president of Digital Experience, said the combination of SEMrush's "outside-in" search intelligence with Adobe's "inside-out" content management creates what he called "the most comprehensive brand visibility solution in the market." The integrated product will debut at the Cannes Lions Festival of Creativity later this month. 
 Enterprise momentum remains strong. Customers with over $10 million in ARR grew more than 20% year-over-year. Over 1,500 customer trials are underway for agentic web offerings, and AI-first ARR across the company surpassed $500 million, growing 3x year-over-year. 
  Raised Guidance Despite Headwinds  
 Adobe raised its full-year fiscal 2026 targets. Revenue is now expected at $20.5 billion to $20.6 billion, with non-GAAP EPS of $24.35 to $24.45 and a non-GAAP operating margin of approximately 45%. For Q3, the company guided to revenue of $6.67 billion to $6.72 billion and non-GAAP EPS of $6.05 to $6.10. 








































    Metric  FY2026 Guidance  Q3 FY2026 Guidance      Total Revenue  $20.5B – $20.6B  $6.67B – $6.72B    B&#x26;C Sub Revenue  $7.44B – $7.48B  $1.87B – $1.89B    C&#x26;M Sub Revenue  $18.21B – $18.27B  $4.61B – $4.64B    Non-GAAP EPS  $24.35 – $24.45  $6.05 – $6.10    Non-GAAP Op Margin  ~45%  ~44%    Total ARR Growth  10.2% YoY  —    
 The ARR growth target of 10.2% — down from the 12.5% exit rate in Q2 — reflects both the SEMrush addition and the freemium headwinds. Management signaled that ARR phasing will be weighted approximately 40% in Q3 and 60% in Q4, with Q3 bearing more of the freemium transition impact and Q4 benefiting from seasonal enterprise strength. 
  Leadership in Transition  
 The quarter also brought leadership changes. CFO Dan Durn has departed for an opportunity outside the software industry. Steven Day, a 20-year Adobe veteran, will serve as interim CFO. Narayen, who previously announced his intention to transition to board chair, said the CEO search is "progressing well" with the goal of having a successor in place to shape fiscal 2027 planning. 
 "The leadership team that exists in the finance organization is absolutely seasoned and top-notch," Narayen said. "I am confident that we will not miss a beat." 
  Analyst Scrutiny  
 Analysts pressed management on the economics of the freemium bet. Alex Zukin of Wolfe Research asked about the payback period on the roughly $500 million ARR investment. Narayen pointed to Firefly's 50% quarter-over-quarter ARR growth as an early signal: "That will play out, I think, over 2027 and beyond. More important, it sets the company up for the right sort of really growing our customer base, which, like we have done with Reader, pays off for decades." 
 Kirk Materne of Evercore pressed on lifetime value economics. Wadhwani responded that users arriving through intent-based search "tend to have much higher engagement and usage patterns than those that go directly into paid, which translates to long-term lifetime value." 
 Brent Thill of Jefferies asked whether Adobe should be investing even more aggressively. Narayen pushed back, saying the company is "spending the money" on models, marketing, and product: "This is not about balance. This is about saying, be clear about your strategic intent."
