# SGRY — Surgery Partners, Inc.

**Event confirmed: YES.** Q2 2026 results release **before the market open on Monday, August 10, 2026**, conference call **08:30 ET**, per the company's own July 24, 2026 8-K/press release [1][2].

**What this print is about.** This is not a headline-EPS quarter. Surgery Partners is a levered (~$3.71bn gross debt, 4.3x credit-agreement net leverage) short-stay surgical facility operator whose equity has been repriced twice in nine months on guidance credibility — down 25.4% on the Q3 2025 print and down 12.3% on the Q4 2025 print/2026 guide [6][8][11]. Management reset 2026 to revenue $3.35–3.45bn and adjusted EBITDA "at least $530m" on March 2, 2026, then reaffirmed it on May 5 and again on **July 24, 2026 — roughly three and a half weeks after the quarter closed** [1][10][11]. The market therefore already knows the quarter was survivable; what it does not know is (a) whether Q2 landed inside the explicit phasing framework management gave on the Q1 call (Q2 revenue = 24.0–24.5% of the annual target, Q2 adjusted EBITDA = 23.0–23.5% of the annual target [12]), and (b) how the $795m Idaho Falls proceeds get spent — deleverage, buy back stock under the untouched $200m authorization, or fund ASC M&A. Overlaid on that: an activist (Ortelius) publicly demanding the surgical hospitals be sold and management replaced [19], a Bain Capital 38.2% block that walked away from a $25.75 take-private in June 2025 [18], 11.55% of float short with 11.4 days to cover [16], and an industry payer-mix shock (ACA enhanced-subsidy expiry) that is visibly hurting HCA and Tenet outpatient case counts [24][25][26]. The trade is guidance-and-capital-allocation, not EPS.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Pro-forma FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance and the use of the $795m Idaho Falls proceeds — not Q2 EPS.**

Why: on 2026-07-24 Surgery Partners agreed to sell its interests in Mountain View Hospital and Idaho Falls Community Hospital to Intermountain Health, with the two facilities valued at ~$1.15bn and **~$795m of consideration to Surgery Partners** [1][2][3][36][37]. That is ~40% of the current $2.01bn market cap arriving at a company carrying $3,713.9m of debt, $182.3m of cash and 4.3x credit-agreement net leverage (5.1x on a GAAP net-debt/adjusted-EBITDA basis) [10][12]. The company reaffirmed 2026 guidance **"excluding the impact of the transaction"** [1] — meaning the August 10 call is the first scheduled forum where management must (i) size the EBITDA leaving with the hospitals, (ii) restate the FY26 bridge, and (iii) say what the cash does.

**What the market expects, and how I know:**
- A **re-based, lower headline FY26 EBITDA** number that is *mechanically* lower but leverage-accretive. The risk is presentational: a "$530m → lower" headline can be misread as a cut, and this stock has twice gapped double digits on guidance headlines [6][11].
- **Deleveraging plus buyback.** The board authorised **$200m of repurchase on 2026-02-26** and the company bought **zero** in Q1 [11][12]. Ortelius has explicitly demanded monetisation of the hospitals followed by buybacks and debt reduction [19]. With 11.55% of float short and 11.4 days to cover [16], an announced buyback funded from proceeds is the single highest-torque possible announcement.
- **Timing of the promised 2026 investor day**, which management tied to "having something meaningful completed within portfolio optimisation" [12] — Idaho Falls is that event.

**Secondary metric to watch:** same-facility case growth. Q1 2026 was **+0.6%** with revenue/case +3.8% [10]; Q4 2025 was +1.3% on rev/case +2.1% [11]. Volume, not rate, is the disputed variable, and peers just printed *negative* surgical case growth (Tenet USPI same-facility cases −1.2%; HCA same-facility outpatient surgeries −3.4%) [24][25].

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

**Model.** 180 surgical facilities (122 consolidated) as of 2026-03-31, up 16 y/y; JV structure with physician partners, so a large share of facility economics runs through non-controlling interests [10][39]. Revenue is patient service revenue from short-stay surgical facilities plus ancillary (physician practices, urgent care, **anesthesia services**) [39]. Mix is deliberately shifting to higher-acuity musculoskeletal work: **total joints in the ASCs +14.6% y/y in Q1 2026**, ~140 physicians recruited in the quarter, concentrated in orthopaedics, ophthalmology and GI [35].

**Q1 2026 actuals (last print) [10]:**
- Revenue $810.9m, +4.5% y/y; same-facility revenue +4.4% (cases +0.6%, revenue/case +3.8%; total revenue/case $5,142, +6.2%).
- Adjusted EBITDA $102.3m vs $103.9m — **margin 12.6% vs 13.4%**, i.e. ~80bp of y/y compression.
- Net loss $35.9m; GAAP EPS −$0.28; adjusted EPS −$0.03 (vs a −$0.12 street estimate).
- Operating cash flow only **$11.7m**; capex $16.0m → negative free cash flow in the quarter (seasonally weakest quarter).
- Cash $182.3m; total debt $3,713.9m; revolver availability $666.1m; net leverage 4.3x (credit agreement), 5.1x GAAP.
- M&A spend just **$4.2m** against a $200m annual target — deployment is running far behind plan [12][39].

**FY2025 for scale [11]:** revenue $3.31bn (+6.2%), adjusted EBITDA $526.2m (+3.5%), adjusted EPS $0.47, GAAP EPS −$0.61, operating cash flow $274.3m (down from $300.1m). Q4 2025 alone: revenue $885.0m (+2.4%) but adjusted EBITDA **−4.2% to $156.9m**, same-facility cases only +1.3%. That deceleration is the origin of the current derating.

**What changed since the last print:**
1. **Idaho Falls sale signed** (July 21 escrow / July 24 announcement), ~$795m to SGRY, closing "in the coming months" subject to physician-member and board approvals, regulatory clearance and HSR [1][2][36][37].
2. **Guidance reaffirmed on July 24**, ex-transaction [1] — a post-quarter-end reaffirmation, which is meaningful de-risking evidence for Q2 itself.
3. **Known cost headwinds landing in Q2:** reinstatement of incentive compensation (management said pressure begins in Q2 and is "more pronounced in Q3"), new provider taxes in two states plus a 4% Medicaid rate cut in one state (~$2m/quarter, ~$8m FY) [12].
4. **A non-recurring comp:** Q2 2025 results included a portfolio-optimisation transaction that does not repeat in 2026 [12] — a real y/y drag the street may or may not have modelled.
5. **SWB at 30.5% of revenue in Q1 2026**, improved y/y [12] — the labour line is the swing factor for the margin debate.

**At stake:** with 5.1x GAAP leverage and $2.0bn of equity value against $3.7bn of debt, this is an equity stub. A 5% miss to FY EBITDA is roughly a 5% move in enterprise value and something closer to a 13% move in equity value at constant multiple. That mechanical gearing is why the reactions are bimodal.

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

- **The dominant sector variable is the expiry of enhanced ACA premium tax credits.** HCA (reported 2026-07-24) raised its estimated 2026 exchange payer-mix drag from $600–900m to **$1.0–1.2bn**, with exchange equivalent admissions **−15%** and uninsured **+15%** — near one-for-one migration [25][26]. Critically for SGRY, HCA's **same-facility outpatient surgeries fell 3.4%** y/y and inpatient surgeries −2.3%, even as admissions rose 2.5% [25].
- **Tenet/USPI (reported 2026-07-23)** is the closest comparable: USPI revenue +9.3%, adjusted EBITDA +8.8% to $542m at a 39.0% margin, but **same-facility surgical cases −1.2% with revenue per case +6.3%** [24]. USPI stock reaction was strongly positive — THC is +28.3% over the last 20 sessions and +35.8% year-to-date [6]. **Read-through: the ASC model is working, but on acuity and rate, not on volume.** If SGRY prints same-facility cases anywhere near flat with revenue/case up 3–4%, it is *in line with the industry*, not a miss.
- **SGRY's own ACA exposure is limited by construction:** management stated on the Q1 call that it has minimal historical exchange-patient exposure because its facilities are elective and have no emergency departments [12]. Commercial mix was ~50% in Q1 2026, moderating from Q4 2025 pressure but not fully recovered [12]. This is the key differentiator versus HCA/UHS and it cuts in SGRY's favour.
- **Reimbursement:** CMS's CY2027 OPPS/ASC proposed rule (released 2026-07-02) proposes a **+2.4% ASC update** and continues phasing out the Inpatient Only List with additions to the ASC Covered Procedures List — structurally favourable to site-of-service migration into ASCs. Comments close 2026-08-31 [27].
- **Sector regime:** healthcare services staged a sharp Q2 2026 reversal and is now outperforming the S&P 500 and Nasdaq year-to-date, but **hospitals/rehab/LTC remained the laggard at a −4.8% median** as ACA subsidy expiry squeezed volumes [28]. Relative performance since 2026-01-02: THC +35.8%, IWM +13.0%, XLV +6.0%, **SGRY +1.8%**, HCA −16.9%, UHS −18.5% [6]. SGRY is in the middle — it has neither the ASC re-rating THC got nor the exchange punishment HCA took.
- **Rate/FX/commodity sensitivity:** no meaningful FX or commodity exposure (100% US, services). **Rates matter a lot**: $3.71bn of debt against $2.01bn of equity means the equity is a duration-like instrument on credit spreads; the December 2025 refinancing [22] and the Idaho Falls proceeds are both about that. I did not source the fixed/floating split or the maturity ladder — see coverage gaps.
- **Supplier read-through:** ortho device demand healthy (Stryker ortho +9%, Zimmer +4.8%) [29] — supportive of SGRY's MSK acuity strategy.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull.** The bar has been reset to almost nothing and management already told us the quarter was fine. Consensus is $0.02–$0.06 EPS against $0.17 a year ago [13][14], and management's own phasing guide asks for only $122–125m of Q2 adjusted EBITDA versus $129.0m last year [12][17] — a guided *decline*. Guidance was reaffirmed on 2026-07-24, three and a half weeks after quarter-end, with the quarter's books effectively in hand [1]. Meanwhile $795m of cash is inbound against a $2.01bn market cap [1][2], the $200m buyback authorisation is entirely unused [11][12], an activist is publicly demanding exactly that deployment [19], ~20% of voted shares withheld from the Bain-linked directors in June [21], and 11.55% of the float is short with 11.4 days to cover [16]. The nearest comparable, Tenet's USPI, just printed +8.8% EBITDA growth at a 39% margin and re-rated hard [24][6]. Aug-21 call-side skew is 23 vol points above put skew at the $15 strike and 1,501 upside calls traded on 2026-08-07 [7]. A clean quarter plus a credible capital-allocation statement is a squeeze.

**Bear.** This management team has destroyed guidance credibility twice in nine months — −25.4% and −12.3% [6][9] — and the market's default response is to sell first. The specific Q2 headwinds are known and unmodelled by many: incentive comp is reinstated starting this quarter, new provider taxes and a Medicaid rate cut cost ~$2m/quarter, and Q2 2025 contained a portfolio-optimisation benefit that does not repeat [12]. On top of that, the one piece of alt-data available says SGRY was **the only operator among peers with worsening nursing openings and bonus metrics through Q2** [13] — an idiosyncratic labour-cost signal in the exact line item (SWB, 30.5% of revenue [12]) that drove the Q4 2025 margin collapse. Q1 already showed 80bp of y/y margin compression on +0.6% same-facility cases [10], and peers just printed negative case growth [24][25]. Operating cash flow was $11.7m against $16.0m of capex in Q1 [10]. Guidance is explicitly back-end weighted, so an in-line Q2 leaves the entire year's risk stacked into H2 — and re-basing FY26 for the Idaho Falls disposal gives the company an easy, headline-friendly excuse to lower the number. At 5.1x GAAP leverage the equity is a stub; a 5% EBITDA disappointment is a low-double-digit equity move.

**Base case (mine).** A modestly in-line quarter — revenue $825–840m, adjusted EBITDA $122–127m, same-facility revenue +3.5–4.5% on roughly flat cases — accompanied by a re-based-but-not-cut FY26 framework and, most importantly, a capital-allocation statement on the $795m. That is a small-move outcome (four of the last seven reactions were under 2%). The tilt is slightly positive because the post-quarter-end July 24 reaffirmation genuinely de-risks the print, the estimate bar is at a two-year low, and the short base is unusually large and slow-moving into a catalyst that is fundamentally about giving cash back. But I hold that view with limited conviction: the base rate here is 6-of-7 down, and this company has repeatedly found a way to disappoint on the guidance line rather than the reported line.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **an FY2026 adjusted EBITDA number below ~$500m ex-Idaho Falls, or a refusal to quantify the Idaho Falls EBITDA at all.** Concretely: if management re-bases guidance and the implied *organic* run-rate (i.e. after adding back a stated Idaho Falls contribution) is lower than the $530m floor set on March 2, the market will read it as the third consecutive guidance failure and the stock trades to the low end of its 52-week range regardless of the Q2 print. The tell to watch on the call is whether the company gives an explicit Idaho Falls EBITDA figure and an explicit pro-forma leverage target. If it does not, assume the H2 bridge is in trouble.

The mirror-image flip on the upside is a **specific, sized buyback commitment funded from the proceeds** (e.g. an upsized authorisation or a stated ASR) announced on the same release. With 14.8m shares short, 11.4 days to cover and a 38.2% strategic holder shrinking the free float [16][20], that single sentence is worth more than the quarter.

A third, lower-probability flip: **SWB as a percent of revenue above ~31.5%** in Q2, which would corroborate the worsening-nursing-openings alt-data [13] and re-open the structural margin bear case that the March derating was supposed to have priced.
