## 3. The one metric that matters

**It is the FY2026 revenue guide, and specifically whether management holds the implied second-half re-acceleration.** Not EPS — the Q1 print was a small EPS *miss* on the Nasdaq basis (-0.84%) and the stock still gapped **+14.17%**, because Migoya said Q1 "appears to mark the trough of this cycle" and guided Q2 back to sequential growth [21][8][11].

**The arithmetic (my calculation, from sourced components):**

| Component | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 2026 revenue (actual) | $607.1M | [4] |
| Q2 2026 revenue (guide midpoint) | $613.0M | [4] |
| → H1 2026 implied | **$1,220.1M** | calc |
| H1 2025 actual (Q1 $611.4M implied from -0.7% YoY, Q2 $614.18M) | ~$1,225.6M | [4][12] |
| → H1 2026 YoY | **-0.4%** | calc |
| H2 2025 actual (Q3 $617.14M + Q4 $612.47M) | $1,229.6M | [12] |
| H2 2026 required at FY low ($2,462M) | $1,241.9M → **+1.0% YoY** | calc |
| H2 2026 required at FY midpoint ($2,485M) | $1,264.9M → **+2.9% YoY** | calc |
| H2 2026 required at FY high ($2,508M) | $1,287.9M → **+4.7% YoY** | calc |

To hit the midpoint, Q3 and Q4 must average **~$632M**, ~+3.1% sequentially versus the Q2 guide midpoint — and Q4 is seasonally the *softer* quarter (Q4-25 $612.5M < Q3-25 $617.1M [12]), so Q3 alone would need to be roughly $635–645M. That is a step-change from a business that has printed $607–617M every quarter for six quarters.

**What management said it rests on** (Q1 call, 14 May 2026) [8]:
- Urthiague: sequential growth driven by "4 large customers with whom we have already signed contracts" now scaling, plus **higher working days in H2**.
- Urthiague on the downside case: the **lower end of the FY range "would imply a significant deterioration"** of the Middle East business (~2% of revenue).
- Migoya: AI Pods ARR **$32.8M as of March**, pipeline **$352M**, embedded in **40% of top-20 accounts (up from 30%)**, "clear path to coverage in 70% of our top 20 clients."
- Top-50 clients +5.2% YoY; top-10 +4% YoY.
- Revenue per Glober "north of $90,000, +8% YoY."

**How I know the market is watching this and not EPS:** the direction of the last two reactions inverted precisely when the guidance narrative inverted, while EPS surprises stayed small and mixed [21][11]; William Blair's 30 June downgrade to Market Perform was explicitly on "reduced visibility for a second-half growth reacceleration" [24]; and Citi, Deutsche Bank, Wells Fargo and TD Cowen all cut targets in the 8–27 July window without changing the near-quarter estimate materially [22][26].

**Secondary metric to watch:** AI Pods ARR. The June/July Anthropic and Vercel alliances and the 6 August Glob.AI launch (pay-per-output / consumption pricing rather than per-seat or per-hour) have made this the number the equity story now hangs on [9][10][27]. A print that shows ARR compounding from $32.8M toward, say, $60M+ with pipeline conversion would let management defend the FY guide on mix rather than volume.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull.** The setup is a compressed spring. 9.15M shares short (~21% of shares outstanding, 25.08% of float, 7.16 days to cover) sit against a $1.67B market cap trading at 5.93x forward EPS and 4.95x EV/EBITDA, with $36.1M of quarterly FCF and a live buyback worth ~7.5% of market cap over six quarters [11a][14][4][8]. The Q2 bar is beatable: guidance midpoint $613M sits at consensus, and Globant has printed at or above the top of its own revenue guide in each of the last four quarters [4][12]. Management named a specific downside trigger — "significant deterioration" in the Middle East — and the Middle East visibly de-escalated in the week before the print [8][34]. A director bought 25,000 shares on the open market at $38.87 on a discretionary, non-10b5-1 basis, essentially at today's price, and no insider has sold since [39]. Pzena went from 6.9% to 8.9% of the company between March and June [35][36]. The AI Pods flywheel is producing verifiable commercial artefacts, not just slides: Anthropic and Vercel alliances, the Glob.AI output-priced marketplace, and a FIFA win announced the day before the print [9][10][27][30][31]. The last two prints, off a reset bar, gapped +6.17% and +14.17% [11]. If management holds the FY range and shows AI Pods ARR compounding well beyond $32.8M, the covering alone can carry this through the 13.9% implied move.

**Bear.** The FY guide is arithmetically a promise Globant probably cannot keep. Holding the $2,485M midpoint requires H2 revenue of $1,264.9M, +2.9% YoY, versus H1 2026 running at -0.4% YoY — a ~330bp acceleration in a quarter where the two closest large peers both told the market the opposite [4][12][16][17]. Accenture's bookings fell year-on-year and its stock had a record one-day decline; EPAM beat on everything except the year and still cut FY revenue growth explicitly because legacy-services replacement by AI work is running *slower* than hoped and large AI deals now push meaningful revenue into H1 2027 [16][17][19]. Estimate revisions are 0-up / 2-down on every horizon over four weeks [21]. Three of six recent sell-side actions carry price targets at or below spot ($33 Deutsche, $37 Citi, $39 Itaú) [22][26]. The demand evidence inside the company is not improving: headcount -8.3% YoY, attrition up from 9.5% to 13.6%, $1M+ accounts down from 341 to 333, top-10 clients growing only 4% [4][8][29]. And the stock has already rallied +25.29% in a month and +39.7% off its low on narrative, not numbers [11] — in the two prior instances where Globant entered a print with a flat-to-up ten-session tape, it gapped -27.81% and -23.61% [11]. The options are call-skewed in September and the put/call OI is 0.755, meaning the marginal holder is long, unhedged and recently arrived [13].

**Base case (mine).** Q2 revenue prints at or slightly above the top of guide (~$616–620M) with adjusted EPS at or a cent or two above $1.50 and adjusted operating margin in the 14–15% band, and AI Pods ARR is disclosed at a number well above $32.8M with a bigger pipeline figure — all of which is *necessary and not sufficient*. The decision variable is the FY revenue range. My read is that management most likely **narrows the FY range upward at the bottom and trims or holds the top** (e.g. to something like $2,470–2,495M), presenting it as "reaffirming the midpoint" — the exact move they made in May. Whether that trades up or down depends on the Q3 guide embedded in it: a Q3 guide of ~$630M+ validates the acceleration and squeezes; a Q3 guide of $618–625M tells the market the H2 hockey stick has quietly become an H1-2027 hockey stick, exactly as EPAM said, and the +25% run unwinds. Given that peer evidence, revision direction and the run-up regime all point the same way, and only positioning and valuation point the other, I lean **modestly negative with low-to-medium conviction**: preliminary direction score **-18**, prob(up) **43%**, with the realistic distribution being fat-tailed and roughly `-20% / +18%` at the wings rather than a tidy ±13.9%.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The single most credible reversal is **a Q3 2026 revenue guide of $632M or higher accompanied by an unchanged or raised FY2026 top end and an AI Pods ARR above ~$70M.** That combination would do three things at once: it would confirm that the four already-signed large contracts Urthiague cited are scaling on schedule [8]; it would make Globant the first of the three large listed IT-services names this cycle (after Accenture and EPAM) to *raise* rather than trim, which reprices the entire "AI eats billable hours" thesis for the group; and it would force covering from a short base equal to a fifth of the shares outstanding into a stock with 1.6M shares of average daily volume [11a][11]. In that scenario the 13.9% implied move is far too small and a +20–25% session is realistic, because the sell-side has to move estimates *up* from a Hold-rated, 5.9x-forward base with a $47 consensus target [14][21].

The mirror image — the specific thing that would confirm the bear case rather than merely disappoint — is management **withdrawing or materially lowering the FY revenue range while attributing it to pipeline conversion timing rather than the Middle East**. Urthiague pre-committed the Middle East as the named cause of a low-end outcome [8]; if the cause given is instead broad pipeline conversion or North America, then the trough call from May was wrong on substance and every downgrade note from July gets a second leg, with $33 (Deutsche) as the visible marker below.

A third, lower-probability flipper: **disclosure of a large output-priced Glob.AI contract with committed minimums.** Everything announced since June — Anthropic, Vercel, FIFA — has been architecture and logos with no dollars attached [9][27][30][31]. One contract with a disclosed committed value would convert the AI Pods line from a $32.8M ARR curiosity into a backlog item, and would let the market underwrite FY2027 rather than argue about Q3.
