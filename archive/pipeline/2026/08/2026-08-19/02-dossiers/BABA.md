# BABA — Alibaba Group Holding Limited

**What this print is about.** Alibaba reports the June 2026 quarter (fiscal Q1 FY2027) before the US open on Thursday 20 August 2026, with the call at 07:30 ET. This is not an EPS print — non-GAAP EPS has collapsed by design and consensus already models a ~27% YoY decline. It is a **verification print on a pre-announcement**. On 8 July 2026 Alibaba held an analyst briefing at which, per local media (Jiemian) reporting picked up by Investing.com, it indicated that June-quarter instant-commerce losses had narrowed materially and that group profitability was "steady"; the ADR rose ~11–12% that day, Citi and UBS lifted fiscal-Q1 cloud growth forecasts to ~45%, and the stock has since travelled from a 26 June 52-week low of $91.99 to $128.15 (+39%). So the two questions the tape will trade on are (1) does the quick-commerce loss actually print near the ~RMB10bn level that was briefed, down from ~RMB16bn in the March quarter, and (2) does Cloud Intelligence external revenue clear the *raised* ~45% bar with an improving cloud EBITA margin and a credible AI-ARR trajectory (management targeted RMB10bn / ~US$1.4bn annualised in the June quarter, RMB30bn by year-end). The asymmetry cuts both ways: options price ~6.6% versus an 8-quarter realised mean absolute reaction of 7.8%, but the good news has been pre-briefed and the stock has already been paid for it, while the China consumer backdrop deteriorated sharply through the quarter (618 online GMV +4% vs +15.2% a year earlier; July retail sales +0.6% vs +1.5% expected) and the market has just punished Baidu (-13% on 18 Aug) for exactly the "AI cloud growth cannot offset core weakness" shape.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Consolidated adjusted EBITA, and specifically the quick-commerce loss line that drives it — read against the ~RMB10bn number Alibaba itself briefed analysts on 8 July.**

Why this and not cloud or EPS: the entire 2026 drawdown was a margin story, not a growth story. March-quarter consolidated adjusted EBITA fell **84% YoY to RMB5,102mn** with the group margin at ~2%, and non-GAAP net income was **RMB86mn — a 100% decline** [11][59]. Alibaba explicitly attributed it to "investment in technology businesses, quick commerce and user experiences" [11]. FY2026 free cash flow was an **outflow of RMB46,609mn**, with the March quarter alone an outflow of RMB17,300mn versus a RMB3,743mn inflow a year earlier [11]. The stock's 39% rally off the June low began on the single day (8 July) that Alibaba told analysts this line was mending [19]. So the market is not asking "is cloud growing" — it has decided cloud is growing. It is asking "was the July briefing true, and is the trough behind us."

**What the market expects, and how I know:**
- Quick-commerce loss **~RMB10bn**, down from ~RMB16bn sequentially, per the Jiemian-sourced 8 July briefing reporting that Investing.com and 24/7 Wall St. carried, and which coincided with an 11–12% single-day move — the largest since September per contemporaneous reporting [19][58].
- Corroborated bottom-up: quick-commerce revenue was RMB19,988mn (+57%) in the March quarter and RMB78,520mn (+47%) in FY2026, with management stating "unit economics and average order value steadily improved quarter-over-quarter" [11].
- Corroborated by peer: **JD.com's Q2 2026 (13 Aug)** showed New Businesses operating loss narrowing to **RMB9.9bn from RMB14.8bn** a year earlier, with "continued narrowing of loss at JD Food Delivery" and the CEO calling it "a clear inflection in our profit trajectory" [36]. **Meituan's** Q1 2026 operating loss had already narrowed sharply to RMB6.5bn [64] (`snippet_only`) and Meituan does not report Q2 until 2 Sep 2026 [63] — so Alibaba reports into a peer set that has already confirmed the subsidy war is de-escalating, but before the direct competitor's own numbers.

**Secondary metric with near-equal weight: Cloud external revenue growth vs the raised ~45% bar, plus the AI-ARR confirmation.** The Street moved this bar *up* on a leak in July; a 41–43% print would be a growth acceleration on the reported number and simultaneously a miss versus positioning. Watch also the cloud EBITA margin against Citi's 11.5% [17], because Baidu's 18 August lesson was that AI-cloud revenue growth alone (+50% infra, +283% GPU cloud) did not stop a 13% drawdown when the core line was weak [39][40].

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Alibaba already told analysts on 8 July that June-quarter instant-commerce losses narrowed toward ~RMB10bn from ~RMB16bn and that group profitability was steady [19]; JD's 13 August print independently confirmed the loss cycle turning (New Businesses loss RMB9.9bn vs RMB14.8bn, "clear inflection") [36]; Citi and UBS have cloud at ~45% growth with cloud EBITA margin ~11.5% versus 40% external growth and +57% EBITA last quarter [17][11]; the 30-day EPS revision is **+5.8%**, i.e. the estimate impulse is positive [14]; short interest is negligible at 1.75% of shares out with ~3.5 days to cover, so a beat faces no supply from covering [5][6]; the options market prices 6.6% against an 8-quarter realised mean of 7.8% and median 9.3%, and BABA has exceeded implied in 5 of 8 and delivered +14.9%, +14.3% and +10.2% moves in that window [7][8]; and management has an unused shareholder-return lever (buyback ran at only US$1.0bn in FY2026 versus a US$19.1bn remaining authorisation) plus a US$1.5bn+ Lingxi Games disposal to point at [11][53][46]. If consolidated adjusted EBITA rebounds from RMB5.1bn toward the ~RMB25bn adjusted net income the Street models [13] and Eddie Wu can put a number on AI ARR at or above RMB10bn [23], the stock re-rates off a 14x forward multiple [6] toward a consensus PT that sits 48% above spot [3][6].

**Bear case.** The good news is pre-briefed and pre-paid: the stock is +39% off the 26 June low and +2.76% on 18 August into the event [3][4][6], the Street has *raised* cloud to 45% on a leak [17], and three desks have raised targets in the last week [16] — so the bar is now the raised bar, not the May bar. Underneath, the demand backdrop deteriorated inside the quarter: 618 online GMV growth collapsed to +4% from +15.2% [43][44] and July retail sales printed +0.6% against +1.5% expected [42], which lands directly on CMR — already only +1% reported last quarter [11]. Baidu's 18 August −13% is the template for what happens when AI-cloud growth (+50%) meets a weak core (−19% ads) [39][40]. Alibaba has missed non-GAAP EPS in each of the last four quarters, with the March quarter missing by ~89% (RMB0.62 vs RMB5.74) [14]. FY2026 free cash flow was **−RMB46.6bn**, capex RMB126.1bn, and management has said it will *overshoot* the RMB380bn plan and sustain investment intensity for two more years [11][22][59] — the exact message Tencent was marked down for (capex +190%, FCF −RMB13.8bn, −26% YTD) [41]. Buyback support has effectively been withdrawn (US$1.0bn in FY26 vs US$11.9bn in FY25) [11]. And the President sold his direct position down to 28,000 shares on 29 June in a **non-10b5-1 Rule 144** sale, days after the low and a week before the positive briefing [24]. Overlay unresolved 1260H litigation with no hearing date [33][34] and the Anthropic distillation allegation [31][32].

**Base case (mine).** A quarter that is *fine on the pre-briefed lines and unremarkable on the multiple*: revenue near RMB268–272bn, cloud external growth in the low-to-mid 40s, quick-commerce loss at or slightly better than RMB10bn, consolidated adjusted EBITA sharply higher sequentially from the RMB5.1bn trough but still well down YoY, and a forward message that reiterates heavy AI investment without quantifying the payback date. In that world the print is a modest positive that the stock struggles to monetise because positioning is already one-sided long with no short base [5][6][48] — a 0% to +5% day, with the fat left tail coming from CMR/GMV given the macro [42][43] and the fat right tail from an AI-ARR or capital-return surprise [23][53]. Given the bimodal history (four of eight prints >8%, three <2.5%) [8], I expect a large move more than a small one, but I do not have a confident direction. **Preliminary: direction score +10, prob_up 55, conviction Medium.**

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The single most credible reversal is **a capex/investment-envelope upgrade delivered without a monetisation clock.** Consensus right now is "the margin trough is behind us and the AI story is free optionality," which is why 47 of 50 analysts are at Buy [13] and targets are being raised into the print [16]. That view breaks concretely if, on the 07:30 ET call, Eddie Wu or Toby Xu (a) puts a number above RMB126bn on FY2027 capex or explicitly raises the RMB380bn three-year commitment [22], while (b) declining to reaffirm the RMB30bn year-end AI ARR target [23], and (c) CMR prints negative or barely positive on a reported basis against a 618 quarter that grew only 4% [43][44]. That combination converts "investment with a return" into "open-ended spend against a decelerating core" — precisely the frame that cost Baidu 13% on 18 August [39][40] and Tencent 26% year-to-date [41] — and it would reprice a 14x forward multiple [6] as cheap-for-a-reason rather than cheap.

The mirror-image flip, less likely but higher-payoff: **quick-commerce losses print materially *below* RMB10bn with an explicit breakeven quarter named, plus a re-upsized buyback.** The company has US$19.1bn of authorisation it has barely touched [53] and US$75.5bn of cash and liquid investments [11]. Pairing a named breakeven date with a capital-return signal would answer both the margin and the FCF objections at once, and with only 1.75% short interest the move would have to come from re-rating rather than covering — i.e. it would be gappy and durable rather than a one-day squeeze.
