# HOV — Hovnanian Enterprises, Inc.

**What this print is about.** Hovnanian reports fiscal Q3 2026 (quarter ended 31 July 2026) before the open on Thursday 20 August 2026, with the call at 11:00 a.m. ET [1]. This is not an EPS print — sell-side coverage is effectively one analyst and the published revenue consensus ($628.2M) sits *below* the low end of the company's own guidance ($650–750M) [8][11], so a headline "beat" is close to mechanical. The trade is the **fourth-quarter guide**. On 21 May 2026 Ara Hovnanian committed publicly to "a significant step-up in our fourth quarter performance, particularly in volume and gross margins," driven by deliveries from newer, higher-margin communities [5]. HOV guides only one quarter forward, so on 20 August the company must put a number on that promise against a lapping comp of $817.9M of Q4 FY25 revenue [12], and it must do so days after July single-family housing starts collapsed 9.9% m/m to an 808k SAAR — the weakest pace since 2022 and −15.7% y/y [22]. ## 3. The one metric that matters

**The fiscal Q4 2026 guidance package — specifically the revenue midpoint and the adjusted gross margin range — read against management's own 21 May promise of "a significant step-up in our fourth quarter performance, particularly in volume and gross margins."** [5]

Why this and not EPS:
- HOV's GAAP EPS is close to un-forecastable and has been distorted two quarters running. Q1 FY26's headline $2.62 diluted EPS was built on a **$26.8M non-cash gain on consolidation of joint ventures** disclosed in a footnote to the income statement — against total GAAP pretax income of $28.7M for the quarter [19]. Crucially, the company's *adjusted* pretax figure of $31.1M, described as "above the high end of the guidance range," also included that gain, because the adjustment only strips land-related charges and debt extinguishment [19]. Prior-year Q1 FY25 similarly carried a $22.7M gain on contribution of assets to a JV [19]. Ex-gain, Q1 FY26 adjusted pretax was roughly $4.3M (my calc). The market has learned to look past the EPS line.
- Q2 FY26 GAAP pretax was $0.3M and net loss $0.6M, while adjusted pretax was $9.1M — an $8.8M wedge of inventory impairments and land option write-offs, up from $3.1M a year earlier [10][17]. Land charges are running hot; GAAP EPS can be negative even on an in-line adjusted quarter.
- Because there are only ~6.4M weighted diluted shares and a fixed **$2.669M/quarter preferred dividend** on the 7.625% Series A (5,600 shares × $25,000 liquidation preference) [17][19], every $10M of pretax swing is roughly $1.10–1.50 of EPS. EPS is a leveraged, noisy readout of a small pretax number.

**What the market expects for the Q4 guide, and how I know.** No published Q4 guidance estimate exists that I could source. The observable anchors are: (a) management's own words on 21 May — "significant step-up… particularly in volume and gross margins" and "we expect a rebound in adjusted pretax income during the fourth quarter" [5][10]; (b) the Q4 FY25 comp of $817.9M revenue [12]; (c) Wedbush's Q4 FY26 EPS estimate of **$1.16** [9], which back-solves to roughly $10–12M of net income to common, i.e. ~$18–20M of pretax — a genuine step-up from the $0–10M Q3 range. **My read: the bar for Q4 guidance is adjusted pretax income of roughly $15–25M and revenue with an 8-handle. That is the number to watch.** Labelled as my inference.

Secondary metrics, in order: (2) Q3 adjusted gross margin vs. the 14–15% guide; (3) domestic contracts and contracts-per-community for the May–July quarter, plus any August month-to-date colour; (4) community count trajectory — flat at 125 consolidated / 148 including JVs for four straight quarters, with the CFO conceding "we have continued to have challenges with getting communities open timely" [5]; (5) buyback pace against the $67.4M remaining authorisation [17].

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The published revenue consensus of $628.2M is *below* the low end of HOV's own $650–750M guide [8][11], so a revenue "beat" is nearly arithmetic, and HOV has met or exceeded its own guidance in each of the last three quarters — with adjusted GM above the high end in Q2 [10] and adjusted pretax/EBITDA above the high end in Q1 [19]. The margin-recovery mechanism is now independently corroborated: Beazer's June quarter showed adjusted gross margin **+130bp sequentially** on "reductions in direct construction costs and a larger share of closings from newer, higher-margin communities" [20], and Lennar's incentive rate fell to 12.9% from 14.1% with management calling it "a trend" [42] — matching HOV's own −70bp sequential incentive decline to 11.9%, its first drop in nearly two years [5]. If Q3 adjusted GM prints at or above 15% and management puts a genuine Q4 number behind the promised "significant step-up" — Wedbush's $1.16 Q4 EPS implies ~$18–20M of pretax [9] — then the stock is being handed a validated inflection at 1.06x book with $442M of liquidity, no senior maturity before 2031 [17], and **$67.4M of buyback authorisation equal to 9% of the market cap** [17], on a 4.3M-share float. The two most recent prints delivered +2.6% and +17.8% [own calc from 30]. A 15%+ up-day is entirely available.

**Bear case.** Management's "significant step-up in the fourth quarter" [5] was promised on 21 May, when the June-quarter tape looked survivable. Since then July single-family starts fell 9.9% m/m to the weakest pace since 2022, −15.7% y/y [22], existing-home sales fell 1.7% [41], the HMI has now spent sixteen straight months below 40 with 35% of builders still cutting prices [39][40], and mortgages sit at 6.67% after touching an 11-month high [37][38]. HOV must guide Q4 against an **$817.9M** revenue comp [12] with community count flat for four quarters and the CFO conceding continued difficulty opening communities on time [5], and controlled lots down 20.8% y/y [10]. The precedent for a light forward guide is precise and brutal: on 4 December 2025 the Q1 FY26 guide of $550–650M / 13–14% GM took the stock down **22.51% in one session** [own calc from 30]. This time the stock is *not* washed out — it is +32.3% in three months versus ITB +11.7% [30], sitting at **1.06x book against the 0.8x book Dream Finders just paid for Beazer** [20][21][30]. Reported GAAP results carry a phantom-stock headwind the guidance explicitly excludes (stock closed the fiscal quarter at $123.90 vs the $112.44 guidance baseline, +10.2%) [10][30], land charges have tripled y/y to $8.75M [17], and the last two "beats" were flattered by a $26.8M non-cash JV consolidation gain that ran through both GAAP and adjusted pretax income [19]. The CEO sold 12,880 shares at $125.78 on 22 June with no 10b5-1 designation, and a director trimmed at $142.57 three days later [16]; no insider bought.

**Base case.** HOV clears the low published revenue bar and lands adjusted gross margin inside the 14–15% guide — most likely near the top, on the Beazer read-through — with adjusted pretax somewhere in the $3–10M range and headline GAAP EPS a small number of either sign once preferred dividends, land charges and phantom stock are absorbed. The stock then trades entirely on the Q4 FY26 guide and on the August order commentary. My read is that management softens the Q4 language a notch relative to May — not abandoning the step-up, but qualifying it — because the July demand data forces them to, and because they have already conditioned the market with "it's difficult to provide meaningful visibility beyond the next quarter" [10]. Combined with a 32% three-month run-up, a 1.06x book multiple that the Beazer deal comp does not support, and a base rate of five negative reactions in seven quarters, I lean modestly negative into a move whose realised magnitude has averaged 12.3%. **Direction score −15, probability of an up move 44%, conviction Low** — the absence of any options-implied anchor and the presence of exactly one contributing analyst mean the distribution here is genuinely fat-tailed in both directions.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **a large, disclosed Q3 share repurchase paired with a concrete Q4 revenue guide of $800M+ and an adjusted gross margin guide with a 15-handle.** Here is why it is credible rather than hopeful: HOV bought 90,507 shares — 1.8% of the Class A count — in a single quarter at an average of $104.60 [10]; the Board topped the authorisation up by $50M on 27 February 2026 to $67.4M remaining [17]; the CFO said on the record that more repurchases could occur "if land opportunities remain insufficient" [5]; liquidity was $442M against a $170–245M target [10]; and the stock traded as low as $98.01 during the May–July fiscal quarter [30]. If management discloses that it retired, say, 3–5% of the float in Q3 at an average in the $110s *and* backs the Q4 step-up with numbers, the bear framing collapses in one move: the buyback proves management believes the book value, the guide validates the land-vintage margin thesis that Beazer already corroborated [20], and a 4.3M-share float with 7.8% short interest [13] and no options market to absorb the flow has to reprice upward violently. The 21 May 2026 print — +17.80% on a quarter that showed a GAAP net *loss* [10] — is the existence proof.

The mirror-image flip, which would harden the bear case into a large down move, is management explicitly deferring the Q4 step-up into fiscal 2027, or guiding Q4 revenue below roughly $750M. That is the 4 December 2025 setup, and it was worth −22.51%.
