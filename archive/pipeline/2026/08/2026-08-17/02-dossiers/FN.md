# FN — Fabrinet

**Event confirmed: `true`.** Fabrinet reports Q4 and full-year FY2026 (quarter ended
26 June 2026) **after the close on Monday 17 August 2026**, with the call at 5:00 p.m.
EDT (21:00 UTC). Confirmed from the company's own GlobeNewswire release dated 27 July
2026 [1] and mirrored on Fabrinet's IR press-release page [2].

**What this print is about.** Fabrinet is not really a story about the June quarter. It
guided Q4 revenue to $1.25–1.29B and non-GAAP EPS to $3.72–3.87 back on 4 May [3], the
Street has parked itself at the *top* of both ranges ($1.28B / $3.85 [4]), and the
company's average EPS surprise over the trailing four quarters is a mere 2.64% [4]. So
the June quarter is close to pre-scored. What is genuinely undecided is (a) whether the
**datacom** line — which grew only 4% YoY and *fell 6% sequentially* in Q3 FY26 because
Fabrinet could not source lasers, memory and certain ASICs [5][6] — re-accelerated as
the two new 800G hyperscale programs began ramping, and (b) what the **Q1 FY2027 guide**
looks like against a Street that now models $5.66B of FY27 revenue [7]. The context is
brutal for a low-margin name: in the six sessions before this print, Lumentum reported a
109%-growth blowout and rose ~15% [8], while Coherent beat, guided Q1 revenue to
$2.2–2.4B against a $2.05B quarter, and *still fell* 4–6% [9][10][11]. Fabrinet is the
same AI-optics demand curve expressed through a 12.1% gross margin [5] and a $41M
nine-month free cash flow [5]. The market has already voted on that distinction: the
stock is ~24% below its 14 May 2026 high of $748.89 [12][13] and Needham cut its target
from $800 to $650 on 22 July [14].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Datacom revenue and the Q1 FY27 revenue guide — specifically, evidence that component
supply (lasers, memory, ASICs) has unblocked the 800G/1.6T transceiver line.**

Why, with the evidence:

- In Q3 FY26 Fabrinet's segments diverged violently. Optical Communications $889M
  (+35% YoY), of which **telecom a record $628M (+55% YoY)** and **DCI $197M (+90% YoY)**
  — but **datacom $260M, only +4% YoY and −6% sequentially** [5][6][4].
- The cause was explicitly supply, not demand. CEO Seamus Grady: *"We could have shipped
  a lot more if we had those components."* Shipments and revenue were *"well below demand
  levels"*, with constraints across *"lasers, memory… and also certain ASICs"* [6].
- Management guided datacom growth to be *"more measured"* in Q4 FY26 because those
  constraints persisted — Seeking Alpha's headline on the guide was literally "Fabrinet
  forecasts $1.25B–$1.29B Q4 revenue as datacom supply constraints persist" [29].
- Two new hyperscale 800G datacom transceiver programs were already shipping in small
  volumes with the *"initial ramp starting in the fiscal fourth quarter"* — i.e. the
  quarter being reported tonight — ramping through FY27, plus multiple merchant
  transceiver wins targeted for production in H2 calendar 2026 [6][4].
- The industry backdrop says the constraint is real and industry-wide: 800G transceiver
  lead times past 40 weeks on 5nm/3nm DSP shortages, production running 40–60% below
  demand through 2027 per McKinsey, and Nvidia locking up EML laser supply with a $4B
  investment in Lumentum and Coherent [30][31]. Server DRAM contract prices are guided
  +13–18% QoQ in Q3 2026 as capacity reallocates to HBM [30] — the memory line item
  Fabrinet named as a constraint.

**What the market expects for it.** No published datacom-line consensus exists (recorded
in coverage gaps). The Street's *implicit* expectation, visible in the FY27 revision to
$5.66B/$16.29 EPS [7], is that datacom re-accelerates through FY27 on the two hyperscale
programs. Zacks frames the Q4 datacom expectation only qualitatively — "initial ramp
starting in the fiscal fourth quarter" [4]. So the market is long an unquantified
inflection. That asymmetry is the whole trade: a datacom number that shows sequential
re-acceleration validates the FY27 line; another flat-to-down datacom quarter with the
same supply excuse means the AI-optics beta was borrowed, not earned.

Secondary signals to watch, all sourced from the Q3 call: HPC program slipping to ~$150M
quarterly run-rate around June 2027 (already delayed one quarter) [6]; Building 10
clean-room floors coming online June and Sept/Oct 2026 toward $8.5B of total revenue
capacity [6]; and whether management commits to Buildings 11/12 [6].

**Segment mix (Q3 FY26) [5][6][4]:** Optical Communications $889M (+35%) — telecom
$628M (+55%, record), DCI $197M (+90%), datacom $260M (+4% YoY, −6% QoQ). Non-Optical

EPS [28]; management referenced ~34% FY26 growth and said growth could "sustain or
potentially accelerate" beyond it [6].

**Unit economics and the margin problem.** This is the crux of the bear case and it is
fully sourced. Fabrinet earns a **12.1% gross margin** [5] on the same AI-optics demand
that just produced a **50.4% non-GAAP gross margin, +1,260bps YoY, at Lumentum** [8] and
40.2% at Coherent [9]. Fabrinet is a contract manufacturer: it converts volume, not
value. CFO Csaba Sverha attributed near-term margin pressure to *"ramping a large number
of new programs… sometimes creates short-term inefficiencies"* plus FX headwinds, and
guided Q4 to "similar dynamics" [6]. So the operating leverage the market pays 34.7x
forward for [13] has to come from mix and volume, not price.

**Capital intensity is rising sharply and FCF is thin.** Q3 capex alone was $64M toward
Building 10 [6]; nine-month capex $160.6M against $201.8M of operating cash flow left
just $41.1M of FCF on a ~$4.5B revenue run-rate [5]. Management's defence is a genuinely
strong unit-economics argument — *"six months' worth of operating profit would pay for
the entire 2 million square feet"* [6] — but the reported cash-flow deterioration is
exactly what press coverage cited as an "operational red flag" in the July selloff [32].
Capacity roadmap: current footprint supports $4.8B of revenue, all planned additions
take it to $8.5B, and potential Buildings 11/12 add $3.0B; an 8-acre Navanakorn campus
with a 200k sq ft building was bought for $11M for a further $500M of capacity [6].

**Balance sheet, buyback, dilution.** $945.3M cash and short-term investments [5]. **No
meaningful share repurchase in Q3**, with $169M of authorisation remaining — management
explicitly prioritised capacity over buyback [6]. Share count guided at ~36.3M diluted
[3] vs 35.83M outstanding [13]. There is no buyback bid under this stock into the print.

**Customer concentration.** Fabrinet declined to quantify forward: *"We never predict
which customer may or may not become a 10% customer. We only talk about that looking
back"* [6]. Neither the Q3 8-K exhibit nor the transcript excerpts disclosed customer
percentages [5][6]. Commentary consistently names concentration as the top structural
risk — "customer concentration is a risk, as NVIDIA won't sit still" [33]. The strategic
hedge is visible: a **$32M minority stake (14%) in Raytek Semiconductor** for co-packaged
optics, deepening direct hyperscaler transceiver engagement [6][34], with CPO prototypes
being tested for 2027/2028 [33].

**What changed since the last print.** (i) Needham cut its target $800→$650 on 22 July
while keeping Buy [14]. (ii) The stock fell from $617.09 on 22 June to $527.35 on 21
July, −14.5% in 30 days and −23.6% over the quarter [12]. (iii) Peers reported
spectacularly (Lumentum, Coherent) in the six sessions before this print [8][9].
(iv) FY27 Street estimates were revised *up* to $5.66B/$16.29 [7]. (v) CEO PSUs vested
11 August on certified performance targets [35]. (vi) Optical component shortages
intensified industry-wide [30][31].

run into calendar 2028 and Lumentum is "effectively sold out" on pump lasers, up >80%
YoY, hitting its $1.25B quarterly target more than a quarter early [8][50]. Two things
follow. (1) **Bullish for FN's volumes:** Fabrinet manufactures into this same demand
curve and the constraint has been components, not orders [6][30][31]. (2) **Bearish for
FN's reaction function:** Coherent beat *and* guided revenue up ~12% sequentially and
still fell 4–6%, with the cited reason being **gross-margin guidance of ~40.5% landing
soft relative to elevated revenue targets, plus $1.1B of capex** [11][51][52]. That is
almost exactly Fabrinet's profile — heavy capex [5][6], ramp-diluted margins [6], no
pricing power. The differentiator between LITE (+15%) and COHR (−5%) was the *magnitude
of the guide-up*: Lumentum guided ~+23% sequential, Coherent ~+12%. **My inference: the
market's current clearing bar in optics is a very large sequential guide-up, and
Fabrinet's plausible Q1 FY27 guide (my arithmetic above: ~+6% sequential to hold FY27
consensus) sits closer to Coherent's outcome than Lumentum's.**

**Rate / FX / commodity sensitivity.** FX is a *named, quantified-in-direction*
headwind: Fabrinet manufactures in Thailand and management cited FX headwinds on gross
margin in both the Q3 result and the Q4 guide [6]. Baht strength is a direct margin tax
on a 12%-gross-margin cost base — the highest-leverage macro variable in the model. Input
cost inflation is live via memory: server DRAM contract prices guided **+13–18% QoQ in
Q3 2026** as capacity reallocates to HBM [30], and memory is one of the three components
Fabrinet named as constraining shipments [6]. Rate sensitivity is modest — net cash
position of $945.3M and no material debt disclosed in the exhibit [5]; beta 1.22 [16].

**Customer / supplier read-throughs.** Nvidia reports Q2 FY27 on **26 August 2026**,
having guided to ~$91B [53] — nine days after FN, so it is a *forward* overhang, not a
resolved input, and management will be asked about it. Nvidia's $4B EML laser lockup with
Lumentum and Coherent [31] is double-edged for Fabrinet: it secures the industry's laser
supply (helps volumes) but concentrates value capture at the component vendors, not the
contract manufacturer. The structural risk on a 2027/2028 horizon is co-packaged optics
displacing pluggables; Fabrinet's answer is the Raytek stake and CPO prototypes for
2027/2028 [6][33][34], and CPO penetration of optical networking is forecast to go from
<1% to >35% by 2030 [33] — a real but not-this-quarter threat that management will be
asked to address on the call.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The constraint breaks and the print reveals it. Fabrinet's problem in Q3
was explicitly that it *could not ship what was ordered* — "we could have shipped a lot
more if we had those components" [6] — and the two new hyperscale 800G programs were
scheduled to begin ramping in exactly this quarter [6][4]. Its two closest peers just
printed 109% and 34% growth with bookings into calendar 2028 [8][9][10], and the
industry's supply is running 40–60% below demand [31], meaning every incremental
component Fabrinet sources converts straight to revenue. The stock enters the event ~24%
below its 14 May high [12][13] with shorts *reducing* from 1.96M to 1.66M shares [16],
no buyback bid to have supported it artificially [6], a forward P/E of 34.7 that is far
below the "above 50x" narrative in the July commentary [13][32], FY27 Street estimates
revised *up* to $5.66B/$16.29 [7], and an average target of $732 — 28% above spot
[13][14]. If datacom re-accelerates sequentially and the Q1 FY27 guide has a $1.35B+
midpoint, the FY27 line is validated on a de-risked base and the July de-rating unwinds
violently. Precedent for the magnitude exists: +28.6% in August 2024 [12].

**Bear case.** The bar is set at the top of guidance and the reaction function is
hostile. Consensus EPS of $3.85 sits against a guide topping out at $3.87 [3][4], and
Fabrinet's average surprise is 2.64% [4] — there is almost no room for a beat that
matters. Zacks Rank is **#4 (Sell)** [4]. The company is a 12.1%-gross-margin contract
manufacturer [5] whose CFO has already told the Street to expect the *same* margin
dilution from ramp inefficiencies plus FX in Q4 [6], and whose nine-month free cash flow
is $41.1M against $160.6M of capex [5] — precisely the "operational red flag" pattern
cited in the July selloff [32]. Coherent proved the regime six sessions ago: it beat,
guided revenue up ~12% sequentially, and *fell 4–6%* because gross-margin guidance and
$1.1B of capex disappointed [11][51][52]. Needham's most-accurate analyst cut $150 off
his target three weeks ago [14][38]. And the base rate is bad: FN fell after 5 of its
last 8 prints and 3 of its last 4, including −17.1% and −19.9% tails [12], with Trefis's
longer 20-report sample showing only 45% positive [20]. Datacom flat again with the same
supply excuse and a $1.2-handle Q1 guide is a straightforward double-digit down day.

**Base case.** Fabrinet delivers revenue near or slightly above the top of its guide
(~$1.28–1.32B) and non-GAAP EPS of roughly $3.85–3.95, i.e. the customary low-single-digit
beat [3][4], with telecom and DCI again carrying the quarter and datacom improved but
still supply-limited [5][6][29]. Gross margin lands near 12% with FX and ramp
inefficiency cited as guided [6]. The Q1 FY27 guide is solid in absolute terms but not
Lumentum-shaped — good enough to keep FY27 estimates roughly intact, not good enough to
re-rate a 34.7x forward multiple [13] in a tape that just punished Coherent for the same
profile [11][51]. **The realised move is large — the 12% implied is fair against a
11.96% eight-quarter mean [12] — but the direction is close to a coin flip with a mild
downward tilt** from the base rate, the consensus sitting at the top of guidance, and the
group's demonstrated "a beat is not enough" reaction. The strongest offset, and the
reason I am not more bearish, is that unlike February and May the stock is *not* coming
in near a high: it is 24% below it. Fabrinet then presents at Rosenblatt the next morning
[46], which typically truncates one-way drift.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The consensus view into this print — visible in 7 Buys, a $732 average target [13][14]
and FY27 estimates revised up to $5.66B [7] — is that datacom weakness is a temporary
supply artefact and the 800G/1.6T ramp inflects through FY27.

**The most credible reversal, stated concretely: management reports a third consecutive
quarter of flat-to-down sequential datacom revenue (Q1 FY26–Q3 FY26 already showed
datacom at $260M, −6% QoQ [5][6]) and, critically, reframes the cause from *supply* to
*customer program timing, requalification, or insourcing* — while guiding Q1 FY27 revenue
to a midpoint below ~$1.30B.** That specific combination breaks the thesis because it
severs Fabrinet from the peer demand curve: Coherent's bookings run to calendar 2028 and
Lumentum is sold out [8][10], so if Fabrinet's datacom line still does not move, the
constraint is not the industry's — it is Fabrinet's *position* in the industry. Given
that management explicitly refuses to quantify customer concentration forward [6] and
that "NVIDIA won't sit still" is the named structural risk [33], the market would
immediately re-price FN from AI-optics beta to cyclical EMS, and a 34.7x forward multiple
[13] on a 12% gross margin [5] has a very long way to fall. This is the reversal that
justifies more than the 12% implied move.

**The mirror-image flip (bull direction), for completeness:** a Q1 FY27 revenue guide
with a midpoint at or above ~$1.40B — matching the FY27 quarterly average implied by
$5.66B consensus [7] — *plus* datacom up double-digit sequentially would prove the
constraint broke a quarter early. Lumentum's +15% on exactly that shape of guide-up [8]
is the template for what the tape pays for.
