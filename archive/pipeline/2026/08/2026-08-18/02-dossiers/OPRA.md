# OPRA — Opera Limited

**What this print is about.** Opera has beaten and raised on every quarter of the recent record, so the question is never "will they beat the guide" — the guide is engineered to be beaten. Management said so out loud in April: the FY26 range "continues to allow for later upside potential in the second half" [14]. The question is whether *the way they beat it* is enough for a stock that has already run +58% in six months [computed, 12] and trades at 13.5x forward earnings with 7-of-7 Buy ratings [5][6]. Two of the last four prints were textbook beat-and-raises and both were **sold** (−4.9% Aug'25, −3.4% Oct'25); the two that worked were the ones carrying a *second* item — a $300M buyback (+23.4% Feb'26) and a large FY guidance step-up (+5.3% Apr'26) [computed, 12][23]. So this print trades on the second item. Three candidates are live and all are unusually well-flagged: (i) whether the FY26 guide is lifted enough to break the implied H2 deceleration to 13–17% growth [14], (ii) any news on the **Google non-US search agreement, which expires 31 December 2026** and represents a customer group worth 33% of FY25 revenue [15], and (iii) OPay — Opera's 9.5% stake, carried at $294.6M, ~17% of market cap, with banks hired for a US IPO [15][33][34]. Layered on top: on **10 August the company moved its own earnings date forward six days**, from 25 August to 19 August, without explaining why [1][2][3]. This company pre-announced upside on 2 February 2026 and the stock rose 13.5% that day [26][computed, 12]; an unexplained pull-forward at a company with that habit is, in my read, the single most interesting unpriced fact in the setup. Triage called OPRA an "under-covered multi-segment browser/ad/gaming name" — that is right about the coverage (7 analysts, no PT change since 14 July [6]) but the *gaming* leg is a rounding error in this print; the mix that matters is advertising (67%) versus query (33%) [13].

### What has to happen just to hold the stock flat — my inference

Purely my arithmetic off the guide and the pattern, not sourced:

1. **Revenue above $178M** (the high end). Opera beat the high end by $4M in Q1 [13]; anything inside the range would be the first non-beat in the recent record and would read as a stall.
2. **Adjusted EBITDA at or above $42M.** Note the guide is for *sequential margin compression* — 23.2% in Q2 versus 24.0% delivered in Q1 [13]. Simply hitting $42M means flat EBITDA dollars quarter-on-quarter on higher revenue.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**It is not EPS.** OPRA's adjusted-EPS consensus is not a usable bar: providers disagree between $0.21 and $0.32 for this quarter [23][24][40], and the last two prints were reported by TheFly as EPS *misses* — Q4'25 "adjusted EPS 28c, consensus 37c" and Q1'26 "adjusted EPS 27c, consensus 31c" [4] — while the stock rose 23.4% and 5.3% respectively [computed, 12]. MarketBeat scores Q4'25 as $0.30 vs $0.33, a miss [23]; Public.com scores Q1'26 as $0.34 vs $0.30, a beat [24]. The tape has demonstrably ignored the EPS line.

**The metric this print actually trades on is the FY2026 revenue guidance midpoint, and specifically whether the implied H2 growth rate rises above ~18%.** Everything about Opera's guidance behaviour points here: management explicitly runs a "bottom-up estimate… we rather leave upside for things to scale better than what we built into guidance in the later parts of the year" [14], and each of the last three prints raised the FY number. The market has been trained to price the raise, not the quarter.

**The expectation.** Street FY26 revenue is $736.72M [6], versus a guide midpoint of $733.5M. Applying the Q1 pattern — Q1 beat the high end by $4M and the FY range was lifted $7M at the low end and $5M at the high end [14] — the mechanical expectation is roughly **$734–747M**, midpoint ≈$740M, i.e. about +0.5% versus the Street. That is a low-voltage outcome. To be a positive surprise the FY guide needs a midpoint materially above $745M, which requires management to release some of the H2 conservatism they have twice said they are holding.

**The two wildcards that could displace it.** (a) **Google.** The non-US search distribution agreement runs to **31 December 2026** and the separate US agreement to **31 January 2027** [15]; "Customer group 1" — Google — was $204.7M of FY25 revenue, **33.3% of the total** (down from 40.2% in FY24 and 42.4% in FY23) [15]. On the Q1 call the CEO said renewal talks run "more towards the second half of the year… we'll provide an update when such is available," and "we don't expect any surprises" [14]. A signed renewal announced on this call is the single largest positive catalyst available; an unresolved renewal four months from expiry is a live overhang. (b) **OPay.** The 9.5% stake is carried at $294.6M at 31 Dec 2025 — about 17% of the entire market cap — is a KPMG **critical audit matter**, and is valued by a probability-weighted expected return model with scenario equity values spanning **$0.2B to $6.0B** [15]. Press reports say OPay has engaged Citigroup, Deutsche Bank and JPMorgan for a US IPO targeting ~$4B, and Opera's own filing implies a $3.1B valuation with an **85% probability of an IPO within two years** [33][34][41]. Any mark-up, or IPO timing news, drops straight into GAAP net income and into the sum-of-the-parts.

### Segment mix and unit economics (Q1 2026) [13]

- **Advertising $117.0M, +24% y/y, 67% of revenue.** Fastest-growing vertical is e-commerce partners. Travel is the next vertical being rolled out on a performance basis; management called it "a big opportunity" but explicitly said it is *not* built into guidance [14].
- **Query $58.3M, +23% y/y, 33% of revenue.** Within that, **pure search revenue grew ~14% y/y** in Q1 — the strongest of any quarter in 2025 — with the balance from newer non-search "intent-based query" monetisation [14]. This distinction matters: the company is deliberately broadening the category away from pure Google search, which is both the growth story and the hedge against the Google renewal.
- **Other revenue $0.5M.**
- **MAUs 288M** (+4M q/q), annualised **ARPU $2.43, +25% y/y** [13].
- **Opera GX 35M MAUs**, +1M q/q [13].
- **MiniPay 15M cumulative activated wallets**, +123% y/y; CFO said the broader ecosystem around MiniPay generates "about $20 million of revenue" [13][14].

### Margin trajectory — the structural bear point

Gross margin has fallen from **60.41% in Q1'24 to 50.91% in Q1'26** [7], a ~950bp compression driven by Opera Ads mix (a lower-gross-margin, cost-of-inventory business than owned-and-operated). Management guides cost of revenue to **~38% of revenue for FY26**, "slightly higher in Q4" with the seasonal advertising peak [14]. Operating margin has nonetheless *expanded* (15.28% → 17.31% y/y in Q1) because cash compensation and marketing are falling as a share of revenue — from 36% in 2025 to ~33% in 2026 [14]. The offsetting negative: "all other opex pre-adjusted EBITDA" was re-guided up from ~15% to **just over 20% growth** for FY26 on hosting costs, AI usage and "pricing impact of constrained supply" [14]. That is AI-inference cost creep entering the P&L.

March execution: 1.14M shares at an average $14.88, $17.0M total, structured so that Hong Kong Kunlun Tech sells pro rata at the same price, preserving Kunlun's ownership percentage [13][15]. $12.8M settled in Q1, $4.1M carried into Q2 [13].

Declining, but still a third of revenue on a contract that expires in 4.5 months. The 20-F risk factor is explicit: "Google, our largest partner, continues to represent a material share of our total revenues… our business and results of operations could be adversely affected by… non-renewal or termination" [15]. Second search partner is Yandex, renewed effective 1 Jan 2025 on a three-year agreement in the UAE [15].

## 7. Forensics

- **Filer status change.** Six Form 3s were filed on 18 March 2026 and Opera has filed Form 4s since — a change from the pure foreign-private-issuer regime it operated under previously (it still filed a 20-F for FY2025 on 27 March 2026) [16]. Practical effect: insider transactions are now visible in near-real time, which they were not a year ago. This is a governance *improvement*, not a red flag.
- **CEO and CFO discretionary sales, June 2026 — the most notable item.** CEO **Song Lin** sold 25,600 ADS on 17 June at a weighted $18.88 and 44,400 on 18 June at $18.52, leaving 120,846 ADS [17]. Both filed Form 144s on 17 June for 70,000 ADS each, $1,330,700 each, broker **Lake Street Capital Markets**, securities acquired 10 January 2026 as **equity compensation** [21][22]. - **Regime.** Tape into the print: S&P 500 7,745.06 (−0.5%), Dow 53,459.78 (−0.5%), Nasdaq Composite 26,644.91 (−0.3%) on 17 August 2026 [14, page footer]. OPRA's −3.49% that session was a ~3pt underperformance of the Nasdaq with no identified catalyst.
- **Alphabet Q2 2026 (reported 22 July) is the most important peer datapoint.** Revenue $119.80B (+24%) versus consensus $117.02B; **Google Search and other $63.27B, +17%**; revenue ex-TAC $103.62B (+27%) versus consensus $101.07B; operating income $40.77B (+30%); Google Cloud $24.77B (+82%) [30]. Search demand is unambiguously healthy — supportive for Opera's query line. **But TAC was $16.2B, +10%** [30], growing well below the 17% growth in Search revenue it funds. My inference: Google is paying distribution partners a shrinking share of a growing pool. For a partner whose non-US agreement is up for renewal on 31 December, that is a quietly unhelpful data point and the mechanism by which a renewal could land on worse economics.
- **Ad-tech Q2 2026 season was brutal and bifurcated.** AppLovin, Criteo, Magnite and Taboola reported 5 August; PubMatic and The Trade Desk 6 August; Perion 10 August [31][32]. Criteo: revenue $428M, **−11% y/y**, cut FY guidance for the second time in three months, replaced its CFO, **stock −24%**. On the other side, PubMatic **+20.8%** and Magnite **+8.6%**, the latter raising four full-year targets on 36% CTV growth [31][32]. Read-through: (a) the sector multiple has been de-rated and investors are punishing any deceleration hard — the market is in a "growth isn't enough" mood; (b) the pain is concentrated in retail-media and open-web display, not in owned-and-operated inventory, which is where Opera's advertising sits; (c) **Google policy changes are now demonstrably hitting named partners' P&Ls**, which raises the perceived hazard on Opera's renewal.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Opera has beaten its own high end every quarter in the visible record and told the market, twice, that the FY guide deliberately holds back H2 upside [13][14]. The CFO said in April that "the second quarter is shaping up in a similar way" to Q1 [13], and Q2 guidance was set *above* the then-consensus of $171.99M [4]. Then, in the middle of the quarter, the company volunteered a Q2 KPI it does not normally publish — mobile MAUs +66% UK, +40% US [27] — which is the behaviour of a company with a good quarter in hand. It then moved its own earnings date forward six days without explanation [1][3], at a company that pre-announced upside seven months ago and gained 13.5% doing it [26]. The options market is paying up for calls at every tenor (put/call IV ratio 0.83–0.89) [10], shorts have covered 38% off the May peak [11], there is no run-up (5-day −1.65%) and the stock sits 6.8% below its 52-week high [computed, 12]. On top of the operating result there are three separate optional catalysts — a Google renewal, a larger Q2 buyback print, and OPay IPO progress against a $294.6M carrying value that is 17% of market cap [15][33][34]. At 13.5x forward earnings, 14.8x EV/EBITDA, a 0.70 PEG and a 4.1% dividend yield [5], very little of any of this is in the price.

**Bear case.** The base rate says beat-and-raise gets sold here: Q2'25 and Q3'25 were both beats with raised FY guidance and both traded down, −4.94% and −3.41% [computed, 12][28][29]. The only two prints that worked carried a *second* item, and the two obvious second items — the buyback and the big FY raise — have already been used. Worse, the arithmetic of the current guide is deflationary: H1 at guidance is $354M, so the $727–740M FY range implies H2 growth of only **13.4–17.3%** against +23–25% in Q2 [7][13], and the Street at $736.7M is already in the upper half of that [6]. Management is guiding Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin *down* sequentially, 23.2% versus 24.0% delivered [13]. Gross margin has fallen 950bp in two years to 50.91% [7] and other opex growth was re-guided up from 15% to 20%+ on AI hosting costs [14]. Google — 33.3% of FY25 revenue — has an agreement that expires 31 December 2026 with no renewal announced [15], and Alphabet's own Q2 showed TAC growing 10% against Search revenue growing 17% [30]. The ad-tech tape just punished Criteo −24% and Taboola −24% for exactly this kind of deceleration [31][32]. And the CEO and CFO each sold 70,000 shares in June in transactions the filings flag as **not** made under a 10b5-1 plan [17][18][21][22]. With 7-of-7 Buy ratings and a PT 34% above spot [6], there is no upgrade left to buy the dip.

**Base case (mine).** Revenue lands at or slightly above the $178M high end (+24–26% y/y), adjusted EBITDA at $42–43M, and the FY26 guide is nudged to roughly $737–748M with adjusted EBITDA $172–177M — a real raise, but only ~1% above a Street already sitting at $736.7M. Q2 buyback execution is disclosed and is larger than March's $17M. On the Google renewal, management repeats the April language — good dialogue, no surprises, update when available — without a signed deal. That combination is a modest positive, not a repricing event: it clears the operating bar but does not clear the *second-item* bar that the last two rallies required. I would centre the distribution slightly right of zero, with fat tails on both sides: a signed Google renewal or an OPay IPO filing on the call is a double-digit up day; an FY raise that leaves H2 growth below 18% while the ad-tech complex is being de-rated is a high-single-digit down day. Net: **Lean Up, low-to-medium conviction**, with the honest caveat that my two strongest bullish inputs — the date pull-forward and the call skew — are circumstantial rather than fundamental.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **the Google renewal landing badly, or not landing at all with a change of tone.** Consensus (7 Buy, $26.29 PT [6]) is underwriting Opera as an AI-browser monetisation compounder in which the search relationship is a stable annuity that is anyway shrinking as a share of the mix — 42.4% → 40.2% → 33.3% of revenue over three years [15]. That thesis breaks if management, on this call, says anything other than "renewed" or "on track." Concretely, the flip is: the CEO repeats the April framing but adds a qualifier about terms, scope or timing; or the renewal is disclosed as shorter-dated, or with revised revenue-share economics. Alphabet's Q2 gave the mechanism — TAC +10% against Search revenue +17% [30] — and Taboola just showed the market what a named Google policy change does to a partner's P&L and share price (−24%, >$20M of ex-TAC gross profit) [31][32]. With $204.7M of FY25 revenue in that one customer group [15] and 4.5 months to expiry, a 10% haircut to the renewal economics is roughly $20M of high-margin annual revenue — about 2.7% of FY26 revenue but a far larger share of EBITDA, and it would force the Street to re-rate the multiple, not just the estimates. The symmetric flip is nearly as powerful: a **signed multi-year renewal announced on the call** removes the largest discount in the valuation and, at 13.5x forward earnings [5], is worth more than the quarter itself.

A secondary reversal, lower probability but higher amplitude: **an OPay IPO filing or pricing.** The stake is carried at $294.6M on a PWERM whose scenarios span $0.2B to $6.0B of OPay equity value [15]; press reports put the IPO target near $4B with Citi, Deutsche Bank and JPMorgan engaged [33][34][41]. A visible market print on that stake would mark ~17–22% of Opera's market cap to a hard number and change what the sum-of-the-parts looks like overnight.
