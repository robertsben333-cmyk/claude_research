# BEKE — KE Holdings Inc ("Beike")

**What this print is about.** BEKE is no longer a growth story and everyone knows it —
China's new-home market is in a 37-month downturn and Beike's Q1 2026 revenue fell 19%
year-over-year. What the stock has re-rated on since late 2025 is *earnings quality*: a
cost-optimisation programme that took operating expenses down 22% YoY and pushed Q1 2026
non-GAAP operating margin to a seven-quarter high of 8.8% while the top line shrank. The
Street has now extrapolated that. Consensus for Q2 2026 is revenue of RMB23.14bn (−11.0%
YoY) with non-GAAP EPS/ADS of RMB2.12 (+36.8% YoY) — which mathematically requires a
record ~10.3% adjusted net margin against 7.0% a year ago and 8.5% last quarter [13].
That is the bar, and it sits against the toughest year-ago comparison of the fiscal year
(Q2 2025 GTV +4.7%, revenue +11.3% [43]). Layered on top: China's July macro deteriorated
sharply (retail sales +0.6%, property development investment −19.2% YTD [29][30]), and
Beijing responded on 18–19 August by unlocking the RMB10.9tn housing provident fund for
renovation, property-management fees and rent — a direct read-through to two of Beike's
five segments and the reason the ADR jumped 3.0% on 19 August [27][28]. The print is
therefore a collision between a demanding margin bar plus a hard comp on one side, and a
two-day-old policy tailwind plus a three-quarter streak of positive earnings reactions on
the other. Management's colour on July–August transaction trends on the 8:00 a.m. call is
likely to matter more than the reported quarter — that is exactly what broke the stock
after the Q2 2025 call [21].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Non-GAAP net margin (and its driver, the opex run-rate) — validated against management's
July–August GTV colour on the call.** Not revenue, not GTV, not GAAP EPS.

Why I believe this:

1. **The market has already demonstrated it.** On 2026-05-19 BEKE printed revenue −19.0%
   YoY and GTV −15.6% YoY — an unambiguously bad top line — and the stock rose 5.17%,
   because non-GAAP operating profit rose 45.1% to RMB1.67bn and non-GAAP operating margin
   hit a seven-quarter high of 8.8% [15][16][10]. Conversely on 2026-03-16 the market let a
   28.7% revenue decline pass with a +1.02% reaction because the story was intact. Revenue
   direction is not the transmission mechanism; margin is.
2. **The Street's number is a margin number.** RMB2.12/ADS on RMB23.14bn is arithmetically
   a ~10.3% adjusted net margin — a company record and +1.8pp on Q1 2026's 8.5% (my
   calculation from [13][15]). Every RMB of the consensus beat/miss has to come out of the
   cost line.
3. **Management has framed 2026 exactly this way.** Investing.com's Q1 preview described
   guidance "pointing to ~10.5 percent non-GAAP net income margin for FY2026, framing 2026
   as a year of earnings quality" [26] — I flag this as `snippet_only` and I could not
   corroborate the 10.5% figure in a company document, so treat the specific number with
   caution; the *framing* is corroborated by the Q1 call [15].
4. **The forward comment is the second half of the trade.** After the Q2 2025 print Jefferies
   cut its PT from $24.50 to $22 explicitly because "management highlighted softer industry
   trends seen in existing and new home sales since June during the conference call" [21].
   The identical seasonal risk exists now, and July's national data was materially worse
   than June's [29][30].

**Supporting sub-metrics to watch:** existing-home GTV YoY (the profitable, resilient leg —
41.3% contribution margin in Q1, a seven-quarter high [15]); whether new-home GTV's −37.2%
decline moderates [14][15]; renovation revenue, which fell 20.6% in Q1 and is the segment
the new provident-fund rules directly subsidise [15][27].

  $3.64 per ADS on its own definition [5] — the two are not directly comparable (restricted
  cash and customer deposits sit inside the RMB53.9bn figure).

  efficiency programme in one line. Per-agent transaction volume was +26% YoY as of Q3 2025

- **Diversification:** rental units under management >740,000, +47% YoY [16]; rental is now

  mix even as it stabilises revenue. Renovation carries a far higher contribution margin
  and is the segment the policy change targets [27][46].

**What changed since the last print (2026-05-19):**
1. National macro rolled over — July retail sales +0.6% vs 1.5% expected; FAI −6.7%;
   property development investment −19.2% for 7M26 [29][30].
2. New-home prices fell 0.1% m/m in July with only 17 of 70 cities up [31]; Fitch cut its
   2026 new-home sales forecast to −11% to −13% [33].
3. Existing-home demand stayed comparatively firm — Beijing second-hand transactions +5.7%
   YoY in H1 2026 to a five-year high for the period [34]; 20 key cities' second-hand
   transaction area +17% YoY in April [35]; secondary transactions were ~69% of the market
   in 5M26 [33]. **This bifurcation is the single most important fundamental fact: Beike's
   profitable leg is holding up while its shrinking leg collapses.**
4. Policy: the State Council revised the Housing Provident Fund regulations (signed by
   Premier Li Qiang, effective 2026-09-20), unlocking RMB10.9tn (US$1.6tn) for home
   renovation, property-management fees, and removing the income-proportion threshold for
   rent withdrawals [27][28]. Direct subsidy to renovation and rental — two Beike segments.
5. The stock fell to a 2026 low of $14.26 on 2026-06-26 and has recovered 21.9% since [10].

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The efficiency programme still has runway and Q2 is where it compounds.
Q1 2026 delivered a 22.3% YoY opex cut with S&M down 39%, and those actions were taken
*during* the quarter, so Q2 gets a full-quarter benefit off a Q2 2025 base that was itself
depressed (adjusted net income fell to RMB1.815bn, adj. margin 7.0%) [15][43]. Mix is
working in Beike's favour: the collapsing leg is new homes (25.7% contribution margin,
−37% GTV) while the resilient leg is existing homes (41.3% contribution margin, a
seven-quarter high) and the national data says second-hand demand held up through Q2 —
Beijing +5.7% in H1, 20 cities +17% in April [15][34][35]. Layer on the 18–19 August
provident-fund reform, which subsidises precisely the renovation and rental segments Beike
has been investing into [27][28], and management has a genuinely good story for the
forward-looking half of the call. Positioning is not stretched — short interest 5.44% and
falling, IV/RV only 1.07, no crowded long — and the stock is on a three-quarter streak of
positive earnings reactions with $2.7bn of buyback executed and a control shareholder whose
stake is mechanically rising [5][9][10][36]. A margin print at or above ~10% with a
constructive July/August comment gets this to the $19+ area where the May high sits.

**Bear case.** The bar is a record. Consensus RMB2.12/ADS on RMB23.14bn requires a ~10.3%
adjusted net margin, +3.3pp YoY and +1.8pp on last quarter's already-best-in-seven-quarters
result — and it was set in May, before July's data [13][15][29][30]. Q2 2025 is the hardest
comp of the fiscal year: GTV +4.7%, revenue +11.3% [43], so the YoY optics on GTV and
revenue will be the ugliest of any quarter, and Beike has to overcome that purely on cost.
The macro turned decisively worse *inside and after* the quarter — retail sales +0.6%, FAI
−6.7%, property development investment −19.2%, Fitch cutting 2026 new-home sales to −11/−13%
[29][30][33] — which makes cautious current-quarter commentary the base case, and cautious
current-quarter commentary is exactly what cost the stock its bid after the 2025-08-26 call
when Jefferies cut on "softer industry trends since June" [21]. Underneath, the franchise is
shrinking: agents −4.2%, active agents −7.6%, MAU 42.7m vs 44.5m [15]. And the tape has
already flagged the doubt — Hong Kong faded the entire 19 August policy pop on 20 August, with
the ADR indicated at $16.93 pre-market versus a $17.38 close [10][11][41]. Estimates have not
been walked down; if July's weakness shows up in the numbers or the tone, there is nothing
between the print and the $22.88 consensus target being cut.

**Base case (mine).** Beike beats on margin and misses on GTV/revenue optics — the shape of
the last three quarters — because the cost programme is real and mechanical, while the
market's revenue model is anchored to a comp that cannot be met. The stock's reaction then
hinges almost entirely on the 08:00 ET call. I expect a report that is *good enough on
profitability but not the record 10.3% adjusted net margin the Street has modelled*, paired
with management guidance-by-tone that acknowledges July softness while pointing to the
provident-fund reform as a Q4 tailwind. That combination historically produces a small
negative-to-flat reaction, not a rout: think a move inside the 5.2% implied, skewed slightly
down. I also weight the fact that the effective starting price is ~$16.9 after the HK fade,
which removes some of the downside cushion the 19 August close appeared to provide.
Direction score **−10**, probability of an up move **46%**, and I hold that view with **low**
conviction because the single most important input — the Street's RMB2.12 — rests on one
data vendor whose adjacent row was corrupted, and because a cost story that has surprised
three quarters running is exactly the kind of thing that surprises a fourth time.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

**The most credible reversal is a renovation-and-rental re-rate on the back of the provident
fund reform.** Concretely: Beike reports adjusted net income at or above RMB2.4bn (≈RMB2.12/ADS,
≈10.3% margin), *and* on the call management quantifies the Housing Provident Fund revision —
effective 2026-09-20, RMB10.9tn unlocked for owner-occupied renovation, property-management
fees and rent [27][28] — as a direct, sizeable demand channel for the home renovation segment
(RMB2.339bn revenue, 36.2% contribution margin, currently shrinking 20.6% YoY [15]) and the
rental business (>740,000 units under management, +47% [16]). That would convert Beike's
narrative from "shrinking brokerage cutting costs" to "policy-subsidised housing-services
platform with a growth segment," which is a different multiple. The tell would be renovation
revenue decline moderating from −20.6% toward flat, plus any management commentary putting a
number on provident-fund-linked demand. If that happens, the $22.88 consensus target stops
looking stale and the Jan-2027 $40 call stack [9] stops looking absurd.

**The symmetric flip on the downside** would be a Q4 2025 repeat: that quarter the company
missed adjusted EPS by 51% [13] and printed a small operating loss [7]. If new-home
receivables force a provision, or if the rental business's 14.8% contribution margin
deteriorates as units under management scale, the "earnings quality" thesis breaks in one
line and the −7.25% tail (2025-03-18) becomes the reference reaction rather than the recent
+1 to +5% cluster.
