# OSIS — OSI Systems, Inc.

**Event confirmed: YES.** Fourth-quarter and full fiscal-year 2026 results (fiscal year ended 2026-06-30), conference call **Thursday 2026-08-20 at 1:30 p.m. PT / 4:30 p.m. ET** — i.e. **after the US close (amc)** — announced by the company on 2026-08-11 [1][2]. No date change; no pre-announcement. The company has filed **no 8-K of any kind since the Q3 earnings 8-K on 2026-05-04** [3].

**What this print is about.** OSI Systems is a $3.64bn-market-cap security-screening and electronics manufacturer whose stock has been cut 28.7% from its 2026-04-17 all-time closing high of $309.68 to $220.92, and −13.4% year-to-date, despite beating consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters [4][5][6]. The reason is a two-print pattern that now defines the name: on 2026-01-30 and again on 2026-05-05 OSIS beat on revenue and non-GAAP EPS, declined to raise guidance, showed a lower gross margin, and was sold **−7.27%** and **−17.01%** respectively [4][7][8][9]. So the Q4 number itself is close to irrelevant. The Street's Q4 bar sits in the **bottom 13% of management's own revenue guidance range and the bottom 24% of its own EPS range** (my arithmetic off sourced figures — see §2), and Zacks carries a **+1.86% Earnings ESP and a Rank #2 (Buy)** into the print [10]. What actually prices the stock tonight is the **initial fiscal-2027 guidance**, which management explicitly deferred to this call while saying "fiscal 2027 could be a very, very exciting year for us" [11][12]. Consensus FY27 non-GAAP EPS is **$11.42** (6 estimates, $11.10–$11.75) — **+9.7% over the $10.41 FY26 consensus** [13]. OSI's own initial-guide convention has been +8–11% (FY26) and +8–13% (FY25) EPS growth [14][15], which brackets consensus almost exactly. That is the trap: the modal outcome is an initial guide that merely *meets* the Street, and the tape has punished "merely meets" twice running. Against that sits an unusually loud two weeks of order flow — a **$200m CBP IDIQ (8/17)**, an **$85m CBP IDIQ with a $21m initial task order (8/19)**, three separate ~$10–11m orders (8/10, 8/11, 8/13) and the **LA28 Olympic screening designation (8/19)** [16][17][18][19] — a record **$1.9bn backlog at 1.3x book-to-bill**, and the **roll-off of the Mexico revenue headwind** that suppressed FY26 optics [11][12]. This is a genuinely two-sided event where the quarter beats and the guide decides the tape.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Initial fiscal-2027 non-GAAP EPS and revenue guidance — specifically, whether the EPS guide midpoint clears $11.42 and whether the revenue guide shows growth accelerating above the ~6.9% FY26 rate.**

**Why it, and not the quarter.** Management pre-committed to this on the Q3 call: CFO Alan Edrick said "with the strong backlog and robust opportunity pipeline that we have out there, fiscal 2027 could be a very, very exciting year for us," with **formal guidance to be provided in August** [11][12]. The last two prints both beat EPS and both fell hard specifically because the *forward* commentary disappointed: on 2026-01-30 the stock fell 7.27% on a gross-margin decline despite a beat and a raise [7][33]; on 2026-05-05 it fell 17.01% with reporting attributing it to margin optics plus "the lack of a raised forecast following the quarterly outperformance" [9][34].

**What the market expects for it, and how I know.**

- **Sell-side consensus FY27 EPS: $11.42**, 6 estimates, range $11.10–$11.75, with 1 upward revision and 0 downward in the last 4 weeks [13]. FY27 revenue consensus is **unavailable** to me; Simply Wall St's model has revenue growing ~6.2%/yr over three years [35].
- **OSI's own initial-guide convention sets the reference point.** Initial FY2025 guide (issued 2024-08-22): revenue $1.620–$1.650bn, non-GAAP EPS **$8.80–$9.15**, described as +8–13% growth [14]. Initial FY2026 guide (issued 2025-08-21): revenue $1.805–$1.850bn (+5.4–8.0%), non-GAAP EPS **$10.11–$10.39 (+8.0–11.0%)** [15]. **Applying the same +8% to +11% convention to a ~$10.40 FY26 base gives $11.23–$11.55, midpoint ≈$11.39 — essentially identical to the $11.42 consensus.**
- **Therefore the modal outcome is an initial FY27 guide that lands *on* consensus, not above it.** And OSIS has historically beaten its own initial guide comfortably (FY25 actual revenue $1.713bn and EPS $9.36 both exceeded the *top* of the initial guide) [14][15] — so a conservative-looking guide is not, on the fundamentals, bad news. The question is whether the market grants that or repeats the last two quarters' reaction function. **This is my inference, not a sourced claim.**

**The three sub-questions the guide has to answer:**
- **Does the Mexico headwind actually roll off?** Mexico Security revenue fell from $69m in Q3 FY25 to **$11m in Q3 FY26**; ex-Mexico, Security grew **+25% YoY**. Management said the headwind would be smaller in Q4 and "largely roll off as the company enters fiscal 2027" [11][12]. If FY27 revenue guidance shows growth of only 6–8% *with* Mexico gone, the underlying business is decelerating and the bull case breaks.
- **Does Security margin re-expand?** Q3 FY26 Security non-GAAP op margin 18.3% (vs 18.1%), Opto 13.5% (vs 14.0%), Healthcare a **GAAP operating loss of −$2.366m** vs +$1.308m a year earlier [11][22].
- **Does free cash flow show up?** 9M FY26 operating cash flow was **$93.794m vs $97.030m** in 9M FY25, on 7.8% higher revenue [22]. Management flagged **$74m of Mexico receivables collected after Q3-end** and guided to "sizeable operating cash flow and strong free cash flow conversion" in Q4 [12][36].
