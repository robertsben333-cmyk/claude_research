# NTNX — Nutanix, Inc.

**Event confirmed: YES.** Nutanix reports fiscal Q4 and full-year FY2026 (year ended 31 July 2026) **after the US close on Wednesday 26 August 2026**, with the call at 4:30pm ET / 1:30pm PT, per the company's own IR press release dated 6 August 2026 [1][2]. No date change, no pre-announcement, no negative pre-release.

**What this print is about.** This is not a quarter about EPS. Nutanix has already told the market what Q4 looks like — the FY26 guide of $2.82–2.84bn boxes Q4 into roughly $723.5–743.5m against a $725–745m explicit guide, and consensus of ~$737.5m sits in the upper half of a range the company has beaten in each of the last three quarters [3][4][5]. The print trades on **the initial FY2027 revenue guide**, and specifically on whether management is willing to underwrite ~13–14% growth (Street ~$3.18–3.20bn) while the server DRAM shortage runs through 2027 and while three plaintiff firms are investigating the company for exactly the sin of over-promising and then shifting revenue out [6][7][8][9]. The setup is uncomfortable: the stock is +26% in a month, +43% since late May and +95% off its 10 April low; five brokers raised targets in the last six sessions; and yet the 17-analyst consensus price target of **$64.58 is *below* the $66.44 spot** [10][11][12][13]. Meanwhile the options market is pricing a **±14.49% move** — roughly 2.6x the 5.5% median realised move of the last seven prints, and a repeat of last quarter when a 15.58% implied move produced a 4.8% close [14][15][16]. August is also structurally Nutanix's hardest print: it is the quarter in which management sets the annual bar, and the last time they set one (Aug 2025, FY26 at $2.90–2.94bn) they had to cut it within 90 days and the stock fell 17.75% in a single session [17][18].

## 3. The one metric that matters

> **The initial FY2027 revenue guide — and whether management underwrites ≥13% growth despite the memory/server supply constraint.**

Why this and not EPS: Q4 revenue is effectively pre-announced by the FY26 guide arithmetic above, and non-GAAP EPS has beaten by 27–34% in each of the last two quarters without the stock moving more than 4.8% [27][29][16]. What has moved this stock double-digits, twice, is the *annual* number: −17.75% when the FY26 guide was cut in November 2025, and the entire Feb–Aug 2026 round trip as it was rebuilt [17][19].

**What the market expects for it, and how I know:**
- Street consensus FY2027 revenue is **~$3.18bn** [34] *(snippet_only)*, i.e. ~12.5% growth off the $2.83bn FY26 consensus [11].
- **Oppenheimer explicitly expects management to guide FY27 revenue "above consensus estimates of more than $3.20 billion"** (20 Aug 2026) [6].
- **KeyBanc models an acceleration from 12% in FY26 to 14% in FY27** on the resolution of license-delivery and OEM shipment delays [32].
- **UBS is the moderate: ~13% total revenue growth, "aligns with Street"** — and explicitly flags that hardware availability and pricing remain headwinds, "stable rather than deteriorating" [12].
- Management's own medium-term frame, set at the April 2026 Investor Day: **mid-to-high-teens revenue and ARR growth by FY2029**, non-GAAP operating margin mid-to-high 20s, FCF margin high 20s, Rule of 40 in the low-to-mid 40s — explicitly assuming "a normalized supply chain environment" and "a stable market backdrop" [35][36].

**Second-order metric to watch:** whether the "revenue shift from Q1 to future periods" — the disclosure that triggered the November collapse and the plaintiff-firm investigations — has actually reversed into Q3/Q4, or whether it recurs. CFO Sivaraman said in November: "We expect that the revenue over time remains unchanged... We expect this dynamic to continue and have factored it in" [37]. A Q4 in the *lower* half of guide would mean it did not resolve, and would re-open a credibility question that currently carries litigation attached.

**Third:** the ARR growth number itself. $2.51bn consensus = ~13%, down from 15%/16%. Management needs to frame that deceleration as duration/timing rather than demand.

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Sector / factor regime.** Enterprise software is in the middle of a violent mean-reversion rally. **IGV is +18% since its 23 July 2026 low, sharply outperforming the Nasdaq 100 and mega-cap tech** [71]. Earlier in 2026 the group had been crushed on "SaaSpocalypse" fears — IGV was −10.5% YTD against the S&P +10.8% and logged its worst quarter since 2008 in early 2026, on fears that AI agents cannibalise seat-based software and that IT budgets reallocate to AI compute [72]. Bloomberg framed this week explicitly: **"Software Stocks Get an Earnings Gut Check After Hot Summer Rally"** (25 Aug 2026) [73]. NTNX's +26% month is substantially a beta expression of that rotation, layered on the short cover.

**Same-day event congestion.** NVDA, CRM, CRWD and **Everpure (the enterprise-storage comp)** all report on 26 August 2026 [14][74]. Implied moves for that cohort: NVDA ±6.17%, CRM ±8.01%, CRWD ±8.98%, HPQ ±10.00% — **NTNX's ±14.49% is the largest on the board** [14]. *Inference (mine):* NTNX's post-print tape on 27 August will be partly hostage to NVDA's reaction and to whatever the software complex does, which raises the variance of the single-name read and is a genuine argument for the fat implied move.

**Rate / FX / commodity sensitivity.** Beta 0.60 [10] — historically low-beta for a high-multiple software name, which is itself a signal that the recent move is idiosyncratic rather than factor-driven. EMEA is the weak geography in consensus (+1.5% YoY vs US +13.9%) [4], and the Middle East exposure management flagged is "mid single digit percent of revenue" [39]. **Quantified FX sensitivity: unavailable.**

**The commodity that matters is DRAM.** Server DRAM prices rose sharply into 2026 (one source cites DRAM +172% YoY by end-Q3 2025 and DDR5 contract prices up as much as 100%), the shortage is expected to persist into 2027, and no meaningful new fab capacity arrives before late 2027/2028 [7][8]. This is a *demand-timing* tax on Nutanix, not a COGS tax — Nutanix sells software, but its software ships on servers its customers must buy. It cuts partly the other way via NC2 (Nutanix Cloud Clusters on public cloud), where management reported QoQ deployment increases specifically attributed to server price inflation, including a Fortune 500 financial-services customer choosing NC2 on AWS because of hardware constraints [39][59].

**Peers who already reported.**
- **Everpure (enterprise storage)** guided FY2027 revenue of $4.3–4.4bn with midpoint sales growth of 21.5%, up 300bps from prior, and adjusted operating income growth up 650bps to a 32.5% midpoint — i.e. the storage adjacency is accelerating, not decelerating [74]. But Everpure reports its Q2 FY27 **on the same day**, so there is no clean pre-read.
- **Dell** posted record Q1 FY27 revenue of $43.8bn (+88% YoY) with $24.4bn of AI orders — but that is AI-server driven and reports again **3 September 2026**, after NTNX [75][76].
- **HPE** delivered record Q2 FY26 revenue in June 2026 on infrastructure modernisation and AI [77].
- **Broadcom/VMware** is the *source* of Nutanix's demand, not a comp: Forrester projects up to 20% of VMware's enterprise installed base migrates off entirely, and analysts expect ~35% of VMware workloads on alternative platforms by 2028; Nutanix has reportedly attracted 30,000 VMware customers [39][78]. William Blair positions Nutanix as the top VMware alternative, strongest in mid-market with growing enterprise footholds [78].

**Customer/supplier read-through:** the honest read is that the *demand* side of Nutanix's story is confirmed by multiple independent channel checks (UBS, Oppenheimer, KeyBanc) and the *fulfilment* side is confirmed as constrained by the same channel checks and by the company's own commentary. The disagreement between bulls and bears is entirely about whether management guides FY27 to demand or to fulfilment.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The channel checks are unusually strong and unusually specific: a UBS partner's Nutanix pipeline is up 40–50% YoY, Oppenheimer's HyperConverged demand score jumped from +0.15 to +1.05 with 50% of respondents above internal plan and only 7% below, and the Nutanix-vs-VMware performance gap is *widening* [12][6]. Q3 was a blowout against a low bar — revenue $703m against a $680–690m guide, 22.3% operating margin against 16–17% guided, RPO 5% above consensus, 730 new logos +18% [3][27]. The 4 August 5% headcount reduction gives management a fully-funded, already-charged path to guiding FY27 operating margin above FY26's ~22.5% [43]. Consensus EPS has been revised +3.1% higher in 30 days and Zacks has it at Rank #1 [4]. Short interest is still 11.8m shares at 5.0 days to cover with the sector bid firmly on (IGV +18% since 23 July) [50][71]. If management guides FY27 revenue to $3.25bn+ with a mid-20s margin and FCF above $850m — which Oppenheimer and KeyBanc are explicitly modelling toward — the five brokers who just moved targets to $75–84 have room to run, the Hold bloc capitulates, and the historical conditional median positive move of +8.7% is the right order of magnitude [6][32][20].

**Bear case.** The most concrete bearish fact in this file is that **17 analysts' average price target ($64.58) is below the $66.44 spot, after five upward revisions in six sessions** [11]. The sell side has already spent its ammunition and still cannot justify the tape. The stock is +26% in a month, +43% since May, +95% off its April low, on a 69.5x trailing P/E, with 8 of 17 analysts at Hold [10][11][12][19]. The demonstrated behavioural pattern is a fade: on 26 February 2026 NTNX gapped **+19% premarket** on a large beat plus the AMD deal and **closed +4.0%** [79][80][20][16]; on 28 May 2026 it went up 5%+ after hours and closed +4.8% [21][15]. Meanwhile the *hard* constraint has not moved — server DRAM is short through 2027, lead times run to six months (one preview says 6–10 months), and management said in May that this pressures customer budgets *through fiscal 2027* [7][8][39][30]. This is a management team that guided FY26 to $2.90–2.94bn last August, cut it within 90 days, watched the stock fall 17.75% in one session, and is now the subject of three plaintiff-firm securities-fraud investigations for exactly that disclosure [25][17][66][67]. Their rational move on Wednesday is a conservative FY27 guide — into a Street that Oppenheimer has primed for ">$3.20bn" [6]. Add that consensus ARR of $2.51bn already implies deceleration to ~13% from 15%/16% [4][3][29], that the short-cover fuel is 55% spent [50], that the guided diluted share count is *rising* to ~292m despite $382.5m of FY26 buyback [3][41], and that NTNX carries the largest implied move on a board that includes NVDA on the same night [14].

**Base case (mine).** Nutanix beats Q4 — probably revenue near $740–745m, EPS above $0.50, ARR at or slightly above $2.51bn, FY26 FCF inside guide. That part is close to consensual and largely priced. The print then hinges on a single sentence about FY27, and I think the distribution of that sentence is skewed toward "in line to modestly above $3.18bn with a strong margin and FCF guide" rather than the ">$3.20bn plus acceleration" that the most bullish previews want. That combination — good quarter, adequate-but-not-thrilling annual guide, into a 26% run and a consensus target below spot — historically produces a fade, exactly as it did in February and May, but from a much more extended starting point than either of those. I therefore lean modestly negative on direction with genuinely low conviction, and I expect the realised move to come in **well inside** the ±14.49% implied, consistent with the last two quarters. The tail I respect most is upside: if the FY27 revenue guide starts with a 3.2 and is paired with a mid-20s margin, the Hold bloc has nowhere to hide and this trades like the +8.7% conditional median.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

**The single most credible reversal is an FY2027 revenue guide of $3.25bn or better (≥15% growth) paired with a non-GAAP operating margin guide of 24%+ and FCF guidance above $850m.**

Concretely, that requires three things to be said on the call: (1) that the license-delivery and OEM shipment timing issue that caused the November 2025 revenue shift has fully unwound and is not recurring; (2) that the 5% restructuring is margin-accretive from Q2 FY27 rather than merely cost-neutral; and (3) that NC2/public-cloud attach is converting the DRAM shortage from a revenue-timing headwind into a mix tailwind — management already has the anecdote (a Fortune 500 financial-services customer choosing NC2 on AWS because of hardware lead times) and needs to attach a number to it [39].

If those land, the bear case collapses in one move: the "deceleration to 13% ARR growth" framing becomes "reacceleration toward the FY29 mid-to-high-teens target," the 8 Holds are forced to move, and the $47-low target in the range becomes indefensible [11][35]. The mirror image — an FY27 guide at or below ~$3.15bn, or a Q4 landing in the *lower* half of the $725–745m range — would confirm that the revenue-shift dynamic never resolved, revive the disclosure-credibility question that carries live litigation, and in my view produce a move toward the lower bound of the historical negative distribution (−7.8% median, −17.8% precedent).
