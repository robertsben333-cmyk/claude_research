# OKTA — Okta, Inc.

**Event confirmed: YES** — Q2 FY2027 (quarter ended 31 July 2026), Wednesday **26 August 2026, after the U.S. market close**, call at 2:00 p.m. PT / 5:00 p.m. ET. Confirmed from Okta's own IR release and the Businesswire wire copy [1][2].

**What this print is about.** Okta is the one large-cap SaaS name that has *won* the 2026 "SaaSpocalypse." While the software complex was repriced on the fear that AI agents cannibalise per-seat licences — IGV fell 24% in Q1 2026, its worst quarter since Q4 2008, and roughly $2T of software market cap was erased [30] — Okta ran the opposite narrative: agents are *more* identities to govern, not fewer seats. That narrative was validated on 28 May 2026, when Q1 FY27 beat, guidance was raised, net retention ticked to 107% and RPO grew 16%, and the stock closed **+30.14%** the next session [3][8][15]. The stock is +51.0% YTD and was +83.6% over six months as of the 25 Aug close [8]. The problem is that the re-rating has already happened: forward P/E went from roughly 24x to ~34x [7][20], Street FY27 revenue and EPS now sit exactly on company guidance midpoints [20], and eight sell-side price-target raises landed in the five sessions 20–24 August — Morgan Stanley $115→$180, JPMorgan $120→$165, BMO $120→$168, Cantor $125→$170 [21]. Meanwhile the stock itself fell 15.7% from its 13 August closing high and 9.25% in the last five sessions [8], alongside a sharp cybersecurity-group de-rating (CRWD −12.9%, ZS −9.3%, PANW −9.2% over the same five sessions) [8]. So this print is not really about EPS — consensus is bolted to the guide. It is about whether **cRPO** re-accelerates enough to justify the multiple, in a quarter that carries an embedded ~1-point professional-services headwind and a ~1-point interest-income headwind that management flagged in advance [15][16].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Current remaining performance obligations (cRPO) — the reported Q2 number against the $2.505–2.515B guide, and, more decisively, the Q3 FY27 cRPO growth guide.**

Why, and how I know:
- Okta guides cRPO explicitly and by name every quarter, in the press release, alongside revenue [3][16]. It is the company's own designated forward-bookings metric and the only forward number management commits to besides revenue and margin.
- Revenue is close to mechanically visible out of backlog at 9% growth. cRPO is where the *news* is.
- The +30.14% move on 29 May was explicitly attributed to backlog, not the P&L: "Remaining performance obligations climbed 16% to $4.7 billion, with bookings building faster than Okta could recognize them as revenue, and that gap is what traders bid up" [13]. The entire 2026 re-rating rests on the claim that AI-agent identity is re-accelerating bookings.
- The gap is the tell: Q1 FY27 RPO grew **16%** while cRPO grew **12%** [3][4]. Long-duration bookings are outrunning near-term ones. Bulls read that as multi-year agentic-identity commitments landing; bears read it as duration-stretching that flatters RPO without converting to revenue. Q2's cRPO print adjudicates it.
- **Consensus for the metric: $2.51B** [17], i.e. the guide midpoint (+11% y/y — a *deceleration* from Q1's +12% and from the year-ago +13% [3][6]).

Secondary signals on the same axis, in rough order of importance: (1) the FY27 revenue guide — raise or reiterate; (2) dollar-based net retention, which ticked to **107%** in Q1 after sitting at 106% for several quarters [22] — a second consecutive uptick would be a genuine inflection, a slip back to 106% would break the story; (3) any first quantification of AI-agent/Agent-SSO contribution, which management has so far described as "not materially contributing" and "a little bit in the guide, but not significant" [15].

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

**Where the business is.** FY26 (ended 31 Jan 2026) revenue was $2.919B, +12% [26]. Q1 FY27 (ended 30 Apr 2026): revenue $765M (+11%), subscription $750M (+11%), RPO $4.719B (+16%), cRPO $2.499B (+12%), GAAP operating income $56M (7% margin), non-GAAP operating income $191M (25%), non-GAAP EPS $0.91, operating cash flow $277M (36% of revenue), free cash flow $271M (35%), cash and equivalents $2.589B [3][4].

**What changed at the last print (28 May 2026):**
- Dollar-based net retention **106% → 107%** — the first sequential improvement after a multi-quarter plateau [22].
- $100k+ ACV customers **5,180, +6% y/y**, +80 net adds in the quarter, now ~**85% of total ACV, up from 80%** [15][22]. Mix is shifting decisively to large enterprise.
- FY27 revenue and EPS guidance **raised** [3][13][14].
- Headcount ~6,445 at quarter end [22].

**What is embedded in the Q2 guide that will make the optics messy** — management pre-flagged both on the Q1 call [15]:
1. A **professional-services transition to global systems integrator partners**, worth "about a 1-point impact" to FY27 revenue and "expected to materialize beginning in Q2." Consensus already models PS&other revenue **down 37.7% y/y** to $10.59M [17]. Subscription revenue growth (+9.9% consensus) is therefore the cleaner read than total revenue (+8.8%).
2. **~1 point of lower interest income**, from the buyback and the settlement of the convertible notes.

**Margin.** Guided Q2 non-GAAP operating margin of 26% is **two points below** the 28% Okta actually printed in Q2 FY26 [3][6]. That is a deliberate reinvestment step, but it means the y/y margin-expansion story that supported the stock in FY26 is on pause this quarter.

**Balance sheet and capital return.** Net cash roughly $2.18B ($2.589B cash less $411M debt) [9]. A **$1B share repurchase authorisation** was approved 5 January 2026 [25]. In Q1 FY27 Okta bought back ~3.0M Class A shares for **$241M**, leaving **$680M** [15][25]. The $350M convertible note matured 15 June 2026 [25]. Buyback pace at Q1's rate is roughly 1.7% of shares out per quarter — a real, non-trivial offset to SBC dilution and a live bid under the stock.

**Customer concentration.** Okta does not disclose a material single-customer concentration; the relevant concentration is that ~85% of ACV now sits with 5,180 $100k+ accounts [15][22]. That raises deal lumpiness and renewal-timing risk in any single quarter — a plausible mechanism for a cRPO miss that is timing, not demand.

**Product.** On **24 August 2026**, two sessions before the print, Okta announced general availability of **Agent SSO**, bringing the open Cross App Access (XAA) standard into the product used by 20,000+ customers, positioning AI agents as first-class governed identities [27]. This is the commercial vehicle for the whole agentic thesis. It is brand new — it cannot be in Q2 numbers, and management has already said the AI products are not material [15].

**Not disclosed.** Okta does not break out Workforce Identity vs. Customer Identity (Auth0) revenue [26]; there is no segment mix to analyse.

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**The regime.** 2026 has been the year of the "SaaSpocalypse": IGV fell 24% in Q1 2026, its worst quarter since Q4 2008, with roughly $2T of software market cap erased on the thesis that AI agents destroy per-seat licensing. ServiceNow grew revenue 21% and Salesforce's Agentforce ARR grew 205% and both stocks were still down over 30% YTD [30]. **Okta has been on the right side of that trade all year** — identity for agents is additive, not cannibalistic — which is exactly why it is +51% YTD [8] while the group was repriced. That asymmetry is the thing at risk in this print.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Okta guides prudently by management's own repeated description [15], has beaten the Zacks consensus in all four trailing quarters at an average +7.65% surprise [18], and beat Q1 revenue by ~$13–14M [4][14]. The leading indicators turned at Q1: net retention 106%→107% after a multi-quarter plateau [22], RPO +16% running four points ahead of cRPO [3], $100k+ customers now ~85% of ACV up from 80% [15], and a "record-setting" AI pipeline with AI-specific deal sizes "significantly larger" than average [15] — none of which management put in the guide. Agent SSO went GA on 24 August into a base of 20,000+ customers [27]. The stock has already de-risked 15.7% from its 13 August high and 9.25% in five sessions [8], short interest is at a four-month low with 2.7 days to cover [28], retail is trimming [29], and the front-week book is paying for $129 puts [10] — the pain trade is up. Six of the last eight reactions were positive with a mean signed move of +5.5% [8], the last three all positive. Ten sell-side firms have just re-anchored targets at $162–180 [21], giving the stock an immediate re-rating runway on any cRPO beat plus FY27 raise. Net cash of ~$2.18B and $680M of buyback authorisation remaining [9][25] provide a bid.

**Bear case.** Every consensus line sits exactly on the guidance midpoint — revenue, EPS, cRPO, and the FY27 numbers [17][20] — so an in-line quarter is a de-rating at ~34x forward EPS [20], up from ~24x before the May pop. The guide itself embeds *deceleration*: revenue +9% vs Q1's +11%, cRPO +11% vs Q1's +12% and the year-ago +13% [3][6], with non-GAAP operating margin stepping down to 26% from 28% a year ago [3][6]. Two self-inflicted headwinds land specifically in Q2 — the ~1-point professional-services shift to GSI partners and ~1 point of lost interest income [15] — with consensus already modelling PS revenue −37.7% [17]. The CFO sold 65,000 shares (~$7.62M) ten days after the +30% pop on a Form 4 carrying no 10b5-1 footnote, while three other insiders' filings in the same window did cite plans [34b]; total insider selling since the last print is ~$21.8M with zero buys [34]. The entire cybersecurity complex de-rated 7–13% in the last five sessions with no company-specific catalyst [8][38], the group has twice this year been gapped down on AI-disruption headlines [36][37], and CrowdStrike reports in the same after-hours window [40]. Palo Alto/CyberArk is now a bundled, better-capitalised competitor with 60% NGS ARR growth [41][42]. Eight price-target raises in five sessions is analysts chasing price, not new information — and it means the marginal buyer is already in. GF Value pegs fair value at $104.53, ~29% below spot [32]. Two of the last eight reactions were −16.2% and −17.6% [8], both after run-ups.

**Base case (mine).** Okta beats a guide it set conservatively — revenue in the $800–810M area, EPS $1.00–1.03 — and reports cRPO at or slightly above the $2.515B high end. It raises FY27 revenue guidance modestly, to roughly $3.21–3.23B, and reiterates the 25–26% margin and 27–28% FCF bands. Management repeats that AI-agent revenue is not yet material but points to pipeline. That is a *good* quarter that is not obviously good enough to re-rate a stock at 34x forward into a de-rating security tape — but it also lands into positioning that has already been cut hard, with the shares 15.7% off their high, short interest at a four-month low, and ten firms carrying $162–180 targets. I read the modal outcome as a **modest positive move, well inside the 13% implied**, with a fatter left tail than right tail: the up-scenario is +4% to +8%, the down-scenario is −12% to −18%. Probability-weighted that is close to a coin flip with a slight upward tilt from the base rate (6 of 8 up, mean signed +5.5%) and the pre-print drawdown. **Direction score +10, probability up 55, and my conviction in that read is genuinely low** — the evidence is unusually complete and unusually two-sided.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

**The single most credible reversal:** cRPO prints *inside* the guided $2.505–2.515B range rather than above it, and management guides **Q3 FY27 cRPO growth to 11% or below** while raising FY27 revenue only to the top of the existing range (~$3.20–3.21B) rather than through it.

Why that specifically breaks the thesis: the whole 2026 re-rating is the claim that AI-agent identity re-accelerates bookings, and cRPO is the only number Okta publishes that can test the claim within a quarter. A third consecutive quarter of *decelerating* cRPO growth (13% → 12% → 11%) with AI contribution still described as "not significant" [15] converts the narrative from "inflection" to "9%-grower trading at 34x on a story." At that point the $162–180 targets set in the week of 20–24 August [21] become downgrade fodder rather than support, GF Value's $104.53 [32] becomes the reference point, and the two historical −16%/−18% reactions [8] become the template. I would flip to Lean Down at that point, and I would flip *hard* if it coincided with a weak CrowdStrike print in the same after-hours window [40].

**The mirror-image flip to the upside:** cRPO ≥ $2.53B (≥12% growth), Q3 cRPO guided to ≥13%, FY27 revenue raised above $3.22B, net retention to 108%, and any *quantified* AI/Agent-SSO contribution. That combination would validate the RPO/cRPO gap as real multi-year agentic bookings rather than duration stretching, and would send the stock at the upper end of the implied move — the 28 August $165 calls that traded 468 lots [10] are the expression of that view.
