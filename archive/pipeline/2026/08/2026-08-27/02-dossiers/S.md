# S — SentinelOne, Inc.

**What this print is about.** SentinelOne reports fiscal Q2 2027 (quarter ended 31 July 2026) after the close on Thursday 27 August 2026, and it does so into the most favourable cybersecurity tape of the year and the least favourable set-up of its own making. Overnight, CrowdStrike printed a record net-new-ARR quarter and raised (+8.0% pre-market) and Okta beat and raised (+19.0% pre-market), dragging S up ~4.3% pre-market before it has said a word [1][2][3]. That is the problem: S is being re-rated on someone else's numbers into a print where management's own Q2 revenue guide of $289–291M sits exactly on the $290.03M consensus [4][5], the buyback is exhausted [6], the sell side has already chased its price targets up to roughly where the stock trades [7], and the stock's own post-earnings record is one of the worst in software — 8 of the last 11 reports down, mean absolute move 10.3%, with a −8.2% reaction last quarter on a revenue miss and an 8% workforce cut [8][9][10]. Options price an 11.4% move on Friday's expiry [11]. The question is not whether cyber demand is good — CRWD and OKTA just answered that — but whether a company that guided to consensus, whose billings grew 13.6% against 21% revenue growth, and whose second-quarter CFO is five months into the job, can clear a bar that the sector rally has just raised for it intraday.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Net new ARR, and whether the 23% total-ARR growth rate holds — with the FY27 revenue guide as the sign-off.**

Why, and how I know:
- Management has explicitly repositioned the story around ARR, not revenue. Q1 FY27's headline was "**record $44 million in net new ARR, +55% year over year**" and "ARR growth **accelerated** to 23%" — that framing was the entire defence of a quarter in which revenue came in at $277M against a $277.38M estimate [19][23].
- Yet the stock still fell 8.16% [8]. The record net new ARR did not save it, because **billings grew only 13.6% YoY to $229.8M** [24] against 21% revenue growth — a leading indicator running well behind the reported line. If Q2 shows the same divergence, the ARR headline will again be discounted.
- The new CFO put the target on this metric herself. Sonalee Parekh said she would be "extremely focused on unit economics", named improving net retention as her priority, and noted "every point we improve on Net Retention creates a tailwind on revenue" [19]. Q1 NRR in the $100K+ cohort was **above 110%**, improved both sequentially and YoY [19][24].
- Zacks' own preview frames the quarter around exactly this: "continued new-logo additions, improving net retention, and multiproduct expansion" and whether "new federal and channel wins support the recurring revenue base" [4][25].
- CrowdStrike just reset the benchmark hours earlier: **record $332.8M net new ARR, +51% YoY, ARR +25% to $5.84B** [2]. Any S net-new-ARR number that decelerates will be read directly against that.

Secondary metrics that will move the stock: non-endpoint / "emerging solutions" share of ARR (reached ~50% in Q1 [19]), Prompt Security ARR (nearly doubled sequentially in Q1 [19][4]), $100K+ customer count (1,702 in Q1, +17% [19]), and whether the $45M annualised restructuring savings [10] show up as an FY27 operating-margin raise above the 10% midpoint.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The demand environment is verifiably excellent right now and S has the operating leverage to convert it. CrowdStrike just posted record net new ARR of $332.8M, +51% YoY, and raised FY guidance [2]; Okta beat and raised and jumped 19% [46] — two July-quarter-end security names, both stronger than expected, which is the cleanest possible read on the exact quarter S is about to report. S's own Q1 already showed the same shape: **record $44M net new ARR, +55% YoY, ARR growth accelerating to 23%, RPO +30%, NRR >110%, non-endpoint solutions at ~half of ARR, Prompt Security ARR nearly doubling sequentially** [19]. On top of that the company holds $45M of annualised cost savings it has not yet banked into guidance [10], a fresh AWS Bedrock AgentCore integration that opens console-native, pay-as-you-go consumption across all commercial AWS regions [27], and a Zacks-recorded record of beating the EPS consensus in each of the last four quarters with an average surprise of +47.5% [4]. Balance sheet: $812M cash, zero debt [19][16]. If S posts revenue above $293M, holds ARR growth at 23%+, and raises FY27 revenue above $1.205B while lifting the margin outlook on the restructuring savings, an 11%+ gap higher is entirely available — the stock did +16.6% on 2023-12-06 and +7.1% on 2025-08-29 [8].

**Bear case.** Guidance is exactly consensus on both lines — $289–291M against $290.03M, $0.06–0.08 against $0.07 [4][5] — which means there is no cushion and Zacks' Earnings ESP is 0.00% with a Rank of #3 [4]. The leading indicator is already deteriorating: **billings grew 13.6% in Q1 against 21% revenue growth** [24], and revenue has come in at or below the estimate repeatedly, with Motley Fool counting only one revenue beat in the last four quarters [18]. The reaction record is the single most damning fact in this dossier: **8 of the last 11 reports down, mean signed reaction −3.93% across 20 events and −5.22% across the last 8** [8][14]. The stock has no support underneath it — the $200M buyback was fully exhausted by December 2025 with $0 remaining [6] — while SBC runs at ~27–30% of revenue [29] and the share count grows 4–5% a year [29]. Positioning is crowded long: put/call volume ratio 0.21, negative IV skew, only 5.6% short with 3 days to cover, 83% institutional [17][34][16]. Six price targets were raised in the six sessions before the print, taking the consensus to $21.71 — and the stock is *already at $21.40 pre-market* [1][7], meaning the sector rally has consumed the entire upside to consensus fair value before management speaks. And a five-month-old CFO who used the phrases "extremely focused on unit economics" and "back-end loaded quarter… expected to continue" [19] is far more likely to guide FY27 to the high end of the existing range than to raise it.

**Base case.** S beats its own revenue guide modestly (revenue $292–295M, ARR ~$1.23–1.25B, ARR growth 22–23%) and beats the $0.07 EPS line comfortably on the restructuring savings, because the CRWD and OKTA prints say the quarter was good for everyone [2][46]. But the FY27 revenue guide moves to the top of, rather than above, $1.195–1.205B, ARR growth holds flat rather than accelerating again, and billings growth again lags revenue. Against a stock that has already gapped ~4.3% higher on the sector halo and sits at the consensus price target, that is a "good but not good enough" outcome — the classic S set-up. Modal outcome: a move well inside the 11.36% implied, with the distribution skewed left. The 20-event mean signed reaction of −3.93% [8] is a reasonable centre of gravity, and the fat left tail (three of the last eight reactions worse than −11%) is what the options are pricing.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **an FY27 revenue guide raised decisively above the current $1.205B top end — say to $1.215–1.225B — accompanied by ARR growth *accelerating* past 23% and net new ARR above roughly $60M**, i.e. a second consecutive record. That combination would break the bear thesis at its root, because the bear case is not "demand is weak" (CRWD and OKTA just disproved that) but "S cannot convert sector demand into its own accelerating recurring revenue, and its guidance philosophy caps the upside." A guide raise of that size from a five-month-old CFO who has been signalling conservatism would be a genuine information event, not a beat-and-maintain, and it would force the 13 Holds and 1 Sell among the 35 covering analysts [7] to re-underwrite. A **new share repurchase authorisation** announced alongside — the previous $200M programme is fully spent and expires 2026-12-31 [6] — would be the confirming signal, because it would restore the bid that has been absent since December 2025 and would say management thinks the stock is cheap at $21 after a 37% YTD run.

The mirror-image reversal of the bull case: **billings decelerating again into the low teens while net new ARR fails to grow YoY**. Given the Q1 divergence (13.6% billings vs 21% revenue) [24] and the fact that Q2 FY26 was itself a record net-new-ARR comp [20], this is a live risk, and if it happens the sector-halo gap of ~4.3% reverses first and the historical −11% to −14% reaction tail opens up.
