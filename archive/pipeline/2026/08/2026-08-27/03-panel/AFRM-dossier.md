## 2. What the market cares about

The FQ4 (quarter ended 2026-06-30) headline is a low-drama formality — consensus
($1.11B revenue, GMV ~$13.40B) already sits at the high end of management's own May
guidance, and Affirm has beaten every quarter this fiscal year. The metric that actually
trades is the **initial FY2027 guide**: GMV growth against the >25% medium-term
framework, RLTC as a percentage of GMV (currently running at 4.3%, above the 3.75-4.0%
target band), adjusted operating margin (30-35% target), and the trajectory of the
credit-loss provision. A guide that merely restates the existing framework, or a visible
step-up in provisioning, is the disappointment case even on a clean quarterly beat.

## 5. Thesis

**Bull case:** Card GMV +146% YoY, unit economics running above the medium-term
framework, first GAAP operating profit as a public company, and a Strong Buy consensus
(25 Buy/9 Hold, mean target ~$92-93) with sell-side raising targets into the print
(Oppenheimer to $100, TD Cowen $117). Insider/communication forensics found zero
information content in insider selling and an unusually unhedged, confident credit
narrative from management.

**Bear case:** The Feb-2026 precedent is exact — a clean beat and raised guidance still
produced a 14.47% fall when the credit-loss provision jumped and the GMV guide implied
deceleration. Delinquency (30+ days ex-Peloton) is up 29bps YoY to 2.8%, the allowance
for credit losses rose to 6.0% of loans held for investment from 5.4% QoQ, and BTIG's
ABS-trustee data shows 30-59 day delinquencies at all-time highs across Affirm's newest
securitization pools. Sector read-through is punitive: both Klarna and Sezzle beat their
quarters and still fell 19-34% on guidance concerns this month.

**Base case:** A likely clean FQ4 beat with an FY27 guide that broadly restates the
existing medium-term framework and a credit-provision trend the market has already
priced through once (in May). The panel's near-zero net score reflects that this
outcome could plausibly break either way depending on specific numbers not yet public.

## 6. What flips it

The red-team persona (score +8, reversal risk 65, weighted 1.5x) built the panel's
sharpest bear case — the Feb-2026 beat-yet-fell precedent plus rising delinquencies and
a take-rate framework below the current 9.0% run-rate — but concluded its own modal
outcome is still up, because the market absorbed materially similar credit deterioration
in May and bought the stock +21.8% anyway. That case did not survive as a directional
call, which is itself the signal: two other personas (options-positioning, macro-peers)
independently flagged the same beat-and-fade risk from separate angles, which is why
reversal risk stays High (60.6 consensus, 65 red-team) even though no single persona's
score is decisively negative. What would tip it down: an FY27 guide below the medium-term
framework or a visible provisioning step-up ratifying the BTIG vintage data. What would
tip it up: a framework-intact guide with delinquencies flat-to-down YoY.

## 8. Insider / communication / alt-data findings

No discretionary insider selling found — every 2026 disposition traces to a pre-existing
10b5-1 plan with a verified footnote (mechanical monthly director sales, plan-scheduled
option exercises), and there were no open-market purchases and no executive departures.
The board added a consumer-credit-experienced director (ex-Capital One) to the Audit
committee in July 2026, which one persona read as inconsistent with an impending credit
problem. Communication forensics found management's FQ3 shareholder letter *added*
disclosure rather than narrowing it, and its credit language was unusually direct about
both the good (healthy consumer) and the bad (rising ex-Peloton delinquency) — a
transparency pattern that argues against a hidden deterioration. The one real
contradicting alt-data point: BTIG's 2026-08-21 note on elevated ABS-pool delinquencies,
which is correlational and vintage-mix-confounded but genuinely available pre-print.
