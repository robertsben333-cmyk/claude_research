# SNOW — Snowflake Inc.

**Event confirmed: YES.** Two independent high-quality sources — Snowflake's own IR press release dated 03 Aug 2026 [1] and TipRanks' earnings calendar, which flags the date as "After Close (Confirmed)" [2] — put the Q2 FY2027 print (quarter ended 31 Jul 2026) after the US close on **Wednesday 02 September 2026**, with the call at 14:00 PT / 21:00 UTC. No date change and no pre-announcement was found.

**What this print is about.** Snowflake enters this quarter having just delivered the single largest one-day earnings move in its history (+36.48% on 28 May 2026 [3][2]) on the back of a 34% product-revenue acceleration, a raised FY27 guide, a $6B AWS commitment and the Natoma acquisition [4][5]. The stock has since re-rated another ~34% to $319.80 [6] and now trades at roughly 16.3x forward sales and >120x forward non-GAAP earnings [7]. This print is therefore not about whether Snowflake beats — the company has beaten the Zacks consensus in four straight quarters by an average of 22% [7] and sell-side channel checks going into the quarter are close to unanimous (Citizens: 88% of data points positive vs 53% the prior quarter [8]). It is about **whether a 3%-magnitude beat plus an FY27 raise is enough at a price where the Street's own average target sits at or below spot**, in a week where three data-infrastructure peers (MongoDB, Elastic, Datadog) all sold off after their own prints, and on a tape that turned risk-off the day before on a re-priced Fed. My read: the bar, not the business, is the variable.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**The FY27 product-revenue guide, and specifically the *increment* to it — not Q2 EPS, not Q2 revenue.**

Why. Q2 revenue is essentially known: management guided $1,415–1,420M, checks are uniformly positive, and every broker with a published view models a beat of 3–4% [24][19][8]. A beat is priced. What is *not* priced, and what the stock traded on last quarter, is the forward number: on 27 May the stock rose 36% on a **$180M raise to the FY27 product-revenue guide** ($5,660M → $5,840M) *plus* a 100bp operating-margin raise, *plus* the $6B AWS deal, *plus* Natoma [4][5][14]. That is the comparison set.

**What the market expects for it, and how I know.** No published consensus FY27 product-revenue figure was found (coverage gap). My inference from the sourced pieces: if Q2 product revenue lands at ~$1.46B (+$43M vs guide) and management flows that through plus a modest H2 raise, FY27 product revenue guidance goes to roughly **$5.95–6.00B**. Cantor's explicit point that the beat magnitude is **narrowing from 5.5% to 3%** [24] is the single most important sourced datum in this dossier, because it says the *incremental* raise is likely smaller than last quarter's — against a stock 34% higher.

**Secondary metrics that will be read as confirming or contradicting:** RPO (Q1: $9.21B, +38% y/y [4]), NRR (Q1: 126% [4]), and CoCo/Snowflake Intelligence adoption (Q1: CoCo in >7,100 accounts, Snowflake Intelligence accounts more than doubled q/q, AI capabilities used by >13,600 accounts) [5][19]. Snowflake does not break out AI revenue as a line item; it last disclosed a **$100M AI revenue run-rate** milestone in Q3 FY26 [27]. A first hard AI-revenue dollar disclosure would be a material new datum in either direction.

**Trajectory.** Product revenue growth has *accelerated*: 29% (Q3 FY26) → 30% (Q4 FY26) → 34% (Q1 FY27) [26][4]. Q1 was described as the strongest sequential dollar growth in company history [5]. This is the opposite of the deceleration story that halved the stock in H1 2026 [16], and is the core of the re-rating.

**What changed since the last print:**
- **$6B multi-year AWS commitment** (Graviton compute + AI accelerators), more than doubling the prior agreement, with reduced bandwidth costs; >$7B lifetime AWS Marketplace sales [5][28].
- **Natoma acquisition** (~20 employees) to extend Snowflake Intelligence and CoCo into action workflows [5].
- **Observe acquisition** (announced 8 Jan 2026, reported ~$1B, Snowflake's largest ever [29]); 173 employees added in Q1 and management flagged **~150bp of margin headwind** from it [5].
- **$200M OpenAI partnership expansion** [5]; a prior **$200M Anthropic partnership** making Claude models available to 12,600+ customers [30].
- **Cortex AI Gateway** launched (governance/orchestration for first- and third-party AI agents), plus integrations from Alteryx, Aembit and 1Password (late Jul / early Aug 2026) [31].
- **CRO transition**: Jonathan Boulier became Chief Revenue Officer; **co-founder Benoit Dageville stepped away from day-to-day operations in mid-June 2026**, remaining on the board [5].

**Unit economics / margins.** Non-GAAP product gross margin guided flat at 75.0% for FY27 *despite* AI mix shift — management says AWS bandwidth savings and AI efficiency offset it [5]. Non-GAAP operating margin guided to 13.5% FY27 vs 10% FY26 [26][5]. Organic hiring was near-frozen in Q1 (17 employees ex-Observe) [5] — a real operating-leverage lever, and a thing to watch for reversal.

**The GAAP problem.** Snowflake is not GAAP-profitable: FY26 net loss $1.33B, four-quarter average GAAP EPS −$0.88, SBC ~$1.6B or ~35% of revenue [32]. Consensus GAAP EPS for Q2 is a −$0.51 loss [9]. Forward non-GAAP P/E is quoted between 121x and 172x depending on source [26][9][10], forward P/S 16.3x vs an internet-software industry 4.08x [7]. EV $109.22B, EV/Sales 21.70x [10].

**Capital structure / dilution.** Buyback authorisation totals $4.5B ($2.0B Feb 2023 + $2.5B Aug 2024, expiry extended to March 2027); **~$1.1B remained available as of 31 Jan 2026** [33], of which $300M was used in Q1 FY27 [4]. FY26 repurchases: 4.9M shares for $873.5M at a $177.37 weighted-average price [33] — a good use of capital in hindsight, but the remaining authorisation buys far fewer shares at $320. Convertible notes: $2.0B ($1.0B due 2027 + $1.0B due 2029) placed September 2024 with capped calls [34]; the 2027 tranche is a near-term maturity/conversion item worth watching in the 10-Q.

**Customer concentration.** No single-customer concentration disclosed; the model is broad (13,900+ customers [9]). This is a *contrast* with Datadog, which just flagged a usage reduction from its largest customer [35] — a relative positive for Snowflake.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Snowflake's growth is genuinely accelerating — 29% → 30% → 34% product revenue over three quarters [26][4] — and the acceleration is AI-driven and early: CoCo is in 7,100+ accounts within a quarter of launch, Snowflake Intelligence accounts more than doubled q/q, AI touches >13,600 accounts, and management said CoCo was "the largest driver to the increase in our forecast" [5][19]. Pre-print checks are the best in years: Citizens at 88% positive data points vs 53% [8], UBS seeing accelerating AI demand [8], Morgan Stanley's CIO survey showing SNOW with the biggest expected acceleration in large-cap software [57]. The base rate is strongly with the bulls — six of the last seven prints were up, four of the last four beat EPS by an average 22% [7], and Robins has beaten his own guide by 3–5.5% consistently [24][5]. Options are cheap versus history (12.9% implied vs 17.0% realised mean [3]), skew is flat [13], and flow is 2:1 calls [11]. If product revenue lands at $1.47B+ and FY27 goes to $6.0B with another margin raise, the stock re-rates again — and unlike MongoDB, Snowflake has no disclosed large-customer concentration risk to trip over [35].

**Bear case.** Everything the bull case cites is already in the price and in the Street's models. The consensus price target across four aggregators sits at $302–$332 [20][9][6][21] against a $319.80 spot [6] — the Street's own published upside is roughly zero after twelve target raises in three weeks. Cantor, the most bullish target on the tape at $405, explicitly models the beat **narrowing from 5.5% to 3%** [24]; Robins has told the market a 3% beat is merely "very solid" [5]. Three data-infrastructure peers reported in the last four weeks, all beat, and all fell — MongoDB −13.7% on a beat-and-raise [53][54], Elastic −28.7% [55], Datadog −5.4% [56]. The macro turned on 1 September: Nasdaq −1.3%, yields spiking, oil spiking, and the market now pricing a **September Fed hike** rather than cuts [47][48][49] — the worst regime for a 16.3x-sales, >120x-forward-earnings name [7][26]. Insiders have sold $1.04B in twelve months with zero buys [32], Slootman twice liquidated 300,000 exercised shares in five weeks [42], and retail sentiment on Stocktwits has been *bearish* through the entire August melt-up [38][8]. Snowflake still loses $1.33B GAAP a year on $1.6B of SBC [32], and Databricks is compounding at ~80% [59].

**Base case (mine).** Snowflake beats: product revenue ~$1.46–1.48B (3–4% above the $1,417.5M midpoint), non-GAAP EPS $0.48–0.52 against $0.45 consensus, NRR holding 125–127%, RPO still growing 30%+, and an FY27 product-revenue guide lifted to roughly $5.95–6.00B. That is a good quarter and a worse *setup* than last quarter's, because the increment to the forward number will be smaller against a stock 34% higher. I expect a violent move — the implied 12.9% is if anything too low given a 17.0% realised mean — with the distribution slightly skewed to the downside because (a) the buy-side bar is ~$1.46B and the sell-side model is ~$1.42B, so "beat" and "beat enough" are different events [25][24]; (b) the three freshest peer prints all sold off on beats [53][55][56]; and (c) the consensus target is below spot, so a re-rating requires *new* targets, not existing ones. I put this at roughly a 46/54 up/down split with low conviction — the fundamental signal is bullish and the positioning/valuation/macro signal is bearish, and I do not think the evidence lets anyone resolve that cleanly.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **a second consecutive guidance step-up of last quarter's magnitude, made explicit and attributed to a disclosed AI revenue number.** Concretely: FY27 product revenue guided to **$6.05B or higher** (a ≥$210M raise, larger than May's $180M), *plus* a non-GAAP operating margin raise above 13.5%, *plus* a first hard dollar disclosure for Cortex/CoCo revenue (the last such disclosure was a $100M run-rate in Q3 FY26 [27]) showing a multiple of that. That combination would do to the bear case what May's print did — it would prove the AI contribution is a durable second growth curve rather than a one-quarter forecast adjustment, and it would force the twelve brokers whose targets now bracket the spot price to move again. The mirror image is equally concrete and equally credible: **a Q2 product beat of ~3% with an FY27 raise of only ~$100M or less, and NRR flat-to-down**, which would be read as MongoDB's quarter — beat, raise, decelerate — and, on a 12.9% implied move with no put skew, would very plausibly produce a double-digit decline.

A secondary reversal path, lower probability but worth naming: **any commentary that AI consumption is cannibalising rather than adding to core warehouse consumption**, or that cost-control measures (management already flagged "cost limits at an account level or at a particular agent level" on the Q1 call [5]) are suppressing credit burn. That is the mechanism by which the "consolidation is a bear case" argument [19] would become a number rather than a worry.
