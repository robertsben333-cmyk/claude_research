**What this print is about.** Almost nothing about the September quarter itself is in dispute. Management pre-set Q3 with unusual precision on 3 June: ~$29.4bn revenue, $16.0bn of AI semiconductor revenue (+200% y/y), ~74% gross margin, ~67% non-GAAP operating margin [2][17]. Against: Google has visibly begun multi-sourcing its TPU program (a Marvell commercial agreement signed 29 July and disclosed 19 August, plus reports of AMD on TPU v10), and one bank has already cut Broadcom on that thesis [26][27][28]. The stock enters at $369.68, essentially on top of max pain ($370) with dealers short gamma [3][10]. This is a guidance event with a pre-announced P&L, into a de-rated stock, in a tape that has been punishing even good AI prints (Marvell beat, raised, and fell 10.3% four sessions ago [29][30]).

## 3. The one metric that matters

**The fiscal-2027 AI semiconductor revenue framing — specifically whether "in excess of $100 billion" is replaced with a higher number, and what Q4 AI revenue is guided to.**

How I know:
- The June 3 selloff is directly attributed to this. Broadcom beat revenue *and* EPS and still fell 12.6%; the cause reported was "management's decision not to raise its fiscal 2027 AI semiconductor revenue target above the previously stated 'in excess of $100 billion'" [24], alongside a software miss ($7.18bn vs $7.32bn StreetAccount) [48].
- The pre-print framing is explicit: "The critical test involves whether management raises its full-year 2027 AI semiconductor revenue target" [15]; and the Sept-1 MarketWatch headline on the company page is "Broadcom earnings need to answer these questions for the stock to rally again — investors question whether the company will update AI revenue guidance" [5].
- **Where the bar actually sits:** Morgan Stanley (Overweight, maintained into the print) **models $120bn** of FY27 AI revenue, while "some investors have quietly set a bar at more than $150 billion." The bank's own words: "the gap between those two numbers is where near-term volatility lives" [16].
- Consensus FY27 revenue of $173.4bn [6] is only reachable with AI semis well north of $120bn, so the sell-side model is already above the guide by a wide margin.

**Secondary metrics that can move it:** (i) quarterly AI bookings — Q2 was ">$30bn against the $10.8bn we shipped," with "visibility… all the way to 2028" [18][17]; a bookings number that decelerates would be read as the Google share-loss thesis confirming; (ii) gross margin versus the guided ~74%, since the December 2025 −11.4% session was a *margin* selloff, not a revenue one [19]; (iii) any comment on Google TPU share or the AI XPV financing vehicle.

**My inference on likelihood:** Broadcom's convention has been to set the next fiscal year's AI number on the **Q4 (December) call**, and Tan has now held the "in excess of $100 billion" phrasing across three consecutive calls (it first appeared in March 2026 as "significantly in excess of $100 billion" [22a]). I therefore put a *below-even* probability on a formal numerical raise tonight and a higher probability on qualitative upgrade ("tracking well above," bookings colour, a new customer disclosure). That is precisely the ambiguity the ±8.4% straddle is paying for.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull.** Expectations have already been cut hard while the underlying demand data got better. AVGO enters −25.3% from its 52-week high and −22% since the last report [4][5], at ~19x FY27 EPS versus ~25x at the high [18], after a 14-session −11% slide driven by two *narrative* shocks (a security CVE and a competitor's Google contract) rather than by any change in Broadcom's own numbers [4][28][34]. Meanwhile Nvidia guided ~70% growth for its next fiscal year and rallied 9% [31], TSMC's July revenue rose 45% [32], and Broadcom's own book is 3x its shipments with visibility into 2028 [17][18]. Tan has a clean path to a positive print: Q3 was guided precisely and beats have been the norm (four straight, +2.19% average surprise [12]); Q4 AI guidance of ~$21bn+ against the ~$20.8bn implied by the $56bn FY26 guide (my arithmetic on [2][17]) plus any upgrade of the FY27 phrasing — "well in excess," a hard number, or a new named customer of the type that produced the +9.4% September-2025 session [20] — would be the release valve. With dealers short gamma at $371 and a call wall at $400 [10], an upside break would be amplified, and there is $10bn of authorised buyback capacity running to year-end [46].

**Bear.** The single freshest, most directly comparable data point is Marvell: same end market, same customer, beat and raised *both* fiscal years four sessions ago, and closed −10.3% [29][30]. The market is not paying for beats in this tape; it is paying for step-function raises, and AVGO has three consecutive quarters of *not* delivering one (Dec-25 margin warning −11.4% [19], Jun-26 no-raise −12.6% [24]). The FY27 bar has drifted to $120bn (Morgan Stanley) or >$150bn (some investors) against a $100bn guide [16], and Street FY27 revenue of $173bn [6] cannot be met with a reiteration. Layer on a genuinely new structural problem — Google's TPU share moving 95% → 80% → 65% on Macquarie's numbers [26], with Marvell contracted [28] and AMD reported on v10 [27] — that a good Q3 cannot refute, plus top-five customer concentration rising to ~45% [45], plus CoWoS-S supply constraints cutting 2H26 TPU output [27], plus gross margin already guided down 310bp to ~74% [25], plus an off-balance-sheet financing structure whose contingent exposure has never been quantified in a filing [36]. The sell-side is 42% above spot with targets still rising into a falling stock [6][12][43] — that is downgrade fuel, not support. Short interest of 1.2% [3] means there is nobody to squeeze.

**Base.** My central expectation: Broadcom beats Q3 (revenue $29.4–30.0bn, non-GAAP EPS $3.25–3.40), guides Q4 revenue in the $35–36bn area with AI semis around $21bn (all my inference from [2][6][17]), lands gross margin at or slightly better than the guided 74%, and **repeats "in excess of $100 billion" for FY27 with more emphatic qualitative language and probably a new bookings number rather than a new headline figure.** That combination is genuinely two-sided: it is better than June (because the tape's AI-capex evidence has improved and the stock has already de-rated 25%) but it does not clear the $120–150bn bar that has been publicly articulated [16]. I expect a move of roughly the implied size in either direction — the last five realised moves averaged 8.6% against an 8.4% straddle [7][8] — with a mild upward tilt from the depth of the drawdown, the short-gamma amplification working from a max-pain pin, and Nvidia's read-through. Conviction is low; this is close to a coin flip on direction with a fat two-sided tail.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The consensus view going in is "beat is certain, guidance is the risk, stock is cheap after −25%." The most credible reversal of that is **an explicit FY27 AI semiconductor number at or above $130bn, delivered tonight rather than deferred to the December call, accompanied by a Q3 AI bookings figure above the $30bn Q2 print and a named fifth or sixth large customer.** That single combination would (a) resolve the June grievance directly, (b) mathematically overwhelm the Google share-loss arithmetic — even at Macquarie's 65% TPU share by 2028 [26], a $130bn+ FY27 base implies the non-Google book (OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, the two undisclosed $6bn customers [17]) has more than absorbed the loss — and (c) force the 49-analyst "Strong Buy" cohort to raise numbers rather than cut targets. It is credible because the bookings coverage is already 3x shipments with 2028 visibility [17][18], because the Apollo/Blackstone $35bn facility removes the customer-funding objection [35], and because Tan has a documented history of dropping a market-moving customer disclosure on a Q3 call — that is exactly what produced +9.4% on 5 September 2025 [20].

The mirror-image reversal, which would flip the "already de-rated, so downside is limited" half of consensus: **Q4 AI revenue guided below ~$20bn** (implying the $56bn FY26 guide is being walked back), or any acknowledgement of CoWoS-S-driven volume cuts [27], or first-time quantification of guaranteed lease exposure under the AI XPV structure [36]. Any of those turns a valuation story into an estimate-cut story, and the $350 put wall [10] becomes the next reference point rather than support.
