# PVH — PVH Corp.

**Event confirmed: yes.** PVH releases Q2 fiscal-2026 results Wednesday 2 September 2026 **after the close**, with the conference call the *following* morning, Thursday 3 September at 09:00 ET — hosted by CEO Stefan Larsson and **Interim** CFO Melissa Stone [1][2]. Note the unusual AMC-release / next-morning-call split: the tape gets the numbers and the guidance table ~15 hours before it gets management's voice.

**What this print is about.** It is not about Q2 EPS. On 3 June 2026 PVH beat its own Q1 guidance by a wide margin and the stock still fell 17.2% close-to-close — the second consecutive June collapse, after −23.4% in June 2025 — because it cut FY26 revenue guidance to "approximately flat" and blamed the Iran war's drag on EMEA [6][5]. This print is a referendum on whether that June cut was the floor or the first of several. Three things make it unusually loaded: (i) Street consensus now sits at the *top* of company guidance on both the EPS and revenue lines, leaving zero cushion [7]; (ii) roughly **$1.70 of the guided $3.00–$3.10 Q2 EPS is a one-time tariff refund** — PVH is, per Evercore ISI, the only name in its coverage embedding that refund in FY26 guidance [9]; and (iii) the EMEA excuse has got *worse*, not better — the US-Iran ceasefire collapsed in July and the two-month peace window expired on 17 August with no deal [20][21], while Hugo Boss reported the same calendar quarter with sales −10% and explicit consumer declines in Germany, the UK and France [17]. Against that, the stock is at **5.94x forward earnings** [4] versus a five-year median of 7.73x [7], 9.84% of the float is short [4][11], and PVH has beaten its own guide every quarter for two years. This is a genuine two-sided setup priced for a ±9.2% move.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**It is the FY26 EMEA revenue trajectory embedded in reaffirmed (or cut) full-year guidance — and specifically whether management can defend "approximately flat" FY revenue with a credible H2 EMEA recovery.**

Why this and not EPS:
- EMEA is PVH's largest region: **$946.1m of Q1's $2.025bn, ~47%** [6]. In Q1 it was +2% reported but **−5% constant currency**, with both DTC and wholesale down mid-single digits cc [6].
- The June cut was *entirely* an EMEA revenue story. EPS guidance was **reaffirmed** at $11.80–$12.10 that same day [6] — and the stock still fell 17.2%. That is as clean a natural experiment as you get: this stock trades on the EMEA revenue line and on management credibility, not on the EPS line.
- Larsson's own framing on the Q1 call: the war hits three ways — direct Middle East wholesale demand, a knock-on in Turkey ("a big and important market"), and the broader EMEA consumer — with **roughly half the headwind direct (Middle East + Turkey) and half indirect (broader Europe)** [36][35].
- Management explicitly said the effect would be **"more pronounced in the second quarter"** [6]. So Q2 EMEA weakness is pre-announced and should be forgiven. What is *not* pre-announced is H2.

**What the market expects for it, and how I know.** The reaffirmed FY26 "approximately flat" revenue guide [6], set against a Q2 guided at −3% to −4% and a Q1 actual of +2%, arithmetically requires H2 reported revenue to be roughly **flat to modestly positive** — i.e. an EMEA sequential improvement (my arithmetic on [6]). Zacks' segment consensus for Q2 is Calvin Klein **$953m** and Tommy Hilfiger **~$1.1bn**, with DTC **~$1.0bn** [7]; a regional consensus split was not sourceable.

**Secondary metric to watch: the buyback.** PVH repurchased **nothing** in Q1 but reaffirmed "at least $300m for full year 2026" [6]. That is **~8.9% of the $3.36bn market cap** (my arithmetic on [3][6]). PVH has form here — a $500m ASR announced 31 March 2025 with 4.6m shares delivered at $76.43 [38]. An ASR announced alongside this print, at 5.9x earnings, is the single most plausible bullish surprise on the tape.

**Segment mix and unit economics.** Two brands are effectively the whole company: Tommy Hilfiger ~53% and Calvin Klein ~44% of Q1 revenue [6]. Geographically EMEA ~47%, Americas ~30%, APAC ~19% [6]. The DTC/wholesale mix shift is the margin story — DTC +6% with digital +11% while Americas wholesale fell mid-single digits [6]. Gross margin held at 58.6% *despite* tariffs, which is genuinely good execution [6].

**Margin trajectory and the tariff distortion — the crux of the quality-of-earnings case.**
- The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on **20 February 2026** [23]; a CBP refund portal opened in April and the first refunds flowed from **12–13 May 2026** [22].
- FY26 guidance embeds: gross tariff drag of **~$3.30/share (~$195m EBIT)** on an assumed 15% rate from 24 Feb 2026, partially offset by mitigation actions; **+$1.70/share of tariff refunds**; and **+$0.40/share of FX translation** [6][37]. Management targets offsetting ~60% of tariff costs in 2026, up from 30% in 2025 [37].
- Q2's guided ~9.5% operating margin **includes ~470bp from tariff refunds** [6]. On the guided ~$2.09bn revenue base that is **~$98m of pre-tax benefit**, or **~$1.66/share** after the guided ~22% tax on 46.11m shares (my arithmetic on [3][6]). Evercore ISI independently puts it at **$1.70** [9].
- **Therefore: ex-refund, Q2 non-GAAP EPS is approximately $1.30–$1.44 against $2.52 a year ago — down roughly 43–48%** (my arithmetic on [6][9][31]). Essentially the *entire* FY26 tariff-refund benefit lands in this one quarter.
- Evercore ISI's downgrade rationale, in its own words: PVH is **the only company in its coverage universe** to include a one-time tariff-refund benefit in FY26 guidance, worth ~100bp to FY EBIT margin, and **without it PVH could not hold FY26 EBIT margins and EPS would be $1.70 lower** [9].
- **My inference:** the optically strong "+22% y/y EPS growth" headline is an accounting artefact. Anyone reading past the first line sees underlying earnings roughly halving. That is a real risk for the *day-two* reaction even if the headline beats.

**Free cash flow and balance sheet.** TTM free cash flow **$550.5m** [4]; total debt **$4.23bn** including leases, cash **$592.5m** [4] (the 8-K's $2.282bn is funded debt [6]). Q1 operating cash flow was seasonally negative on a $333.8m payables unwind — normal for the apparel cycle, not a red flag [snippet_only, see gaps]. PVH refinanced on 24 June 2026, entering a new Credit Agreement with PVH B.V. and terminating a prior facility (8-K items 1.01/1.02/2.03, filed 29 June) [14]; **the size and maturity of that facility could not be extracted and is a gap.** In FY25 PVH redeemed $500m of 4⅝% notes due 2025 and issued $500m of 5½% notes due 2030 [snippet_only].

**Capital returns / dilution.** Zero buyback in Q1; ≥$300m committed for FY26 [6]. The quarterly dividend is a token **$0.0375/share** (0.21% yield), declared 5 August 2026, payable 23 September to holders of record **2 September** — i.e. the record date is the print date [4][43]. Share count 46.11m [3], down heavily from prior years via the 2025 ASR programmes [38].

**Customer concentration.** Not separately sourced; the relevant concentration is channel and geography (EMEA wholesale) rather than any single customer. **Gap.**

**What changed since the last print (my summary).** (1) The Middle East conflict deteriorated rather than resolved [20][21]. (2) PVH named a permanent CFO — Alexis Rollier, ex-Sephora/LVMH Global COO & CFO — joining **early September 2026**, i.e. within days of this print [12]. (3) JPMorgan downgraded to Underweight [28]. (6) The dollar weakened further against the euro (Q2 EUR/USD averaging ~1.16, +1.4% q/q) [40], a reported-revenue tailwind.

- Zac Coughlin announced his departure in November 2025, leaving at end-2025 for Sirius XM [13]. **Melissa Stone (EVP Global FP&A) has been Interim CFO since 1 January 2026** [12] — she will have signed off on three consecutive quarters including two guidance revisions.

- **My inference, and it cuts both ways.** Rollier is *not* on the 3 September call — it is Larsson and Stone [1]. That argues *against* a classic incoming-CFO "kitchen sink" at this print, because an interim CFO has little incentive to take a large reset on her way out and no authority to bind her successor. But it also means the guidance issued on 3 September is guidance the *incoming* CFO did not set, which raises the odds of a subsequent Q3 rebase. Net: this print is less likely to be a kitchen-sink than the *next* one.

1. **Hugo Boss is the most relevant peer and the most negative datapoint.** It is the closest analogue to PVH's EMEA wholesale-plus-DTC premium-apparel mix, and it reported the *same calendar quarter*: sales −10%, with explicit consumer-spending declines called out in **Germany, the UK and France** [17]. PVH's June thesis was that half the EMEA drag was Middle East/Turkey-specific and half broader-European [36]. Hugo Boss says the broader-European half is not recovering. **This is my single strongest piece of bear evidence.** Caveat: Hugo Boss is also mid-strategic-realignment, so some of the −10% is idiosyncratic, and it *held* its full-year outlook [17].
2. **Tapestry is the template for how this tape punishes guidance.** A 27% EPS beat and 9% sales growth were worth −16.5% because the *forward* revenue midpoint missed [19]. That is precisely PVH's June experience, re-run three months later on a different name. It tells you the marginal buyer this season is not paying for the printed quarter.
3. **Ralph Lauren shows the upside case is live** — but note *why* it worked: a raise, on Asia and North America strength, with a dividend increase [18]. RL's growth is not PVH's growth, and RL has far less EMEA wholesale exposure.
4. **Adidas +13%** confirms that the weakness is not "apparel" broadly — it is premium/heritage EMEA-weighted apparel specifically [42]. That is bad for PVH, because it removes "the whole sector is soft" as an excuse.

**Rate / FX / commodity sensitivity.**
- **FX is a genuine tailwind and it is underappreciated.** Q1 EMEA was +2% reported against −5% constant currency — a **~7-point FX benefit** [6]. In Q2 2026 the dollar weakened further, with USD/EUR averaging 0.8607, up 1.37% q/q, and EUR/USD around 1.17 in June [40]. FY26 guidance embeds only **+$0.40/share** of FX translation [6]. **My inference: PVH is well positioned to beat the *reported* revenue guide of −3%/−4% on translation alone, while constant-currency stays ugly. Watch for the gap between the two — a reported beat on a cc miss is exactly the kind of "low-quality" print an analyst community already primed by Evercore's margin-quality critique [9] will discount.**
- **Rates:** Q2 interest expense guided ~$18m, FY ~$75m [6]; $4.23bn total debt [4] refinanced in June 2026 [14]. Not a swing factor this print.
- **Tariffs / policy:** the dominant commodity-adjacent variable. $195m gross FY EBIT drag at an assumed 15% rate, 60% mitigation targeted [6][37], against $1.70/share of refunds [6][9]. Political risk is live — Senator Warren is publicly pressing corporations to pass tariff refunds back to consumers [snippet_only], which introduces headline risk to a benefit PVH has already booked into guidance.

**Customer / supplier read-throughs.** Wholesale caution in APAC was flagged as a headwind by Zacks [7]. Puma's disclosed shift of US-bound sourcing away from China toward Vietnam, Cambodia and Indonesia [snippet_only] is the industry-wide pattern PVH is also navigating — and it is the same sourcing shift that sits behind China's UEL investigation [34].

## 9. Bull / bear / base

**Bull case.** PVH is a 5.94x-forward-earnings, 7.13x-EV/EBITDA business [4] whose brands are demonstrably working — Q1 DTC +6%, digital +11%, gross margin held flat at 58.6% through a tariff shock, inventory down 5%, APAC +6% cc [6] — trading at a 26% discount to a $91.83 average price target [3]. The Q2 bar was set on 3 June *with* full knowledge of the Middle East drag, and management pre-flagged Q2 as the most pronounced quarter [6], so the quarter itself is de-risked. PVH has beaten its own guide every quarter for two years (trailing four-quarter surprise +16.5%; Q1 26 beat the guide high end by 12%) [7], and a weakening dollar [40] makes the *reported* −3%/−4% revenue guide look beatable on translation alone. Positioning supports a squeeze: 9.87% of float short at 4.9 days-to-cover [11], with the sell-side already capitulated (JPM Underweight, BofA, Evercore In Line) [28][29][9]. And there is a live, large, self-inflicted catalyst — **≥$300m of FY26 buyback, none of it spent in Q1** [6], equal to ~8.9% of the market cap [3], from a company that has executed a $500m ASR before [38]. A reaffirmed FY26 guide plus an ASR announcement at these levels is a credible +10–15% night.

**Bear case.** Consensus sits at the **top** of company guidance on both lines — $3.08 against a $3.00–$3.10 guide, ~$2.1bn against a $2.080–$2.102bn guided range (my arithmetic on [6][7][31]) — so there is no cushion, and Zacks' ESP is 0.00% with the Most Accurate Estimate not above consensus [7]. The 30-day revision trend is **negative** (−1.7%) even as the 60-day looks positive [7][8]. The excuse for the June cut has deteriorated: the US-Iran ceasefire collapsed in July, the blockade was reimposed, and the two-month peace window expired on **17 August** with Trump threatening Oman [20][21] — so the H2 EMEA recovery implied by "approximately flat" FY revenue [6] has no visible support. Hugo Boss, reporting the same quarter with the closest comparable mix, printed **−10%** with Germany, the UK and France consumer declines [17]. And the print's optics are hollow: **~$1.70 of the guided $3.00–$3.10 is a one-time tariff refund** [6][9], meaning ex-refund EPS falls ~43–48% y/y (my arithmetic on [6][9][31]) — with Evercore already on record that PVH is the *only* name in its coverage embedding that benefit and that without it FY26 margins and EPS do not hold [9]. Tapestry showed this tape will pay −16.5% for a 27% EPS beat with a soft guide [19]. A third consecutive credibility failure on EMEA is the −10% to −15% scenario.

**Base case (mine).** PVH beats the Q2 EPS guide — probably $3.15–$3.30 — and beats the reported revenue guide on FX translation while constant currency lands at or below the −4% to −5% guided [6][40]. The stock then trades entirely on two lines in the release: the FY26 revenue phrasing and the FY26 EPS range. I put the most likely outcome at a **narrowed-but-maintained FY EPS range with softer FY revenue language and a heavier constant-currency caveat** — technically not a cut, but enough hedging to read as one, producing a **−4% to −9%** reaction, inside the 9.2% implied. The reasons I do not go further negative: the June cut already repriced the stock by a quarter, 9.87% short interest is covering not building [11], the sell-side has capitulated, and 5.94x forward is a genuine floor bid. The reasons I do not go positive: every piece of *fresh* information since 3 June — Iran [20][21], Hugo Boss [17], July US retail sales [27], the JPMorgan downgrade [28] — points the same way, and consensus is at the top of the guide with nowhere to hide. **Preliminary direction score −15; probability of an up move 45%; conviction Medium.**

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

**The single most credible reversal is a simultaneous FY26 reaffirmation plus a new accelerated share repurchase announced in the release.** Concretely: PVH reaffirms FY26 non-GAAP EPS at $11.80–$12.10 *and* revenue at "approximately flat", states that EMEA constant-currency declines moderated through the quarter with Q3-to-date trending better, and announces an ASR of $300m or more [6][38]. At a $3.36bn market cap [3] that is ~9% of shares off the register at 5.9x earnings, and it would convert the tariff-refund cash — the very item Evercore attacked as low-quality [9] — into a permanent per-share benefit, neutralising the bear thesis on its own terms. With 9.87% of float short and 4.9 days to cover [11], that combination is a +12% to +18% night, not a +5% one.

Three lower-probability flips, in descending credibility: **(a)** the reported/constant-currency gap comes in wide enough on a weak dollar [40] that PVH raises the *reported* FY revenue guide back to "up slightly" while leaving cc unchanged — a cosmetic raise the tape would nonetheless reward, given the June cut was framed in reported terms [6]; **(b)** a resolution or de-escalation headline in the Strait of Hormuz standoff between now and the 3 September call [20][21], which would remove the entire stated cause of the June cut; **(c)** management quantifies the Q2 tariff refund as *incremental to* rather than *included in* the $1.70 FY figure, which would mean underlying earnings are materially better than the Evercore framing implies [6][9].

**The bear-side flip that would take this well beyond the implied move:** a cut to FY26 *EPS* (not just revenue). PVH held EPS at $11.80–$12.10 through the June cut [6]; breaking that range would be the first EPS guide-down of this cycle, would land on a stock whose only remaining support is a 5.9x multiple, and — arriving one quarter before a brand-new CFO takes the chair [12] — would read as the start of a reset rather than the end of one. That is the −15% to −20% tail.
