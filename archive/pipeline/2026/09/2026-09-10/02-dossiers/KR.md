# KR — The Kroger Co.

**Event confirmed: YES.** Kroger reports Q2 fiscal 2026 (13-week quarter ended
2026-08-15) **before market open on Friday, 2026-09-11**, with an 8:00 a.m. ET
conference call, per the company's own IR announcement issued 2026-08-14 [S1]. No
date change or pre-announcement was found.

**What this print is about.** This is not a print about the headline penny. Kroger's
CFO told the street on 18 June that Q2 adjusted EPS would be "*in line with last year*"
and identical sales without fuel "*roughly in line with the first quarter*" — i.e. about
$1.04 and about +1.0% [S12]. Consensus has simply copied that ($1.05, +1% YoY [S6][S18]).
So the quarter itself is pre-negotiated. What is *not* pre-negotiated is the full-year
guide. Kroger reaffirmed FY26 adjusted EPS of $5.10–$5.30 at Q1 and staked it entirely on
a back-half acceleration funded by cost savings ("*growth accelerating in the back half as
our cost saving initiatives continue to ramp*" [S12]). If Q2 lands where management said
it would, H1 will be tracking roughly +4% YoY against a full-year guide that requires
+5.2% to +9.3% — which means the back half has to do **+6% to +15%** (my arithmetic from
sourced figures, see §4). Since that guide was set, US diesel hit an all-time high of
$5.85/gal [S19], transportation was already a 15bp gross-margin headwind at *lower* oil
[S12], SNAP-driven grocery unit declines have deepened [S31], and every reporting peer —
Albertsons, Sprouts, Ahold USA — has printed flat-to-negative comps [S21][S22][S23].
Citi put the stock on a formal 30-day *downside* catalyst watch on 1 September with a
street-low $57 target, expecting the FY EPS range to be trimmed to $5.00–$5.20 [S15].
The stock is $56.44 — already below that street-low target, already below its post-Q1
crash close, 26% off its 52-week high. The print is a referendum on whether a
seven-month-old CEO defends a number he inherited six weeks before his October 20
Investor Update, or clears the decks first.

## 4. The one metric that matters

**It is the fiscal-2026 adjusted EPS guidance range, and specifically whether $5.10–$5.30
survives.**

Why, arithmetically (my calculation from sourced components):

| Component | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FY2025 adjusted EPS (actual) | $4.85 | [S11] |
| Q1 FY25 / Q2 FY25 / Q3 FY25 / Q4 FY25 adjusted EPS | $1.49 / $1.04 / $1.05 / $1.28 | [S9][S13][S38][S11] |
| Q1 FY2026 adjusted EPS (actual) | $1.58 (+6.0%) | [S9] |
| Q2 FY2026 consensus / company framing | $1.05 / "in line with last year" | [S6][S12] |

- H1 FY25 = $2.53. H1 FY26 at consensus = $1.58 + $1.05 = **$2.63, i.e. +4.0% YoY.**
- H2 FY25 = $1.05 + $1.28 = $2.33.
- FY26 guide $5.10–$5.30 implies H2 FY26 of **$2.47–$2.67 = +6.0% to +14.6% YoY**,
  midpoint +10.3%.

So the guide requires the back half to grow adjusted EPS roughly **two-and-a-half times
as fast as the first half**, in a quarter where the company has already flagged
transportation as a live headwind at oil prices below today's [S12] and diesel has since
set an all-time record [S19]. Kennerley's defence is explicit and quantified: the $400M
e-commerce profitability improvement from closing the Ocado sheds is "*ahead of schedule
and accelerating faster than we thought*," and "*the savings are bigger than the
investments*" [S12][S24].

**What the market expects for it:** Citi expects $5.00–$5.20 and comps narrowed to
+1.0–1.5% [S15]; the broader preview coverage frames the guide, not the quarter, as the
risk ("*the concern is not the Q2 print itself but the guidance*") [S18]. **How I know:**
Citi's note is dated 2026-09-01 and is the street-low target; the consensus FY27 number of
$5.21 [S33] is consistent with a street that has not yet marked down FY26.

**Secondary metrics that will move the stock, in order:** (a) identical sales ex-fuel and
its composition — how much of the ~1% is pharmacy/GLP-1 versus grocery units; (b) FIFO
gross-margin rate direction; (c) any quantified transportation/fuel-cost headwind for H2;
(d) whether management pre-frames the **October 20 Investor Update** [S12][S35] as the
place where numbers get reset.

Cash and balance sheet at 2026-05-23 [S10]: operating cash flow $1,774M (down from
$2,149M), capex $1,293M, **implied FCF ≈ $481M**, dividends paid $215M, **share
repurchases $213M**, total debt $16,995M, cash $2,873M, net total debt $14,340M. Net total
debt / adjusted EBITDA **1.75x** against a 2.30–2.50x target range — the balance sheet is
*under*-levered relative to policy [S9]. A separate provider puts total debt at $24.19B
and net cash at −$21.32B, which almost certainly includes operating lease liabilities
[S4]; I use the company's $16,995M for like-for-like debt.

**Composition of the comp — this is the part that matters.** Q1's +1.0% ID sales included
a **130bp headwind from the Inflation Reduction Act** and a further **40bp from the
accelerating brand-to-generic prescription shift**, plus **64bp of egg deflation** [S12].
Kennerley was explicit that the brand-to-generic drag is *profit-positive* despite being
sales-negative, and that IRA rebate mechanics are "*neutral from a profit perspective*"
[S12]. So the reported comp materially understates underlying grocery volume — and
management said loyal households have grown for 17 consecutive quarters, Our Brands
outpaced national brands by 175bp, and Q1 was Kroger's best performance against Circana's
Rest-of-Market benchmark in over three years [S12]. **That is the bull's factual base.**

**What has changed since 18 June:**

1. **Diesel to an all-time record.** AAA national average diesel $5.85/gal on 2026-09-04,
   above the June 2022 record, driven by the Iran war and Strait of Hormuz disruption plus
   Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries [S19]. Transportation cost Kroger 15bp of gross
   margin in Q1 at *lower* oil, and Kennerley said "*we expect some pressure to persist,
   while oil markets remain elevated*" and openly hoped US–Iran negotiations would
   "*alleviate some of those pressures*" [S12]. They did not.
2. **Giant Eagle, $1.65B, announced ~1 July 2026** — ~$1.25B cash plus ~$400M of assumed
   liabilities, ~200 supermarkets and pharmacies, expected to close in 2027, subject to
   antitrust review with limited divestitures anticipated [S28][S29]. It would take
   Kroger's Numerator grocery share from 8.3% to 8.7%, ahead of Costco [S20]. The stock
   made its $54.15 52-week low after the announcement [S3][S25]. The National Grocers
   Association has objected on antitrust grounds and a second request is considered likely
   [S29]. This is a live overhang that management will be asked about.
3. **A new EVP & Chief Store Operations Officer, Mark Ibbotson (ex-Walmart/Asda), starts
   2026-09-14** — three days after the print — on a package including $1M salary, 110%
   bonus target, $2.5M of restricted shares/performance units in December 2026 and a $5M
   target annual equity grant in March 2027 [S14].
4. **DOJ antitrust probe into retail beef pricing** widened in July 2026 to eight grocers
   including Kroger, seeking six years of pricing, margin and meatpacker-purchasing data
   [S30]. Separately, at least twelve states have introduced bills restricting electronic
   shelf-label/dynamic pricing technology [S30].
5. **Market share still contracting.** Numerator, 12 months to 2026-06-30: Kroger and
   Albertsons both lost a fraction of a point of share; Amazon/Whole Foods gained the most
   (>0.5pt), then Costco, then Walmart [S20].
6. **Foot traffic decelerating** to +0.4% from +1.7% YoY, per Citi's work [S15]
   (single-source, tertiary — treat as directional only).

**Structural items still in the numbers:** the FY2025 Q3 **$2.6B impairment** on the
automated fulfillment network drove a $(1,541)M GAAP operating loss and $(2.02) GAAP EPS
that quarter [S38], plus a **$350M payment to Ocado** for cancelling the Charlotte CFC
[S26]; those are why trailing GAAP P/E reads 34.6x against a 10.8x forward [S2][S4]. The
strategic pivot is to store-based fulfilment plus Instacart/DoorDash/Uber Eats, targeting
**~$400M of e-commerce profit improvement in 2026** [S24].

**Capital returns:** a $2B repurchase authorisation approved December 2025 is expected to
be *completed by the end of fiscal 2026* [S9][S10]. Only $213M was executed in Q1 [S10],
so roughly $1.8B would have to be spent across Q2–Q4 — at $34.6B of market cap that is
~5% of shares. Either the pace steps up materially from here (supportive of EPS and a
plausible positive line in the release), or the authorisation quietly slips. **I regard
the buyback pace disclosure as the most under-watched line in this release** — that is my
inference.

**Customer concentration:** not applicable in any meaningful sense — Kroger serves >11
million customers daily across >400,000 associates [S1]. Supplier concentration and
multi-employer pension obligations are the relevant analogues; I did not source current
figures for either.

## 9. Macro & peer read-through

**Regime.** Food-at-home is in a low-inflation, negative-unit regime — the worst mix for a
grocer's comp. Like-for-like grocery inflation ~0.6% and falling on egg/dairy deflation
[S44]; food CPI +2.9% YoY at 2026-07 [S31]; grocery units ~−2% YoY since February 2026 as
HR-1's SNAP changes bit, with total SNAP EBT dollars projected ~$10B lower in 2026 [S31].
Kroger's own Q1 had 64bp of egg-deflation drag [S12].

**Rate / FX / commodity sensitivity.** FX: negligible, essentially all-domestic. Rates:
matters through a $17.0B debt stack at 1.75x net leverage [S9][S10] and through the
staples multiple; beta 0.41 [S4]. **Commodity: this is the live one.** Diesel at a record
$5.85/gal [S19] hits Kroger twice — directly through distribution (15bp of Q1 gross margin
at lower prices [S12]) and indirectly through supplier cost pass-through, with diesel
being 15–30% of total food cost per Citi's framing [S15]. The offsetting channel is retail
fuel margin: Kroger cited "stronger fuel margins" as a gross-margin tailwind in the
year-ago Q2 [S13], and Q1 FY26 benefited from them too [S38]. **In a spiking crude market,
retail fuel margins typically compress on the way up and expand on the way down** — so the
usual pattern would be a *negative* fuel-margin quarter for a period ending 2026-08-15
during an oil rally. I flag this explicitly as **my inference, not a sourced fact**, and I
could not source Kroger's Q2 fuel gallons or cents-per-gallon.

**Peers who already reported — all three landed badly on comps.**

The read-through is uncomfortable in one specific way: **Ahold USA named the exact same

than idiosyncratic. And ACI's −14.4% on a −0.8% comp and a 22% EPS miss is the tape's
demonstrated willingness to mark a US grocer down hard [S21].

**Customer/competitor read-through.** Target's food & beverage sales grew 7% in the quarter
ended 2026-08-01 — its fastest in three years — with store traffic +3.6% [S44]. Costco's US
grocery share rose to 8.2% from 7.9% [S44]. Amazon/Whole Foods gained the most share in the
12 months to June [S20]. Walmart deployed tariff-refund dollars into grocery rollbacks
[S15]. **Every one of Kroger's larger competitors is either gaining share or spending to
gain it, while Kroger's share contracts** [S20]. That is the structural case for why the
comp guide is hard to hit without more price investment, and why more price investment
makes the EPS guide harder to hit.

## 10. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The stock has already been marked down to a level below the street's lowest
target — $56.44 versus Citi's $57 [S16] — at 10.8x forward earnings and 6.9x EV/EBITDA
[S4] with a 2.5–2.8% yield and 20 consecutive years of dividend growth [S2][S33]. Positioning
is not crowded (4.5% of float short, 4.3 days to cover [S4]) and the option market prices
only ~1.2x mean realised. The operating story underneath the optics is better than the
headline comp: Q1's +1.0% absorbed 130bp of IRA and 40bp of brand-to-generic drag that are
*profit-neutral to profit-positive*, plus 64bp of egg deflation [S12]; loyal households
have grown 17 straight quarters; Our Brands beat national brands by 175bp; and Q1 was the
best showing against Circana's Rest-of-Market benchmark in three years [S12]. E-commerce
grew 19% with KPM profit +20% [S9], and Kennerley said the $400M e-commerce profit
improvement is "*ahead of schedule and accelerating faster than we thought*" and that "*the
savings are bigger than the investments*" [S12]. Net leverage of 1.75x against a 2.30–2.50x
target [S9] plus ~$1.8B of a $2B authorisation still to execute by fiscal year-end [S9][S10]
gives management a lever it has not pulled. Zacks' Most Accurate Estimate sits above
consensus (ESP +1.76%) [S17]. If Kroger simply holds $5.10–$5.30 and prints a 1%-handle
comp, a heavily de-risked stock re-rates: the last time Kroger surprised a bearish set-up,
on 2025-06-20, it moved +10.6% [S6].

**Bear case.** The guide requires the back half to grow adjusted EPS +6% to +15% after a
first half tracking +4% (my arithmetic from [S9][S11][S12][S13]), and the two things that
were supposed to fund it have both got worse since it was reaffirmed. Diesel set an
all-time record of $5.85/gal on 2026-09-04 [S19] after transportation had already cost
15bp of gross margin at lower oil, with the CFO warning "*we expect some pressure to
persist*" [S12]. Demand deteriorated: grocery units ~−2% YoY on SNAP cuts [S31], food
inflation decelerating to ~0.6% like-for-like [S44], Kroger foot traffic down to +0.4%
from +1.7% [S15]. Every peer that reported printed flat-to-negative comps and Albertsons
fell 14.4% for it [S21][S22][S23]. Kroger is losing share to Amazon, Costco and Walmart
[S20] and must spend more on price to stop it, which is exactly what compresses the margin
funding the guide. Citi's 30-day downside catalyst watch and street-low $57 target
[S15][S16] say the smart-money read is a guide-down to $5.00–$5.20. Meanwhile a
seven-month-old external CEO has an **October 20 Investor Update** at which he must set a
multi-year framework [S12][S35] — the textbook moment to rebase first. The last time this
management team disappointed on this axis the stock fell 8.4% in a day, its worst in five
years, and never recovered the level [S24]. Senior-executive churn is at a decade high
[S26], and the only discretionary insider trade in the window was an EVP selling 30,000
shares, explicitly *not* under a 10b5-1 plan, at $58.80 [S45].

**Base case (my read).** Q2 prints close to the pre-negotiated numbers — adjusted EPS
$1.04–$1.06 and ID sales ex-fuel in a 0.7–1.2% band — because management told the street
what to expect eleven weeks ago and Kroger has beaten or matched on EPS in three of the
last four quarters [S17]. The release then **narrows rather than holds** FY2026 adjusted
EPS, most likely to something like $5.05–$5.25 or $5.00–$5.20, with the diesel/transport
line cited and the substantive re-set deferred to 20 October. Because the street already
expects roughly that [S15], the reaction is smaller than the implied 5.79%: I'd centre it
on a 2–5% move, skewed down. The bimodal risk is real in both tails — an unqualified
reaffirmation with a strong e-commerce/cost-savings number is worth mid-to-high single
digits up given how washed out the price is; an outright cut below $5.00, or a comp with a
0-handle, is worth an ACI-style high-single-to-low-double-digit fall. On balance I am
**modestly negative: direction score −22, probability of an up day 43%, conviction Low.**
The conviction is deliberately low — the price already sits below the street's most bearish
target, and that is the single best argument that the bear case is in the stock.

## 11. What would flip the consensus view

The consensus view going into this print is "*the quarter is fine, the guide gets
trimmed*" [S15][S18]. **The most credible reversal is Kroger reaffirming $5.10–$5.30
unqualified and putting a bigger, quantified number on cost savings.** Concretely: at Q1,
Seth Sigman of Barclays asked directly whether there was "a bigger, maybe more inclusive
number for savings," and Kennerley declined to quantify beyond the $400M e-commerce piece,
saying only that "*the savings are bigger than the investments*" and deferring to the fall
[S12]. If on 11 September Kennerley puts a *company-wide* cost-savings figure on the
record — a number with a dollar sign attached, materially above $400M — with the FY guide
intact and FIFO gross margin turned positive, the entire bear thesis (that the back-half
acceleration is a hope, not a plan) loses its factual footing in one sentence. That would
also mean he chose *not* to save it for 20 October, which is itself the confidence signal.
At 10.8x forward with the stock below the street-low target and only 4.5% short interest,
there is nothing in the positioning to absorb that; the move up would be violent, and the
+10.6% on 2025-06-20 [S6] is the template.

The mirror-image flip, less likely to surprise but larger in magnitude: an FY guide cut
*below* $5.00 combined with a sub-1% comp would break the "cheap staple, self-help
underway" frame entirely and put the $54.15 low, then the pre-2024 range, in play — with
21 analysts still carrying a $71 mean target [S16][S33] providing a long queue of
downgrades behind it.
