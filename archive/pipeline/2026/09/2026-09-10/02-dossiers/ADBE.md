# ADBE — Adobe Inc.

**What this print is about.** This is not an EPS print. Adobe has beaten revenue and EPS in six straight quarters and nine of the last ten [1], and the stock has still fallen after ten of its last twelve reports [1]. The debate is whether Adobe can convert a deliberately ARR-dilutive freemium land-grab into a credible AI monetisation story fast enough to stop a de-rating that has taken the multiple to ~14.6x trailing / ~9.8x forward earnings [2]. Compounding that, the company walks into this call with a **lame-duck CEO** (Shantanu Narayen hands over to Anil Chakravarthy on 1 Dec 2026 [3][4]), **no permanent CFO** (Dan Durn left for Marvell on 15 Jun 2026; Steve Day is interim [5][6]), and the **head of the creative business — roughly three-quarters of revenue — walking out the door** (David Wadhwani, announced alongside the CEO news, 3–4 Sep [7][8]). The market marked the stock down 12.9% in the six sessions between 31 Aug and 9 Sep on that news alone [9]. So the print is a referendum on two numbers — net-new ARR and Firefly/AI-first ARR — delivered by a management team that is, in three of its most important seats, temporary.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Net-new total ARR, and specifically the organic (ex-Semrush) ARR growth rate — with Firefly ending ARR as the corroborating tell.**

Why, and how I know:

1. **The last time this metric missed, the stock fell 7.6% on a clean beat-and-raise.** Q1 FY26: revenue $6.40 bn (+12%), non-GAAP EPS $6.06 beating by 3.18%, record Q1 operating cash flow $2.96 bn — and net-new Digital Media ARR of **$400 m against a $450–460 m consensus**. Shares fell 7.58% [18]. That is the cleanest natural experiment available on what this stock trades on.
2. **Management has explicitly told the Street this metric will get worse on purpose.** On the Q2 FY26 call Adobe raised FY26 revenue and EPS but **lowered H2 ARR growth expectations**, attributing roughly half the reduction to deferred Creative Cloud line optimisations and half to accelerating freemium acquisition [28]. Headline FY26 total ARR growth guidance of **10.2%** only holds because **Semrush (~$480 m of acquired ARR, closed April 2026 for $1.9 bn) is now inside it** — strip it out and **organic ARR growth guidance falls to ~8.3%** [28][20][29].
3. **The bar is now explicit and low.** Barclays models **$400 m** of Q3 net-new ARR with upside to **$420 m+** [24]; FactSet has ending ARR at **$27.47 bn** against $27.10 bn exiting Q2 [23][20] — i.e. ~$370 m of implied net-new. RBC expects a beat on that [23].
4. **The AI corroborator.** AI-first ARR **exceeded $500 m** in Q2, tripled y/y; Firefly ending ARR approached **~$300 m** exiting Q2, growing ~50% q/q; Acrobat AI Assistant ARR ~3x with paid MAU +150%; GenStudio ARR +25% [20][30]. The sell-side framing is that Firefly needs to approach **~$400 m** at the Q3 print to keep the ~50% q/q cadence and validate the freemium bet; a stall is the bear case's proof point [24].
5. **The freemium input metric.** Creative freemium MAU crossed **90 m**, +70% y/y (from >50 m), with adobe.com C&CP traffic +50% y/y [28]. Adobe will lead with this. The question the panel should hold onto: does the market accept MAU as a leading indicator, or does it price the ARR deceleration it is causing? On 12 Jun it priced the deceleration.

**Expectation, summarised:** Q3 net-new ARR **≥$420 m** and Firefly ending ARR **approaching ~$400 m** is the pass mark. $400 m and a flat Firefly is a fail even on an EPS beat.

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

**Q2 FY2026 actuals (reported 11 Jun 2026)** [20]:
- Revenue **$6.62 bn**, +13% y/y (+11% cc), vs ~$6.45 bn consensus. Subscription $6.416 bn; product $89 m; services & other $113 m.
- Business Professionals & Consumers subs **$1.85 bn** (+16% y/y); Creative & Marketing Professionals subs **$4.54 bn** (+13% y/y).
- GAAP operating income $2.24 bn; non-GAAP operating income **$2.95 bn** (~44.6% margin). GAAP net income $1.71 bn; non-GAAP $2.40 bn.
- GAAP diluted EPS $4.25; **non-GAAP diluted EPS $5.96** (vs $5.81–5.82 consensus) [19].
- **Total Adobe ARR $27.10 bn** exiting Q2, including ~$480 m from Semrush.
- **RPO $22.27 bn**, +13% y/y; **cRPO 67%**.
- Operating cash flow **$2.17 bn** — down ~1.2% y/y despite 13% revenue growth, on working capital and deferred-revenue timing [31]. FCF ≈$2.1 bn, flat-to-down y/y [31].
- Buyback: ~**8.5 m shares** repurchased in the quarter; **~$27 bn** authorisation remaining [31].

**Segment mix / unit economics.** Creative & Marketing Professionals is ~69% of subscription revenue and, per press coverage of the Wadhwani departure, the creative business is **roughly three-quarters of total revenue** [7]. That concentration is the whole risk: the AI-substitution thesis attacks precisely the segment that funds everything else. Business Professionals & Consumers (Acrobat, Express) is the faster grower (+16%) and is where freemium is working best.

**Margins.** Non-GAAP operating margin guided ~44% for Q3, ~45% FY26 [20] — essentially flat. There is no margin-expansion story here to offset a growth story that is decelerating; the EPS growth (+14.5% y/y guided) is being manufactured largely by the buyback.

**Balance sheet.** Net cash position **−$1.45 bn** (debt exceeds cash) [2]; $6.15 bn senior notes plus $500 m commercial paper against $5.63 bn cash and short-term investments [32]. Trailing FCF **$10.28 bn** [2] — against a $101.31 bn cap that is a **~10.1% trailing FCF yield** (my arithmetic from [2]).

**Buyback / dilution.** Buyback yield **5.84%** [2]; ~$27 bn authorisation remaining [31]. Shares outstanding 397.50 m [2]. Adobe is retiring roughly 6% of the float a year — the single most reliable support under the stock.

**Customer concentration:** no material single-customer concentration disclosed for Adobe; not a factor. `unavailable` as a quantified figure.

**What changed since the last print:**
1. Anil Chakravarthy named President & CEO effective **1 Dec 2026**; Narayen becomes **Executive Chair** (announced 3 Sep) [3][4][33].
2. **David Wadhwani, president of the creativity & productivity business, is leaving** — announced the same week; he was seen as the leading internal CEO candidate and was behind the abandoned Figma bid [7][8].
3. **CFO seat still vacant.** Durn left 15 Jun for Marvell; Steve Day (SVP corporate finance) is interim [5][6][34].
4. Semrush fully integrated into the ARR base (~$480 m) [20][29].
5. Stock round-tripped: $205.02 on 30 Jun → $292.79 on 31 Aug → **$254.86 on 9 Sep** [9].

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The bar is genuinely low and the price already reflects the worst headlines. Consensus sits at the *midpoint* of Adobe's own guide on both revenue ($6.69 bn vs $6.67–6.72 bn) and EPS ($6.08 vs $6.05–6.10) [1][20][21], and Adobe has beaten both lines six quarters running and nine of the last ten [1]. Citi calls it an "achievable set-up" for a beat-and-raise [25] and RBC expects ARR **above** the FactSet $27.47 bn [23]. The net-new ARR bar is $400 m — the same number that disappointed in Q1 — so Barclays' $420 m+ upside case clears it [24]. Meanwhile the stock has already given back 12.95% in six sessions on the leadership news [9], short interest is a benign 4.5% of float with 3.4 days to cover [2], and the valuation is 9.8x forward earnings [2] on a ~10% trailing FCF yield with a 5.84% buyback yield and $27 bn of authorisation left [2][31]. If Firefly ARR prints near $400 m and management quantifies the freemium-to-paid conversion funnel, this is a stock that can gap 8% on relief alone. The Salesforce print showed the software tape will pay for a credible AI story [61][62].

**Bear case.** The base rate is brutal and the mechanism is specific. Shares have fallen after **10 of the last 12** reports [1] and **5 of the last 6** [17], and both 2026 declines came *on clean beats* — Q1 −7.6% on a $400 m net-new ARR miss versus $450–460 m expected [18], Q2 −6.8% when the CFO's exit swamped a record quarter [19]. Management has already told the Street that H2 ARR growth goes **down on purpose**, with roughly half from deferred Creative Cloud line optimisations and half from freemium acquisition [28], and the headline 10.2% FY26 ARR growth target only survives because **$480 m of acquired Semrush ARR is inside it — organic guidance is ~8.3%** [28][20]. That number, and any FY27 framing, has to be delivered by an **interim CFO**, defended by an **outgoing CEO**, for a **creative business whose president just quit** [5][7][3]. Morgan Stanley is Underweight at $240 and BofA is Sell at $220 on exactly the substitution thesis [39][41]; consensus is Hold with only 1 Buy in the last 9 calls [27]. And Figma, the closest comparable, fell 15.9% after hours on a 48% growth beat because the *forward* number decelerated [62].

**Base case (mine).** Adobe beats revenue and EPS modestly — call it $6.72–6.76 bn and $6.12–6.18 — and reiterates or trims the top of the FY26 range. Net-new ARR lands in the $400–430 m band, close enough to the bar to be argued either way, and management leads with the 90 m+ freemium MAU number rather than a hard conversion metric [28]. Firefly ARR is up but the sequential rate slows from ~50%. Because there is no permanent CFO, FY27 commentary is deliberately vague — which the market reads as risk rather than prudence. The stock trades down mid-single digits, inside the ±7.24% implied [11], with a genuine tail to +8% if net-new ARR prints above $450 m and Chakravarthy uses his first public appearance to reframe the AI story. I put roughly 56/44 odds on down/up, and I hold that view with only medium conviction because the 12.9% pre-print drawdown has already done some of the work the bear case wants to do.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The single most credible reversal: **Adobe prints net-new total ARR above ~$450 m with Firefly ending ARR at or above ~$400 m, and pairs it with a quantified freemium-to-paid conversion disclosure** — e.g. a stated paid-conversion rate off the 90 m+ freemium MAU base, or a Q4/FY27 ARR growth floor that is *organically* above 9%. That combination would simultaneously (a) prove the freemium bet is a growth investment rather than a euphemism for decay, (b) demonstrate that AI is additive to ARR rather than substitutive, and (c) let the market re-underwrite a 9.8x forward multiple that currently embeds terminal decline. Against a Hold consensus with only one Buy in nine recent calls [27] and an average target barely above spot [27][36], the sell-side would have to chase. On a 4.5%-of-float short base [2] and a stock already 12.9% below its 31 Aug level [9], the upside gap would be violent.

The mirror-image flip, less likely but cleaner: **Chakravarthy or Narayen signals a strategic reset on the call** — a large AI acquisition, a restructuring, a pricing-model overhaul, or a withdrawal of the FY26 ARR growth target. Any of those, from a company with three temporary seats, would be read as capitulation and would take the stock through the low end of the implied move.
