# CPRT — Copart, Inc.

**Dossier as of 2026-09-10 ~11:00 UTC. `event_confirmed: true`** — the date, session and call time are confirmed by Copart's own press release of 2026-09-01 [1][2].

**What this print is about.** This is not an EPS print. It is the first quarterly report under a returning CEO of a compounder that has just lost roughly a third of its market value in a year, and the market's question is whether Jay Adair — back in the chair since 2026-07-31 after Jeff Liaw's abrupt exit — uses the fiscal-Q4 call to reset the earnings base downward in exchange for a growth story. Copart's US insurance unit volumes have been negative for six straight quarters while its only real competitor, RB Global's IAA, has been growing units and taking share (notably at Progressive); the company has papered over that with record ASPs and $1.63bn of buyback in nine months [15][19][26]. Adair has already said publicly he will re-fire international expansion, do M&A across three pillars, and treat AI "in quarters, not years," and Copart is reported to be a live bidder for CCC Intelligent Solutions at a headline $6.5–7.5bn [24][25][30]. So the print carries two loosely-coupled payloads: (a) did the June-quarter CAT-heavy, record-total-loss-frequency backdrop finally stop the US unit bleed, and (b) how much money is the new regime about to spend. Consensus already has EPS down 7.3% y/y, so the bar on the number itself is low; the bar on the narrative is not. Options price a 6.2% move against a six-quarter realised mean absolute move of 3.8% — and against six consecutive *negative* post-earnings sessions.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**US insurance unit volume growth, year over year, ex-catastrophe — and, second, whatever Adair says about FY27 spending.**

Why, and how I know:
- Copart obtained **81% of vehicles processed in FY2025 from insurance company sellers**, and 83.0% of FY25 revenue came from the US segment [27]. Units, not price, is the disputed variable: ASPs have been at records for six quarters.
- The last three prints were *decided* by this line. Q3 FY26 (2026-05-21): EPS beat $0.43 vs $0.41 and revenue beat $1.24bn vs $1.20bn, and the stock still fell ~1.8%, with contemporaneous coverage attributing it to global insurance units −2.7% and US insurance units −4.2% [15][8][10]. Q2 FY26 (2026-02-19): global insurance units −9% (−4% ex-CAT), US units −10.7% (−4.8% ex-CAT), EPS $0.36 vs $0.39/$0.40 consensus, stock down [14][23].
- The competitive read is the reason it matters more than usual: RB Global's IAA sold ~659,000 automotive lots in calendar Q2 2026, **+11% y/y**, with US insurance ASP +4% and explicit "continued net market share gains" [26]. Copart's overlapping fiscal quarter cannot plausibly be both +11% industry and −4% company without share loss.

**What the market expects for it:** No published sell-side unit-volume point estimate was findable. The revealed expectation embedded in consensus (revenue +0.9% y/y with ASPs still rising mid-single-digit) is **US insurance units roughly flat to modestly down** — i.e. an improvement on Q3's −4.2%. The strongest recent evidence for that is JPMorgan's 2026-09-03 upgrade to Overweight (PT $32 → $40), which the firm justified partly with **proprietary web-scraping data showing Copart market-share gains across several states with a specific carrier** [7][20]. That is the bull's whole case in one datapoint, and it directly contradicts RB Global's disclosure. One of them is wrong, and this print settles it.

**Secondary metric:** capital expenditure and the FY27 investment framing. 9M FY26 capex was $258.6m against $1.25bn of operating cash flow — one commentator computed yard/infrastructure capex **down ~46% y/y** [12][29]. Adair has said he will restart international expansion and do M&A [24]. A capex/opex step-up guided on this call is the most likely mechanism for a negative reaction.

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

**Q3 FY26 (quarter ended 2026-04-30), the last reported quarter [12][15]:**
- Revenue $1,237.1m, +2.1% y/y; service revenue $1,056.1m (+2.1%, ~85% of total); vehicle sales $181.0m (+2.3%).
- Gross profit $572.6m, +3.7% (gross margin ~46.3%); operating income $464.3m, +2.8%; net income $402.4m, **−1.0%**; diluted EPS $0.43, **+2.4%** (EPS grew while net income fell — that is the buyback).
- Global insurance units −2.7% (−1.9% ex-CAT); US insurance units −4.2% (−3% ex-CAT). US insurance ASP **+4.1% y/y, a seasonally-adjusted all-time record**; global ASP +4.6% [15].
- Total loss frequency cited on the call at **23.6%** [15].
- US revenue essentially flat at $1.003bn vs $1.006bn; −1.8% over nine months. International (UK, Germany, Canada) is where the growth is; >1/3 of auction volume and nearly half of proceeds now come from international buyers [15][29].

**Nine months FY26:** revenue $3.5bn (−0.2%), gross profit $1.6bn (+0.8%), operating income $1.28bn (flat), net income $1.157bn (+0.1%), diluted EPS $1.20 (+1.7%) [12].

**Balance sheet and capital returns (2026-04-30) [12][15]:**
- Cash and equivalents $3.35bn; ~$4.2bn including held-to-maturity securities; ~$5.5bn total liquidity; **no debt**.
- Total assets $9.65bn; stockholders' equity $8.77bn.
- 9M operating cash flow $1.25bn; capex $258.6m; **share repurchases $1.63bn** — >43.4m shares in FY26 via a mix of 10b5-1 and open-market [15]. Share count is down to 925.81m [6]. One analysis computes TTM repurchases at ~122% of post-dividend FCF, i.e. the buyback is being funded partly out of the cash pile, and the average repurchase price is above today's price [28].

**Customer concentration:** "No single customer accounted for more than 10% of our consolidated revenues for fiscal 2025, 2024, or 2023," but "a limited number of vehicle sellers historically have collectively accounted for a substantial portion of our revenues" [27]. Progressive is the named pressure point (§9).

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Sector/factor regime.** Copart was moved out of Russell Top 200 and Russell 1000 Growth and into Russell Midcap Value at the June 2026 reconstitution (effective after the 2026-06-26 close) [41]. Beta 1.03 [6]. The name has de-rated from a quality-compounder multiple to ~20x forward [6] — it is now being priced as a cyclical, which mechanically raises the sensitivity of the stock to a single quarter's unit-volume print. Rate sensitivity is second-order given no debt and $4.2bn of cash-equivalents; FX matters modestly (17% of FY25 revenue is international, GBP/EUR/CAD-weighted [27]).

**The industry pool is growing; Copart's share of it is not.**
- **CCC:** total loss frequency reached a record **23.1% of all claims** (23.9% of non-comprehensive) in its Crash Course 2026 report (published 2026-03-31) [61]; RB Global cited CCC data showing total loss frequency **+90bps y/y to 23.3%** in calendar Q2 2026 [26]. Copart's own Q3 FY26 call cited 23.6% [15]. All three point the same way: **structurally more total losses**, which is a volume tailwind.
- **RB Global / IAA (reported ~2026-08, calendar Q2 = overlaps Copart's fiscal Q4):** automotive lots sold ~659,000, **+11% y/y**; automotive GTV +13%; US insurance ASP +4%; management explicitly cited "continued net market share gains" while "broader industry volumes remain under pressure," and said leading indicators of total-loss frequency "have improved modestly." Shares were little changed on the print [26]. **This is the most important peer datapoint in the dossier and it is a direct negative read-through for Copart's units.**
- **The Progressive dynamic.** Progressive historically routes a majority of salvage to IAA (reported at 75/25 IAA/Copart, the inverse of most top-10 carriers), and reporting from December 2025 said RB Global had won a further tranche taking its share of Progressive units to **~90%**, with Copart −2.8% units against IAA +4.7% at the time [19][62]. **Date caveat: that reporting is from 2025-12-15, so the shift is largely already in the base by fiscal Q4 FY26** — it explains the last three quarters more than it predicts this one. It is nonetheless why the sell side disagrees so violently about the run-rate.
- **ACV Auctions (Q2 CY2026, reported 2026-08-10):** record revenue $214m, +10%, but the **dealer wholesale market contracted ~6% y/y in Q2 and ~8% in July**; units ~211,472, roughly flat; a 600bp unit-growth headwind from a buyer/seller price disconnect as used values fell in June and July; management expects stabilisation in H2. Shares sank on the print [63]. Read-through: the non-salvage wholesale channel was weak through Copart's fiscal Q4, which is a mild negative for whole-car/dealer volumes and for auction conversion.

**Commodity / price backdrop for ASPs.** Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index: **May 2026 212.6 (+3.6% y/y), June 212.9 (+2.1% y/y), July 210.0 (+1.3% y/y)** — positive but decelerating sharply through the quarter, roughly 1% below March highs [64]. ## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The bar has already been cut and the stock has already been destroyed. Consensus EPS is $0.38, down 7.3% y/y and trimmed 2.6% in both the last 30 and 90 days [5][31]; the stock is at 19.7x forward against a 52-week decline of 34.3% and sits 6.8% below its own 200-day [6]. Into that, fiscal Q4 was a genuinely favourable quarter for the input that matters: record industry total-loss frequency (23.1–23.6% [15][61]), RB Global reporting improving total-loss leading indicators and +11% automotive lots [26], the most active June for tornadoes since 2010 [65], and Manheim still positive y/y across all three months [64] to support the ASP records Copart has posted for six straight quarters. JPMorgan upgraded on 2026-09-03 specifically on proprietary scrape data showing **Copart** gaining share with a carrier, and raised its PT to $40 [7][20]. A 4% share-count reduction adds a point of EPS support [6][12]. And the board just struck 700,000 options for the President and CFO at $31.61 with a $39.51 performance hurdle [52][53] — insiders are being paid to get this stock 23% higher. If units inflect to flat-or-positive and Adair frames FY27 as growth-funded-from-cash, a 6.2% implied move is cheap in the up direction.

**Bear case.** Six consecutive negative post-earnings sessions [22][10] on a name where the last two prints *beat* on EPS and still fell [15][8]. The comp is the hardest of the year — Q4 FY25 grew gross profit 12.4% and EPS 24.2% [16][18]. The competitive evidence is unambiguous and comes from a counterparty's audited disclosure rather than a web scrape: IAA +11% units with explicit share gains in the overlapping quarter [26], on top of a Progressive book now reported at ~90% IAA [19][62], while Copart's US revenue has been flat-to-down for nine months [12][29]. Above all, **this is the first print of a new/returning CEO who has already publicly committed to re-firing international expansion, doing M&A across three pillars, and investing in AI "in quarters, not years"** [24][25] — after a nine-month period in which capex ran at $258.6m and yard/infrastructure spend fell ~46% y/y [12][29]. The classic mechanic is a Q4 kitchen-sink: reset the expense base, guide FY27 investment up, and let the multiple absorb it. Layered on that is the CCC bid — a $6.5–7.5bn deal at 14–16x forward EBITDA [40] would consume the entire $4.2bn cash pile plus leverage, end the buyback that has been carrying EPS, and convert a debt-free compounder into a levered software-integration story. A director sold 80,000 discretionary shares at ~$27.55 two weeks after the CEO news [57]. Positioning is 1.8:1 calls with a 0.26 put/call and a +110% call-volume spike [21][43] into a stock that has run +8.95% in 20 sessions [6] — thin protection if the message disappoints.

**Base case (mine).** Copart delivers a number close to the reset bar — EPS $0.37–$0.40 on revenue $1.13–$1.17bn — with US insurance units improved from Q3's −4.2% but not convincingly positive ex-CAT, and ASPs at another record. The stock's reaction is then determined almost entirely by the call, where a returning CEO with a fresh option package, a new President, a new M&A-and-governance director, and a live $6.5–7.5bn bid on the table has both the mandate and the cover to reset FY27 spending expectations. I therefore expect a realised move materially **smaller than the 6.2% implied** (six-quarter mean absolute move is 3.78% [22]) with a **modest downward skew** driven by the spending/M&A narrative and the six-for-six negative reaction pattern, not by the earnings line. My preliminary read: **direction score −18, prob(up) 43%**, conviction **Low** — the JPMorgan scrape and the RB Global disclosure cannot both be right, and I have no way to adjudicate them before the print.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The single most credible reversal is **an explicit, quantified US insurance unit inflection paired with a "no CCC, capital returns continue" message.** Concretely: if Copart reports **US insurance units flat or positive year over year excluding catastrophe** — versus −4.2% (−3% ex-CAT) in Q3 FY26 and −10.7% (−4.8% ex-CAT) in Q2 FY26 [14][15] — and Adair states on the call that Copart has *won back* assignment share (which would validate JPMorgan's scrape data [7][20] and falsify the "structural share loss to IAA" thesis built on RB Global's +11% [26]), while simultaneously ruling out a transformational acquisition and reaffirming the buyback, then the bear case collapses to a valuation argument on a 19.7x-forward stock trading 34% below where it was a year ago. In that scenario a move well above the 6.2% implied, to the upside, is plausible — and it would be the first up-reaction in seven quarters.

The mirror-image flip, equally credible: **Adair confirms a CCC transaction or guides FY27 capex/opex materially higher.** Either converts the story from "cheap cash-rich compounder pausing" to "levered turnaround with a spending cycle ahead," and the $25 Barclays target [7] becomes the anchor rather than the outlier.
