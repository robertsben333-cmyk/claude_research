# IOT — Samsara Inc.

**What this print is about.** Samsara reports fiscal Q2 2027 (quarter ended 1 August 2026) after the close on Thursday 3 September 2026. The mechanical beat is close to a formality: published consensus ($483.3M revenue, $0.16 non-GAAP EPS) sits *exactly on* the company's own guidance midpoint, and Samsara has beaten its own revenue guide by 4.3–5.3% and its own non-GAAP operating-margin guide by 400–600bp in each of the last four quarters [2][4][5][6][7]. So this print does not trade on the beat. It trades on three things: (1) **Q2 net new ARR** against a Street bar of roughly $130–136M and a bearish published estimate of $120M [18]; (2) the **size of the FY27 revenue-guide raise** relative to beat flow-through; and (3) whether the **non-GAAP gross margin**, down 300bp y/y in Q1 against a CFO promise of "roughly flat for FY '27", stabilises [2][20]. The context is hostile and the precedent is discouraging: last quarter Samsara delivered the largest beat-and-raise in the series and the stock still fell 1.16% [10], and IOT has dropped 12.1% in the three sessions into this print alongside a violent AI-disruption de-rating of the whole software complex [10]. Options price ~11.5% [12], slightly *below* the six-quarter median realised move of 13.3%.

**Event confirmed: YES.** Confirmed from the company's own press release: results after the U.S. market close on Thursday 3 September 2026, webcast 2:00pm PT / 5:00pm ET, for the quarter ended 1 August 2026 [1]. No date change and no pre-announcement found.

**Note on the triage rationale.** Triage cited a "~9.86% implied move." That figure is stale. It implies a spot of roughly $40.87, which is where IOT traded around 27–31 August. On the 2 September close ($36.79) the front-expiry straddle prices ~11.4–11.6% [11][12]. Triage's second claim — that margin trajectory is under-synthesised — I judge **correct and materially so**, but the under-covered fact is the opposite of the obvious one: operating margin is expanding fast while *gross* margin is contracting 300bp y/y [2].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Q2 FY27 net new ARR — and the FY27 guide raise that follows from it.**

Headline ARR growth is a nearly useless discriminator this quarter because the base is so large: on my arithmetic, net new ARR of $120M still prints 28.7% y/y ARR growth while $136M prints 29.7%. The *quarterly net-new* number is where the deceleration would show.

ARR and net new ARR history (ARR from company filings; net new is my subtraction, and the two the company disclosed agree with it) [2][4][5][6][7]:

| Quarter | ARR | Net new ARR | y/y net new |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 FY26 | $1,535.4M | $77.5M | — |
| Q2 FY26 | $1,640.1M | **$104.7M** | — |
| Q3 FY26 | $1,745.1M | $105.0M | — |
| Q4 FY26 | $1,889.9M | $144.8M (company-stated) | +33% |
| Q1 FY27 | $1,990.6M | $100.7M (company-stated) | +30% |

**The expectation, and how I know it:**
- Extrapolating Q1's +30% y/y net-new growth onto the $104.7M Q2 FY26 base gives **~$136M** (ARR ~$2.127B). At +25% it is **~$131M** (ARR ~$2.122B). *That range is my inference from company-reported ARR.*
- **Piper Sandler, in the note downgrading the stock, explicitly modelled "$120 million in net new annual recurring revenue growth"** [18]. That is +14.6% y/y — a halving of net-new growth. It is the only publicly attributable buy/sell-side number I found for this metric.
- So the metric has a live, published bear/bull spread of roughly **$120M vs $136M**, about 13%. My read is that anything at or above **$130M** with an FY27 raise to ~$2.045B+ is a clean beat; **$120–125M** with a flow-through-only raise is where the stock breaks.

**Second-order metrics that will be read alongside it, with the Q1 comparison [2][21]:**
- $100k+ ARR customers: 3,363; ARR from that cohort $1.2B, **+37% y/y and accelerating three straight quarters**.
- $1M+ ARR customers: 190; that cohort's ARR **+62% y/y, accelerating four straight quarters**. Any break in either acceleration streak is the tell.
- Emerging products: >20% of net new ACV for two consecutive quarters.
- International: 18% of net new ACV, "tied for a quarterly record".
- Dollar-based net retention: ~115% for core customers (management's stated target) [20].
- **Non-GAAP gross margin**: 76% in Q1 vs 79% a year earlier. The CFO said on the Q1 call: "we think that we're going to be able to keep gross margins, let's say, roughly flat for FY '27" [20]. FY26 non-GAAP GM was 78% [4]. Q1 already printed 2pts below that. Q2 FY26's comparison base is 78% [6]. **If Q2 FY27 non-GAAP GM prints 75–76%, management's own gross-margin guidance is broken**, and that is the single most falsifiable statement in the file.

When it is collected it is a **~$30M one-time FCF tailwind** that will flatter a future quarter's cash conversion. Separately, Motive won the ITC patent case (Inv. 337-TA-1393), so Samsara did not get its exclusion orders [29].
6. **Hardware/COGS structure**: management flagged software-only deals (Hertz) as gross-margin accretive, and cloud + cellular costs as the driver of the gross-margin decline [3][20]. That is a mix question, not a pricing question — for now.

**What is at stake.** Samsara is being valued at ~10x EV/revenue on FY27 guidance in a sector where the multiple regime has broken. The operating model is working — 19% non-GAAP OM, GAAP-profitable, 15% FCF margin, zero customer concentration, no debt. The vulnerability is that ~all of the GAAP profitability comes from adding back $84M/quarter of SBC-related charges against $7.2M of GAAP operating income, and that gross margin — the one line that cannot be fixed with opex discipline — is going the wrong way while management has promised it will not.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Consensus is literally the guidance midpoint [16][17] while the company has beaten its own revenue guide by 4.3–5.3% and its own operating-margin guide by 400–600bp four quarters running [2][4][5][6][7]. Every leading enterprise indicator Samsara discloses was *accelerating* at the last print — $1M+ customer ARR +62% y/y accelerating four straight quarters, $100k+ cohort ARR +37% accelerating three straight, 11 deals with $1M+ net new ACV (second highest ever), 96% of $100k+ customers on multiple products [2][21] — and the company is doubling job postings and adding quota-carrying reps aggressively [20][33], which TD Cowen read as pipeline confidence when it raised its target to $48 on 20 August and told clients to expect a beat and an FY27 raise [19]. The stock has already given back 12.1% in three sessions [10], short interest has fallen 32% from its June peak so there is little forced-buying but also little bear conviction left [14], the front straddle prices 11.4% against a six-quarter median realised move of 13.3% [10][11][12] — i.e. **the options are not expensive** — and both prior fiscal-Q2 prints delivered +13.60% and +17.44% [10]. GitLab proved on 1 September that a real beat-and-raise still gets paid in this tape [44].

**Bear case.** On 4 June Samsara delivered the largest beat in the series — revenue 5.2% above the guide midpoint, EPS $0.17 vs a $0.12–0.13 guide, and an FY27 raise of $39M against a $23.8M beat — **and the stock fell** [2][10]. That is direct, recent, ticker-specific evidence that the buyside bar is the ~5%-beat number and not published consensus, which strips the bull case of its central mechanism. Piper Sandler downgraded on 10 August with the most specific forward evidence anyone has published: Samsara app downloads, which its model treats as the variable most correlated with revenue, went +28% (Jan) → +17% (Apr) → **−14% (the reported quarter)** → **−44% (July)**, alongside tougher H2 comps and a growth mix shifting to cross-sell; it models only $120M of net new ARR versus the ~$130–136M the Q1 trend implies [18]. Management themselves pre-flagged "timing volatility in the second half" [20]. Non-GAAP gross margin is down 300bp y/y against a CFO commitment to keep it "roughly flat for FY '27" [2][4][20], so the margin story has a live credibility test embedded in this very print. And the sector regime is doing the pricing: software has shed ~$2T of market cap on AI-disruption fears [42][43], IOT fell 5.81% on 2 September into a +0.44% SPY tape [10], and at ~10x EV/revenue on FY27 guidance the multiple has plenty of room to compress further regardless of what the quarter says.

**Base case (mine).** Samsara beats — revenue somewhere in the **$498–508M** range, non-GAAP operating margin **21.5–23%**, non-GAAP EPS **$0.19–0.21** — and raises FY27 revenue to roughly **$2.040–2.050B**. Net new ARR lands between Piper's $120M and the trend-implied $136M, most plausibly **$126–133M**, i.e. net-new growth decelerating from +30% to roughly +20–27% y/y while headline ARR growth holds around 29%. That is a *good* quarter that is *not* an acceleration, into a tape that is punishing anything that is merely good. Because the stock already gave back 12% in three sessions, I think the downside from a merely-good print is more contained than it was in June, and the September seasonal plus the de-risked positioning give a mild upward tilt — but the distribution is genuinely fat on both sides and the front straddle is not overpricing it. **Preliminary direction score +12, prob_up 55, low conviction.** The honest summary is that I have high confidence in the *fundamental* outcome and low confidence in the *price* outcome, and the panel should weight it accordingly.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **Piper Sandler being right about the app-download series** — and it flips the view in one specific, checkable way.

Concretely: if Q2 net new ARR prints **$120M or below** (versus $104.7M in Q2 FY26, i.e. net-new growth collapsing from +30% to under +15%), **and** management raises FY27 revenue only by the amount of the Q2 beat (to ~$2.030B rather than ~$2.045B+), then the four-quarter "structural sandbagger" thesis that underpins every bull note on this name is dead in a single line of the press release. The Street's model becomes: guidance is no longer conservative because the demand cushion that made it conservative has gone. At ~10x EV/revenue in a sector already repricing on AI-disruption fear [42][43], with the *lowest* published price target still above spot [9], there is nothing holding the multiple. On the six-quarter distribution (max |move| 19.54%, median 13.32%) that is a −15% to −20% outcome, not a −5% one.

The mirror image is nearly as clean and would flip the bears: **net new ARR of $140M+ with FY27 taken to $2.05B or above and non-GAAP gross margin back at 77–78%**. That simultaneously falsifies the download panel, restores the CFO's gross-margin commitment, and re-establishes the beat cadence — and against a stock 18.6% off its 52-week closing high with a de-crowded short base, that is a +15%-type outcome. Both tails are live; that is exactly why the 11.4% straddle does not look expensive to me.

The secondary flip, independent of the numbers: **Zscaler's after-hours print on the same evening** [45]. Samsara's reaction will be measured in a tape that Zscaler moves first.
