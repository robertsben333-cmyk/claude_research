# PL — Planet Labs PBC

**Event confirmed: yes.** Planet's own press release (BusinessWire, 2026-08-06) states results
for the quarter ended **31 July 2026 (fiscal Q2 2027)** will be released **after market close on
Thursday, 3 September 2026**, with the call at **5:00 p.m. ET / 2:00 p.m. PT** [1].

**What this print is about.** Planet is not a "did they beat" story this quarter — it has beaten
its own revenue guidance in each of the last several prints and the demand backdrop just got
confirmed by two peers. It is a story about **whether management can be trusted with the
capital structure**. On 4 June 2026 Planet posted a record quarter (revenue +42% YoY, FY27
guidance raised) and the stock fell **26.0%** the next day — not because of the numbers, but
because the morning after the call the company filed an **at-the-market program for up to
$1.5 billion of Class A stock** with fourteen sales agents and forward-sale mechanics [12][13].
That $1.5bn was ~10% of the market cap at the time; at today's $7.1bn it is ~21%. The stock has
since fallen 61% from its 28 May high. So this print carries three simultaneous questions: (a) how
much of the ATM was actually drawn between 5 June and 31 July, which the Q2 10-Q must disclose;
(b) whether backlog re-accelerated after stalling sequentially in Q1 ($900M → $906M, with RPO
actually *falling* $852M → $816M) [10][11][9]; and (c) whether the FY27 guide can be lifted above
a consensus that already sits **above the guidance midpoint**. The options market is pricing
~18.4% for the move; PL's last eight reactions have averaged 28% in absolute terms.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Not revenue, not EPS. It is: how much of the $1.5bn ATM was drawn in the quarter — disclosed in
the Q2 10-Q share count and financing cash flow — read jointly with the FY27 revenue guide.**

Why:

1. The June print *beat* on revenue, *raised* FY guidance and *raised* the margin guide, and the
   stock still fell 26.0%. The differentiating variable was the ATM, filed the **morning after**
   the call [12][13]. The Q1 FY27 call transcript contains no mention of a $1.5bn ATM program
   [25] — management discussed the convertible and warrant exercises as its capital-raising
   activity and left the ATM to a next-day filing. That is the single fact that reset how this
   stock trades.
2. The size is now enormous relative to the equity: $1.5bn against a $7.12bn market cap is ~21%
   potential dilution, versus ~10% when it was announced [30]. Every dollar drawn at $20–30 is
   dilution at prices management itself would have called cheap in May.
3. What the market expects for it: **unknown, and that is the point.** There is no disclosed
   figure. Last hard datapoint is the 10-Q cover: **332,908,730 Class A + 23,493,796 Class B as of
   2026-06-01** [9] — i.e. essentially the 30 April balance and *before* the ATM was signed on
   5 June. No 424B5 has been filed since 2026-06-05 and ATM draws do not require one, so silence
   is not evidence. **My read:** a disclosure of ~zero-to-minimal draw is a genuine positive
   surprise worth several points; a disclosure of $200M+ sold in the $25–35 range is the bear
   case confirming itself.

**Secondary metric with nearly equal weight: sequential backlog and RPO.** Backlog was $900M at
31 Jan 2026 and $906M at 30 Apr 2026 — **+0.7% QoQ** — while RPO *fell* from $852M to $816M
[10][11][9]. Against $94.15M of revenue recognised, that implies book-to-bill of roughly 1.06x in
Q1, a sharp deceleration from the bookings explosion of FY26 (backlog +245% YoY at Q2 FY26,
+216% at Q3, +79% at Q4, +72% at Q1 FY27 — the YoY rate is decelerating hard as the German/NATO
comparables anniversary). Management said 40% of the $906M backlog is applicable to the next
twelve months [25]. **If backlog does not clearly break above $1bn this print, the "excellent
visibility" argument that Berenberg and the bulls lean on [26] weakens materially.**

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Regime.** Planet is a high-beta (5-yr beta 2.12 [3]), unprofitable, 20x-EV/Sales momentum name
inside a thematic complex — "space" — that had its own idiosyncratic supply shock this summer.
**SpaceX IPO'd on 2026-06-12 at $135/share, raising ~$75bn at a ~$1.8tn valuation**, peaked at
$225.64 intraday on 6/16, and by late August was back at ~$137, with lockup expirations releasing
up to 1.37bn shares from early August [31][33]. Press coverage explicitly ties Planet's retreat to
"the sell-off in other space companies following the SpaceX IPO" [19]. **Inference (mine):** a
large part of PL's 54% three-month decline is a sector de-rating and a capital-supply event, not a
company-specific fundamental deterioration — which is precisely why the fundamentals in this print
have room to matter again.

**The complex stabilised in August.** Over one August stretch, Voyager +54%, Redwire +38%, ASTS
+29%, RKLB +28%, with FLY, LUNR, SPIR, BKSY, SPCE, VSAT and **PL also posting double-digit gains**,
SPCX the lone decliner [33]. PL did run $20.48 (7/31) → $23.93 (8/7), +16.8%, before giving it all
back into month-end [5]. Sector risk appetite is present but fragile.

**Peers who have already reported, and how they traded — this is the most useful evidence here:**

- **BlackSky (BKSY), Q2 CY2026 reported 2026-08-06.** Revenue **$33.3M, +50% YoY and +60%
  sequentially**; adjusted EBITDA **+$4.7M** (14.2% margin, a $7.5M YoY improvement); imagery/AI
  subscriptions crossed a **$100M annual run rate**; up to **$200M of YTD bookings**;
  **international revenue +150% YoY**. Full-year 2026 guidance **reaffirmed** at $130–150M revenue
  and $12–24M adjusted EBITDA. **Stock +4.93% on the day** [32]. Note the contrast with Q1 2026,
  when BKSY revenue *fell* to $20.8M with a −$5.1M adjusted EBITDA [32] — the sequential swing is
  enormous and it happened in exactly Planet's fiscal Q2 window.
- **Satellogic (SATL), Q2 CY2026.** Revenue **$15.9M, +259% YoY** from $4.4M; **first positive
  operating income ($0.3M) and adjusted EBITDA ($2.8M)**; signed a **>$18M one-year international
  defence agreement** and a **$12M sovereign satellite ownership-transfer deal**. Commentary
  attributes it to "a structural shift in the earth observation market from transactional imagery
  purchases to persistent monitoring subscriptions" [33].

**Read-through (mine): unambiguously positive for Planet's May–July revenue and bookings.** Two
independent EO operators reported sharp acceleration driven by sovereign/defence subscription
demand in the identical calendar quarter. It is hard to construct a world in which Planet — with
65% of revenue in D&I and 86% YoY growth in EMEA — misses its $102–107M guide. **The caution is
the BKSY calibration: a big beat plus a reaffirm earned only ~+5%.**

**Policy tailwind.** European rearmament is the structural driver: the EU's ReArm Europe framework
of up to ~€800bn, NATO's move toward a 5% spending benchmark, and France alone committing €4.2bn
to military space for 2026–2030 (total ~€10.2bn) [43][44]. 2026 defence budgets are described as
pivoting to "Total Situational Awareness," naming BlackSky and Planet as beneficiaries [44].

**Sensitivities.** Rates: as a long-duration, unprofitable grower with beta 2.12, PL is a rate- and
risk-appetite proxy. FX: EMEA is 37% of revenue with subsidiaries in Germany, Luxembourg, Slovenia,
Austria, the Netherlands and the UK [9] — a stronger EUR is a tailwind to reported revenue. No
meaningful commodity exposure; launch cost is a capex input and is falling.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The demand evidence is already in from peers: BlackSky +50% YoY with a +60%
sequential jump and Satellogic +259% with its first operating profit, both in Planet's exact
fiscal Q2 window, both driven by sovereign defence subscriptions [32][33]. Planet beat the high end
of its own Q1 guide by 3.5% [9][11] and beat consensus revenue by 11.6% and 12.6% in the two
quarters before that [21][22], with D&I +67.5% and EMEA +86.0% YoY [9] against a European
rearmament backdrop that is accelerating, not fading [43][44]. Positioning is the opposite of the
June setup: the stock is 61% off its high and 25.7% below its 200-day, RSI 35, shorts have already
covered 14.4% month-on-month [3][5], and Berenberg put a fresh Buy on it the session before the
print [26]. Two mechanical positives land with this report: the warrant liability is gone, so the
headline GAAP loss collapses from −$0.40 to roughly −$0.07 [10][8], and the ATM's existence is
already known and already punished — it cannot surprise twice. If management pairs an FY27 raise
above $441M with backlog through $1bn and confirms minimal ATM usage, the reverse call skew and
41,286 contracts of Oct-$30 open interest [6] describe exactly the fuel for the kind of +35% and
+48% reactions this stock produced in December and September 2025 [5].

**Bear case.** The bar is quantitatively higher than it looks: consensus FY27 revenue of $436.15M
sits above the $433M guide midpoint and *every* analyst is at or above midpoint [8], so a beat plus
a proportional raise lands exactly on consensus and does nothing. Meanwhile the leading indicator
has already rolled — backlog grew just 0.7% sequentially in Q1 and **RPO fell $36M** [9][10][11] —
which is what deceleration looks like before it reaches revenue, and Civil Government revenue was
already *down* 0.7% YoY [9]. The economics are getting worse as the mix shifts to lower-margin
satellite services: non-GAAP gross margin 59% → 56% → guided 52–55%, adjusted EBITDA +$1.2M →
−$1.0M, FCF −$2.5M with capex nearly doubling YoY and guided to $80–95M for the year [9][10].
Against that, a **$1.5bn ATM equals ~21% of the current market cap** [12][30], the company filed it
the morning after a call on which it was not mentioned [25][12], and the Q2 10-Q will finally show
how many shares were sold in the $22–35 zone — with a further ~38.5M shares of in-the-money
convertible dilution sitting behind it [9]. Add customer concentration (one customer = 33% of AR,
two = 26% of revenue [9]), continuing scheduled insider selling by the CEO, CFO and CSO with
1.03M and 461k share plan capacity still open [9][17], a 20.5x EV/Sales multiple even after a 61%
drawdown [3], and a sector still absorbing SpaceX lockup supply [31][33] — and a disappointing
capital-allocation disclosure could take this to the $16.25 straddle breakeven [6] without any
revenue miss at all.

**Base case (mine).** Planet beats the $102–107M revenue guide — the peer evidence makes that close
to a formality — prints adjusted EBITDA at or above the $0–5M range, and raises FY27 to roughly
$434–444M. That is a *small* raise relative to a consensus already at $436.15M, so the numbers
themselves are roughly neutral. The reaction is then decided almost entirely by two disclosures:
sequential backlog (needs a visible break above $1bn) and ATM utilisation. I put the modal outcome
at a mildly positive reaction from a washed-out base — the sector's August stabilisation, the
warrant-noise removal, the fresh Berenberg initiation, the falling short interest and the
non-crowded stock positioning all lean the same way — but with genuinely fat tails in both
directions and a realised move that I expect to **exceed** the 18.4% priced. Direction score **+15**,
probability of an up move **56%**, conviction **Medium**.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The single most credible reversal is **an ATM disclosure at scale**. Concretely: if the Q2 10-Q
shows Class A shares outstanding materially above ~340M (versus 332,908,730 on 2026-06-01 [9]) or a
financing-activities line showing several hundred million dollars of "proceeds from issuance of
common stock under the ATM programme," then management sold equity into a 50% drawdown at $22–35
after telling investors in June it already had a "fortress balance sheet" with $731M of cash and
$730M more of undrawn authorisation [10][12]. That would (a) confirm that the June ATM was not a
shelf-of-convenience but a funding requirement, (b) reprice the capex plan as under-funded rather
than opportunistic, and (c) destroy the "beat and raise" read regardless of what revenue prints. In
that scenario I would expect the reaction to trade toward or beyond the −18% side of the straddle,
and the bull case collapses to a valuation-support argument at 15x EV/Sales.

The mirror image — the reversal that would flip a bear — is **zero or near-zero ATM utilisation
disclosed alongside backlog above $1.05bn**. That combination says the $1.5bn was insurance, not
need, and that the Q1 bookings pause was timing. Given call-heavy open interest and reverse skew
[6], that outcome is where the +30%-type historical reactions live.

A third, lower-probability flip: **a named eight- or nine-figure sovereign contract disclosed with
the print** (Planet has form — the €240M German deal accompanied the September 2025 report, which
produced a +47.93% session [5]). This is the specific mechanism by which PL has historically
blown through its implied move to the upside.
