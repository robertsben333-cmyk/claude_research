# LULU — lululemon athletica inc.

**Event confirmed: yes.** Q2 fiscal 2026 (quarter ended 02 Aug 2026), Thursday **03 September 2026, after the close**, call at 4:30 p.m. ET, confirmed by the company's own press release of 20 Aug 2026 [1][2].

**What this print is about.** This is not an EPS print. Q2 EPS is effectively pre-announced: management guided $1.76–$1.81 on 04 June 2026 and the Street has converged to $1.79 [3][20]. The trade is entirely about **what happens to the FY2026 EPS guide of $10.95–$11.15** — and secondarily about whether the North America comparable-sales trough that management guided to (a *low-double-digit decline* in Q2) is in fact a trough or a step down. Three things make this print unusually asymmetric in both directions. (1) The company has an **incoming CEO, Heidi O'Neill, who starts on 08 September — five days after this call** [8][9]; interim co-CEOs Meghan Frank and André Maestrini have every incentive to hand her a de-risked plan, and UBS explicitly expects a second FY cut to $9.70–$9.90 [30]. (2) The buyside bar is already far below the printed guide — UBS puts the unofficial bar at **$10.20–$10.40** vs. sell-side consensus $10.93 [30] — so a cut is partly priced. (3) There is a **$230 million unrecognised IEEPA tariff refund claim** disclosed in the Q1 10-Q, explicitly excluded from guidance [24][19]; peers Gap, Target and Nike have already booked such refunds and *raised* guidance on them [26][27][28]. The stock is at $120.07, 10.9x forward earnings, down ~42% YTD, with 10.3% of float short [4][5][32]. The options market prices 8.1%; the last six prints averaged 12.4% [6][7].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**The FY2026 diluted-EPS guidance range, and the North America comparable-sales number that justifies it.**

Not headline Q2 EPS: the guide is a $0.05-wide band and consensus sits inside it. The print trades on whether the second-half recovery embedded in the FY guide survives contact with Q2 actuals.

What the market expects, and how I know:
- Management guided Q2 **North America comps down "low double digits"**, with the U.S. "also in that range", against −6% in Q1 [16][17]. That is the single number that will be read first.
- Management guided **China Mainland +mid-to-high teens** in Q2 (vs. +30% revenue / +13% comps in Q1, which itself included ~4pp of Chinese-New-Year calendar benefit) and **~20% for the full year** [16][17]. China is the last growth engine; a print below the mid-teens is the bear's confirmation. Third-party modelling puts Q2 China at +19.5% and Rest of World at +14.6% [31].
- Management guided **Q2 gross margin −410bp**, decomposed as tariffs −150bp gross with ~100bp of offsets, markdowns +50bp, plus store and DC investment; **SG&A deleverage of 500bp**; operating margin ~11.6% vs. 20.7% [16]. Q2 was explicitly called the **"high watermark" for markdowns**, with sequential improvement expected after [16]. If Q2 markdowns come in worse than +50bp, the "high watermark" framing breaks and the FY guide goes with it.
- The FY guide requires full-price sales to go from "down mid-single digits in Q2" to "flat to slightly better for the full year" [17] — i.e. an H2 inflection driven by product newness (assortment newness moving 23%→35%, currently 30%) and a "bolder" H2 marketing plan [16].

**Secondary swing factor — the $230M tariff refund.** The Q1 10-Q states: *"The Company paid $230 million of tariffs under the IEEPA and has commenced submitting refund claims for eligible IEEPA tariffs paid, including associated interest... it has not recognized an asset in relation to IEEPA refund claims"* [24]. On the Q1 call: *"While we are participating in the refund process, our guidance assumes no recovery of tariffs paid under IEEPA"* [16]. $230M pre-tax on 113.5M shares is ~$2.03/share gross, ~$1.42/share after the guided 30% tax rate. **Inference (mine):** if LULU recognises any material part of this in Q2 — as Gap, Target and Nike have already done in their comparable quarters [26][27][28] — reported EPS and the FY guide can be raised on a one-timer even as the underlying business deteriorates. That creates a real possibility of a *confusing* print in which the headline beats and the guide rises while the operating story worsens.

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Regime.** Low-multiple, high-short-interest consumer discretionary names into a promotional apparel cycle. LULU's beta is 0.86 [4], so this is an idiosyncratic single-stock event rather than a factor trade. Rate sensitivity is negligible — zero debt, $1.5B cash [24]. FX is a genuine swing: Q1 revenue was +4% reported vs +2% constant currency and China +30% reported vs +23% cc [19][24], so a weak dollar has been flattering the international line by ~2pp at the group level and ~7pp in China.

**Tariffs — the dominant macro variable.**
- SCOTUS struck down IEEPA tariffs **20 Feb 2026** (6–3) [58]. The CIT ordered CBP to begin paying refunds **04 Mar 2026**; by 09 Apr, ~82% of IEEPA entries were electronically refundable and ~$128.7B of refunds had been accepted for processing [59].
- The administration replaced IEEPA with a **10% global Section 122 tariff effective 24 Feb 2026**, which cut the incremental apparel tariff burden from ~15% to ~8% relative to 2024 [60].
- Section 122 **expired 24 July 2026** and was replaced by **USTR Section 301 forced-labour tariffs of 10% or 12.5%** on ~60 economies, effective 24 July 2026 [35][36]. Section 232 and 301 duties are **not refundable** [61].
- **Implication:** LULU's $230M of IEEPA duties is recoverable; its go-forward duty burden is not. The company guided FY26 to a **$380M gross tariff cost with ~$160M of mitigation** [38].

**Supply-chain / customer read:** none applicable — LULU is ~88% DTC by channel (stores + e-commerce) [24], so there is no wholesale customer whose commentary reads through. The relevant read-through is landlord/mall traffic and the promotional posture of DKS/Foot Locker, both negative.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Expectations have already broken. The stock is $120.07 at 10.9x forward and 5.6x EV/EBITDA with zero debt, $1.5B cash and $1.28B TTM FCF, down ~42% YTD and ~47% from its high [4][32][24][33]. UBS itself concedes the buyside bar for FY26 EPS is **$10.20–$10.40 versus a printed guide of $10.95–$11.15** [30] — meaning a cut of up to ~7% is already in the price. **10.33% of float is short with 4.38 days to cover** [32], retail is 72% bullish [34], and there is **$1.1B of buyback authorisation** unused [33]. On top of that sits a **$230M unrecognised IEEPA refund claim explicitly excluded from guidance** [24][16], in a quarter where Gap, Target and Nike all booked comparable refunds and Gap *raised* full-year EPS on it [26][27][28]. Inventory is clean (units −4% [19]). The last two times LULU printed into a beaten-down tape it went **+9.6% and +3.8%** despite bad guidance [13][14]. If management simply reiterates the FY range, or trims it into the $10.4–$10.7 zone while booking any part of the refund, the short base is the fuel and a move above the 8.1% implied is easy to construct.

**Bear case.** The operating trend is still deteriorating and the guide it has to defend is heroic. Management has to deliver a **low-double-digit North America comp decline** and then claim the second half inflects, on the strength of assortment newness going 30%→35% and a "bolder" marketing plan [16]. The peer tape argues against it: **Athleta −12%**, **Foot Locker −3.6%**, DKS explicitly calling the athletic apparel market "increasingly promotional" with industry-wide inventory build and cutting its own outlook [65][64]. That directly threatens the "Q2 is the markdown high watermark" assumption [16]. **China — the only growth engine — is guided from +30% revenue in Q1 down to mid-to-high teens**, and Exane BNP downgraded to Underperform with an **$88** target citing exactly that deceleration [18][16]. Alt-data shows DTC share going 30%→24% while Alo went 8%→14% [46]. **UBS expects the FY guide cut to $9.70–$9.90, below even the buyside bar** [30]. And the governance setup is maximally conducive to a kitchen sink: interim co-CEOs, one of whom stays on as CFO, handing a plan to a new CEO who starts **five days later** [8][9] — Meghan Frank does not want to own a number Heidi O'Neill misses. Four of the last six reactions were down, averaging −15.3% on the down prints [10][11][12][16].

**Base case.** A Q2 that lands inside the guided band (it is a $0.05 EPS range given nine weeks into a thirteen-week quarter — the risk of missing it is low), a North America comp near or slightly worse than the guided low-double-digit decline, China somewhere in the mid-teens, and an **FY EPS guide trimmed to roughly $10.2–$10.6** — a cut, but not the $9.70–$9.90 UBS is modelling. The tariff refund is discussed as a possible future benefit but not fully recognised, because the 10-Q language ("the ultimate amounts that it may recover remain uncertain") suggests the company is being deliberately conservative on recognition [24]. Management defers strategy to the incoming CEO, which caps the upside narrative — UBS explicitly expects "few big strategic updates" for this reason [30]. In that world the stock moves within the implied band with a downward tilt, because the *only* offsets to a second guidance cut are positioning and valuation, and both were already true on 04 June when the stock fell 8.6%. **My preliminary read: modestly bearish, low conviction, with a fat right tail from the refund and the short base.**

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The consensus view going in is "another cut, but a lot of it is priced". The most credible reversal, stated concretely:

**Management reiterates the $10.95–$11.15 FY EPS range while recognising a material portion of the $230M IEEPA refund, and reports July North America comps that improved sequentially from the low-double-digit trough.** That combination would (a) invalidate the UBS $9.70–$9.90 thesis outright, (b) hand the incoming CEO a *raised* rather than reset base, and (c) force the 10.8M-share short base to cover into a 4.4-day-to-cover book. The tell would be gross margin better than the guided −410bp with markdowns at or below +50bp — because that is the specific line where the promotional pressure DKS described would show up first. Given LULU has exceeded its implied move in six of eight prints [6], such an outcome would plausibly produce a move well beyond +8.1%.

The mirror reversal — the way the bulls lose worst — is an FY guide **below $9.70** *combined with* a statement that the second-half product and marketing reset is being deferred to the new CEO. That converts the stock from "cheap and washed out" into "no earnings floor and no plan until at least Q3", and the −18.6% / −19.8% precedents [11][12] become the reference class.

## A note on the triage rationale

Triage selected LULU on: *"Guided to a revenue decline and ~410bp tariff-driven gross-margin hit plus brand-traffic damage; a live guidance/execution question the market will punish or reward hard."*

That is **directionally right but incomplete**, and I would restate it. The 410bp gross-margin decline is not primarily tariff-driven: management's own bridge attributes only **150bp gross (≈50bp net of ~100bp of offsets)** to tariffs in Q2, with markdowns +50bp and the balance from store/DC investment and deleverage [16]. Treating this as a tariff print would be a mistake. More importantly, triage missed the two facts that actually define the setup: (a) the **incoming CEO starts five days after the call**, which is the single strongest argument for a conservative reset and simultaneously caps the strategic-narrative upside [8][9][30]; and (b) the **$230M unrecognised IEEPA refund** explicitly excluded from guidance, in a quarter when direct peers have been booking exactly such refunds and raising guidance on them [24][26][27][28]. The "punish or reward hard" instinct is well supported — mean absolute realised move 12.4% against an 8.1% implied — but the mechanism is the FY EPS guide against an already-lowered buyside bar, not the quarter.
