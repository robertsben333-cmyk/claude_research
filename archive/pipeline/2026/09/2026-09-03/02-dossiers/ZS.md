# ZS — Zscaler, Inc.

**What this print is about.** This is not an EPS event. On 26 May 2026 Zscaler beat Q3 FY26 on
every reported line — revenue $850.5M (+25%), ARR $3,525M (+25%), record 23% non-GAAP operating
margin — and then lost roughly a third of its market value in a single session because management
volunteered a *preliminary* FY27 framework of 16–17% ARR and revenue growth (versus a Street then
modelling ~18.4%), cut FY26 free-cash-flow margin guidance from 26.5–27% to 22.8–23.3% on a memory-driven
capex surge, and disclosed the departure of two sales leaders under CRO Mike Rich. On 3 September
Zscaler gives that FY27 number *formally* for the first time. Everything else — the Q4 beat, the
$1.09 consensus, the ARR exit print — is scenery. The market is asking one question: was 16–17% a
prudence floor set by a management team that had just lost two sales leaders and wanted room, or is
it the real shape of a business decelerating from 25% while CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Okta and
Netskope all just beat and raised into an accelerating security-spend cycle? The stock enters the
print at $172.73, down ~49% from its 52-week high, at 7.5x forward P/S against a peer group at
14–33x, with options pricing a 13% move and a track record of blowing through that number.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**The formal FY27 ARR growth guide — and specifically whether the number comes in above the
preliminary 16–17% frame, or is accompanied by evidence that 16–17% is a floor rather than a
run-rate.**

How I know it is this and not EPS:

- The May print *beat* on revenue, EPS, ARR and operating margin and the stock still fell 27.6% in a
  session (intraday −31%, its worst day ever) purely on the FY27 framework and the FCF cut [30], [16].
- Multiple independent pre-print notes converge on the same metric. TIKR: management must "firm up"
  the 16–17% guidance or provide evidence it is "a floor" rather than a ceiling on deceleration [17].
  Option Beast: the trade "depends entirely on guidance direction, not Q4 beat/miss" [31]. Motley Fool:
  forward guidance, not current-quarter results, will determine sentiment [32]. Barchart: "the number
  that will most likely move the stock is Zscaler's forward guidance" [33].
- Oppenheimer expects the formal FY27 targets to "broadly match" the 16–17% frame [24] — meaning an
  in-line guide is now the modal expectation and therefore not, by itself, a positive catalyst.

**What the market expects for it:** 16–17% ARR and revenue growth, formally confirmed. FY27 revenue
consensus of $3.9B (+17.2%) [18] sits at the top of that frame. The Street was at ~18.4% before the
May print [34]; it has since capitulated to the guide.

**Secondary metrics that will be read alongside it, in order of weight:**

1. **FY27 free-cash-flow margin.** FY26 was cut to 22.8–23.3% and management flagged FY27 capex up a
   further **~200bp as a percentage of revenue** on the memory crunch and price spikes [30], [29]. A
   second FCF cut would be the most damaging single line item on the release.
2. **Organic net new ARR.** Q3 total NNARR $166M (+24%); **organic $153M (+14%)**; organic total ARR
   growth 21% versus 25% reported [28], [29]. Red Canary exit ARR was $127M [28].
3. **The second sales-leadership vacancy.** One replacement appointed internally, the second "in late
   stages" as of May [35], [29]. TIKR frames "second sales leadership role filled" as a condition for
   the Hold camp to gain conviction [17].
4. **Zero Trust Everywhere customers** (700+ in Q3, up from 550+ in Q2 and 210 a year earlier) and
   **Z-Flex TCV** ($480M in Q3, +60% QoQ from $290M, $1B+ over the trailing twelve months, ~4-year
   average term) [13], [29].
5. **Non-seat-based / metered ACV mix** — just over 30% of new ACV, associated ARR growing >100% YoY,
   up from ~25% [17], [29]. This is the AI-monetisation proof point management will lean on.

operating margin and a 30%-growing RPO backlog, and that the FY27 frame was a prudence artefact set
in the same week two sales leaders walked. The FCF line is the swing factor on valuation: the capex
step-up is memory-cost driven and, on the company's own May framing, gets worse in FY27 before it
gets better.

- **Kevin Rubin (CFO), 2026-08-25:** sold **503 shares** across five open-market transactions at

  footnote states the sale was **"effected pursuant to a Rule 10b5-1 trading plan adopted on
  March 24, 2026"** — i.e. **pre-planned, not discretionary**, and adopted before the May print [51], [52].
- **Jagtar (Jay) Singh Chaudhry (Chairman/CEO, 10% owner), 2026-03-17:** a transaction characterised
  in search results as **tax withholding**, not an open-market sale [53]. `snippet_only`.
- **Aggregate:** zero insider buys and 32 insider sell transactions over the trailing twelve months
  [52] — normal for a company running $617.4M of nine-month SBC [5], and not a signal on its own.
- **No insider buying after a 49% drawdown** is a mild negative tell. My inference, not fact.

**8-K / filing cadence.** EDGAR shows a quiet summer: Form 4 (2026-08-26), Schedule 13G (2026-07-30),
Form 4 (2026-07-28), Form 4 (2026-07-07). **No 8-K between the 26 May earnings 8-K and the print** —
so **no pre-announcement, no guidance revision, no executive-departure 8-K, and no auditor or
restatement event** in the intervening fourteen weeks [26], [54]. The absence of a negative
pre-announcement is itself mildly reassuring.

**Executive / departures.** Two sales leaders under CRO Mike Rich departed at the end of Q3 FY26.
CFO Kevin Rubin attributed the guidance posture to a "prudent approach" amid the transitions; CEO Jay
Chaudhry said Mike Rich "has built a strong sales engine" and acknowledged the changes "could have an
impact in the short term." One replacement was appointed internally; the second was "in late stages"
as of the May call. **Management declined to say whether the departures were voluntary, and declined
to quantify the guidance impact** [35], [29]. Mike Rich remains listed as CRO and President of Global
Sales on Zscaler's leadership page [55]; a search snippet referencing "James Dwyer" joining as CRO
could **not be corroborated** and is not treated as fact.

**Litigation.** At least five plaintiffs' firms — Faruqi & Faruqi, Bronstein Gewirtz & Grossman,
The Schall Law Firm, The Gross Law Firm and Johnson Fistel — announced securities investigations or
class-action activity between June and July 2026 arising from the 27 May decline [56], [57], [58].
The allegations centre on the Q4 revenue guide falling short of the ~$879M the Street sought, the
reduced FCF-margin projection, and the disclosure of senior sales-executive departures [56].

> **My inference:** pending securities litigation over a guidance-related drop is a structural
> incentive for management to guide FY27 *conservatively* again. A company being sued for allegedly
> optimistic disclosure does not stretch on its first formal guide for the year in question. This is
> the single strongest forensic argument against the "management raises the frame" bull case.

**Tone / language shift.** The May call introduced language that had not previously appeared:
"prudent approach," "new logo weakness," "limited coverage in the 2,000–10,000 user segment," and an
unusual decision to give a *preliminary* next-year framework a quarter early [29]. Volunteering
FY27 guidance in May, rather than waiting for the September Q4 call, is itself atypical and reads to
me as management pre-loading bad news — which cuts both ways for this print.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Expectations are genuinely on the floor and positioning is not short. FY27 consensus
has already capitulated to the company's own frame ($3.9B revenue, +17.2%; EPS $4.58) [18], Zacks
Rank is a neutral #3 [18], and the stock is at 7.54x forward P/S versus an 18.51x industry and 32.97x
for CrowdStrike [13] after underperforming its own sector by ~100 points YTD [13]. Five firms raised
targets between 19 and 26 August [20]–[23], and Oppenheimer explicitly calls the estimates
"de-risked" at a $250 target [24]. The business underneath is not broken: organic ARR +21%, RPO
+30% to $6.46B [5], record 23% non-GAAP operating margin, Zero Trust Everywhere customers 700+ from
210 a year earlier, Z-Flex TCV $480M (+60% QoQ, $1B+ trailing twelve months), and non-seat metered
ACV now >30% of new business with associated ARR growing >100% [12], [13], [29]. The whole sector
just proved AI is *expanding* security budgets (CRWD +51% net new ARR, PANW NGS ARR +63%, OKTA +29%
on the print, NTSK raised) [62], [65], [66], [64]. If Zscaler guides FY27 to 17%+ with a filled sales
org and a stabilised FCF margin, a 13% implied move is too low on the upside and the last two
sessions' 8.3% drawdown has already cleared the froth.

**Bear case.** The formal FY27 guide is being set by a management team that (a) volunteered 16–17% in
May specifically to buy itself room, (b) has since been named in at least five securities
investigations over exactly that disclosure [56]–[58], and (c) has still not publicly confirmed the
second sales-leadership replacement [35], [29]. Companies in that position guide *at or below* their
own preliminary frame. Meanwhile the Q4 bar itself is demanding: exit ARR of $3.740–3.749B requires
$215–224M of net new ARR (my arithmetic on [12], [27]) versus ~$195M a year ago, when organic net new
ARR grew only 14% in Q3 [28]. The FCF story is structurally worse, not better: FY26 margin already
cut 350–420bp and FY27 capex flagged up another ~200bp on memory costs [16], [29] — a second cut is
live. Netskope grew ARR 27% and raised the night before [66]; CrowdStrike and Palo Alto are taking
the sector's growth. And the regime is punishing: the 10-year at 4.79% and a 66% implied odds of a
Fed *hike* [59] compress long-duration multiples independent of the print — Palo Alto beat and fell
5–7% into exactly this tape [65], [60]. The stock has fallen double digits on three consecutive
prints and exceeded the implied move in five of eight [6].

**Base case (mine).** Zscaler beats Q4 revenue and EPS — that is near-certain given four consecutive
revenue beats and an ~11.4% average EPS surprise [13], [17] — hits the low-to-mid of its ARR exit
range, and guides FY27 to something in the 16–18% band, most likely bracketing the preliminary frame
rather than clearly exceeding it, with FY27 FCF margin the wildcard. That outcome is *already the
consensus expectation* [24], which means it is not by itself a positive catalyst, and in a
rising-rate tape with call-skewed options positioning it more likely produces a negative than a
positive move — but a modest one relative to the 13% implied. The tails are what make this hard:
a genuine 18%+ guide with the sales org repaired and FCF stabilised is worth a lot more than +13%
against a 7.5x multiple; a second FCF cut or a 15-handle on FY27 ARR growth is worth a lot more than
−13%. I rate direction only slightly negative with low conviction and very high reversal risk.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The single most credible reversal — and it is genuinely credible, not a strawman:

**Zscaler guides FY27 ARR growth to 18% or better, holds FY27 free-cash-flow margin flat to up
versus FY26's 22.8–23.3%, and announces the second sales-leadership seat filled — while pointing to
the metered/non-seat AI business (>30% of new ACV, ARR +100% YoY) as the mechanism.** That
combination would retroactively reclassify the May frame as exactly what management said it was —
prudence during a sales transition — and would land on a stock at 7.54x forward P/S with a
$199 average target, a 74.8th-percentile options open interest already leaning long calls, and a
sector that has just re-rated CRWD, PANW, OKTA and NTSK on the same AI-security thesis. Against a
13% implied move and a 49% drawdown from the 52-week high, I would expect that outcome to produce a
move materially larger than +13% and to invalidate the bearish read entirely.

The mirror-image flip, for completeness: a **second consecutive FCF-margin cut** would break the
"the multiple is the floor" argument, because it converts Zscaler from a temporarily-decelerating
compounder into a structurally lower-return one — and at that point 7.5x P/S is not cheap.

## Note on the triage rationale

Triage selected ZS on the basis that "options imply a 13% move and ZS has blown through implied moves
before (−27.6% in May); security-spend narrative is heavily covered so edge is moderate rather than
large." **I broadly agree, with two corrections.**

First, the framing that "the security-spend narrative is heavily covered" is the wrong axis for this
name. The security-spend narrative is not the variable — CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Okta and Netskope
have already settled it in the affirmative. The variable is Zscaler-specific: whether a formal FY27
guide validates or repudiates a preliminary frame set fourteen weeks ago during a sales-leadership
disruption, with securities litigation now pending over that same disclosure. That is a much narrower
and less-covered question, and it argues the edge is *larger* than triage assumed, not smaller.

Second, triage's implied-move figure is correct but its as-of date matters: the 13% is from an
Investing.com/Bloomberg article dated 2026-08-27, when the stock was at $187.30. It has since fallen
7.8% to $172.73. The panel should not treat 13% as a last-session reading.
