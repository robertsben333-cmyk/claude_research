# UNFI — United Natural Foods, Inc.

**Event confirmed: YES** — Tuesday 2026-09-08, before market open, 8:30 a.m. ET call, fiscal Q4 and full-year FY2026 (13 weeks / 52 weeks ended 2026-08-01). Confirmed from the company's own press release [1][2].

**What this print is about.** This is not a quarter print, it is a *guidance* print. UNFI has spent FY26 deliberately shrinking its top line — exiting a ~$1bn East-region customer, closing Allentown and Sturtevant DCs, winding down low-margin conventional volume — in exchange for a step-change in profitability (39-week adjusted EBITDA +21.3%, adjusted EPS +138%, net leverage 3.3x → 2.5x). Management has told the Street that fiscal 2027 is when the wholesale business "returns to low single-digit sales growth," and the December 2025 Investor Day set a FY28 frame of ~$33bn sales and ~$800M adjusted EBITDA [23][24]. Q4 itself is heavily pre-guided: management narrowed FY26 ranges only three months ago, so the 39-week actuals plus the FY guide box Q4 into a $156–176M adjusted EBITDA / $0.45–0.65 adjusted EPS band, and consensus sits near the top of that band. The trade therefore hinges almost entirely on the **first FY27 guide** — delivered by a CFO who started on 2026-08-10 and has never guided this company, into a quarter where on-highway diesel is up ~50% year over year and management has already said it is accelerating technology and supply-chain investment into the back half. The stock has fallen 23.5% from its 2026-06-08 high and 10.3% in the last 21 sessions into the event, so some of that risk is in the price.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**The fiscal 2027 adjusted EBITDA (and adjusted EPS) guidance range, and specifically whether the FY27 EBITDA midpoint lands at or above ~$740M.** Secondary but tightly linked: the **Q4 natural-segment growth rate**, which is the only clean read on whether "return to low-single-digit wholesale sales growth in FY27" is real.

**Why this and not headline EPS.** Q4 EPS is already boxed in by the guidance arithmetic above — the range of plausible outcomes is $0.45–0.70 and half of that is already priced. FY27 is a blank sheet. Management said on the Q3 call that FY27 marks "a return to growth for the broader wholesale business as larger optimization actions are lapped," and that it would "step up investment in technology, supply chain and commercial capabilities as fiscal 2026 closes" [22][25]. Both halves of that sentence go into the FY27 guide, and they point in opposite directions.

**What the market expects, and how I know.** Two anchors: (a) the Investor Day framework of ~$33bn sales / ~$800M adjusted EBITDA / ~$300M FCF for FY28, published 2025-12-10 [23][24]; (b) FY27 consensus adjusted EPS of $3.21 [13]. The precedent for how the market treats an initial guide is the 2025-09-30 print, when UNFI's *initial* FY26 adjusted EPS guide of $1.50–2.30 came in against a $1.42 consensus and the stock rose **+18.45%** [26][5] — so an initial guide is not automatically read as a sandbag here; it is read literally.

**My inference (labelled as mine, not fact):** the odds of a conservative FY27 guide are higher than usual for four converging reasons — (i) **Alfredo Luchini started as CFO on 2026-08-10** [27][28] and this is his first guide, and new CFOs systematically reset bars; (ii) EIA on-highway diesel was **$5.599/gal on 2026-08-31, +$1.865 (+50%) y/y** [29][30], a direct hit to a private-fleet distributor that has already told investors Q4 embeds "expected pressure from fuel and transportation" [22][25]; (iii) management has explicitly pre-announced an *increase* in technology and supply-chain investment; (iv) the CEO said on the Q3 call that "the reduction in SNAP funding is beginning to make an impact" on lower-income consumers [25], which pressures the independent-grocer customer base. None of these is a fact about what will be guided — they are reasons to weight the low end of the range.

**Unit economics.** This is a ~1% operating-margin business (0.87–0.94% TTM per providers [6][7]) with 13.4% gross margin. Every 20bp of gross margin is roughly $60M of annualised gross profit on a $31bn base — which is why the whole equity story is margin and cost, not volume. Q3 opex fell ~7% and DC productivity improved >7%, on-time delivery +4% y/y, aided by AI-based supply-chain and fleet tools [25][31].

**Free cash flow and balance sheet.** 39-week FY26 FCF $243M versus $153M prior year (+58.8%); operating cash flow $343M; capex only $100M against a ~$250M full-year capex/cloud plan, so Q4 carries a very heavy spend load [10]. Net debt $1.63bn, lowest since FY2018; net leverage 2.5x versus 3.3x at FY25 year-end [10][18]. A voluntary **$115M prepayment** on the 2028 senior notes and a refinanced **$2.53bn ABL** (annual borrowing cost down ~$2M) were executed in Q3/Q4 [25][32].

**Buyback / dilution.** $200M four-year authorisation, ~$138M remaining as of December 2025 [33]. ~750k shares for ~$25M in Q2; ~1.0M shares for $38M at an average $37.88 fiscal year to date through Q3 [21][25]. At $43.62 the remaining authorisation is ~5% of the market cap — small but not trivial for a $2.6bn company.

**Customer concentration.** The largest customer (Whole Foods Market / Amazon) is roughly 20–25% of net sales depending on the source, and the distribution agreement now runs to **May 2032** [34][35][36]. That is the single most important de-risking fact in the story and it is already done — it will not be a catalyst on this print. The offsetting concentration risk is the *loss* already taken: a ~$1bn/yr East-region customer whose supply agreement terminated in FY25, driving the Allentown DC closure in Q1 FY26 [37].

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

- **Diesel is the live macro variable.** EIA on-highway national average **$5.599/gal at 2026-08-31, up $1.865 (~+50%) y/y** from $3.734 a year earlier; the average rose from $5.257 (Aug-10) to $5.454 (Aug-17) before easing 5.3c in the final week of August [29][30]. The larger point is forward: FY27 guidance has to be set against a diesel curve 50% above last year.
- **SNAP is the demand variable.** OBBBA-driven SNAP reductions have removed benefits from millions of households and SNAP is no longer indexed to food inflation [48]. CEO Sandy Douglas said on the Q3 call that "the reduction in SNAP funding is beginning to make an impact," particularly on lower-income consumers [25]. UNFI's independent-grocer and conventional customer base is the most SNAP-exposed part of the book.
- **Sector/factor regime.** Consumer staples distribution, low-beta (5Y beta 0.81 [6]), a deleveraging/self-help equity rather than a growth or AI-factor name. ## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull.** The quarter itself is a near-lay-up and the market is under-positioned for it. Q4 laps a cyber-crippled comp (adjusted EPS −$0.11, adjusted EBITDA $116M [18]) against an implied guide of $156–176M EBITDA, UNFI has beaten by an average of 29.9% across four quarters [11], and 90-day estimate revisions ran 10 up / 0 down [16]. The structural fix is real and verifiable in the filings: gross margin +20bp, opex −7%, DC productivity +7%, net leverage 3.3x → 2.5x, net debt the lowest since FY2018, $115M of voluntary note prepayment and an active buyback [10][25]. The stock is 23.5% off its high, 10.3% lower over 21 sessions, trades at 13.2x forward earnings [7] and below every published price target [7][13][14], with only 4.5% of float short [39] and literally no options crowd [8]. Both prior quarters that entered with a comparably negative 21-day run-up printed up days (+11.6%, +4.63%) [5]. If FY27 EBITDA is guided anywhere near the Investor Day path (~$740–750M implied by $695M → ~$800M FY28 [23][24]), the name re-rates off a washed-out base.

**Bear.** Consensus adjusted EPS of $0.62 sits at the 85th percentile of management's own implied Q4 band of $0.45–0.65, so the Street already requires a top-of-range quarter [10][11]; and management pre-warned that it was pulling productivity benefits *into* Q3 while accelerating technology and supply-chain investment *into* Q4 [25], which mechanically compresses exactly the quarter being reported. On top of that sits a fuel line-item that management itself flagged, against EIA diesel at $5.599/gal, +50% y/y [29][30]. Then the guide: a CFO who started 2026-08-10 [27][28] must set FY27 against 50% diesel inflation, a stepped-up investment plan, a natural segment whose growth has halved three quarters running (+10.5% → +6.7% → +4.4% [20][21][10]) with the project-work unwind not fully cycled until Q3 FY27 [22], SNAP cuts the CEO has already conceded are biting [25][48], and a second-largest natural customer (Sprouts) both comping −1% and insourcing distribution [49][50][51]. Zacks Rank #3 with a 0.00% Earnings ESP gives no positive-surprise signal [11][15]. Two of the last three reactions were down, and the June print gapped −18.8% [5].

**Base.** UNFI beats on Q4 adjusted EPS and EBITDA — the comp is too easy and the sandbagging habit too consistent for it not to — and the print's outcome is then decided entirely in the FY27 guidance range and the first fifteen minutes of the 8:30 a.m. call. I expect the FY27 guide to be *wide* and *centred at or slightly below* the Investor Day-implied ~$740–750M EBITDA, with fuel and investment cited explicitly, because that is what a first-time CFO does with a wide-error-band cost input. That combination — good quarter, cautious guide — historically produces exactly the violent gap-and-reverse pattern this stock has printed in each of the last three quarters [5]. Given a ~10.6% event-implied move against a 9.7% six-quarter mean realised move, the options are priced roughly fairly, and the honest read on direction is close to a coin flip with a modest downward tilt from the guidance-reset asymmetry.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The single most credible reversal of my mild bearish tilt is **a FY27 adjusted EBITDA guide with a midpoint at or above ~$750M accompanied by an explicit statement that reported wholesale sales grow in FY27 including the project-work drag.** That combination would prove (a) the Investor Day path to ~$800M in FY28 is intact and possibly conservative, (b) the diesel and investment headwinds are being absorbed rather than passed to shareholders, and (c) the new CFO is not resetting. On a stock at 13.2x forward earnings, 23.5% off its high, with 4.5% short interest, no retail crowd and coverage that has not published since June, that guide would likely produce a move toward the top of the implied range — the 2025-09-30 precedent (initial FY26 guide above consensus, **+18.45%**) is the template [26][5]. A cheaper, partial version of the same flip: **Q4 natural-segment growth accelerating back above ~6%**, which would break the three-quarter deceleration and remove the core objection to the FY27 growth claim.

The mirror-image flip against the bull case: an FY27 adjusted EBITDA midpoint below ~$720M, or a refusal to guide FY27 sales growth at all.
