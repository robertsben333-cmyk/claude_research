## ABM

Reported adjusted EPS $1.04 against a roughly $1.01 consensus (a modest beat), record
quarterly revenue of $2.3B (+4.2% YoY), and *narrowed* FY26 adjusted EPS guidance to
$3.95-4.10 from $3.85-4.15 — the floor moved up, not down. FY26 free cash flow guidance
was raised to ~$210M from $185M. Close-to-close: $47.05 → $50.60, **+7.55%**.

The panel called Neutral (consensus -8.9, mixed panel_alignment, disparity 31.2) and
that reads as a hit under the below-expected-move convention: the actual move landed
almost exactly on the panel's own 7.3% unsigned expectation and stayed under the 8.26%
implied move. It is a genuinely close call — 7.55% is a hair above 7.3% — but nothing
about this move should have surprised the panel.

What is more interesting is *why* it went up. The seven-persona panel was itself split
4-2 bearish (fundamental, options, red-team, forensics all -18 to -22 on a "the back-half
EPS math is too aggressive" thesis) against sentiment and macro reading facilities-services
peer strength and an uncrowded setup. The red-team persona's own stated bull case — the
thing that would break their bearish call — was: *"A Q3 print at or above $1.04 with
segment operating margin up year over year, plus a narrowing of FY26 guidance into the
upper half and a third straight quarter of positive FCF."* EPS landed at exactly $1.04
and guidance narrowed with the floor raised. That is close to a direct hit on a specific,
falsifiable mechanism stated four days before the print, not just a lucky direction call.

## UNFI

Reported adjusted EPS $0.69 against a $0.61 consensus (a clear beat), revenue slightly
light at $7.64B against ~$7.69B expected, and issued its first FY2027 guide of $3.00-3.50
adjusted EPS. Close-to-close: $43.93 → $44.93, **+2.28%** — a genuinely quiet reaction to
what the panel treated as the day's higher-conviction, higher-reversal-risk name
(reversal_risk_tier High, red-team reversal risk 72, the day's ceiling).

The panel's own thesis for the risk being asymmetric ("whichever way it breaks, it likely
breaks big," per the early_prediction note) did not play out — this was one of the
smallest reactions in UNFI's own 8-print history (mean abs 9.69%, this print 2.28%), well
under the low end of the panel's own 5.1-19.4% band. The specific reversal case the
red-team flagged (a 15-20% squeeze on an in-line guide plus a sub-2x leverage
confirmation) also did not fire — the "first FY27 guide from a four-week CFO" catalyst
that both the bear and bull cases were built on turned out to be far less binary in
practice than either side modeled.

## What the process got right and wrong

- **Right:** both Neutral calls correctly signaled "no confident directional edge" and
  neither move broke its implied move — the options market priced both prints
  reasonably.
- **Right, and specific:** ABM's red-team persona named the exact bull mechanism (EPS at
  or above $1.04, margin up, guidance floor raised) that then happened. That is a
  finding worth carrying forward, not just a lucky Neutral hit.
- **Wrong:** the single deep-researcher preliminary read leaned bearish on both names
  (-18 ABM, -8 UNFI) four days out, and both were wrong-signed against the actual
  (small) positive moves. On ABM specifically the preliminary read missed the same
  upside case the panel's own red-team later flagged — the panel's diversity of view
  caught a scenario the lone researcher didn't weight.
- **Fixable finding, not a market-was-irrational one:** UNFI's reversal-risk framing
  (High tier, 61.6 consensus reversal risk) implied a fat-tailed, binary outcome that
  did not materialize. A four-week-tenure CFO's first guide is a reasonable reason to
  raise reversal risk, but this is the second name in the ledger (after AI on 09-02)
  where a High-reversal-risk tag preceded one of the quietest reactions in the sample —
  worth watching for a systematic "reversal risk is anti-correlated with actual
  reversal" pattern rather than assuming it is noise.
