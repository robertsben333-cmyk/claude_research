# DELL — Dell Technologies Inc.

**Event confirmed: YES.** Dell issues Q2 FY2027 results after the US close on **Tuesday 1 September 2026**, with the conference call at **3:30 p.m. CDT (4:30 p.m. ET / 20:30 UTC)**; the press release, prepared remarks and guidance presentation are published *before* the call [1][2]. Independently confirmed as "next confirmed earnings date … Tuesday, September 1, 2026, after market close" [3]. A minority of secondary calendars (TipRanks, Investing.com, some Stocktwits syndications) still carry **September 3** — that is stale; the company's own 18 August 2026 release is dispositive [1].

**What this print is about.** Dell has stopped being a PC-and-storage company and become the largest merchant AI-server integrator in the world. Revenue is guided to grow ~50% to $167B in FY27 on ~$60B of AI-optimised servers [4][5]; the stock is up **+262% YTD** [6]. Demand is no longer in dispute — Nvidia's 26 August print (Data Center $89.0B, +117% y/y; Q3 guide $108B) and ~$725B of 2026 hyperscaler capex settled that [7][8]. What *is* in dispute is whether Dell earns anything on it. Company gross margin fell from 21.1% to 17.8% (GAAP) / 18.1% (non-GAAP) year over year, and **ISG operating margin fell from 14.8% in Q4 FY26 to 10.5% in Q1 FY27** while management guided to *sequential improvement* in Q2 [9][10][11]. Into that, the memory-cost shock is now visibly hitting peers: HP Inc. reported 26 August with Personal Systems margin down to 4.6% from 5.2% q/q, PC units −16%, and an explicit warning that memory/storage/CPU costs will pressure margins — the stock fell ~5–9% [12][13]. Conversely Super Micro, the purest AI-server comparable, printed 17.5% gross margin (from 9.9% q/q) and guided FY27 revenue $65–72B versus $54.4B consensus [14]. Dell sits between those two read-throughs, with ~66% of revenue in the SMCI-like ISG bucket and ~33% in the HPQ-like CSG bucket. The print trades on **ISG operating margin and the FY27 raise**, not on headline EPS.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**ISG operating margin — specifically, whether it improves sequentially from the 10.5% printed in Q1 FY27.**

Why this and not EPS:
- Dell's ISG operating margin path is **14.8% (Q4 FY26) → 10.5% (Q1 FY27)**, and **management explicitly guided to "sequential improvement in the segment's operating margin this quarter"** [11]. That is a checkable, pre-committed promise, and it is the exact line where the memory bill lands.
- Q1 FY27 arithmetic: ISG revenue $29.0B, ISG operating income $3.055B = **10.53%** [5]. Company GAAP gross margin $7.782B on $43.842B = **17.75%**, versus $4.937B on $23.378B = **21.12%** a year earlier — a **337bp** compression [9]. Non-GAAP gross margin fell **350bp to 18.1%**, "primarily driven by a shift in mix towards our AI-optimized servers" [10].
- Sell-side framing agrees: consensus models ISG operating margin flat at ~11% through FY27/FY28 versus a prior 13% range, and the bear scenario is a path to ~9.4% [27]. A single point of ISG margin is described as "worth more to the model than several billion of extra revenue" [25] — at $29B+ of quarterly ISG revenue, 100bp ≈ $290m of operating income ≈ ~$0.35 of quarterly EPS on 656m diluted shares (*my arithmetic*).
- Management's stated position is that the compression is transitory — one-time supply-chain costs and aggressive pricing on early Blackwell deals, to be recovered through value engineering, scale and enterprise mix [27]. Jeff Clarke on the Q1 call: "we are repricing it feels like every day," while flagging an "inflationary environment" in DRAM and NAND and warning that persistent component inflation "could eventually slow customer acquisition or defer purchases" [28].

**Secondary metrics the tape will read, in order:** (2) AI server revenue recognised versus the ~$15.5B Street number and versus Q1's $16.1B [17][25]; (3) closing AI backlog versus the record $51.3B and new orders versus $24.4B [4][24]; (4) the FY27 AI-server number versus $60B; (5) CSG operating margin, given the HP Inc. read-through.

**A structurally important detail on the AI-revenue bar (fact, then inference).** Fact: FY27 AI-server guidance is ~$60B and Q1 delivered $16.1B, leaving ~$43.9B across Q2–Q4, i.e. an average of **~$14.6B per quarter — roughly 9% *below* Q1** [25]. Inference: Dell's own guidance embeds a sequential *decline* in AI revenue at a moment when its sole critical supplier just grew Data Center +18% q/q and guided +12% q/q [7]. That is a conspicuously low bar and, on my read, the most likely place for an upside surprise.

- **Segment mix has inverted.** ISG is now 66% of revenue and 72% of segment operating income; AI servers alone are 37% of company revenue [5][29]. Storage — the highest-margin ISG line and, per management, "a key driver of overall ISG profitability" — grew only 8% [5][10].
- **Unit economics.** Nvidia earns ~75% gross margin on the silicon; Dell's ISG operating margin is 10.5% [27][7]. The bull framing (Piper Sandler's James Fish) is that margin *rate* compression is tolerable if gross *profit dollars* still grow — and they did, +57% to $7.95B non-GAAP [29][10]. The bear framing (Damodaran, quoted in the same piece) is that "lower gross margins indicate worse unit economics, and to the extent that this is not temporary, it has to be built into Dell's continuing profitability story" [29].
- **Balance sheet and working capital — the least-discussed risk.** From the Q1 FY27 10-Q [9], versus 30 Jan 2026: **inventories $15.052B vs $10.437B (+44%)**; **accounts receivable $25.854B vs $17.585B (+47%)**; **accounts payable $45.261B vs $33.630B (+35%)**. Operating cash flow of $4.081B was only achieved because the $11.661B increase in payables offset the $8.331B AR build and $4.715B inventory build. Total debt principal $31.413B (short-term $7.550B, long-term $23.611B); cash and restricted cash $11.753B; **total stockholders' equity is a deficit of −$1.404B**; current ratio 0.95, quick ratio 0.61, debt/EBITDA 2.22, interest coverage 7.08 [9][16]. *My inference: the AI-server model is being funded by supplier payables. That works while volumes rise; it inverts violently if orders slow or vendor terms tighten.*
- **Remaining performance obligations $97B** as of 1 May 2026, ~80% expected to be recognised within twelve months; deferred revenue $27.452B [9]. This is a genuinely large forward-visibility number and is distinct from — and larger than — the $51.3B AI backlog.
- **Capital returns / dilution.** Q1 buybacks $1.628B plus $0.537B for tax withholding, dividends $0.464B [9]. TTM buyback yield 5.78%, shareholder yield 6.33%, dividend $2.52/yr (0.55% yield), payout ratio 18.5% [16]. Share count down 5.78% y/y — genuine, sizeable shrinkage. TTM FCF $9.44B ($14.61/sh); consensus FY27 FCF $11.61B [16][26].
- **Financing since the print.** Dell issued **$3.0B of senior unsecured notes on 11–16 June 2026** in three tranches: $1.0B 4.750% due 2031, $750m 5.000% due 2034, $1.25B 5.250% due 2037, for general corporate purposes including potential debt repayment [30][31].
- **Customer concentration.** Evercore names **CoreWeave and SpaceX** as "two of Dell's largest customers," alongside xAI historically [24][32]. Dell counts >5,000 AI-server customers across neocloud, sovereign and enterprise [4][17], but the revenue is not evenly distributed: press analysis has estimated that three customers alone could exceed ~1.7x the *entire* prior-year AI-server business [32]. Dell's 10-Q discloses no single-customer concentration threshold being breached [9]. Dell also secured a **$9.7B Pentagon contract in May 2026** [33].
- **Valuation.** Trailing P/E 36.45, **forward P/E 23.66**, P/S 2.20, P/FCF 31.21, PEG 0.79 [16]. On guided FY27 EPS of $17.90 that is ~25.5x; on Street FY27 EPS of $18.82, ~24.2x (*my arithmetic*).

## 7. Forensics

- **Form 4 activity is heavy but is structural, not signal.** Director **Lynn Vojvodich Radakovich** exercised and sold Class C stock on 22 June ($421.00), 22 July ($413.77) and 24 August 2026 ($438.21), all **pursuant to a Rule 10b5-1 plan adopted 24 March 2026** — i.e. pre-scheduled, not discretionary [44]. The dominant flow is **Silver Lake**, which has been steadily converting Class B into Class C and selling: on 8 July 2026 Silver Lake Partners IV sold 72,994 Class C shares for ~$31.1m at $420.21–$438.00, with affiliated entities selling a further ~63,095 shares (~$26.95m) the same day [45][46]. Company 8-Ks confirm the conversion cadence: 4,237,699 Class B→C shares converted March–April 2026, and a further 3,438,364 across ten trading days in June 2026, leaving **325,046,693 Class C and 44,351,394 Class B outstanding as of 15 June 2026** [47][48]. *Inference: a persistent, price-insensitive, mechanically scheduled seller sits on the tape. It is not information about the quarter, but it is a real supply overhang and it partially explains why the stock has not re-taken the August high.* **No purchases by any insider were found.** No Michael Dell open-market sale was found in the recent Form 4 record.
- **Executive departures.** The relevant one is stale but unresolved: CFO **Yvonne McGill stepped down effective 9 September 2025** after 28 years, with **David Kennedy named interim CFO** and a formal external search launched; the company stated the resignation "is not the result of any disagreements with the company on any matter relating to its financial statements, internal control over financial reporting, operations, policies or practices" [49][50]. Kennedy has since been presenting as CFO on the Q1 FY27 call [10][28]. **Whether the "interim" tag has been removed is unconfirmed** — I could not source a permanent-CFO announcement. *Inference: a company adding $54B of revenue in one year while running an interim-titled CFO is a mild governance flag, not a red one; Kennedy is a 27-year veteran and prior CSG CFO.*
- **Auditor / restatement:** no auditor change, material weakness, restatement or non-reliance 8-K found in the recent filing record [51].
- **8-K cadence and content.** The 8-K flow since the last print is unusually dense but almost entirely mechanical: Class B→C conversion notices (Item 3.02, 20 Apr and 17 Jun), the $3.0B notes offering (10–16 Jun), the annual meeting, and — materially — **the redomestication of Dell Technologies Inc. from Delaware to Texas effective 1 July 2026**, followed by bylaw amendments effective 2 July 2026 electing to be governed by **Texas Business Organizations Code §21.373**, which conditions shareholder derivative suits on an ownership threshold [47][52][53]. *Inference: a governance-negative, litigation-shielding change executed quietly between prints. It has no bearing on the quarter but it is exactly the sort of item a forensic screen should surface.*

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Regime.** This is a momentum/AI-capex factor trade, and the factor is intact but choppier than it was: the 15 July session saw a synchronised AI-hardware drawdown (DELL −14% intraday, HPE −8%, SMCI −5%) on valuation and margin concerns with no company-specific catalyst [40].

**Demand side — unambiguously positive:**
- **Nvidia, 26 August 2026 (Q2 FY27):** revenue **$96.2B** (+18% q/q, +106% y/y); **Data Center $89.0B, +117% y/y**, ~92% of revenue; gross margin 75.0%; **Q3 guide $108.0B** (+$11.8B q/q, with zero China compute assumed) [7]. Dell rose +2.73% on 26 Aug and +1.82% on 27 Aug [6].
- **Hyperscaler capex:** Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Amazon guiding to roughly **$725B of combined 2026 capex, up ~77% from ~$410B in 2025**; Alphabet raised its ceiling at Q2, Meta raised twice (to $125–145B) citing higher component and data-centre costs; Microsoft disclosed ~$80B of Azure orders it cannot fulfil for lack of power [8].
- **Super Micro, 11 August 2026 (Q4 FY26):** net sales **$11.1B** (nearly double y/y), **gross margin 17.5% versus 9.9% the prior quarter and 9.5% a year earlier**, non-GAAP EPS $1.70; **FY27 guide $65–72B versus $54.4B consensus**, Q1 FY27 $14.5–15.5B [14]. *This is the single most bullish peer datum for Dell's margin question: the purest AI-server comparable just demonstrated that scale plus repricing can expand gross margin ~760bp in one quarter.*

**Cost side — unambiguously negative:**
- **Memory.** Contract DRAM prices rose **80–90% q/q in Q1 2026**; 64GB server RDIMM went from ~$450 contract in Q4 to **>$900 in Q1** and was expected to breach $1,000 in Q2; 256GB of DDR5 ECC RDIMM that cost $800–1,200 in early 2025 now costs $2,000–3,500. Samsung, SK hynix and Micron have diverted wafer capacity to HBM, which earns 3–5x the revenue per wafer [56]. Memory now runs **~35% of a PC's bill of materials, up from 15–18%** [56]. Cisco was flagged for potential 200bp of margin erosion from the same cause [56].
- **HP Inc., 26 August 2026 (Q3 FY26):** revenue $15.7B (+12.5%), non-GAAP EPS $0.83 (+10.7%, including an $0.11 tariff-refund benefit), FY26 EPS and FCF guidance *raised* — **and the stock still fell ~5–9%** because PC units fell 16% y/y and **Personal Systems operating margin fell to 4.6% from 5.2% q/q**, with management guiding to continued input-cost pressure in H2 [12][13]. *This is the direct analogue for Dell's CSG, and it is the reason DELL fell 3.39% on 28 August.*
- **HPE reports Q3 FY26 on 2 September 2026 — the morning after Dell.** Guided revenue $11.5–12.1B, non-GAAP EPS $0.88–0.93, with AI orders/backlog and ~21.6% margins in focus [57]. *Inference: a same-window peer print means the sector read-through is two-sided within 24 hours; a good Dell number that is followed by a weak HPE could be partially given back.*
- **Tariffs / trade.** A **Section 232 25% ad valorem duty on certain advanced computing semiconductors and derivative parts took effect 15 January 2026**, keyed to Tensor-Processing-Performance and DRAM-bandwidth thresholds — an unresolved cost variable for US server assembly [58]. Fresh semiconductor-tariff headlines were cited as a driver of DELL's 28 August decline [22].
- **FX.** Dell carries $15.28B notional of FX contracts and $6.81B of interest-rate contracts; non-designated FX derivatives produced a **$101m loss** in Q1 FY27 versus a $298m gain a year earlier, running through "Interest and other, net" [9]. *Note as a quality-of-earnings item: "Interest and other, net" swung from −$82m to +$292m y/y, a $374m pre-tax tailwind — roughly $0.45 of pre-tax EPS — that is not operating income [9].*

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Dell's own FY27 AI-server guidance of ~$60B implies **~$14.6B per quarter for Q2–Q4, about 9% below the $16.1B it just printed** [25], and consensus only carries ~$15.5B for Q2 [17][11] — while Dell's sole binding supplier grew Data Center 18% sequentially and guided another 12% [7], and hyperscalers are adding ~$725B of 2026 capex [8]. Supply, not demand, is the constraint, and supply is loosening. A print of $16–17B of AI revenue on a backlog above $51.3B, with the FY27 AI number raised above $60B, is the exact shape of the last two reports, which delivered **+21.9% and +32.8%** [6]. Super Micro has already demonstrated that this cohort can expand gross margin 760bp in one quarter through repricing [14], and Clarke told the market in May that Dell had been "repricing it feels like every day" [28] — i.e. Dell saw the memory bill early. Positioning does not look stretched: −7.79% from the closing high, RSI 52.5, retail sentiment bearish for ten days on extremely low volume, put-tilted flow, short interest up into the print, and a straddle priced at 11.4% against an 8-quarter realised mean of 11.6% and a Schaeffer's Volatility Scorecard of 91 [6][16][20][21][35]. Evercore expects a beat *and* a raise [24]. Forward P/E is 23.7 on 83% EPS growth (PEG 0.79) [16].

**Bear case.** The stock is +262% YTD [6] and the Street model is already **above** company guidance — FY27 revenue $173.3B vs $167B guided and FY27 EPS $18.82 vs $17.90 guided [26] — so Dell must raise beyond the raise the market has already booked. Q2 consensus EPS of $4.91–4.95 sits 1.5–3.1% above Dell's own $4.80 guide [17][20][23][4]. Meanwhile the margin evidence flowing in over the last week is negative on Dell's *other* segment: HP Inc. beat, raised, and still fell ~5–9% because PC units fell 16% and Personal Systems margin compressed to 4.6% [12][13]; IDC has Q2 PC shipments −4.9%, the first decline in nine quarters, and 2026 full-year at −11.3% on memory prices up ~130% [42][43]. Dell's CSG is ~33% of revenue [5]. On the ISG side the run-rate is **14.8% → 10.5%** and management pre-committed to sequential improvement [11] — a promise that is far easier to miss than to beat when 64GB RDIMM contract prices have gone from $450 to above $900 [56]. Under the hood, inventories are +44% q/q, receivables +47%, and the whole build is funded by a $11.7B increase in payables against a −$1.4B equity deficit and a 0.95 current ratio [9][16]. Three of the five most recent sell-side actions came from **Hold**-rated houses (Morgan Stanley $434, UBS $455, Deutsche Bank initiating at Hold $480 the day before the print) whose targets sit at or below the current price [26]. And a scheduled, price-insensitive Silver Lake seller is converting and selling into every rally [45][46][47].

**Base case (my read).** Dell beats on revenue and EPS — the four-quarter beat streak, the 21-to-0 upward revision count, and a self-set AI-revenue bar that embeds a sequential decline make that close to the modal outcome [23][25][17]. It raises FY27, probably to something in the $172–178B revenue / $18.50–19.25 EPS zone and lifts the AI-server number above $60B. The reaction then hinges entirely on one line: if ISG operating margin prints above 10.5% — as management guided — the market reads "Dell can pass memory costs through," the SMCI template applies, and the stock trades meaningfully higher, though I doubt it repeats a 20–30% move from a 23.7x base with a Hold-heavy sell-side. If ISG margin is flat or lower, the HPQ template applies instead, the "$51.3B of backlog and nothing to show for it" narrative wins, and an 11% straddle is not enough to contain the downside. I weight the first outcome modestly higher because the supplier data (Nvidia +18% q/q) and the closest comparable (SMCI +760bp gross margin) both point the same way, and because the market has already spent three weeks de-risking the position. **Preliminary direction score +20, probability of an up move 57%, conviction Medium.** Reversal risk is high in both directions: this is a name with a 32.76% max realised earnings move in the last eight quarters [6].

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The consensus view going in is "demand is proven, so this is a beat-and-raise." **The single most credible reversal is an ISG operating margin that is flat or down sequentially from 10.5%, accompanied by management conceding that memory cost pass-through lags by a quarter or more.** Concretely: ISG operating income below ~$3.4B on ISG revenue of ~$31–32B, i.e. a margin at or under 10.5%, plus any language on the call that the $60B FY27 AI-server number is now gross-margin-dilutive at the group level or that CSG margin is being defended by shedding entry-level volume. That combination converts the whole story from "Dell is monetising the AI build" to "Dell is a pass-through integrator warehousing $15B of inventory and $26B of receivables at 10% segment margins, funded by payables, at 24x forward earnings." It would validate the three Hold-rated banks whose targets sit below spot, and it is the version of the print that the HP Inc. read-through, the IDC PC data and the DRAM contract curve all currently argue for.

The mirror-image reversal, less likely but higher-magnitude: ISG margin back above 12% *with* AI revenue above $17B and backlog above $60B. That would prove the SMCI margin-expansion template applies to Dell, break the "$60B is the ceiling" framing, and force the Hold-rated houses to capitulate on both estimates and ratings at once.
