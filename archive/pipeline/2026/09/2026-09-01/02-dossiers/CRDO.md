# CRDO — Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd

**Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended 1 August 2026), reported after the US close on Tuesday 1 September 2026, call at 2:00 p.m. Pacific.** `event_confirmed: true` — confirmed from the company's own scheduling release [1] and corroborated by three independent data providers [2][4][14].

**What this print is about.** Credo is no longer being asked whether AI connectivity demand exists — Nvidia's 26 August blowout, Marvell's raised FY27/FY28 targets and combined hyperscaler capex guidance of $720–745bn have settled that [28][25][29]. It is being asked whether a company trading at 30.5x EV/Sales and 36.7x forward earnings [3] can deliver a surprise rather than merely a beat, and whether the *second* growth engine — a >$600m FY27 optical portfolio bought partly through the $1.3bn DustPhotonics deal — is ramping on schedule and without wrecking the ~68% gross margin that the AEC franchise earns [5][24][36]. The reflex is well-documented: Credo has beaten consensus in each of its last two prints and sold off both times (−21.6% in March, −4.8% in June) [4][17][18], and in the last four weeks Marvell (−10%), Lumentum, Coherent and Fabrinet have all been sold on record results [25][33]. The offsetting fact is that the "priced for perfection" condition that produced those two sell-offs is materially weaker now: the stock enters this print 26.7% below its 22 June all-time closing high and below its 50-day moving average [2][3]. The single most striking anchor in this dossier is that the options market is pricing a ~12% move against an eight-quarter median realised move of ~22% [4].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**It is not Q1 EPS. It is the Q2 FY27 revenue guide together with the non-GAAP gross-margin guide, read as a joint test of the optical ramp.**

Why:
- Credo told the Street on 1 June that FY27 revenue grows **>80% y/y** with **>$600m from optics**, that optical DSPs, silicon-photonics PICs and ZeroFlap optics will *each* exceed $100m, that optics is **roughly half of the year's absolute dollar growth**, and that **the ramp accelerates in the second half** [5][36]. A back-half-loaded plan means the Q2 guide is the first hard checkpoint on it.
- The Street has already modelled the shape: **~$515m in Q2 building to ~$810m by Q4 FY27** [13]. A Q2 guide with a midpoint below ~$510m breaks the curve that supports the FY27 number, regardless of what Q1 printed.
- Margin is the second half of the same test. **The March 2026 −21.6% reaction was triggered specifically by a gross-margin guide** — Q4 GM guided to 64–66% against 68.6% reported, alongside customer concentration of 39/32/17% [18][20]. Management then delivered 68.3% [5], so the fear proved wrong, but the market has shown it will sell the margin line first and ask questions later. Optics carries structurally lower margin than AEC and DustPhotonics adds opex (non-GAAP opex guided $86–90m vs $81.7m) [5]; the mix shift is the acknowledged bear mechanism [rf. §5].
- Secondary but real: **the customer-concentration disclosure.** Q4 FY26 had four customers each >10%, top three at 34/27/16%, including a *new* customer crossing 10%, and expansion into "NeoCloud" operators [36][13]. Top four ≈ 87% of Q4 revenue [13]. Whether the fifth and sixth names are scaling is the durability question.

**How I know:** all four points are from company disclosure (press release, 10-K, earnings call) plus the documented cause of the March reaction. This is a fact-plus-inference item: the facts are cited; the ranking of the Q2 guide as *the* metric is my judgement.

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

**Scale and trajectory (all company-sourced):**
- FY2026 revenue **$1.335bn**, +206% y/y; non-GAAP net income $661.5m (+>5x); non-GAAP diluted EPS $3.46; GAAP diluted EPS $2.51 [5][8].
- Q4 FY26 (ended 2 May 2026): revenue **$437.0m**, +7.4% q/q, +157% y/y; GAAP GM 68.2%, non-GAAP GM 68.3%; GAAP net income $169.1m, non-GAAP $226.7m; non-GAAP EPS $1.16 vs FactSet $1.02 [5].
- Beat pattern versus its own guide is large and repeated: Q3 FY26 guided $335–345m, pre-announced $404–408m, printed **$407.0m** (+51.9% q/q, +201.5% y/y) — roughly 19% through the top of the guide [6][19].

**Cash and balance sheet:** cash and short-term investments **$1.4bn**; cash and equivalents $1.2bn and working capital $1.8bn at 2 May 2026; total debt only **$25.45m** against $1.44bn cash [5][3]. Q4 operating cash flow **$182.2m** (+$16m q/q), capex **$4.8m**, free cash flow **$177.5m** [FCF/OCF: Zacks summary of the FY26 filings]. Net cash ≈ $1.4bn on a $42.2bn cap — the balance sheet is not a risk factor here.

**Buyback / dilution:** **no share repurchase programme and no plan to authorise one** [same source]. Dilution is the live issue instead: DustPhotonics was $750m cash plus ~0.92m shares upfront, **plus up to ~3.21m contingent shares** on milestones [24]; and the CEO's May 2026 special PSU grant is up to **1,437,000 shares** [21][22]. Combined potential issuance ≈ 5.5m shares ≈ 3.0% of shares outstanding.

**Segment/product mix — the thing that actually changed this year:**
- FY26 growth was almost entirely AEC: **AEC solutions contributed over 99% of the FY26 revenue increase** [aggregated preview coverage].
- FY27 is designed to be different: optics ≈ half the absolute dollar growth, >$600m, back-half weighted, with **ZeroFlap optics the largest optical contributor because of three-digit ASPs versus two-digit ASPs for discrete DSPs and PICs** [36]. Higher ASP, but lower margin than a chip — this is the margin question in one sentence.
- Product cadence supports it: Cardinal 1.6T optical DSP, Robin 400G/800G optical DSP family, ZeroFlap optical transceivers, PCIe retimers, OmniConnect [46][2].

**M&A:** DustPhotonics announced **13 April 2026**, **completed 28 May 2026** [23] — i.e. it closed *inside* the quarter being reported, four days before the Q4 print, so Q1 FY27 is the first quarter carrying it (partially) and the guidance issued on 1 June already contemplated it. Total headline value ~$1.3bn cash and stock [24]. It gives Credo a vertically integrated stack across SerDes → DSP → silicon photonics → system integration [24].

**Litigation cleared:** Credo settled its AEC disputes with **TE Connectivity (27 March 2026, all suits dismissed, terms confidential)** [39] and separately with **Molex** [40]. The IP overhang on the AEC franchise has been removed; that is a quiet positive that has not been much discussed.

**Customer concentration — the standing risk:** top 10 ≈ 90% of FY26 revenue; Q3 FY26 top three at 39/32/17%; Q4 FY26 top three at 34/27/16% with four names above 10%, top four ≈ 87% [8][18][36][13]. Concentration is falling at the margin but remains extreme.

**What is at stake:** if optics ramps as guided, FY27 consensus of **$2.47bn revenue and $6.17 EPS** [reference: stockanalysis forecast page] is achievable and the CEO's first PSU tranche ($2.5bn revenue / $244.70 share price) is close to live [21]. If optics slips a quarter, the >80% FY27 guide is arithmetically hard and the multiple has no support.

**Demand-side facts:**

**Reaction-side facts — this is the part that should worry a long:**
- **Marvell fell ~7% after hours and ~10% the following session** despite the beat and the raise; the read was that it was "priced for perfection and needed a surprise, not just a beat" [25].

- **Lumentum, Coherent and Fabrinet all posted record results and all declined post-announcement**, attributed to sell-the-news and programmatic selling [33].
- Credo's own last two prints, both beats, both negative [17][18].
- Credo's 18 August −13.03% came with **no company news at all**, in a chip-led selloff with the **30-year Treasury yield near a 19-year high** [35]. Rate sensitivity for a 36.7x forward-PE, 3.23-beta name is real and non-trivial.

**Timing overhang.** **Broadcom reports Q3 FY26 on 2 September 2026** — the session *after* Credo's print [31]. Any Credo long is holding through both events. My inference: this asymmetrically discourages pressing longs on a good Credo number, because the same-week AVGO risk sits on top of it; it also means a poor Credo reaction can be partially rescued the next day if AVGO's $16bn AI number lands.

**FX / commodity / rates:** Credo is USD-reporting with a Cayman domicile, TSMC-based supply chain, no disclosed material FX or commodity exposure. The relevant macro sensitivity is **long-duration equity duration versus the long end of the curve**, evidenced directly on 18 August [35]. Amazon flagged **higher memory cost** as a driver of its capex raise [29] — a general BOM-inflation read for the AI hardware chain, not specific to Credo.

**Technology read-through, both directions:** Nvidia continues to use **copper for scale-up inside NVL72/NVL144 racks** while expanding silicon photonics for scale-out and scale-across — Vera Rubin NVL576 is described as rack-internal copper linked rack-to-rack by optics [46]. That is structurally supportive of Credo's dual AEC + optics positioning and argues against a near-term copper-displacement shock. Credo's neutrality — it does not sell switch ASICs, so a hyperscaler can pair a Broadcom or Astera switch with Credo cable without deepening vendor lock-in — is the standing competitive argument for why it keeps winning sockets [rf. §5 competitive coverage].

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Credo has out-delivered its own guide by a wide margin in the recent past — Q3 FY26 guided $335–345m and printed $407.0m, ~19% through the top [6][19] — and Q4 beat FactSet on both lines [17]. The demand backdrop into this quarter was the strongest on record: hyperscaler 2026 capex guidance rose to $720–745bn [29], Nvidia printed +106% y/y and rallied 8.7% [28], Marvell raised FY27 to ~$12bn and FY28 to ~$18bn [25], and Astera raised Q3 guidance by ~50% [27] — all read-throughs for the same AI back-end network Credo sells into. Crucially, the condition that produced the last two sell-offs is absent: the stock enters this print **26.7% below its 22 June closing high and below its 50-day MA** [2][3], with short interest an inert 3.3% of shares out [3], so there is far less long positioning to unwind than in March or June. DustPhotonics closed 28 May, inside the quarter [23], giving the optical line a revenue contribution the guide may have set conservatively. If Q1 lands near $500m with GM at 68%+ and Q2 is guided above $530m, the FY27 >80%/>$600m-optical frame is validated ahead of schedule, the $250 call cluster [15] gets in the money and the 24.6% mean realised move works to the upside — a 12% implied would be badly under-priced [4].

**Bear case.** The single most repeated event in this name's recent history is *beat, then sell*: −21.6% in March and −4.8% in June, both on beats [4][17][18], and the March move was triggered specifically by a gross-margin guide [18][20]. The same reaction function is currently operating across the entire comp set — Marvell beat, raised FY27 and FY28, and fell ~10% [25]; Lumentum, Coherent and Fabrinet all posted records and all fell [33]; Astera spiked and gave back 12% [27]. Estimates for the quarter have **not moved in 30 days**, Zacks ESP is **0.00%** and the Zacks Rank is **#3 (Hold)** [14] — there is no revision momentum, and consensus sits exactly on the guide midpoint with no cushion. Valuation leaves nothing: **30.5x EV/Sales, 36.7x forward PE against a 13.5x industry average** [3][14]. The margin mix is deteriorating by design — optics is roughly half of FY27's absolute dollar growth [36] at lower margin than AEC, non-GAAP opex is guided up to $86–90m from $81.7m [5], and the ramp is explicitly back-half-loaded [36], which means a Q2 guide that merely meets ~$515m [13] reads as a delay. Customer concentration remains ~87% of revenue in four accounts [13][36]. Insiders sold heavily through July at $209–216 [9][37][38], no buyback exists to absorb the ~5.5m shares of potential DustPhotonics-earnout and CEO-PSU issuance [24][21], and **Broadcom reports the very next evening** [31], giving anyone tempted to press a long a reason to wait. With beta 3.23 [3] and the 30-year near a 19-year high [35], a soft guide into this tape does not stop at −10%.

**Base case (mine).** Credo beats its own guide on revenue and EPS — the evidence for that is the guidance-beat cadence plus a demand backdrop that has strengthened, not weakened, since 1 June [6][19][25][27][28][29] — and gross margin comes in at or near the top of the 67–69% guide, as it did last quarter when the feared compression did not materialise [5]. The stock's direction then turns almost entirely on the Q2 FY27 guide relative to ~$515m [13] and on whether management reaffirms ">80% FY27 / >$600m optical" without hedging the second-half weighting. I lean **marginally positive**, for one reason that is structural rather than fundamental: **the run-up that fuelled both prior sell-offs is not there this time.** The stock has already given back 26.7% from its high [2][3] and has done its de-rating in advance. Against that, the peer reaction function over the last four weeks is uniformly punitive and the revision data is flat [14][25][27][33]. Net: a small positive tilt with low conviction, and a realised move that I expect to be **larger than the ~12% implied** in either direction — that magnitude view is materially higher-conviction than the direction view.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The consensus view is "great company, priced for perfection, beat is assumed, guide is the risk." The most credible concrete reversal is **a Q2 FY27 revenue guide with a midpoint at or above roughly $560m, accompanied by non-GAAP gross margin guided at 68%+ and management explicitly raising the FY27 frame above ">80%" — with the optical contribution described as pulling *forward* rather than remaining second-half-weighted.** That combination would do three things at once: it would break the Street's $515m Q2 modelling [13] by ~9%, it would kill the mix-driven margin-dilution thesis that has been the core structural bear argument since August [35], and it would convert the ">$600m optical" claim from a promise into a run-rate. Precedent exists in this exact comp set — Astera Labs guided Q3 to $540–560m against prior guidance of $355–365m and traded +12.6% on the day [27] — and Credo has itself delivered a guide-shattering quarter before, needing an 8-K to pre-announce it [6].

The symmetric reversal, which would flip the *bull* view: a Q2 guide at or below ~$500m, or a non-GAAP GM guide with a midpoint below 67%, or a top-customer disclosure showing the largest account shrinking sequentially. Any one of those reproduces the March 2026 setup — where results and guidance *both* topped estimates and the stock still fell 21.6% [20][18] — and on a 24.6% mean realised move [4] there is no reason to expect a gentle version of it.
