# GIII — G-III Apparel Group, Ltd.

**What this print is about.** G-III is two years into a self-inflicted, fully telegraphed revenue amputation: the Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger licences — 28.0% of FY26 net sales — are expiring on a staggered basis that began 31-Dec-2024 and runs through 31-Dec-2027, taking roughly $470 million of sales out of FY2027 alone [S13][S14]. Nobody is surprised by the amputation. What the market is actually underwriting on 2 September is whether the *replacement* engine — owned brands (57% of FY26 sales), a $102.7m tariff-refund windfall, a 400bp full-year gross-margin expansion promise, and a $500m acquisition of the Marc Jacobs operating business — holds together well enough for management to reaffirm the FY27 non-GAAP EPS guide of $2.15–2.25 [S13]. Three facts make this a guidance event rather than an earnings event. First, Q2 is a rounding-error quarter: consensus non-GAAP EPS is $0.23–0.25 against a full-year $2.24, and the October quarter alone carries ~76% of the year's earnings [S6][S9]. Second, G-III's own June guidance explicitly *excludes* Marc Jacobs, which is expected to close in fiscal Q3 (i.e. now) and is expected to be dilutive for the first twelve months — so the guide the Street is anchored to is mechanically stale [S13][S18]. Third, G-III's earnings-day reaction distribution is violently asymmetric: over the last eight prints the six positive reactions averaged +7.7% while the two negative ones averaged −15.0%, and every one of the big down days (−18.65% on 6-Jun-2025, −11.43% on 12-Mar-2026) was a guidance event, not a results event [S11][S12][S17][S14]. The stock has drifted from $36.40 on 3-Aug to $32.59 on 31-Aug (−10.5%) and fell 2.6% on ~2.0x normal volume in the last session before the print [S12][S2].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**It is the FY2027 non-GAAP guidance package — specifically the Q3 FY27 outlook and whether the $2.15–2.25 EPS / $178–182m adjusted EBITDA range is reaffirmed *after* absorbing Marc Jacobs.**

Why this and not EPS:

- **Arithmetic.** Q3 consensus is $1.71 of a $2.24 year [S6]. In FY26 the October quarter did $988.6m of a $2.96bn year (33% of sales) and $1.90 non-GAAP of a $2.61 year (73% of earnings) [S15][S14]. The seasonality is extreme and it is all in the quarter G-III will guide tomorrow.
- **History.** Both of G-III's double-digit down days in the last two years were pure guidance events:
  - **6-Jun-2025, −18.65%.** Q1 FY26 beat ("both exceeding guidance"), but the company *reaffirmed sales and withdrew* its FY26 net income, non-GAAP net income and adjusted EBITDA guidance, citing ~$135.0m of unmitigated tariff cost [S17]. The quarter was fine; the guide vanished; the stock lost a fifth of its value.
  - **12-Mar-2026, −11.43%.** FY26 non-GAAP EPS came in at $2.61 against the company's own December guide of $2.80–2.90, on a $45.0m after-tax impairment and $17.5m of Saks Global bad debt, and the initial FY27 guide of $2.00–2.10 was set below the FY26 result [S14][S15].
  - Contrast: **4-Sep-2025, +1.88%** on a *massive* quarterly beat ($0.25 vs a $0.02–0.12 guide, $613.3m vs $570m) — because the same release cut FY26 sales guidance to ~$3.02bn and flagged $75m of unmitigated tariff impact "primarily weighted to the second half" [S16]. The stock then fell a further 6.04% the next session [S11]. **A huge beat with a cut guide bought one day of +1.9% and gave it back the next.**
- **The Marc Jacobs wedge.** Guidance excludes an acquisition that is expected to close in the very quarter being guided, is expected to be dilutive for twelve months, and consumes ~$500m of a company whose entire market cap is $1.37bn [S18][S13][S3]. Consensus of $2.24 sits at the top of an ex-MJ range. *[My inference]*: unless management defines the dilution tightly, the mechanical path is a guide-down.

**What the market expects for it, and how I know:** Consensus FY27 non-GAAP EPS of $2.24 and Q3 of $1.71 (Nasdaq, 2 estimates, zero revisions in four weeks) [S6] tell you the Street is modelling a straight reaffirm with no MJ drag. That is the expectation. Every deviation from it is a downside deviation.

The $1.50 GAAP EPS is not real cash earnings. It contains a **$102.7m pre-tax / $77.9m after-tax ($1.75/share) benefit** from the expected recovery of IEEPA tariffs [S13]. On the call, management quantified the mechanics: a **$140m receivable** recorded, **~$120m** taken out of cost of goods sold (relating to tariffs expensed in FY26), and **~$20m** of inventory carrying-value reduction that will flow through COGS **during the balance of FY2027** [S26]. **What is at stake in Q2:** the ~$20m inventory benefit is a real H2 gross-margin tailwind, and management guided **Q2 gross margin expansion of ~450bp** and **full-year ~400bp** (raised from an initial 300bp) [S26]. If Q2 gross margin lands materially below that, the whole 400bp full-year bridge — and with it the $178–182m EBITDA guide — is in question.

### Material weakness in internal control — unremediated, with an adverse auditor opinion

This is the most substantive forensic finding and it is not widely discussed.

- In the FY2026 10-K (filed 24-Mar-2026), management identified **within the KLH (Karl Lagerfeld Holding) subsidiary a material weakness in the operating effectiveness of controls over information technology general controls (ITGCs)** supporting financial reporting. Automated and manual business-process controls dependent on those ITGCs were **also deemed ineffective** [S20].
- **Management concluded G-III "did not maintain effective internal control over financial reporting as of January 31, 2026."** [S20]
- **Ernst & Young LLP issued an adverse opinion on the effectiveness of internal control over financial reporting as of 31-Jan-2026** [S20].
- Management states the weakness "did not result in any material misstatements" and that post-closing procedures support the fairness of the statements [S20].
- **Still unremediated at the most recent 10-Q.** In the Q1 FY27 10-Q (period ended 30-Apr-2026), the CEO and CFO concluded that **disclosure controls and procedures "were not effective"** because of the same material weakness. KLH is disclosed as **~9% of total FY26 net sales** [S19].
- Remediation plan: additional IT risk-assessment procedures, control enhancements, and training; "will not be considered remediated until the remediation plan is complete, and controls have been operational for a sufficient period of time and successfully tested" [S20].
- **No restatement.** No Item 4.02 (non-reliance) 8-K appears anywhere in the filing history [S21].
- Auditor is **Ernst & Young LLP**; no auditor change [S20].

*[My inference]* This is a contained, IT-general-controls weakness at a 9%-of-revenue European subsidiary, not a revenue-recognition or reserve problem. But it is an *adverse* ICFR opinion that is now two quarters old and still open, and it sits alongside a $102.7m tariff-recovery estimate and a $140m receivable that are, by construction, judgemental. ## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

### Bull case

G-III is a $1.37bn market cap with $394m of net cash, $15.4m of debt, and an EV of $1.27bn against guided adjusted EBITDA of $178–182m — **4.85x EV/EBITDA and 0.75x book** [S3][S4][S13][S19]. The owned-brand engine is genuinely working: Donna Karan grew ~40% in Q1 with its site up ~60%, dkny.com up over 40%, Karl Lagerfeld high-single-digit with the best sell-throughs in the portfolio, and comparable store sales positive across KLP, Donna Karan and DKNY [S26]. Gross margin ex-tariff expanded 350bp in Q1 and management guided **+450bp in Q2 and +400bp for the year, raised from 300bp** [S13][S26]. The company sandbags its quarterly guide and has beaten it in four of the last five quarters, once by $0.13 against a $0.02–0.12 range [S16][S17][S13][S15]. Twenty-one per cent of the float is short with **18.2 days to cover** and the short base grew 25% between May and August [S4][S5] — an unambiguous reaffirm on a name this thin is a squeeze, and the two largest up-moves in the sample (+22.0%, +10.4%) both came out of exactly this setup [S12]. All three active analysts carry $38–40 targets, with Telsey reiterating $38 on 27-Aug-2026 [S27]. The Section 122 → Section 301 transition has, on the published rate schedules, left apparel tariffs at or below the ~15% IEEPA-equivalent level that guidance assumed, with a textile/apparel TRQ programme coming online around 1 September [S32][S35] — so the tariff line, which broke this stock in June 2025, may now be a tailwind. And Marc Jacobs, with a licence running to December 2041 plus ten five-year renewals and a stated $1bn revenue ambition, is a credible answer to the "what replaces Calvin Klein?" question that has kept the multiple at 4.9x [S19][S26].

### Bear case

Consensus sits at the **top of company guidance at both the quarter ($0.25 vs $0.15–0.25) and the year ($2.24 vs $2.15–2.25)** [S6][S13] — the Street has left itself no room, and G-III's own November-to-March experience shows what happens when the annual number breaks (FY26 non-GAAP EPS of $2.61 against a $2.80–2.90 December guide; stock −11.4%) [S14][S15]. **The guide the Street is anchored to is mechanically stale**: it excludes a ~$500m acquisition, expected to close in this very quarter, that management has said will be **dilutive for twelve months** [S13][S18]. Q3 carries ~76% of the year's non-GAAP EPS [S6], so all of the risk is concentrated in the one number management guides tomorrow. The buyback that returned $49.8m in FY26 has been **switched off for all of FY27** to fund the deal, with zero shares repurchased in Q1 despite a fresh 10m-share authorisation [S14][S19][S26]. Roughly 43% of revenue runs through Macy's, TJX and Ross [S20]; TJX's Marmaxx comped **+1%** and guided Q3 two cents light [S39], Macy's is down 15.4% in a month into a 10-Sep print [S31], and Burlington fell 7.6% on a revenue miss then another 10.4% on a downgrade the session before G-III reports [S31][S40]. The entire wholesale-brand cohort has been de-rated 9–21% in a month — **Tapestry beat and lost 16.5% purely on guidance** [S44][S45] — which is the exact failure mode G-III is exposed to. The company is still operating under an **unremediated material weakness with an adverse E&Y ICFR opinion**, and its CEO and CFO concluded disclosure controls were **not effective** in the most recent 10-Q [S19][S20], while a judgemental **$140m tariff receivable** sits on the balance sheet and drove operating cash flow to **−$2.0m** versus +$93.8m a year earlier [S19][S26]. The CEO's three-year price/TSR PSU vested at maximum on 18-Aug-2026, two weeks before the print [S22]. And the reaction distribution is brutally skewed: the last two down prints averaged **−15.0%** against **+7.7%** for the six up prints [S12].

### Base case

*[My inference]* G-III beats its own Q2 guide — it almost always does — with sales at or a little above $570m and non-GAAP EPS at or slightly above $0.25, helped by the ~450bp gross-margin expansion and the tariff-related inventory benefit [S13][S26]. That is a *match*, not a beat, against a Street pinned at the top of the range. The stock then trades on two things: the Q3 outlook and how Marc Jacobs is folded in. My central expectation is that management reaffirms the FY27 sales number (~$2.71bn) and either reaffirms the ex-MJ non-GAAP EPS range while quantifying MJ dilution separately, or issues a revised range including MJ that sits below $2.15–2.25. In the current tape — where Tapestry beat and lost 16.5%, TJX raised the year and fell, and Burlington beat EPS by 35% and fell — a technically-clean-but-optically-lower guide gets sold. Offsetting that, 21% of float short with 18 days to cover and three analysts at $38–40 puts a real squeeze bid under any unambiguous reaffirm, and the tariff regime has probably moved in G-III's favour since June, removing the single mechanism that caused the −18.65% day. **Net: modest downside skew, wide distribution, low conviction in direction and high conviction in magnitude.** I would expect the realised move to be larger than the 6-quarter median of 4.5% and to sit closer to the 7–10% band my derived implied move brackets. My direction score of −20 with a 42% probability of an up move reflects a distribution where the up cases cluster around +3% to +6% and the down cases have a fat tail to −12%.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

**The most credible reversal, stated concretely: G-III reaffirms FY27 non-GAAP EPS at $2.15–2.25 and adjusted EBITDA at $178–182m *including* Marc Jacobs, and quantifies the first-year dilution as immaterial or fully offset — on the grounds that the post-24-July Section 301 tariff regime is running *below* the IEEPA-equivalent rates the June guidance assumed, and that the textile/apparel TRQ programme starting ~1 September provides further relief on Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia and Malaysia sourcing** [S13][S18][S32][S35].

That single sentence flips everything. It removes the mechanical guide-down that consensus is exposed to, converts a stale guide into a de-risked one, and lands on a float that is 21% short with 18.2 days to cover [S4][S5] and a sell-side that already carries $38–40 targets against a $32.59 stock [S27]. There is direct precedent for the magnitude: on 5-Sep-2024 and 10-Dec-2024, into a similar short base, G-III printed **+22.0%** and **+10.4%** [S12].

**Two secondary reversal paths:**

- **Q2 gross margin materially exceeds the +450bp guide** with owned-brand growth above the high-single-digit FY plan [S26], demonstrating that the go-forward portfolio can carry the CK/TH cliff faster than modelled. That would re-rate a 4.85x EV/EBITDA business toward its peers.
- **A confirmed, closed Marc Jacobs transaction with disclosed standalone revenue and EBITDA.** The market has never seen Marc Jacobs' financials as a standalone. Anything that makes a $500m outlay look like it was bought at a sensible multiple against a $1bn revenue ambition [S26] converts the deal from a balance-sheet risk into an asset.

**The single fact that would most cleanly invalidate my negative lean before the print:** evidence that the two sell-side models already embed Marc Jacobs dilution. I could not source estimate detail at that level, and if consensus of $2.24 is post-dilution rather than pre-dilution, my central bear mechanism disappears.
