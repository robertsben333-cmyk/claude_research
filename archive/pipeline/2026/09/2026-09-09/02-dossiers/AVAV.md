# AVAV — AeroVironment, Inc.

**What this print is about.** This is not an EPS print. AeroVironment reports fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended 1 August 2026) after the close on 9 September 2026 into a stock that has lost roughly two-thirds of its value from its 52-week high of $417.86 and closed at $148.78 on 8 September, within ~10% of its 52-week low [1][6]. The reported quarter is the one management explicitly pre-warned would be the weakest of the year — on the Q4 FY26 call the CFO guided a 45%/55% first-half/second-half revenue split with Q1 at only ~45% of H1, and the ~$1.7bn SCAR programme that was terminated for convenience leaves roughly a $30m per-quarter revenue hole [12][13][21]. Against that, the last seven days have delivered the strongest news flow in a year: a $464.8m U.S. Army Enduring-High Energy Laser (E-HEL) award on 2 September — the first-ever U.S. production contract for a directed-energy weapon — and a first international LOCUST order worth more than $50m on 8 September [17][18][19][30]. Both landed *after* the quarter closed, so neither is in the reported bookings; both are exactly what management will point at when it addresses FY27 guidance. Layered underneath is an unresolved forensic overhang: an Item 4.02 non-reliance 8-K filed 22 June 2026, an unremediated material weakness in internal control over goodwill impairment, and a pending securities-fraud class action [7][8][22][23]. The print therefore resolves a single question — does the forward order narrative overwhelm a deliberately soft quarter and an accounting cloud, or not.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Bookings and funded backlog — and, tied to it, whether FY27 revenue guidance is raised on the E-HEL award.**

Why I am confident it is this and not EPS: **AVAV's stock has repeatedly traded against its own EPS result.** On 9 September 2025 the company *missed* EPS ($0.32 actual vs $0.34–0.36 expected) and the stock rose **+6.95%**, because it printed record Q1 backlog of $1.1bn and bookings of $399m [20][26][31]. On 29 June 2026 it beat EPS by 24% but the coverage and the +18.76% move were led by funded backlog up 65% YoY to $1.2bn and total backlog of $2.7bn [11][26][32]. Conversely on 9 December 2025 revenue *beat* and the stock fell 12.85% because the adjusted-EPS guidance was cut [25][26].

**What the market expects for it.** FY26 closed with full-year bookings of **$2.7bn**, a **1.4x book-to-bill**, funded backlog **$1.2bn** and unfunded backlog **$1.5bn** — the unfunded figure explicitly *excluding* the $1.5bn of terminated SCAR work [11][13]. Awards announced inside the reported quarter (1 May – 1 Aug 2026): a **$500m** three-year Army IDIQ for layered counter-drone (announced 1 July) and a **$117.3m** Army P550 production contract (20 July) [28]. Awards announced *after* the quarter closed, which flow to Q2 FY27 bookings but will dominate the call: **$51m** Switchblade 600 (26 Aug), **$464.8m** E-HEL LOCUST X3 (2 Sep), **>$50m** first international LOCUST order (8 Sep) [17][18][19][30]. That is **~$566m of Q2 bookings already banked** roughly five weeks into a quarter.

**The specific thing to watch:** whether the company raises the FY27 revenue guide. At least one published sell-side-style view expects a **~$200m raise** and a $240 fair value on the E-HEL award [35]. **My inference:** a raise this early — five weeks into Q2, on a multi-year award, with a continuing resolution pending — is possible but not the base case; a reaffirmation plus aggressive qualitative framing is more likely. The gap between "raise" and "reaffirm" is plausibly the difference between a double-digit up move and a double-digit down move.

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

Two reporting segments: Autonomous Systems (Switchblade loitering munitions, Puma/P550 UAS, Titan counter-UAS) and Space, Cyber & Directed Energy (LOCUST lasers, space, cyber).

**Margins.** Q4 FY26 GAAP gross margin **32%, down from 36%** a year earlier [11]. FY26 adjusted gross margin **30%**, with Q4 at 34% [13]. Q4 adjusted EBITDA **$140.1m (22% margin)**, more than double the prior-year $62m/10% [11][13]. FY26 adjusted EBITDA **$286.1m** [11]. Management expects FY27 gross margin improvement on mix and volume, but says it will be spent: **R&D 7–9% of revenue, SG&A 14–16%** [13].

**Earnings.** FY26 GAAP diluted EPS **−$5.40**; non-GAAP adjusted **$3.31** [11]. The GAAP loss is dominated by a **$151.3m goodwill impairment** in the Space unit following the SCAR stop-work [21]. TTM GAAP EPS **−$5.45**; net income **−$265.12m** [6][7].

**Cash flow — the weakest link.** FY26 operating cash flow **−$78.4m** [11]. **TTM free cash flow −$251m** [7]. Q4 FY26 was the first positive FCF quarter since Q1 FY25, at $73m [13]. Management explicitly **does not expect positive free cash flow in FY27**, with **capex at 12–14% of revenue** funding capacity expansion in Salt Lake City, Huntsville, Albuquerque and Dayton [13]. On top of that the company announced a **$100m company-owned campus in Moorpark, California on 24 August 2026** — and the stock fell 7.5% that day [36][37].

**Balance sheet and dilution.** Cash at 30 April 2026 **$377.3m** [11]; a later third-party summary cites $632m cash against $834m debt [35]. Financing for BlueHalo: a **$700m Term A loan** plus an upsized revolver to $350m [22], and a July 2025 capital raise of **3,528,226 shares at $248.00** alongside **$650m of 0% convertible senior notes due 2030**, ~$1.47bn net proceeds [38]. Shares outstanding went from ~28.2m to **50.42m**, roughly **+74% YoY** [6][38]. **No buyback found.** Note the equity was sold at $248; the stock is $148.78.

**Customer concentration.** Government revenue (prime, sub, and FMS) was **~85% of FY26 revenue**; the DoD alone **~63%**; the **U.S. Army ~25%** [22]. This is a single-customer-complex business, which is precisely why the SCAR termination and the CR calendar matter so much.

**What changed since the last print (29 June 2026):** the $500m Titan IDIQ (1 Jul), $117.3m P550 award (20 Jul), CEO and CAO Form 4 sales in July, two director departures/one retirement and two new director appointments, the $100m Moorpark capex announcement (24 Aug), the $51m Switchblade order (26 Aug), the $464.8m E-HEL award (2 Sep), the >$50m international LOCUST order (8 Sep) — and a **~26% share-price decline from the mid-August highs**.

## 7. Forensics

This is the densest part of the file and the reason I would not treat AVAV as a clean fundamental setup.

**Restatement and material weakness.**
- **17 June 2026:** the Audit Committee determined the unaudited financials in the 10-Q for the three and nine months ended 31 January 2026 required restatement and could no longer be relied upon [24]. The **Item 4.02 non-reliance 8-K was filed 22 June 2026** [7][8].
- **Cause:** the carrying value used in the Space reporting unit's goodwill impairment analysis omitted an allocation of goodwill arising from acquired deferred tax assets and liabilities [8].
- **Impact:** loss from operations understated by **$89.4m** and net loss by **$87.3m** for both the three and nine months ended 31 January 2026; loss per share understated by **$1.75** (3M) and **$1.79** (9M) [8].
- **A newly identified material weakness** in ICFR over the preparation and review of the goodwill impairment analysis; **disclosure controls and procedures concluded ineffective** as of 31 January 2026 [8]. On the Q4 call the CFO confirmed remediation "requires testing over additional quarters to satisfy SOX requirements" — i.e. **not remediated** [13].
- The stock fell **7.6%** on 22 June 2026 on the disclosure [8], and one tracker put the cumulative move at ~11% [8].
- One commentator notes the non-reliance notice "has sat unresolved for more than two months" [7]. **This print is the first quarterly report since; any update on remediation status is a live repricing risk in either direction.**

**Litigation.** *Norrell v. AeroVironment et al.*, No. 26-cv-01429, **E.D. Va.**, alleging §10(b) and §20(a) violations — that the company understated the likelihood it would imminently face competition for the SCAR work. The 20 January 2026 stop-work disclosure is pleaded as causing a **15.77%** decline; a related filing references a 17% drop [23]. Lead-plaintiff deadline was **27 July 2026** [23].

**Governance / departures — an unusual cluster.**

- **24–25 June 2026:** **William J. Lynn III** appointed to the board [24].
- **23/29 July 2026:** director **Stephen F. Page** notified the board he will retire and not stand for re-election [24].

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Sector regime.** Defence-tech and drone names have de-rated hard in 2026 despite record backlogs. AVAV is down ~44.5% YTD and ~39% over twelve months [7][16]; commentary explicitly attributes the August leg to a **risk-off session hitting AI-linked defence names** rather than company news [36]. Peers RCAT and KTOS are also described as down significantly in 2026 on macro and sector headwinds "that mask the mounting potential" [44].

**The budget is the tailwind and the timing is the headwind.** The Pentagon's FY27 request contains **more than $70bn for drones and counter-drone systems** — $53.6bn for autonomy/platforms/contested logistics and $21bn for munitions and counter-drone — the largest such investment ever, including **$994m for Army small counter-UAS** [45][46]. But **both chambers have passed FY27 continuing resolutions and full-year appropriations look unlikely before December** [45]. Nawabi said as much on the Q4 call: a full budget "approved around December or January, with dollars not reaching customer accounts until around March" — which is the explicit basis for the back-half-weighted FY27 guide [13]. **Inference:** the CR is a known, already-guided headwind. The risk is not that it exists; it is that management extends the timeline further on this call.

**Peers who already reported.**
- **Kratos (KTOS), 4–11 August 2026:** Q2 revenue **$458.8m vs $410.4m expected (+11.8%)**, adjusted EPS **$0.21 vs $0.13**, organic growth +19.1%, and it **raised** FY26 revenue guidance to $1.75–1.81bn. The stock rose **+5.47% in the session and a further +3.45% after hours** [47][48]. Read-through: **positive** — beat-and-raise in adjacent unmanned/hypersonics demand was rewarded, and demand is clearly there.
- **Red Cat (RCAT), 6 August 2026:** revenue **$20.19m, 10.6% below consensus**, GAAP EPS **−$0.26 vs −$0.17**; but revenue +527% YoY, gross margin to 16.1%, and full-year $150–180m reaffirmed [44]. Read-through: **mixed** — small-cap drone execution remains lumpy; reaffirmation cushioned the miss.
- The synthesis in the sector press is that "record-breaking backlogs and funded contracts are the story in 2026" while prices have de-rated [44]. **My inference:** the KTOS template — beat plus raise plus a clear demand narrative — is exactly the template AVAV would need to reproduce, and it is precisely the template a guided-weak Q1 makes hardest to hit on the revenue line.

**Rate/FX/commodity sensitivity.** Minimal direct FX or commodity exposure disclosed; ~85% U.S.-government revenue [22]. Rate sensitivity runs through the $700m Term A loan [22] and through duration/multiple compression on a name with a 46x forward P/E [6] and negative free cash flow [7][13] — a genuinely rate-sensitive equity despite a defence label. One sector piece frames it directly as drone-defence balance sheets under rising bond yields [44].

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull.** The market has de-rated AVAV ~26% in seventeen sessions into a print, straight through a $464.8m first-ever U.S. directed-energy production award and a first international LOCUST order, leaving the stock ~10% off its 52-week low with the sell-side average target 52% higher and *no* downgrades after the award [1][6][17][18][19][30][36][37]. Q2 FY27 bookings are already ~$566m five weeks in [17][18][19]. This company has demonstrably traded on the order book rather than EPS — on 9 September 2025 it *missed* EPS and rose 6.95% on record backlog [20][26][31]; on 29 June 2026 it rose 18.76% on backlog up 65% [11][26]. Shorts are 8.2% of float and *adding* [39], skew has calls bid over puts [5], and the ±13% implied move is below the 15.7% median realised. If Nawabi raises FY27 revenue on E-HEL — one published view models a ~$200m raise and $240 fair value [35] — the KTOS beat-and-raise template (+8.9% across session and after-hours) [47][48] is the map, and the move is violent to the upside.

**Bear.** This is the quarter management told you would be bad. The guided cadence — H1 45%/H2 55%, Q1 ~45% of H1 — implies Q1 revenue near **$440m against a widely-quoted $474.57m consensus** and a $454.7m year-ago base, and a call participant already modelled Q1 down YoY on the ~$30m/quarter SCAR hole [11][13][15][20]. Consensus EPS spans $0.22 to $0.34 and revenue $446m to $525m [9][10][16][26][29], so a headline "miss" against the printed consensus is close to a coin-flip regardless of the underlying. Underneath sits an **unremediated material weakness**, an Item 4.02 non-reliance notice unresolved for over two months, ineffective disclosure controls, a §10(b) class action in E.D. Va., a Chief Accounting Officer who sold in the open market twice while that notice was live, a first-time CFO, and five board changes in seven weeks [7][8][13][23][24][43]. Free cash flow is **−$251m TTM**, management does not expect positive FCF in FY27, capex is 12–14% of revenue and they just committed another $100m to a Moorpark campus — an announcement the stock fell 7.5% on [7][13][36][37]. Share count is up ~74% YoY with equity sold at $248 [6][38]. The precedent for a guided-soft quarter is 9 December 2025: revenue beat, guidance disappointed, **−12.85%** [25][26].

**Base case (mine).** Revenue lands in the $440–470m band — soft versus the printed consensus but broadly consistent with what management guided — and adjusted EPS lands near the low end of a very wide range. Management **reaffirms** rather than raises FY27, framing E-HEL and the international LOCUST order as validating the back-half weighting and the FY28+ trajectory rather than as an in-year revenue add. Bookings and backlog are the number that gets quoted, and they should look strong. The move is large in absolute terms — the median realised reaction is 15.7% against a 13% implied — but the direction is genuinely close to a coin flip, tilted marginally up by positioning (crowded cheap short, extreme drawdown, no analyst capitulation left, call-bid skew) rather than by anything fundamental. I would not describe this as a high-conviction directional setup, and the accounting overhang means the left tail is fatter than the option skew implies.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The consensus view going into this — visible in a $225.77 average target held through a 44% YTD decline, 17 of 20+ analysts at Buy or better, all four September notes maintained — is that **AVAV is a temporarily broken story with an intact, accelerating order book, and the de-rating is an entry point** [6][16][29].

**The single most credible reversal is an accounting event, not an operating one.** If on this call the company discloses that the material weakness is **not remediated and has been expanded in scope**, or that the FY26 audit surfaced additional control deficiencies beyond the Space-unit goodwill calculation, or if it delays the Q1 FY27 10-Q, the thesis changes category. It stops being "cheap defence-tech with a lumpy quarter" and becomes "a company whose numbers cannot be relied upon while a §10(b) class action is live." Concretely: the Item 4.02 notice has been outstanding since 22 June with no formal audited resolution [7][8], remediation was already conceded to require multiple further quarters of testing [13], the CFO is nine weeks into the job [24], and the Chief Accounting Officer sold stock twice in the open market during the window [7][43]. That combination is how the sell-side would be forced to move targets in one step rather than in increments — and it is the one outcome for which a 13% implied move is far too small.

The mirror-image flip, less likely but cleanly specified: a **FY27 revenue guidance raise of $150m+** explicitly attributed to E-HEL, plus a Q1 book-to-bill above 1.0x and funded backlog above $1.3bn. That would convert the LOCUST awards from an outlier into a cycle — the exact question the print was framed around [35] — and would leave a crowded 8.2%-of-float short base offside with borrow at only 0.59% [39].
