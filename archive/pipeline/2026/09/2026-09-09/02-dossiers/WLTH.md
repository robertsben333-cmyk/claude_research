# WLTH — Wealthfront Corporation

**Event confirmed: YES.** Confirmed from a company-issued press release (GlobeNewswire, 2026-08-19) and mirrored on the company IR site: fiscal Q2 2027 results (quarter ended 2026-07-31) release **after the U.S. market close on Wednesday, September 9, 2026**, conference call **2:00 p.m. PT / 5:00 p.m. ET the same day**. [1][2]

**What this print is about.** Wealthfront is a nine-month-old IPO (Nasdaq debut 2025-12-12, priced $14.00) that has now sold off hard on all three of its post-IPO earnings prints, including one where it *beat* EPS. That tells you the reaction function is not about the income statement. Roughly 70% of revenue is Cash Management — a deposit-spread business earning ~58bps on ~$44.8bn of client cash — and the market prices this name off the **net-deposit and cash-balance trajectory disclosed in the monthly metrics**, which the company publishes alongside earnings. On 2026-09-09 investors get two unhedgeable disclosures at once: the July (quarter-end) monthly metrics and the **August 2026** monthly metrics, i.e. a data point one month *into* the current quarter. [1][3][8] The setup is a genuine two-sided fight: the bear case is that Cash Management assets are shrinking year-on-year, adjusted opex is growing 16% against 7% revenue growth, and the Street's ~$98m revenue bar implies an ~8% sequential jump that the company's own May/June asset disclosures do not obviously support. The bull case is that the stock has already de-rated ~31% from IPO market cap, the rate regime has flipped from "cuts kill the spread" to a market pricing a possible September *hike*, and every rate-levered peer that reported for the June quarter (Schwab, Robinhood, SoFi) beat on net interest.

**Cash Management net deposits and cash-management asset balances — as disclosed in the July *and* August monthly metrics released with the print — plus the annualised cash-management fee rate in basis points.**

How I know it is this and not EPS:

1. On 2026-01-12 the company **beat** the EPS consensus by 5.0% and posted record revenue of $93.2m and record platform assets of $92.8bn. The stock fell **16.84%** the next day. The explanation carried in contemporaneous coverage and repeated in the securities-investigation notices is the **December net deposit outflow of −$208m against +$874m in December 2024**. [6][7][16]
2. On 2026-03-11 the Q4 release paired a revenue beat with **$360m of cash management outflows**; the stock fell again. [9]

4. Management's only quantified forward statement is about this metric: an expected **57–58bps** cash-management fee rate, against 58bps actual in FQ1'27, down 4bps y/y. [11][23]

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Rate regime — and this is the most important thing that has changed since the January selloff.** The Fed is holding at **3.50–3.75%**, three FOMC members dissented in favour of a *hike*, and the market-implied probability of a **25bp hike at the 2026-09-16 meeting is ~57%**. [42][43] The FOMC decision lands **seven days after** this print, so the call will be held into a live macro event.

This matters because Wealthfront's two worst days as a public company were both, in substance, rate-and-flow stories. The January selloff followed management discussion of "changes in asset flows, client behavior, and the effects of **interest rate cuts**"; the securities investigations name **interest rate sensitivity** explicitly. [7] Cash Management revenue is a spread on client cash: the company advertises **3.30% APY** (unchanged since 2026-01-30 and confirmed unchanged as of 2026-09-01, per a third-party rate tracker) against an effective funds rate near 3.5–3.75%, and books ~58bps. [44][45][23] A hawkish Fed sustains both the spread and the incentive for clients to keep cash on platform. **The macro backdrop into this print is materially better for the cash franchise than it was into either of the two prior downside reactions.** That is a fact-based observation; the inference that it will translate into a better *stock* reaction is mine and is not guaranteed.

**Peers who already reported the June quarter — all three rate-levered comparables beat:**

**Read-through, and the honest caveat.** The consumer-fintech deposit and sweep complex earned *more* net interest in the June quarter, not less — that supports the revenue side of Wealthfront's cash business. But note the pattern in the reaction column: **Schwab and Robinhood both beat and both traded down on the day.** The sector's reaction function in this cycle has been "beat is not enough". Separately, on 2026-09-01, **SoFi fell 4% and Affirm fell 5% as yields hit balance-sheet lenders while Robinhood held steady** — the market is currently discriminating between balance-sheet risk and fee/spread models, and Wealthfront sits on the fee/spread side (it does not hold the client deposits itself; they sit at program banks). [51] Wealthfront is also structurally different from all three: it is far smaller, has one dominant product, is not growing revenue anywhere near peer rates (+7% vs +21% to +43%), and is losing operating leverage while they gain it.

**FX / commodity sensitivity**: negligible — a domestic US retail platform. **Equity-market beta**: Investment Advisory assets ($54.3bn) mark to the market, so a weak equity tape between 2026-07-31 and 2026-08-31 would show up directly in the August monthly metric.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Expectations are low and the macro has flipped in Wealthfront's favour. The stock is 35% below its 52-week high and 31% below IPO-day market cap at 3.88x sales with $428m of cash and an active buyback that bought stock at $8.66 [4][5][15][11]. The rate narrative that produced the January and March selloffs — "cuts compress the spread" — has inverted: the market now prices a ~57% chance of a *hike* on 2026-09-16 [42][43], and every rate-levered peer beat on net interest for the June quarter [46][48][50]. The known two-thirds of the quarter is decent — May +$447m and June +$164m total net deposits sum to more than the entire FQ1'27 quarter's $554m, and assets rose to a record $99.1bn [25][3][11]. Investment Advisory is compounding at +33% y/y with cross-product adoption above 63% [3], the WLTXX 25bps fee is now live [27], and Home Lending has gone from one state to three plus 28 licences [28][29]. If July net deposits are positive and the August metric is clean, a stock that has already been punished three times can gap up on relief alone, with 5.8–7.1% of float short and ~7 days to cover as fuel [15][14].

**Bear case.** The core 70%-of-revenue franchise is shrinking. Cash Management assets are **−2.1% y/y** and bled **−$235m in June alone**; Cash Management revenue was **−1% y/y** in FQ1'27 [3][11]. Adjusted opex is growing **16%** against 7% revenue growth, EBITDA margin has gone 45% → 41% and net margin 31% → 14%, and Home Lending is a pure cost line for now [11][26]. The Street's ~$98m revenue bar implies +8.3% sequential growth against only +2.6% asset growth from April to June — a bar I do not think the disclosed data supports [13][11][3]. Sell-side targets have been cut three separate times since March [12][21][24]. And the reaction record is unambiguous: **three prints, three declines, mean −13.9%**, including one where the EPS number was fine and the stock still fell 17% because a monthly deposit figure was bad [16][6][7]. Two law firms are publicly probing exactly the disclosures at issue — asset flows and rate sensitivity — which constrains how bullish management can be on the call [7][37].

**Base case (my read).** A modest lean down, with genuinely low conviction. The three-for-three downside record and the disclosed June cash-management outflow are the strongest evidence in the file, and the revenue bar looks a touch high against the company's own monthly numbers. But three observations is a small sample, one of them was polluted by a non-recurring IPO charge, the stock has not run up at all into this print (spot $9.63 vs $9.85 the day after the last miss), and the rate backdrop is the friendliest it has been since listing. I would expect a move of a magnitude similar to history — high single digits to low teens — with the direction hinging almost entirely on the **August** monthly metric that nobody outside the company has seen. `preliminary_direction_score: −25`, `preliminary_prob_up: 40`, conviction **Med**.

**On the triage rationale.** The hint was "universe at or below the triage threshold — all eligible names carried forward" — i.e. WLTH was included by default rather than by conviction. *My conclusion: that default inclusion was correct on the merits, for a reason triage would not have seen.* This name has a 13.9% mean absolute earnings move, a unanimous 0-for-3 downside record, and a genuinely identifiable single decision metric. It is a high-variance, well-defined event. What it is *not* is a high-confidence directional call, and the missing implied move means I cannot say whether that variance is priced.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is concrete and lands in the same press release: **the August 2026 monthly metrics show Cash Management net deposits back positive**, reversing June's −$235m, with the cash-management fee rate held at 58bps and total platform assets pushing above $100bn.

That single combination would break the bear chain in one step. It would mean the June cash outflow was seasonal or idiosyncratic rather than structural; it would show that a hawkish rate path is pulling client cash *onto* the platform rather than pushing it off; and — because it is an August number, one month into FQ3'27 — it would de-risk the *next* quarter at the same time, which is exactly the disclosure that has been used against the stock twice. Against a stock trading 35% below its high, with 5.8–7.1% of float short and roughly a week to cover, and a buyback bidding beneath it, that print would very plausibly produce an up-move of the same magnitude as the historical down-moves.

The mirror-image risk is equally concrete: an August metric showing cash-management outflows **larger** than June's −$235m would confirm the structural read, and given management gives no guidance to lean on, there would be nothing in the release to arrest it.
