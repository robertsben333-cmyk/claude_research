# UNFI — United Natural Foods, Inc.

**Event confirmed: YES.** Company press release (BusinessWire, 2026-08-10) states results for the 13-week fiscal Q4 and 52-week FY2026 ended **August 1, 2026** will be released the **morning of Tuesday, September 8, 2026**, with a conference call at **8:30 a.m. ET**. Session = **bmo**. No date change and no pre-announcement: EDGAR shows only two filings since 2026-08-01 (a Form 3 and a Form 4), no 8-K. [S1][S2][S3]

**What this print is about.** UNFI is two and a half years into a profit-over-sales turnaround: it is deliberately shrinking revenue (network optimization, the Allentown DC exit) to expand margin, cash flow and deleverage. That trade has worked — FY26 adjusted EBITDA guidance was raised from a $665M midpoint to $695M, adjusted EPS from $1.90 to $2.50, free cash flow from ~$300M to ~$330M, and net leverage fell to 2.5x, the lowest since FY2018. [S4][S5][S6][S7] But the market has stopped paying for it: the stock is **-20.9% over 63 sessions against XLP +1.4% and the S&P +4.5%**, with no identifiable catalyst. [own calc, S8] This is the **fiscal-year-guidance print** — UNFI has said it expects a *return to sales growth in FY2027* [S9], and the whole quarter reduces to whether the initial FY27 EBITDA/EPS guide, delivered by a **CFO who started 29 days ago**, clears the $3.21 FY27 consensus EPS. [S10][S11] The last two Q4 prints, both guidance-setting, moved the stock **+30.6%** and **+18.5%**. The one before that moved **-27.4%**. There is no small outcome here.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Initial FY2027 adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS guidance — and specifically whether UNFI guides FY27 sales to *growth*.** Not Q4 EPS.

Why I am confident that is the metric:
- Management has already committed publicly to the framing. On the Q3 FY26 call (2026-06-09) UNFI said it **expects a return to sales growth in fiscal 2027**, having spent FY26 absorbing ~450–500bp of optimization drag per quarter that it said would not be fully lapped until Q1 FY2027. [S9][S4][S14]
- The whole FY26 sell-off is a growth-credibility problem, not a margin problem. Q3 FY26 beat on profit (adj EBITDA +16.6%, adj EPS +75%) and the stock fell 10.3% on a 2.0% revenue miss. [S4][S17] Q2 FY26 beat on profit (adj EBITDA +23.4%, adj EPS +182%) and fell 3.0% on a revenue miss. [S5][S20] The market is done rewarding margin.
- The anchors are public and specific, so the guide is directly scorable on the tape: Investor Day (2025-12-10) set **FY2028 ~$33B net sales and ~$800M adjusted EBITDA**, with ~$300M FCF per year FY26–FY28. [S21][S22] From the FY26 $695M EBITDA midpoint, the FY28 target implies roughly a 7.3% CAGR — so a "consistent with the algorithm" FY27 guide is about **$740–755M adjusted EBITDA** (my arithmetic). FY27 consensus adj EPS is **$3.21**. [S11]
- The market has already shown it will punish a disappointing long-term algorithm from this management team: the stock fell **−8.85%** on Investor Day itself, 2025-12-10, on no earnings news. [S13][S21]

**What "hold the stock flat" requires (my read):** Q4 adj EPS ≥ $0.62 *and* FY27 adjusted EBITDA guided with a midpoint at or above ~$745M *and* an explicit FY27 sales-growth number that is positive. Miss any one of the three and the guidance-credibility discount reopens.

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

**Segment mix and the deliberate revenue shrink.** FY25 net sales $31,784M: Natural $16,017M, Conventional $14,667M, Retail $2,342M, eliminations $(1,242)M. [S6] Through FY26 the divergence widened sharply:

| Quarter | Net sales | Natural | Conventional | Retail |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Q1 FY26 | $7,840M (−0.4%) | $4,240M **+10.5%** | $3,325M **−11.7%** | $554M −5.5% |
| Q2 FY26 | $7,947M (−2.6%) | $4,290M **+6.7%** | $3,392M **−12.1%** | $560M −8.2% |
| Q3 FY26 | $7,723M (−4.2%) | $4,342M **+4.4%** | $3,136M **−13.6%** | $515M −10.1% |

Sources [S7][S5][S4]. Two things stand out and both matter for FY27: Conventional's decline is *deepening* (−11.7 → −12.1 → −13.6), and Natural's growth is *decelerating* (+10.5 → +6.7 → +4.4). The bull case needs Conventional's optimization drag to lap out in Q1 FY27 faster than Natural decelerates.

**Margin and cost trajectory.** Q3 FY26 gross margin 13.6% vs 13.4%; opex 12.4% of sales vs 12.7%, with opex down nearly 7% in absolute dollars. Q2 FY26 gross margin 13.2% vs 13.1%; opex 12.2% vs 12.6%, down ~6%. [S4][S5] The margin story is opex-led, not price-led — durable if throughput gains hold, fragile if volumes fall. Lean daily management is now live in **40 distribution centres**, up from 28 at end-FY25. [S9][S6]

**Cash and balance sheet (Q3 FY26 10-Q, as of 2026-05-02).** Cash $43M; AR $973M; inventory $1,994M; total assets $7,187M; AP $1,769M; long-term debt $1,660M; ABL loans outstanding $929M with $1,202M unused credit against $2,330M availability. [S23] Net debt $1.63B and **net leverage 2.5x, lowest since FY2018**, with the year-end target lowered to ~2.3x from ≤2.5x. [S4][S5] The term loan (~$371M) was **repriced on 2026-06-18** via Amendment No. 5, and the ABL was amended and restated on 2026-04-01 to a $2,400M facility. [S24][S25] Both cut FY27 interest expense — a real, already-banked tailwind to the FY27 EPS bridge. Q3 interest expense $32M vs $38M a year earlier. [S5]

**Free cash flow.** FY25 FCF $239M, a $331M improvement on FY24's $(92)M. [S6] FY26 nine-month FCF $243M against a ~$330M full-year guide. [S4][S7][S5] FCF is lumpy by quarter (Q1 −$54M, Q2 +$243M, Q3 +$54M) so the Q4 ~$87M implied figure is not a stretch.

**Buyback and dilution.** Under the September 2022 $200M authorization, UNFI repurchased 824,855 shares at an average $35.16 ($29M) in FY26 through Q3, with **$109M remaining authorized as of 2026-05-02**. [S23] Note the program carries a **four-year term from September 2022** — it lapses around this month, which makes a **new authorization announced alongside FY27 guidance a live possibility** (my inference, not company-stated). Diluted share count 62.7M in Q3 FY26 vs 60.5M a year earlier — the dilution from stock comp is currently outrunning the buyback. [S23]

**Customer concentration.** The largest customer (Whole Foods Market / Amazon) was **~25% of FY25 net sales**, under an amended and restated distribution agreement running to **May 20, 2032**. [S26] One Natural customer exceeded 10% of net sales in FY25. [S26] This is the single largest idiosyncratic risk and, currently, a tailwind: Whole Foods is publicly in expansion mode targeting **at least 30 new stores a year** against ~11 the prior year, from a base of 550+ stores. [S27]

**What changed since the last print (2026-06-09).**
1. **C-suite reshuffle (2026-07-07 8-K).** Matteo Tarditi moved from President & CFO to **President & COO** effective 2026-08-03; Louis Martin became **Chief Commercial Officer**; **Alfredo Luchini joined as CFO effective 2026-08-10**, from Carrier Climate Solutions Americas. [S28][S29]
2. **Chief Corporate Affairs Officer Matthew Echols departed effective 2026-08-24**, disclosed on a Form 4 filed 2026-08-27. [S30]
3. **Wholesale realigned into two product-defined divisions** — Conventional Grocery Products, and Natural/Organic/Specialty & Fresh. [S29]
4. **Surplus distribution centre sold after Q3 close**; the company said it **expects to record an $18M gain in Q4 FY26** and used the proceeds to repay ABL borrowings. [S23] This is a disclosed, known Q4 GAAP item — almost certainly excluded from adjusted EBITDA/EPS, so a headline GAAP beat driven by it should not be read as an operating beat.
5. **Term loan repriced (2026-06-18).** [S24]

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull.** The setup is a de-rated stock into the one print of the year that has historically produced the largest moves, and the moves have twice been violently positive. UNFI enters at $43.93, **20.9% below its June high with no identifiable catalyst for the drawdown** (own calc from [S13]; the 2026-08-03 −6.81% is publicly unexplained [S32]) and **below every post-Q3 analyst price target except one** [S16][S11]. The operating trend is unambiguous and company-sourced: FY26 adjusted EBITDA guidance raised from a $665M midpoint to $695M and adjusted EPS from $1.90 to $2.50 across two raises, net leverage down to 2.5x — lowest since FY2018 — with a year-end target cut to ~2.3x, FCF guide raised to ~$330M, and opex down ~6–7% in absolute dollars for two straight quarters. [S4][S5][S6][S7] The Q4 bar the company itself set is low in absolute terms ($156–176M adj EBITDA vs $116M last year) and the revenue bar has finally been marked down to the guide. Precedent is directly on point: the FY26 guide given at last year's Q4 print landed *above* the EPS consensus and *below* the sales consensus and the stock rose 18.45% [S18][S6]; the FY25 guide print produced +30.56%. Whole Foods — 25% of sales, locked to 2032 — is opening 30 stores a year against 11 [S26][S27], the term loan has been repriced [S24], an $18M asset-sale gain is already banked for Q4 [S23], and $109M of buyback authorization sits unused on a program that lapses this month [S23], making a fresh authorization alongside FY27 guidance plausible. **At an 11.9% implied move against a 25.5% Q4 mean-absolute base rate, the options are pricing less than half of what this print has historically delivered.**

**Bear.** The market has stopped paying for margin and started demanding growth, and growth is what is deteriorating. Natural segment growth halved over three quarters (+10.5% → +6.7% → +4.4%) while Conventional's decline deepened (−11.7% → −12.1% → −13.6%) [S7][S5][S4] — and the channel data has got worse since, not better: Sprouts comps turned **negative (−1.0%)** [S49], Natural Grocers is −14% in 21 days, grocery **units are −2% y/y**, SNAP enrolment is down 5.6 million people year-on-year, and management itself flagged SNAP cuts and consumer pressure on the last call. [S39][S48] Consensus Q4 adjusted EPS of $0.62 sits at roughly the **85th percentile of the company's own guided $0.45–0.65 range** (my calculation from [S4][S5][S7]) with **zero upward revision in 30 days** and a Zacks Earnings ESP of **0.00%** [S14] — the Street is asking for the top of the guide with no positive estimate momentum behind it. The last two prints both punished revenue misses (−2.99%, −10.28%) despite large profit beats [S4][S5][S17][S20]. And the FY27 guide will be delivered by a CFO **29 days into the job** [S28], amid the densest C-suite turnover in years — a new CFO, a new COO, a new CCO, and the Chief Corporate Affairs Officer out as of 2026-08-24 [S30] — with every structural incentive to set a beatable bar against a $3.21 FY27 consensus [S11]. The only directional insider datapoint is a **discretionary, non-10b5-1 sale of 16% of a divisional president's holding at $51.56** two weeks after the last print [S43]. Finally the run-in pattern: **six of the seven prior prints with a negative 21-day run-in produced a negative day-0 move**, and the current run-in is −8.21%.

**Base case.** Q4 lands close to the guided midpoint on revenue and near, but not comfortably above, consensus on adjusted EPS — the profit trend is real and well-telegraphed, but $0.62 against a $0.55 guided midpoint leaves little room. The stock then trades entirely on the FY27 guide. I expect a **conservative initial FY27 guide** — a wide range whose midpoint sits at or slightly below the ~$745–755M adjusted EBITDA implied by the FY28 algorithm, paired with a *low*-single-digit or hedged sales-growth framing rather than a clean growth number — because that is what a four-week-old CFO does, because it is exactly what the company did with the FY26 guide (initial $630–700M EBITDA, later raised to $685–705M) [S6][S4], and because the natural-channel data has softened since June. A conservative guide against a $3.21 consensus is a negative day-0 print in a name whose Q4 moves are measured in double digits. But conviction is genuinely low, because the same conservative-then-raise playbook produced **+18.45%** last year when the market chose to read a below-consensus sales guide with an above-consensus profit guide as credibility rather than caution. **Directionally I lean modestly down; on magnitude I am confident the realised move exceeds the 11.9% implied.**

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

**The most credible reversal: UNFI guides FY2027 adjusted EBITDA to a midpoint of ~$755M or better *and* attaches an explicit positive net-sales growth number, framed as the optimization drag fully lapping in Q1 FY27.**

Concretely, that looks like: FY27 net sales guided to ~$31.5–32.0B (positive growth off a ~$31.2B FY26 base), adjusted EBITDA $745–775M, adjusted EPS at or above $3.20, FCF ~$350M+, and a reaffirmation of the ~$800M FY28 target — plus, ideally, a **new share repurchase authorization** replacing the September 2022 program as it lapses [S23]. That combination would (a) validate the December 2025 Investor Day algorithm that the market rejected with an −8.85% session [S13][S21], (b) confirm that Natural's deceleration is optimization arithmetic rather than end-demand erosion, and (c) come from a brand-new CFO, which would make it materially more credible than the same guide from the architect of the plan.

The falsifiable marker to watch inside the release itself is the **Natural segment growth rate**. If Q4 Natural re-accelerates above the Q3 +4.4% — against Sprouts comps of −1.0% and grocery units at −2% — the end-demand bear case is disproved on the spot and the FY27 guide becomes believable. If Natural prints below +4%, no FY27 guide will hold.

The reverse tail is equally concrete: an FY27 adjusted EBITDA midpoint below ~$730M, or any language deferring the return to sales growth beyond FY27, reopens the guidance-credibility discount and, given a 25.5% Q4 mean-absolute base rate, the downside is not bounded by the 11.9% implied move.
