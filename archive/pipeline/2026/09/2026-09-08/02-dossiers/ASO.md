# ASO — Academy Sports and Outdoors, Inc.

**Event confirmed: YES.** Academy Sports + Outdoors reports fiscal Q2 2026 (13 weeks ended
2026-08-01) **before the US market open on Wednesday, 2026-09-09**, with a conference call at
10:00 a.m. ET, per the company's own results-conference-call press release [1] and corroborated
by MarketBeat [2], Finviz [3] and StockAnalysis [4].

**What this print is about.** This is not an EPS print. It is a *guidance-trajectory* print into
the teeth of a sector that has just been repriced. On 2026-06-09 Academy beat on adjusted EPS
and raised full-year guidance *above* consensus — and the stock fell anyway [5][6]. Since then
the sporting-goods complex has been broken by Dick's Sporting Goods, which cut FY26 adjusted EPS
guidance from $13.50–14.50 to $11–12 and fell 31% in a single session on 2026-08-25, its worst
day since IPO [7][8]; JD Sports reported North America like-for-likes down 6.8% with the
Finish Line/Hibbett/DTLR banners down 8.0% [9]. Academy's own CFO pre-flagged, back in March,
that **Q2 would be "the most challenging" quarter** because it anniversaries the Jordan launch
and the Nike assortment expansion [10] — precisely the categories where the promotional pressure
now sits. The stock enters the print at $44.94, 28% off its 52-week high, below its 50- and
200-day averages, with 12.5% of float short and put/call demand at a 1.32 z-score [3][11]. The
question the tape will answer is whether the FY2026 adjusted EPS range of $6.40–6.80 — which
sits *above* a ~$6.39 Street number and requires a materially better back half — survives
contact with a consumer that just posted the first negative US retail-sales month in nine [12].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**It is the fiscal-2026 back-half guide — specifically whether the adjusted EPS range of
$6.40–$6.80 and the comparable-sales range of 0% to +2% are held, trimmed, or cut.** Not Q2 EPS.

How I know: UBS's Michael Lasser, previewing the print on 2026-09-02, wrote that "the market is
likely to focus less on what Academy delivered in 2Q and more on what its results imply about the
trajectory of the business into the back half of the year" [27]. That is corroborated by the
behavioural record: on 2026-06-09 Academy *beat* ($0.93 vs $0.92) and *raised* the year above
consensus, and the stock still finished down [6][25][15]; on 2026-03-17 the quarter was broadly
fine and the FY guide came in below the Street, and the stock lost 15.8% [10][15]. Academy also
titles these releases "…Results; **Updates Guidance**" [21][33] — a guidance action is expected,
not optional.

**What the market expects for it.** The Street's own FY adjusted number of ~$6.39 [19] sits
below the $6.60 guidance midpoint, which means **analysts have already pencilled in a trim** —
the marginal question is whether the *low end* ($6.40) holds. My read: holding $6.40–6.80 intact
is the bullish outcome; narrowing to something like $6.40–6.60 is roughly neutral-to-slightly-
negative and largely discounted; anything that starts with a 6.2 or lower, or a comp guide cut
below zero, is the tail that produces another double-digit down day.

**Secondary metric to watch on the call:** August/Q3 quarter-to-date comp colour. Academy
routinely gives it [31]. JD Sports explicitly reported that July back-to-school demand was
**deferred into the first half of August** [9] — which falls *outside* Academy's Q2 (ended
2026-08-01) and *inside* Q3. That is a mechanical reason Q2 could look soft while QTD commentary
looks good. *(Inference: mine.)*

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

**Segment/category mix.** Q1 FY2026 category growth: Outdoor +12% (fishing and shooting sports;
ammunition turned positive in February and accelerated), Sports & Recreation +6% (baseball;
double-digit trading-card growth), Apparel +5% (Carhartt, Levi's, Nike, Jordan), Footwear +3%
(cleated and seasonal — Crocs, Birkenstock) [31]. Zacks models Outdoors at ~$455M and Sports &
Rec at ~$380M this quarter, with apparel ~$463M and footwear ~$323M [5] — i.e. footwear is
roughly 20% of merchandise sales, the smallest of the four legs and the one exposed to the
athletic-lifestyle problem.

**Last reported quarter (Q1 FY2026, ended 2026-05-02) [6][34]:** net sales $1,442.0M (+6.7%);
comp +2.9% (versus −3.7% prior year); e-commerce +17.4%; gross margin 33.2% (from 34.0%), with
**110bps of tariff unfavourability** partly offset by freight and shrink; SG&A 28.1% (from
28.8%); GAAP diluted EPS $0.80 (+17.6%); adjusted $0.93 (+22.4%); adjusted EBITDA $89.0M; two
new stores (Canton OH, Muskogee OK) to 324; inventory per store −6.8% in units, −0.8% in dollars.

**Unit economics / growth engine.** The 2024 cohort of 39 stores is "comping in the high single
digits" [31]. Store count 322 at FYE2025 → 324 after Q1 → 3 planned in Q2 → 15–20 in H2, of which
**11 were announced on 2026-08-24 for Q3 across six states** [35]. Analyst Day (2026-04-07)
long-range framework: **~450 stores, $8B+ sales, $9 GAAP EPS, 15%+ e-commerce penetration**
[34][36]; Wells Fargo characterised it as moving "from a regional to a national retailer with
mid-single-digit top-line growth" [28].

**Margin trajectory.** FY2025 gross margin 34.8% (from 33.9%) [23]; FY2026 guided 34.5–35.0%
[6]. Q1 delivered 33.2%, so the guide needs H2 expansion. CFO framing: "modest gross margin
pressure in the first half" then "modest expansion in the back half" [31].

**Free cash flow and balance sheet [6][32][23].** Q1 operating cash flow $160.6M, capex $38.9M,
free cash flow $121.6M. Cash $337.8M (+18.5% YoY). Long-term debt net $480.3M ($85M term loan at
7.53%, $400M notes at 6.00%). ABL: $1B commitment, **zero drawn**, $992.4M available. Merchandise
inventories $1,654.4M at 2026-05-02 (from $1,503.8M at FYE). Debt/equity 0.92; current ratio 1.68
[3]. FY2025 free cash flow $262.8M [23]; FY2026 adjusted FCF guided $250–300M [6].

**Buyback / dilution.** $700M authorisation running to 2026-12-04; **$338.2M remaining at
2026-05-02** — roughly 12% of the current market cap [32]. Q1 repurchases: 1,703,031 shares at an
average **$57.79** [32], i.e. management bought 29% above today's price. Dividend held at $0.15/
quarter, declared 2026-09-02 for the quarter ended 2026-08-01, ex-date 2026-09-16, payable
2026-10-14 [37][3]. FY2025 buyback was $200.8M, down 45.5% from $368.3M [23].

**Customer concentration:** none (retail). **Supplier concentration** is the live issue: Nike/
Jordan is being scaled aggressively (Jordan shops 145 → 200+ during Q2, targeted to be a top-20
brand by year-end [31][38]) into a market where Nike itself describes digital as "too
promotional" with elevated marketplace markdowns [39].

**Tariffs / sourcing.** China is ~50% of inventory procurement, down from >70% in 2019, with
roughly 10% direct exposure as importer of record; private-brand China sourcing cut to 6%;
$85M of domestic receipts pulled forward at pre-tariff prices [40][41]. *(These figures come from
2025-vintage supply-chain reporting — treat as directionally current, not point-in-time.)*

**Consumer bifurcation.** Q1: households above $100k grew mid-single digits; sub-$50k households
declined at a low-single-digit rate, described as "less bad"; high gas prices offset tax refunds
for lower-income households [31].

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The revenue bar is set at management's own quarter-to-date commentary — consensus
$1.66B (+3.8%) against a store base up ~6.9% implies a comparable-sales figure of roughly flat,
which is precisely what Academy said it was running through Memorial Day [5][31]. The EPS bar is
softer than +9.3% looks, because the diluted share count is shrinking fast (63.51M at 2026-05-02
to 62.02M at 2026-06-03, after $99.3M of Q1 buyback and $200.8M in FY2025) [32][6][23]. Two of the
three biggest merchandise legs got clean positive reads from peers in the identical quarter:
hunting and shooting +6.7% at Sportsman's Warehouse and seven straight months of rising NICS for
Outdoors [61][51], and DICK'S +4.9% comp with the World Cup cited for team sports and apparel
[60]. The valuation is 6.5x forward with $338.2M of buyback authorisation left against a $2.78B
market cap and management's own average repurchase price at $57.79 [3][32]. Positioning is
lopsided the wrong way for bears: 12.46% of float short, 4.7–5.2 days to cover, put/call at a
1.32 z-score [3][11]. If the $6.40–6.80 range simply survives, a stock 24% off its half-year high
and 14% below its 200-DMA has real room to gap [3].

**Bear case.** Academy's CFO told the market in March that **Q2 would be the most challenging
quarter of the year**, because it anniversaries the Jordan launch and the Nike assortment
expansion [10] — and the athletic-footwear market is now demonstrably in an inventory glut and
heavily promotional, per Dick's, JD Sports and Nike [7][9][39]. The gross-margin compare is the
year's hardest at 36.0% [21], against a Q1 that ran 33.2% with 110bps of tariff drag [6]. The
back-half maths embedded in guidance is aggressive: $3.35–3.75 of H2 adjusted EPS versus $3.11
last year, +14% at the midpoint, in the same weeks that Dick's cut its own year by roughly 20%
[7][33][23]. July US retail sales fell 0.6%, consumer sentiment printed 51, and the Fed may be
*hiking* in September — the wrong regime for a retailer whose sub-$50k cohort is already
declining [12][64][65][31]. Back-to-school demand deferred out of July into August, i.e. out of
this fiscal quarter [9][52]. Zacks has the name at Rank #4 (Sell) [24], six price targets have
been cut in three months with none raised [17][18], and the Street's full-year number already
sits ~$0.21 below the guidance midpoint [19]. Most damning, the behavioural record: this stock
fell on a beat-and-raise three months ago and has closed lower in three of its last four
earnings sessions [15].

**Base case (mine).** The quarter itself is roughly fine — comp near flat, revenue near
consensus, adjusted EPS near $2.05–2.15 with buyback doing most of the year-over-year work — and
management narrows rather than slashes the year, most plausibly to something like the lower half
of $6.40–6.80. That outcome is *mostly* discounted by a Street already at ~$6.39 and a stock at
6.5x forward near 52-week lows, so the reaction is a coin-flip skewed slightly negative: the
market has punished this specific management's guidance language three of the last four times,
and the one metric that matters is the one most exposed to the sector's promotional reset. I put
the modal outcome as a move of 4–8% with the sign tilted down, and I hold that view with low
conviction because the positioning (12.5% short, elevated put demand) and the Outdoors
read-through cut the other way. **Preliminary label: Lean Down. Reversal risk is high** — this is
a name that has exceeded its implied move in half of its last eight prints [15].

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The single most credible reversal is **an unchanged or raised FY2026 adjusted EPS range
($6.40–6.80 or better) delivered alongside explicitly positive August/Q3 quarter-to-date
comparable-sales commentary.** The mechanism is concrete and already visible in the peer data:
JD Sports reported that July back-to-school demand was deferred into the first half of August
[9], and Academy's Q2 closed on 2026-08-01 — so the deferred demand lands in Q3, not the reported
quarter. If management pairs a soft-but-in-line Q2 comp with a statement that August is running
positive, the entire bear thesis ("the consumer broke in the sporting-goods channel") loses its
load-bearing wall in a single sentence. Against 12.46% of float short at 4.7 days to cover, put/
call at a 1.32 z-score, a 6.5x forward multiple, $338.2M of remaining buyback and a stock 14%
below its 200-DMA [3][11][32], that combination is the setup for a move toward or beyond the
upper end of the 7.5–8.5% implied range [15][16].

The mirror-image flip for anyone currently constructive: **a comparable-sales guide cut below
zero for the year**, or an adjusted EPS range whose low end moves below $6.30. Given the March
precedent (guide below Street → −15.8%) [10][15], that outcome does not stop at the implied move.
