# TTAN — ServiceTitan, Inc.

**What this print is about.** ServiceTitan reports fiscal Q2 2027 (quarter ended 31 July 2026) after the close on 8 September 2026 [1][2]. The company is a vertical SaaS platform for the residential and commercial trades (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing), and its stock has spent 2026 as a hostage of the "SaaSpocalypse" — the market-wide re-rating of per-seat software on fears that AI agents dissolve seat-based pricing. TTAN is down 17.5% YTD and 32% from its all-time high even after August's violent software relief rally [computed from 3]. The print is therefore not really about the headline beat, which the company has delivered six times out of six since IPO [4][5]. It is about whether management raises FY27 revenue guidance far enough above the current $1.130–1.140bn to break the deceleration optics baked into the guide (Q2 guidance of $284–286m implies only +17.3–18.1% y/y against +24.6% delivered in Q1) [6][7], and whether the AI product line — Titan Intelligence / "Max" — is visibly converting into subscription and usage dollars rather than costing them. The setup is unusually charged: the stock fell 11.97% in the five sessions into the event while IGV fell only 4.50% [computed from 3], short interest has been covered down ~27% in 30 days [8], and options positioning is the most call-skewed it has been in months [8, and computed from 9] — a de-risked tape with a one-sided derivatives book.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**The size of the FY27 revenue guidance raise — specifically whether the range moves above $1.15bn — read together with the subscription revenue growth rate.**

Why, and how I know:

1. **The market has already shown it will ignore a beat and trade the guide.** On 2026-03-12 TTAN beat Q4 FY26 and the stock fell 6.41% [3][29]. The proximate cause was the initial FY27 guide of $1.11–1.12bn — only +15.5% growth against FY26's +24% [19]. On 2026-06-04 they raised FY27 to $1.13–1.14bn and the stock rose (though it faded from +14% AH to +4.13%) [6][15][3].
2. **The current guide still embeds heavy deceleration.** $1.130–1.140bn with Q1 at $268.8m and Q2 guided $285m implies H2 FY27 of roughly $576–586m against H2 FY26's $503.2m — about +15% — versus +24.6% actual in Q1. Every quarter that management does not lift that number, the deceleration story is confirmed by their own arithmetic. *(H2 FY26 = Q3 $249.2m + Q4 $254.0m [22][19]; arithmetic is mine.)*
3. **Subscription growth is the direct test of the bear thesis.** The "SaaSpocalypse" claim against TTAN is per-technician seat deflation from AI. Subscription revenue was $202.0m in Q1 FY27, +24.2% y/y [12]. KeyBanc's entire long case rests on "the technician-based subscription model" being protected; Piper Sandler's on "mission-critical service to the trades" with limited seat-deflation exposure [29]. If subscription growth holds ≥24% while Max scales, the bear thesis is falsified for another quarter. If it drops to ~21% or below, the multiple compresses regardless of the EPS beat.
4. **Management pre-committed to a checkable Max milestone.** On the Q1 call, ServiceTitan said it "more than doubled the number of locations on Max" in Q1 and expects to "double again next quarter," with ">10% of jobs at fully ramped Max customers now fully automated" [30]. That is a self-set, verifiable bar for *this* print. Failure to hit it is the cleanest possible confirmation of the AI-monetisation bear case; hitting it is the cleanest refutation.

**Secondary tells on the call:** usage revenue vs GTV growth (management guided usage to grow faster than GTV for FY27 [30], and KeyBanc modelled ~$4m of smoke-driven usage upside [23]); the non-GAAP operating margin trajectory, given the guide of $38–39m is *below* Q1's $40.8m; and the count of >$100k-billings enterprise customers (>2,000, >60% of annualised billings, described as the fastest-growing segment [30]).

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**The regime is the story.** 2026 has been defined by the "SaaSpocalypse": roughly $2tn erased from software market cap, with the sell-off intensifying around February–March on the launch of agentic AI coding tools, and the median SaaS EV/Revenue multiple down to ~4.6x by August 2026 [44][45][46]. TTAN traded at ~6x forward sales in March against a mid-20s revenue growth rate [29]. This is a **multiple-regime problem, not a TTAN execution problem** — the company has beaten every quarter through the de-rating.

**August 2026 was a violent reversal, and it is only twelve sessions old.** IGV rose ~16% in August, crushing SOXX (+1%) [47]. The pivotal session was **2026-08-27**, when Salesforce (+22.6%) and CrowdStrike (+20.5%) reported large beats and triggered the broadest single-day enterprise-software rally in over a year; **IGV rose +7.74% that day and TTAN +9.12%** [48][47][3]. TTAN's August 27 move was sector beta, not company news — I checked for company-specific catalysts and found none. Salesforce's CEO publicly told the market to stop the "SaaSpocalypse nonsense" [48].

**But September has been giving it back, and TTAN more than most.** IGV: 09-01 −3.46%, 09-02 −2.60%, 09-03 +3.41%, 09-04 −2.23% [3]. TTAN has underperformed IGV by 7.47pts over five sessions and 8.49pts over ten [computed from 3]. This is the crux of the setup — either the market is de-risking a high-beta, high-implied-move name into an event (benign) or it knows something (malign). The unremarkable volume argues for the former.

**Peers that have already reported — both positive:**
- **Samsara (IOT)** — the closest comparable (high-growth vertical software, hardware/usage mix, similar Jan-FY end, quarter ended ~1 August). Reported fiscal Q2 2027 on **2026-09-03** (8-K filed that day) [49]: revenue **$508.4m, +30% y/y**, ARR $2.1bn, adjusted EPS $0.20 vs $0.16 expected on $483.3m expected, and raised full-year guidance ~1.7%. Stock +5.33% in the session and +16.4% after hours [50][51]. IOT then added +3.74% on 09-04 while TTAN fell 5.56% [3]. **This is the single most relevant read-through and it is unambiguously positive** — a same-fiscal-calendar, same-archetype peer beat and raised five days before TTAN prints, and the market paid for it.
- **Procore (PCOR)** — construction vertical SaaS: Q2 beat with adjusted EPS $0.47 vs $0.41 and revenue $375m vs $365.9m, raised full-year outlook, delivered first quarter of GAAP operating profitability, revenue +15.8%, non-GAAP operating margin 21.5%; shares +5.82% after hours [52]. Note PCOR is growing at half TTAN's rate and PCOR fell 4.26% on 09-04 alongside TTAN [3].

**End-market (customer) read-through — mixed, mildly better than feared.** Watsco's Q2 2026 showed HVAC stabilising after the A2L transition, with residential equipment sales +5% (2% volume, 2% price) and management describing a return to conventional operating conditions after five years of disruption [53]. That follows a genuinely brutal 2025 in which Carrier, Trane, Lennox and Watsco all warned on cautious consumers, high rates and inventory [53]. WSO traded quietly through the last two weeks (+4.0% on 08-26, then flat-to-down; $315.13 on 09-04) [3] — no distress signal from the channel. Structurally, TTAN's demand base is repair-skewed and non-discretionary (~88% of HVAC jobs are repairs) with a US housing stock whose median age rose from 31 years in 2006 to 41 in 2023 [36], but renovation spend growth is decelerating from 2.9% to 1.6% [36].

**Factor and rate sensitivity.** TTAN is a long-duration, unprofitable-on-GAAP, high-beta growth name with 63-day realised vol of 58.9% [computed from 3]. It is a pure rates/duration and software-factor vehicle; a hawkish surprise or a renewed AI-disruption scare will overwhelm results. Currency and commodity exposure is immaterial — <5% of revenue is non-US and there is no drawn debt [12]. Indirect commodity exposure exists via HVAC equipment prices and tariffs feeding GTV, but GTV is a take-rate base, so it is second order.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull.** The Street's printed consensus of $285.96m sits at the *top* of a guidance range that this company has beaten by 1.3–5.3% in every quarter it has been public, with six-for-six non-GAAP EPS beats averaging roughly +55% and a Q1 operating-income beat of 46% versus the high end of guidance [16][6][19][5]. The one pre-print sell-side note I could source is KeyBanc's 2026-08-24 Overweight/$120 preview calling TTAN a highest-conviction idea, expecting revenue upside "similar in magnitude to the past several quarters," and quantifying roughly $4m of extra usage revenue from an active summer smoke season [23] — with the extra business day management flagged for Q2 on top [30]. The nearest structural comparable, Samsara, beat and raised five days ago and was paid for it [49][50][51], and the sector's existential AI narrative was publicly repudiated on 2026-08-27 by the largest prints in software [48][47]. Meanwhile the equity has been sold down 11.97% in five sessions on unremarkable volume, underperforming IGV by 7.5 points [computed from 3], shorts have covered 27% in a month to 8.7% of float with borrow under 0.5% [8], and the options book is already positioned for upside with 11,800 contracts of Sep-18 calls struck 14–25% out of the money [computed from 9]. A $295m+ revenue print with FY27 taken above $1.15bn into a de-risked cash equity has a credible path to the upper half of the 13% implied move, and the four historical up-moves averaged +10.3%.

**Bear.** The guide itself is the problem: $284–286m is only +17.3–18.1% y/y against +24.6% in Q1, and the FY27 range of $1.130–1.140bn implies roughly +15% in H2 [6][19][18] — the exact deceleration arithmetic that caused a 6.41% drop on the March print despite a beat [3][29]. The margin story pauses this quarter by design: non-GAAP operating income is guided at $38–39m, *below* Q1's $40.8m, with R&D up 27% y/y, a CFO-flagged shift of Q1 expense timing into Q2, and an explicit statement that AI investment "will precede the benefits" [30][12] — a sequential margin decline is the one thing a market that just re-rated software on profitable growth is least willing to forgive. The options are pricing 13.0% against a six-quarter mean absolute realised move of 9.09% and median 8.71% [computed from 3 and 9], so the buyer of the event is paying a ~1.45x premium and needs a large move merely to break even; the June print showed exactly how that fails, with +14% after hours collapsing to a +4.13% close [15][3][13]. Positioning is one-sided — put/call OI of 0.13 on the front month and calls trading 8.5 vol points over equivalent-delta puts [computed from 9], corroborated by ORTEX's 0.35 put/call, 1.4σ below its 20-day average [8] — which means an in-line print unwinds into an IV crush from ~85% to ~64% with no short base to cover. Underneath, GAAP economics remain poor: a $25.8m operating loss, SBC at 20.3% of revenue, negative Q1 free cash flow, 5.2% y/y share-count growth and no buyback [12], against a demand backdrop where renovation spend growth is halving and July web traffic fell 6.5% m/m in peak season [36][34].

**Base case.** My read is that ServiceTitan beats — revenue in the $291–298m band (+20% to +23% y/y), non-GAAP EPS $0.44–0.50, Max locations roughly doubling as pre-committed — and raises FY27 revenue by $15–25m to about $1.148–1.162bn, because the guide-beat cadence has been mechanical, the smoke-season and business-day tailwinds are real and quantified, and the CFO's stated practice of "managing the business on a full year basis" and not rolling quarterly outperformance forward means the raise will be smaller than the quarter's beat [30][23][6]. The market's reaction then hinges on whether the raise clears the deceleration optics. My estimate is that it roughly does, and that a de-risked, short-covered, under-owned equity 32% off its high with an unambiguously positive peer read-through from Samsara trades up — but modestly relative to a 13% implied move, in the +3% to +8% range, with the after-hours print likely to overshoot and fade as it did in June. The distribution is fat-tailed and asymmetric on the downside: an unchanged FY27 guide, or a sequential non-GAAP operating margin decline that management cannot frame as investment, produces a −10% to −15% day because there is no short base to buy it back and the call book has nothing to defend.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **not** a revenue miss — that has never happened here. It is **a beat with an unraised (or token-raised) FY27 revenue guide, accompanied by a sequential non-GAAP operating margin decline that management attributes to AI/inference cost of goods rather than to discretionary investment.**

Concretely: revenue prints $290m+, EPS beats, and management leaves FY27 at $1.130–1.140bn or nudges it to $1.140–1.150bn while reporting non-GAAP operating income of $38–40m (13.3–14.0% margin, down from 15.2% in Q1) and describing gross-margin pressure from serving Max inference. That combination would tell the market three things at once: that the guide-beat cadence has stopped, that the H2 deceleration to ~+15% is now management's real view rather than conservatism, and — most damagingly — that AI is a *cost* line at ServiceTitan rather than a revenue line. That is the SaaSpocalypse thesis validated from the inside by the company the bulls hold up as the counter-example (KeyBanc and Piper Sandler both build their long case on exactly this point [29]). With put/call OI at 0.13, IV collapsing from 85% to ~64%, no short base to cover and 11,800 far-OTM calls expiring worthless, the mechanical unwind would be severe: I would expect −12% to −18%, i.e. through the low end of the implied move, and a re-test of the $70–75 area that framed the March–June trough.

The secondary flip — lower probability, higher severity — is a disclosed change to the payments relationship. A single third-party processor is 12% of revenue and 18% of receivables [12]; any commentary that repricing, disintermediation or a partner change threatens the usage line would hit the fastest-growing (+29% y/y) and highest-narrative-value part of the P&L.
