# SAIL — SailPoint, Inc.

**What this print is about.** SailPoint reports fiscal Q2 2027 (quarter ended 31 July 2026) before the open on Wednesday 9 September 2026, call at 08:30 ET [1][2]. This is not an EPS event — adjusted EPS is guided at $0.07–$0.08 on a ~$10.7bn market cap, so the P&L line is rounding error. It is a **forward-ARR-credibility event**. The last three prints all beat every guided metric and the stock fell on all three (−7.73%, −15.23%, −11.48%), each time on the forward guide rather than the quarter [computed from OHLC, 22]. Management guided Q2 total ARR to $1,218–1,222m, which is **flat year-over-year net new ARR** ($55–59m vs $57m in Q2 FY26) — a comparison that is mechanically depressed by a sequential dollar rally, and which management already had to explain away once in June with a "constant currency" bridge that the market rejected by taking the stock down 11.5% [8][23]. Meanwhile Okta printed a blowout on 26 August and rose ~20–29%, dragging SAIL up 12.24% in one session before it gave most of it back [16][17][22]. So the setup is: a de-rated, heavily shorted, thin-float name into a hostile own-history base rate, a hot sector narrative it has only partly captured, and an options market charging ±16% for the answer.

**Event confirmed: YES** (company press release, 13 August 2026, plus company IR page) [1][2].

**Note on the triage rationale.** The starting hint said "only 5 analysts". That is wrong. Coverage is **20–25 analysts** depending on the vendor (25 per S&P Global via stockanalysis.com; 20 issuing ratings in the last 3 months per TipRanks) [11][20]. The name is well covered and has had **seven price-target raises between 10 August and 1 September 2026** [20][12]. The "under-modelled" premise does not hold; the "ARR/NRR trajectory and peer read-throughs matter more than EPS" premise absolutely does.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Reported net new total ARR versus the $55–59m implied by guidance, and the FY27 ARR range that accompanies it.**

Not EPS. Not headline revenue. Here is why, and here is the arithmetic that makes it the crux.

- Q1 FY26 ARR $925m → Q2 FY26 ARR $982m = **$57m net new** [25][9].
- Q1 FY27 ARR $1,163m → Q2 FY27 guide $1,218–1,222m = **$55–59m net new** [8].
- So the guide embeds **zero year-over-year growth in net new ARR** at the midpoint, for a company that just told investors at Investor Day it will compound to $2.1bn ARR by FY29 [5]. Flat net new ARR and a $2.1bn three-year target are hard to hold in the same hand, and the market has already shown it will not.

**The FX mechanics, which I believe are under-appreciated and which I have quantified myself (this is my inference, built on sourced inputs):**

ARR is a point-in-time metric struck at spot FX, so *net new* ARR absorbs the whole intra-quarter currency move applied to the non-USD installed base.

| Quarter | Intra-quarter EUR/USD | Intra-quarter DXY | Effect on reported net new ARR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 FY26 (Jan'25→Apr'25) | +8.93% | −8.21% | large tailwind |
| Q2 FY26 (Apr'25→Jul'25) | +0.87% | +0.56% | ~neutral |
| Q1 FY27 (Jan'26→Apr'26) | −1.98% | +1.12% | headwind |
| **Q2 FY27 (Apr'26→Jul'26)** | **−1.75%** | **+1.75%** | **headwind** |

Source: FX closes at each fiscal quarter-end [26]. This exactly explains the Q1 anomaly management flagged — net new SaaS ARR "+5% as reported, over 30% constant currency" [23] — because Q1 FY27 was a headwind quarter comped against a quarter with a ~9% euro tailwind.

Sizing it for Q2 FY27: non-US revenue was 36% of total in Q1 FY27 (US $178.8m, EMEA $61.4m, RoW $39.9m of $280.1m) [3]. Using that as a proxy for the ARR mix, the non-USD ARR base is ~$419m. A 1.75% adverse move costs ~**$7.3m**; last year's +0.87% *added* ~$3.6m. **That is an ~$11m year-over-year swing against a ~$57m net new ARR number — roughly 19 points of net new ARR "growth" erased by currency alone.** On constant currency, net new ARR is plausibly growing ~18–20% while printing ~0% as reported.

**The critical, non-obvious corollary:** FX between the guidance date (2026-06-09: EUR/USD 1.1535, DXY 99.91) and the ARR measurement date (2026-07-31: EUR/USD 1.1524, DXY 99.80) moved **−0.1%**, i.e. essentially nothing [26]. So there is **no FX-driven miss risk against the guide itself, and no FX-driven upside either**. Management set the guide with the rate it got. The FX story is purely a year-over-year optics problem that they have already pre-telegraphed once. Whether the market extends credit for the constant-currency bridge on the second telling is the single binary this print turns on.

**Secondary metrics that will move the stock:** net revenue retention (113% in Q1 FY27, down from 114% in Q3 FY26 — a third consecutive tick down would be read badly) [4][6]; customers >$1m ARR (225, +32% YoY in Q1 FY27, decelerating from +48% in Q2 FY26 and +62% in Q1 FY26) [4][9][25]; and **any first quantification of agentic/non-human-identity ARR**, which the company has so far refused to size.

1. **Entro Security acquired** — announced 2026-06-15, closed 2026-06-29 [27][28]. Tel Aviv non-human-identity/secrets-security business folded into the "Agentic Fabric". A press report puts the price at "about $200m"; **I could not confirm the consideration from a company or SEC source** and treat it as unsourced. It will add inorganic ARR and cost into Q2/Q3 — a possible source of noise in the ARR number and a possible source of an ARR "beat" that the buy side discounts.
2. **SailPoint Identity Security launched 2026-08-04** — Agentic Fabric now generally available, paired with "Human Fabric"; inline prompt security, an agent kill-switch, human-ownership attribution [29].
3. **Investor Day 2026-06-17**: FY29 ARR $2.1bn, AI ARR >$800m by FY28, adj op margin 22%+, FCF $400m+, TAM widened to $90bn; Q2 and FY27 guidance reaffirmed [5][30].
4. **GitHub repository breach disclosed** — unauthorised access detected 2026-04-20, furnished under Item 7.01 on 2026-05-08. Root cause a third-party application vulnerability; no evidence customer data in production/staging was accessed; affected customers notified directly [31]. For an identity-security vendor this is reputationally awkward and could surface as a competitive-displacement question on the call. It has not, so far, appeared in any sell-side note I found.

**What is at stake.** The bear case on fundamentals is a mix shift the P&L cannot hide: term subscriptions (recognised largely up front) were $58.1m and +78% in Q2 FY26 and only $43.9m and +10% in Q1 FY27 [9][8]. That single line is the reason revenue growth decelerates from 33% (Q2 FY26) to a guided 17–18%, and the reason adjusted operating margin is guided **down** year-over-year (18.1–18.7% vs 20.4%) despite a bigger revenue base [8][9]. My inference: to hit the $310m revenue midpoint with SaaS at ~+35% and maintenance at ~−8%, term subscriptions must be roughly flat-to-down against the $58.1m comp. If they are worse than that, revenue misses even with SaaS fine — and the company will (correctly) say the SaaS transition is working while the stock falls anyway. That is precisely the March 2026 script.

## 7. Forensics

**Form 4 activity — reads clean, and the headline "insider selling" number is misleading.**

On 7–8 July 2026 **seven officers sold simultaneously**: CEO Mark McClain (211,454 sh at $15.42–$16.73), CFO Brian Carolan (78,470), President Matt Mills (77,797), EVP Product & CTO Chandrasekar Gnanasambandam (34,418), General Counsel Chris Schmitt (42,830), Chief People Officer Abby Payne (38,541), CAO Mitra Rezvan (6,835) [37]. On the face of it that is alarming. It is not: McClain's Form 4 carries an explicit footnote that the trades "were executed under a Rule 10b5-1 trading plan and pursuant to a **mandatory sell-to-cover provision** in the Reporting Person's underlying Restricted Stock Unit Agreement for the satisfaction of tax withholding obligations in connection with the vesting of restricted stock units and consequently **do not represent discretionary trades**" [37]. The corresponding Form 144 confirms the source shares were RSUs that vested 2026-07-05 [38]. The identical pattern recurs on the same calendar rhythm at every vest date: 6–8 January 2026, 6 March 2026, 7–9 April 2026, 7–9 July 2026 [19].

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The identity-security cycle just visibly inflected and SailPoint has not been paid for it. Okta beat on both growth and margin and added ~29% in a session on an explicitly agentic-AI thesis; CrowdStrike grew net new ARR 51% [16][17]. SAIL captured 12.24% of that in one day and has since given all of it back, so nothing is priced in [22]. The mechanical FX drag on reported net new ARR is ~$11m year-over-year (§3), so a merely in-line $1,222m ARR print is ~20% constant-currency net new growth, and management now has a second, better-rehearsed chance to make that bridge. Agentic Fabric went generally available on 2026-08-04 and Entro closed 2026-06-29 [27][29]; a first quantification of AI/non-human-identity ARR against the FY28 ">$800m AI ARR" target [30] would be a genuinely new disclosure into a chain with 2,407 open $20 calls and 1,003 open $25 calls, and 28% of a 71m-share free float short at 78% utilisation [24][11][12]. The stock is 22% below its 52-week high with a $20.06–20.74 consensus PT after seven raises in three weeks [11][20]. Evidence: [16][17][22][8][3][24][11][12][27][29][30].

**Bear case.** This company has beaten every guided metric for three consecutive quarters and lost 7.7%, 15.2% and 11.5% on the day, because what it sells the market is the forward number and the forward number keeps decelerating [22][8][9][13]. Q2 guidance embeds **flat year-over-year net new ARR** ($55–59m vs $57m) and a **lower year-over-year operating margin** (18.1–18.7% vs 20.4%) [8][9][25]. Revenue growth halves from 33% to 17–18% because the +78% term-subscription comp is uncomparable [9]. The FY27 EPS guide has not moved off $0.30–$0.34 in two quarters [13][8]. NRR has ticked from 114% to 113% and >$1m-ARR customer growth has decelerated from +62% to +48% to +32% [4][6][9][25]. Seven price-target raises in three weeks have lifted the bar into the print — ORTEX's own headline is "analyst upgrades set a high bar" [12] — while retail sentiment has *fallen* to neutral [18]. And the sector template of the last fortnight punished exactly this profile: PANW −10.3% and ZS −7.8% over five sessions after beats with decelerating guides [45][22]. Evidence: [22][8][9][13][4][6][25][12][18][45].

**Base case (mine).** SailPoint delivers Q2 at or slightly above the top of guidance — revenue ~$311–314m, adjusted EPS $0.08, ARR ~$1,222–1,228m — nudges the FY27 ARR range up by another $5–10m, and guides Q3 revenue to roughly $328–334m. Net new ARR prints ~flat year-over-year as reported and management leads with a constant-currency bridge and an agentic-pipeline anecdote. The market gets a company doing exactly what it said, in a group where the marginal buyer is currently paying only for acceleration. Given a 1-up/5-down base rate, a 16% implied move that is ~1.85× mean realised, and an upside-skewed but thin options book sitting on a 28%-of-float short, I expect a **wide, gap-driven, two-sided open that resolves modestly negative** — call it a −4% to −9% close with a fat right tail. I am deliberately not confident: the honest read is that direction here is close to a coin flip weighted ~57/43 to the downside by base rate alone, and the magnitude conviction (realised < implied) is far stronger than the direction conviction.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal, stated concretely: **SailPoint quantifies agentic/non-human-identity ARR for the first time and raises the FY27 total ARR range to $1,385m or higher.**

Two things have to happen together, and only together. (a) A **hard number on AI/agentic ARR** — the FY28 target is ">$800m AI ARR" [30] and the company has so far given only directional colour ("50% increase in customers adopting non-human identity capabilities", "agentic pipeline doubled") [23]. Putting an actual dollar figure on it converts the Agentic Fabric from a story into a line item and re-anchors the multiple on the FY29 $2.1bn target rather than on this year's 17% revenue growth. (b) **An FY27 ARR raise of $11m or more** (to ≥$1,385m from $1,364–1,374m), which is more than the token $8m raise the market rejected in June and enough to say the FY29 path is being pulled forward rather than back-loaded.

If both land, the mechanics are violent in SAIL's favour: 20m shares short against a 71m free float at 78% utilisation, an options book already stacked in $20/$22.5/$25 calls with the upside wing bid harder than the downside, and a Street whose average target ($20.06–20.74) sits only 7–10% above spot and would be raised same-day by most of the eight desks that just moved [11][12][20][24]. That combination could produce a move well beyond the +16% implied — the 2025-06-11 precedent is a +14.9% gap that held all day [22].

Conversely the bear thesis flips against *me* if reported net new ARR simply exceeds $59m — i.e. it grows year-over-year *despite* the ~$11m FX drag — because that would mean constant-currency net new ARR accelerated ~35%+, which is Okta-like and would break the deceleration narrative outright.
