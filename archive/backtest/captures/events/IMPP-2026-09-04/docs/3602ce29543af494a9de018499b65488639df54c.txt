The margin of safety is wide. Eyes open.    *Author estimates only. Not investment advice.*  Part 1 - Who is Imperial Petroleum ?     Imperial Petroleum (   IMPP    0.00%↑       ) is a Greek shipping company listed on NASDAQ. It owns tankers and drybulk vessels, earns money chartering them out, and is controlled by Harry Vafias, a well-known figure in Greek shipping with a reputation that divides opinion, which we will get into shortly.   The company was spun out of StealthGas Inc. in December 2021 with just four vessels. Since then it has grown aggressively, almost entirely without bank debt, reaching 20 vessels by early 2026 with six more contracted for delivery through the year. From 4 ships to 26 in five years, zero leverage, that is genuinely unusual in an industry that typically runs on borrowed money.  As of March 2026, the fleet stands at 20 vessels with roughly 1.3 million deadweight tons of capacity: seven MR product tankers, two Suezmax crude carriers, and eleven drybulk ships ranging from handysizes to a Post-Panamax. One of the two Suezmaxes, Suez Enchanted, was reportedly sold in late April 2026 for approximately $48 million, leaving a single crude tanker going forward.                                      The fleet strategy is deliberately split. Tankers run on a mix of spot exposure and short-term time charters, capturing rate spikes when they happen. The drybulk side runs almost entirely on rolling quarterly time charters, providing a stable earnings base. In Q4 2025, around 71% of fleet days were on time charter and 29% spot.  One thing worth flagging: this is not a young fleet. The MR tankers were mostly built in Korea between 2007 and 2011. The Suezmaxes are 2007 and 2008 builds. The drybulk ships are predominantly 2009 to 2013 Japanese-built vessels. Average fleet age sits around 14 to 15 years. In normal conditions this matters, older vessels get discounted by charterers preferring modern eco-ships. In the current dislocated rate environment, charterers are less selective. But it remains a structural consideration for the medium term, and one we will return to in the risks section.  Full year 2025 revenues came in at $161 million with net income of $50 million and operating cash flow of $80.8 million. As of December 31, 2025, the company held $179 million in cash and time deposits with zero bank debt. It is worth noting that through most of 2025, performance was actually solid but unspectacular, tanker rates were weaker than 2024 and the first three quarters saw revenues decline year-on-year. The company was profitable, not exceptional. The recovery was already underway before any geopolitical shock: Suezmax rates had been strengthening through Q4 2025, which is what drove that quarter's revenue to $51 million, nearly equal to the full year two years prior. What the closure of the Strait of Hormuz in late February 2026 did was turbocharge a market that was already moving. We cover that in the next section.  Part 2 - The Market Context                To understand why Imperial Petroleum is interesting right now, you need to understand what is happening in the two markets it operates in: tankers and drybulk. Both are in strong shape. For different reasons, and on different timelines.  Tankers: a market already moving before the war                Tanker rates had already been recovering through the second half of 2025 before anyone started talking about the Strait of Hormuz. Suezmax rates strengthened meaningfully in Q4 2025, driven by ongoing Red Sea disruptions forcing longer routing around the Cape of Good Hope, continued sanctions on Russian crude creating inefficiencies in global supply chains, and generally tightening spot availability. This is what pushed IMPP’s Q4 2025 revenue to $51 million in a single quarter. Deutsche Bank confirmed that by Q1 2026, modern Suezmax spot TCE rates were already averaging approximately $118,530 per day, up 59.8% quarter on quarter, with much of that move happening before the conflict escalated.  Then on February 28, 2026, the United States and Israel launched strikes on Iran. The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly 20% of the world’s seaborne crude transits, effectively shut down. The US Navy instituted a blockade of Iran’s coastline in mid-April, firing on an Iranian-flagged containership that attempted to breach it. A temporary ceasefire broke down. Iran continued pressing for the right to levy fees and conduct inspections on vessels crossing the Strait. What followed was one of the most extraordinary tanker rate environments in modern history.  VLCC rates hit an all-time record of $423,736 per day. Suezmax and Aframax earnings climbed above $300,000 per day. LR2 product tankers reached $226,711 per day as of the week of April 20, up 906% year on year according to Deutsche Bank. MR product tanker rates surged past $100,000 per day in early April before moderating to approximately $64,600 per day by April 20, still up 267% year on year. Pareto Securities raised their full year 2026 MR rate forecast by 54% to $40,000 per day and noted their estimates were 40 to 125% ahead of consensus on Q2 earnings.   Both 160,000 DWT crude carriers, fully exposed, with Suezmax spot rates averaging approximately $155,000 per day as of late April and Evercore ISI estimating a Q2 blended average of $165,000 per day for the segment. The late April sale of Suez Enchanted for $48 million crystallised peak asset values while they were available, a rational capital allocation decision even if it reduces future upside. The remaining Suez Protopia stays on spot.  The MR product tanker picture has been exceptional, though rates have moderated off the early April peak. Atlantic MR rates broke above $100,000 per day in early April before pulling back to approximately $64,600 per day as of April 20. That is still 267% higher year on year and historically extraordinary by any measure. Lloyd’s List described the Atlantic product tanker rally as uncharted territory. Pareto notes that clean petroleum products on water are 3% higher year on year despite lower loadings out of the Middle East, because the dramatic increase in West-to-East trade distances is absorbing the capacity that would otherwise be released. Tonne-miles are winning where volumes are losing.  One important caveat is worth stating plainly. In a note published April 21, Evercore ISI downgraded several crude tanker names citing what it calls reversion risk. Their argument is straightforward: the Hormuz disruption is temporary, resolution is a question of when not if, and investors historically fade record rates well before the actual earnings peak arrives. The same dynamic played out in spring 2020 when tanker stocks peaked in early May despite earnings not peaking until Q2. Notably however, Evercore maintained Outperform ratings on product tanker stocks including Scorpio Tankers and TORM, arguing that product tankers have more constrained orderbooks at 17.2% of fleet versus crude tankers at 22.1%, and greater sustainable rate floors once the conflict normalises. Evercore’s full year 2026 MR rate estimate sits at $40,100 per day, implying a significant H2 fade from current levels but still historically strong. IMPP’s MR fleet sits squarely in this more resilient product tanker category.  Drybulk: a structural bull market building beneath the surface                While tankers have captured most of the headlines, the drybulk market was already exhibiting something historically unusual before the conflict: counter-seasonal strength. January and February are historically the weakest months for dry bulk rates because Chinese New Year curbs industrial production, and China accounts for approximately 47% of total cargo mile demand for bulkers. Yet January 2026 saw the highest BDI reading since 2010, with February showing even greater strength. As Value Investor’s Edge noted in February 2026, when rates stay elevated during seasonal weakness it typically signals a structural shift in supply and demand, not a temporary spike. Capesize rates as of the week of April 20 were up 29% week on week to $33,928 per day, up 132% year on year. Supramax rates stood at $22,786 per day, up 97% year on year.  The bull case for drybulk in 2026 and beyond rests on two reinforcing legs.  On the supply side, the dry bulk orderbook stands at just 12.9% of the existing fleet according to Deutsche Bank, the most constrained of any major shipping segment and well below the crude tanker orderbook at 22.1%. More importantly, the large cohort of vessels delivered between 2009 and 2012 is now crossing the 15-year threshold where charterer preference begins to decline meaningfully. Research from Value Investor’s Edge shows vessels under 15 years old represent 64.69% of the Capesize fleet by number but carry 80.6% of all cargo loaded. As that 2009 to 2012 cohort ages out of preferred status, the effective desirable fleet is shrinking faster than headline numbers suggest.  On the demand side, the Simandou iron ore project in Guinea shipped its first cargo to China in December 2025. By 2030 it is projected to supply up to 120 million tonnes annually, a 4.8% increase to an already well-supplied market that keeps iron ore prices low for Chinese steel mills. But the shipping angle matters more than the price angle: Guinea to China is one of the longest haul routes in existence, meaning every tonne shipped generates outsized cargo mile demand. Guinea-sourced bauxite has already made that lane the third most important Capesize route globally, and iron ore volumes will add substantially to that through the decade.  Now, IMPP has no Capesize vessels. But the Capesize bull market feeds directly into its Supramax earnings through a specific mechanism. Iron ore arrives in China on Capesizes, but the steel produced from that iron ore is exported on midsize vessels like Supramaxes. Chinese steel exports drive over 57% of all Supramax demand originating from China. 2025 saw Chinese seaborne steel exports post a new annual record, with December setting an all-time monthly high. Vehicle exports are growing even faster, up 73% year on year in December 2025, with the BEV supply chain requiring bauxite, lithium, copper and rare earths that all move on drybulk vessels. China’s latest five-year plan elevated a modernised industrial system to its number one priority, reinforcing the structural demand case.  The result as of the April 22 Baltic Exchange report: the Baltic Supramax Index stood at 1,484 points with average daily earnings of $18,760 per day. IMPP’s five Supramaxes and two Kamsarmaxes were rolling their quarterly time charters in March directly into this environment.  Two markets, both strong, for different but complementary reasons. That is the backdrop against which Imperial Petroleum is operating right now.  Part 3 - The Governance Issue                This is the part of the article where we have to be honest. Imperial Petroleum has real governance issues, and any serious analysis of the stock has to address them directly. They do not make IMPP uninvestable. But they do explain a meaningful part of why the stock trades at such a steep discount to peers and to its own intrinsic value.  Harry Vafias controls the company outright                In October 2022, Harry Vafias purchased 16,000 shares of a newly-created Series B Preferred Stock for $200,000. Each of those shares carries 25,000 votes. That is 400 million votes in total, capped by the company charter at 49.99% of total voting power regardless of how many common shares are outstanding. In practice, Vafias can never be outvoted by public shareholders on any matter. He controls the board, controls major decisions, and controls his own compensation arrangements.  This is not unusual in Greek shipping structures. But it is worth being clear about what it means for minority investors: you have essentially no say in how this company is run.  To be fair, Vafias also has genuine skin in the game. As of December 2025, he directly and indirectly owned 13.45 million common shares, representing 30.1% of the outstanding stock, and has not sold a single share since the company’s inception. His economic interests are substantially aligned with outside shareholders. The issue is not that he has no incentive to grow the business. It is that his voting power far exceeds his economic ownership, and minority shareholders have no meaningful recourse if they disagree with how capital is being allocated.  The Stealth Group: manager, counterparty and supplier all at once                IMPP was spun out of StealthGas in December 2021, also controlled by the Vafias family. Since inception, all of IMPP’s vessels have been managed by Stealth Maritime Corporation S.A., a private company controlled by the Vafias family. Stealth Maritime handles everything: crewing, day-to-day operations, maintenance, drydocking, insurance, chartering and back-office functions. For drybulk vessels, Stealth Maritime subcontracts to another Vafias affiliate, Brave Maritime.  The fee structure is disclosed in the 20-F. IMPP pays Stealth Maritime $440 per vessel per day for vessels on voyage or time charter, plus 1.25% of all gross freight, demurrage and charter hire collected, plus 1.0% of the contract price on every vessel bought or sold. On a 20-vessel fleet generating $161 million in revenue, those fees add up to a meaningful ongoing transfer to related entities. The 1% sale commission alone means Stealth Maritime earned approximately $480,000 on the Suez Enchanted sale.  Beyond management fees, a significant portion of IMPP’s vessel acquisitions have been purchased directly from Vafias family affiliates. In August 2025, IMPP agreed to acquire three drybulk carriers from Brave Maritime, a Vafias family entity, for $51.6 million, with 10% of the purchase price payable in IMPP shares at the 30-day VWAP and the remaining 90% interest-free for up to one year. The transaction was described as having been approved by independent directors, but the counterparty was unambiguously a family affiliate. The $164.7 million in seller financing repaid by IMPP during 2025 largely flowed to related parties under similar arrangements across nine vessel acquisitions.  Whether these vessels were acquired at fair market value is impossible to verify from public filings. Independent valuations are not disclosed.  The December 2025 equity raise                On November 28, 2025, IMPP raised $60 million through a registered direct offering at $6.30 per share, placing stock with just two institutional investors. The sole placement agent was Maxim Group, the same firm that is the only sell-side analyst covering IMPP with a Buy rating and that has provided investment banking services to the company. The offering announcement noted proceeds would be used for working capital and general corporate purposes, at a time when the company already held approximately $179 million in cash.  Alongside the shares came two warrant tranches, Class F and Class G, each giving the holders the right to buy an additional 9.5 million shares at $6.30, immediately exercisable and valid for five years. That is 19 million warrants in total, representing roughly 42% potential dilution on today’s share count if fully exercised.  The stock today trades at $4.80. The offering was done at $6.30, which is 31% above the current price. Existing shareholders were diluted at a premium that has since fully evaporated. The warrants are out of the money for now, but they represent a structural ceiling. Any sustained rally toward $6.30 means those two institutional holders can issue new shares into the market at zero effective cost, creating natural resistance at that level.  A $10 million share buyback was announced on February 9, 2026. As of March 2026, $0.9 million had been deployed.  What to make of all this                None of this is illegal. Most of it is disclosed in the SEC filings. Greek shipping families have operated this way for decades and the Vafias group has a long track record of building and running ships profitably.  But investors should go in with eyes open. The related-party fee flows are structural and permanent. Vafias cannot be removed by a shareholder vote. And the capital structure decisions, taken together, suggest that minority shareholder interests are not the primary consideration in how this company is managed.  The question is not whether these governance issues exist. They clearly do. The question is whether the current share price already reflects them and whether the earnings power of the fleet at current rates is large enough to make the stock compelling regardless. That is what the next two sections attempt to answer.  Part 4 — What Could IMPP Actually Earn in 2026?                This is where the article moves from context to numbers. The estimates below are my own, built from the ground up using publicly available fleet data, Baltic Exchange rate fixings, and broker research. They are not consensus estimates, not investment advice, and not guarantees. The rate assumptions in particular involve significant uncertainty and anyone who tells you they can predict tanker rates six months out with precision is selling something. What follows is a structured attempt to frame the range of outcomes.  Rate assumptions                For the tanker side, Suezmax spot rates as of late April sit at approximately $155,000 per day per Deutsche Bank. Evercore ISI’s Q2 blended estimate for the segment is $165,000 per day. I have used a more conservative $130,000 per day Q2 blended assumption to account for the fact that the Suez Protopia was not necessarily fixed at peak rates for the full quarter and that some days will have been lost to repositioning. For H2 I assume a significant but not catastrophic reversion: $60,000 per day in Q3 and $55,000 per day in Q4, consistent with Evercore’s scenario of gradual normalisation rather than an immediate return to pre-war levels.  For MR product tankers, Atlantic spot rates peaked above $100,000 per day in early April and currently sit around $64,600 per day as of April 20. Evercore’s full year 2026 MR estimate is $40,100 per day, which I consider too conservative given the structural tonne-mile gains Pareto has identified in the product tanker market. I have used a blended $50,000 per day full year average, reflecting a strong Q2, a moderating H2, but a higher floor than Evercore assumes. The two MRs on time charter are assumed fixed at approximately $28,000 per day.  For drybulk, I have anchored to Baltic Exchange actuals from April 22: Supramax at $18,760 per day, Handysize at $14,050 per day, and Kamsarmax and Post-Panamax at approximately $17,744 per day using the Baltic Panamax Index as a proxy. These are rolling quarterly time charter rates, so the drybulk revenue line is relatively stable and predictable regardless of short-term market moves.  All utilisation assumptions are set at 90% to account for off-hire, positioning days and drydocking periods. The six new vessels contracted for delivery through 2026 are phased in from Q2 onward.  The P&amp;L                On those assumptions, IMPP’s full year 2026 revenue comes to approximately $196 million, with EBITDA of approximately $84 million excluding the Suez Enchanted vessel sale gain. Including the estimated $30 million sale gain, net income reaches approximately $89 million, or roughly $1.98 per share on the current 45 million share count.                                      Q2 2026 is the standout quarter. Two Suezmaxes earning elevated rates for the full period, five MR tankers on spot in the strongest market in years, and an estimated $30 million vessel sale gain all landing in the same three months. Evercore described its Q2 expectations for the tanker sector as eye-watering. For IMPP, Q2 alone could deliver net income approaching $51 million, more than the company earned in all of 2025.  H2 is where the conservatism bites. With Suez Enchanted sold and Suez Protopia assumed to earn significantly lower rates as the market normalises, tanker revenue drops sharply in Q3 and Q4. The drybulk fleet partially cushions this, growing as new vessels deliver and providing a stable base from rolling quarterly charters. But the H2 numbers are unambiguously softer. This is not a company that will sustain Q2 economics through year-end.  The cash position                What makes the earnings story genuinely interesting is the balance sheet context. Starting the year with $179 million in cash, adding approximately $48 million from the Suez Enchanted sale, generating roughly $84 million in operating cash flow across 2026, and spending approximately $78 million on remaining vessel delivery commitments, IMPP should end 2026 with cash and time deposits of approximately $233 million. On 45 million shares, that is roughly $5.18 per share in cash at year-end, against a current stock price of $4.80.  The company will, in other words, finish with more cash this year than its entire current market capitalisation, while simultaneously delivering six new vessels and maintaining a zero-debt balance sheet.  The sensitivity                                                    The table above shows how EBITDA moves across a range of Suezmax and MR rate scenarios, holding drybulk constant. The base case sits at $84 million. Even in the most bearish scenario modelled, with Suezmax averaging $80,000 per day for the full year and MR at $35,000 per day, EBITDA still comes to approximately $62 million. In the most bullish scenario, with rates staying elevated, EBITDA approaches $120 million.  The point of the sensitivity table is not to predict which scenario will occur. It is to illustrate that even in a significant rate reversion scenario, IMPP generates substantial cash relative to its entreprise value. The margin of safety is wide.  Part 5 — What Is It Worth?                This is the section that matters. We have established what IMPP does, why the market context is exceptional, what the governance risks are, and what the company could plausibly earn in 2026. Now we need to ask whether the current stock price makes sense given all of that.  The answer, across every framework I can apply, is no.  First: the EV situation                Let us start with the most striking number. IMPP’s market capitalisation at $4.80 per share is approximately $216 million. As of today, the company holds an estimated $246 million in cash and time deposits, after adding the roughly $48 million in Suez Enchanted sale proceeds to the $179 million year-end 2025 balance and before accounting for remaining vessel delivery commitments of approximately $78 million. Net of those commitments, committed net cash stands at roughly $168 million. Subtracting that from the market cap gives an enterprise value of approximately $48 million.  That means you are buying the entire operating business — a 20-vessel fleet generating roughly $84 million in EBITDA this year, with six more ships arriving through year-end — for less than $50 million. A single Suezmax tanker just sold for $48 million.  Put another way: IMPP’s EV/EBITDA on our base case is approximately 0.57x. Evercore’s coverage universe trades at a group average of 2.9x for 2026. Even applying a 50% governance discount to the low end of the peer range still implies an EV around $150 million and a stock price in the range of $7 to $8.  Second: an updated NAV                In December 2025, management published a formal NAV estimate of $508 million, or $11.38 per basic share. That was calculated on September 30, 2025 financial data, before the Hormuz conflict, before the Q4 rate surge, and before six new vessel deliveries. It also explicitly excluded the seven vessels contracted in August and December 2025. In other words, it was a conservative snapshot of a company that has since become significantly more valuable in both earnings and asset terms.  I have attempted to update that NAV using recent transaction data. The inputs below are my own estimates and should be treated as indicative rather than precise.  For the Suezmax fleet: Suez Enchanted sold for $48 million in late April 2026, a confirmed market transaction. Suez Protopia is a 2008 Korean-built sister vessel. Using the same $48 million as a reference gives a combined remaining Suezmax value of approximately $48 million for the single vessel still owned.  For the MR fleet: Compass Maritime’s weekly report from April 27, 2026 provides a useful age curve. A 2006-built Korean MR recently changed hands at $13.3 to $16.5 million. A 2014-built Korean MR with scrubber sold for $35 million. A 2015-built scrubber-fitted vessel also achieved $35 million. IMPP’s seven MRs are 2007 to 2011 Korean-built without scrubbers. Based on the age gradient implied by those transactions, and applying a modest discount for the absence of scrubbers, I estimate the fleet value at approximately $22 to $25 million per vessel depending on build year, with a blended average of roughly $23 million across the seven vessels. That gives a total MR fleet value of approximately $161 million.  For the drybulk fleet: the eleven vessels on the water are predominantly 2009 to 2013 Japanese-built handysizes, supramaxes and kamsarmaxes. Using a conservative $15 million for handysizes, $22 million for supramaxes and $28 million for the kamsarmaxes gives a blended drybulk fleet value of approximately $215 million across the eleven vessels. The six contracted vessels not yet delivered are excluded as they are offset by remaining payment obligations.  Adding those fleet values together and netting against the preferred share liquidation value of approximately $20 million and the remaining vessel delivery commitments of $78 million gives an updated estimated NAV of approximately $571 million, or roughly $12.70 per basic share on 45 million shares outstanding.  At $4.80, the stock trades at approximately 0.38x this updated NAV estimate. Even the fully diluted NAV per share, assuming all 19 million warrants are exercised at $6.30 and adding the $120 million in warrant proceeds to the balance sheet, implies a diluted NAV of approximately $691 million on 64 million shares, or roughly $10.80 per diluted share. At $4.80, that is still 0.44x.  For context, Evercore’s coverage universe of tanker stocks trades at P/NAV multiples ranging from 0.66x to 1.39x, with the product tanker names at 0.66 to 0.84x. IMPP at 0.37x to 0.44x represents a discount of 40 to 55% to even the cheapest comparable.  The warrant wall and what it means for the share price                Here is where the valuation argument gets more complicated, and where intellectual honesty requires acknowledging a structural constraint that most bull cases on IMPP tend to underplay.  In December 2025, IMPP placed $60 million of stock at $6.30 per share with two institutional investors, accompanied by 19 million warrants at the same $6.30 strike price, valid for five years. Those warrants are currently out of the money. But they represent approximately 42% of the current share count, and they create a well-defined ceiling for the stock.  As the stock approaches $6.30, the warrant holders can exercise and sell into any rally, effectively capping the price. There is no structural reason for the stock to sustain a price above $6.30 in the near term while those warrants remain outstanding, because the dilutive supply they represent would absorb any buying pressure near that level. This is not speculation, it is simple mechanics.  $6.30 therefore functions as a realistic near-term price ceiling, not a fair value floor. From the current price of $4.80, that represents approximately 31% upside. Not a bad return. But investors expecting a re-rating to full NAV of $11 to $13 will have to wait for one of two things: either the warrants are exercised and the overhang is cleared, at which point the company holds an additional $120 million in cash; or enough time passes that the market begins to look through the dilution risk. Neither is imminent.  Year-end cash per share: the simplest argument                Stripping away all of the above, the simplest version of the bull case is this. On our base case estimates, IMPP ends 2026 with approximately $233 million in cash and time deposits. On 45 million shares that is $5.18 per share. The current stock price is $4.80.  You are being asked to pay $4.80 for a company that will end the year holding more cash per share than the stock costs today, while simultaneously operating a fleet of 26 vessels and having generated the equivalent of the entire market cap in operating cash flow over the course of the year. The entire operating business is, at that point, free.  Verdict                Let me bring the threads together.  Imperial Petroleum is a 20-vessel Greek shipping company trading at $4.80 per share with an enterprise value of approximately $48 million. Its fleet generates an estimated $84 million in EBITDA this year. Management’s own NAV, calculated before the Hormuz conflict and before six new vessel deliveries, stood at $11.38 per basic share. An updated estimate using recent transaction comparables puts that figure closer to $13.00. The company will likely end 2026 holding more cash per share than the stock costs today.  The tanker market is in the middle of an extraordinary dislocation driven by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, but the rate strength predates the conflict. The MR product tanker orderbook is the most constrained of any major shipping segment. The drybulk fleet, overlooked by most commentators focused on the tanker story, is quietly benefiting from a structural bull market anchored in Simandou iron ore volumes and record Chinese steel and vehicle exports, with the Supramax segment directly exposed to the same Chinese industrial production story that drives Capesize demand.  The governance is genuinely problematic. Vafias controls the vote with a $200,000 investment in Series B shares, Stealth Maritime earns recurring fees as manager and counterparty simultaneously, and the December 2025 equity offering diluted shareholders at $6.30 with 19 million warrants attached. Those warrants now function as a structural ceiling on the stock price. The realistic near-term upside is 31%, to $6.30, not a re-rating to NAV.  None of this makes IMPP uninvestable. A 60% discount to updated NAV, sub-1x EV/EBITDA, zero debt and $5 of year-end cash per share against a $4.80 stock price is a genuinely compelling setup. But it requires accepting that you are a minority passenger in a vehicle driven by someone who cannot be removed and who earns fees regardless of how the stock performs.  The margin of safety is wide. Eyes open.  ---  A note to readers                This is my first piece on this platform, and I want to be transparent about what it is and what it is not. Imperial Petroleum (     ) is a Greek shipping company listed on NASDAQ. It owns tankers and drybulk vessels, earns money chartering them out, and is controlled by Harry Vafias, a well-known figure in Greek shipping with a reputation that divides opinion, which we will get into shortly.  The company was spun out of StealthGas Inc. in December 2021 with just four vessels. Since then it has grown aggressively, almost entirely without bank debt, reaching 20 vessels by early 2026 with six more contracted for delivery through the year. From 4 ships to 26 in five years, zero leverage, that is genuinely unusual in an industry that typically runs on borrowed money.  As of March 2026, the fleet stands at 20 vessels with roughly 1.3 million deadweight tons of capacity: seven MR product tankers, two Suezmax crude carriers, and eleven drybulk ships ranging from handysizes to a Post-Panamax. One of the two Suezmaxes, Suez Enchanted, was reportedly sold in late April 2026 for approximately $48 million, leaving a single crude tanker going forward.                                      The fleet strategy is deliberately split. Tankers run on a mix of spot exposure and short-term time charters, capturing rate spikes when they happen. The drybulk side runs almost entirely on rolling quarterly time charters, providing a stable earnings base. In Q4 2025, around 71% of fleet days were on time charter and 29% spot.  One thing worth flagging: this is not a young fleet. The MR tankers were mostly built in Korea between 2007 and 2011. The Suezmaxes are 2007 and 2008 builds. The drybulk ships are predominantly 2009 to 2013 Japanese-built vessels. Average fleet age sits around 14 to 15 years. In normal conditions this matters, older vessels get discounted by charterers preferring modern eco-ships. In the current dislocated rate environment, charterers are less selective. But it remains a structural consideration for the medium term, and one we will return to in the risks section.  Full year 2025 revenues came in at $161 million with net income of $50 million and operating cash flow of $80.8 million. As of December 31, 2025, the company held $179 million in cash and time deposits with zero bank debt. It is worth noting that through most of 2025, performance was actually solid but unspectacular, tanker rates were weaker than 2024 and the first three quarters saw revenues decline year-on-year. The company was profitable, not exceptional. The recovery was already underway before any geopolitical shock: Suezmax rates had been strengthening through Q4 2025, which is what drove that quarter's revenue to $51 million, nearly equal to the full year two years prior. What the closure of the Strait of Hormuz in late February 2026 did was turbocharge a market that was already moving. We cover that in the next section.  Part 2 - The Market Context  To understand why Imperial Petroleum is interesting right now, you need to understand what is happening in the two markets it operates in: tankers and drybulk. Both are in strong shape. For different reasons, and on different timelines.  Tankers: a market already moving before the war  Tanker rates had already been recovering through the second half of 2025 before anyone started talking about the Strait of Hormuz. Suezmax rates strengthened meaningfully in Q4 2025, driven by ongoing Red Sea disruptions forcing longer routing around the Cape of Good Hope, continued sanctions on Russian crude creating inefficiencies in global supply chains, and generally tightening spot availability. This is what pushed IMPP\u2019s Q4 2025 revenue to $51 million in a single quarter. Deutsche Bank confirmed that by Q1 2026, modern Suezmax spot TCE rates were already averaging approximately $118,530 per day, up 59.8% quarter on quarter, with much of that move happening before the conflict escalated.  Then on February 28, 2026, the United States and Israel launched strikes on Iran. The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly 20% of the world\u2019s seaborne crude transits, effectively shut down. The US Navy instituted a blockade of Iran\u2019s coastline in mid-April, firing on an Iranian-flagged containership that attempted to breach it. A temporary ceasefire broke down. Iran continued pressing for the right to levy fees and conduct inspections on vessels crossing the Strait. What followed was one of the most extraordinary tanker rate environments in modern history.  VLCC rates hit an all-time record of $423,736 per day. Suezmax and Aframax earnings climbed above $300,000 per day. LR2 product tankers reached $226,711 per day as of the week of April 20, up 906% year on year according to Deutsche Bank. MR product tanker rates surged past $100,000 per day in early April before moderating to approximately $64,600 per day by April 20, still up 267% year on year. Pareto Securities raised their full year 2026 MR rate forecast by 54% to $40,000 per day and noted their estimates were 40 to 125% ahead of consensus on Q2 earnings.   Both 160,000 DWT crude carriers, fully exposed, with Suezmax spot rates averaging approximately $155,000 per day as of late April and Evercore ISI estimating a Q2 blended average of $165,000 per day for the segment. The late April sale of Suez Enchanted for $48 million crystallised peak asset values while they were available, a rational capital allocation decision even if it reduces future upside. The remaining Suez Protopia stays on spot.  The MR product tanker picture has been exceptional, though rates have moderated off the early April peak. Atlantic MR rates broke above $100,000 per day in early April before pulling back to approximately $64,600 per day as of April 20. That is still 267% higher year on year and historically extraordinary by any measure. Lloyd\u2019s List described the Atlantic product tanker rally as uncharted territory. Pareto notes that clean petroleum products on water are 3% higher year on year despite lower loadings out of the Middle East, because the dramatic increase in West-to-East trade distances is absorbing the capacity that would otherwise be released. Tonne-miles are winning where volumes are losing.  One important caveat is worth stating plainly. In a note published April 21, Evercore ISI downgraded several crude tanker names citing what it calls reversion risk. Their argument is straightforward: the Hormuz disruption is temporary, resolution is a question of when not if, and investors historically fade record rates well before the actual earnings peak arrives. The same dynamic played out in spring 2020 when tanker stocks peaked in early May despite earnings not peaking until Q2. Notably however, Evercore maintained Outperform ratings on product tanker stocks including Scorpio Tankers and TORM, arguing that product tankers have more constrained orderbooks at 17.2% of fleet versus crude tankers at 22.1%, and greater sustainable rate floors once the conflict normalises. Evercore\u2019s full year 2026 MR rate estimate sits at $40,100 per day, implying a significant H2 fade from current levels but still historically strong. IMPP\u2019s MR fleet sits squarely in this more resilient product tanker category.  Drybulk: a structural bull market building beneath the surface  While tankers have captured most of the headlines, the drybulk market was already exhibiting something historically unusual before the conflict: counter-seasonal strength. January and February are historically the weakest months for dry bulk rates because Chinese New Year curbs industrial production, and China accounts for approximately 47% of total cargo mile demand for bulkers. Yet January 2026 saw the highest BDI reading since 2010, with February showing even greater strength. As Value Investor\u2019s Edge noted in February 2026, when rates stay elevated during seasonal weakness it typically signals a structural shift in supply and demand, not a temporary spike. Capesize rates as of the week of April 20 were up 29% week on week to $33,928 per day, up 132% year on year. Supramax rates stood at $22,786 per day, up 97% year on year.  The bull case for drybulk in 2026 and beyond rests on two reinforcing legs.  On the supply side, the dry bulk orderbook stands at just 12.9% of the existing fleet according to Deutsche Bank, the most constrained of any major shipping segment and well below the crude tanker orderbook at 22.1%. More importantly, the large cohort of vessels delivered between 2009 and 2012 is now crossing the 15-year threshold where charterer preference begins to decline meaningfully. Research from Value Investor\u2019s Edge shows vessels under 15 years old represent 64.69% of the Capesize fleet by number but carry 80.6% of all cargo loaded. As that 2009 to 2012 cohort ages out of preferred status, the effective desirable fleet is shrinking faster than headline numbers suggest.  On the demand side, the Simandou iron ore project in Guinea shipped its first cargo to China in December 2025. By 2030 it is projected to supply up to 120 million tonnes annually, a 4.8% increase to an already well-supplied market that keeps iron ore prices low for Chinese steel mills. But the shipping angle matters more than the price angle: Guinea to China is one of the longest haul routes in existence, meaning every tonne shipped generates outsized cargo mile demand. Guinea-sourced bauxite has already made that lane the third most important Capesize route globally, and iron ore volumes will add substantially to that through the decade.  Now, IMPP has no Capesize vessels. But the Capesize bull market feeds directly into its Supramax earnings through a specific mechanism. Iron ore arrives in China on Capesizes, but the steel produced from that iron ore is exported on midsize vessels like Supramaxes. Chinese steel exports drive over 57% of all Supramax demand originating from China. 2025 saw Chinese seaborne steel exports post a new annual record, with December setting an all-time monthly high. Vehicle exports are growing even faster, up 73% year on year in December 2025, with the BEV supply chain requiring bauxite, lithium, copper and rare earths that all move on drybulk vessels. China\u2019s latest five-year plan elevated a modernised industrial system to its number one priority, reinforcing the structural demand case.  The result as of the April 22 Baltic Exchange report: the Baltic Supramax Index stood at 1,484 points with average daily earnings of $18,760 per day. IMPP\u2019s five Supramaxes and two Kamsarmaxes were rolling their quarterly time charters in March directly into this environment.  Two markets, both strong, for different but complementary reasons. That is the backdrop against which Imperial Petroleum is operating right now.  Part 3 - The Governance Issue  This is the part of the article where we have to be honest. Imperial Petroleum has real governance issues, and any serious analysis of the stock has to address them directly. They do not make IMPP uninvestable. But they do explain a meaningful part of why the stock trades at such a steep discount to peers and to its own intrinsic value.  Harry Vafias controls the company outright  In October 2022, Harry Vafias purchased 16,000 shares of a newly-created Series B Preferred Stock for $200,000. Each of those shares carries 25,000 votes. That is 400 million votes in total, capped by the company charter at 49.99% of total voting power regardless of how many common shares are outstanding. In practice, Vafias can never be outvoted by public shareholders on any matter. He controls the board, controls major decisions, and controls his own compensation arrangements.  This is not unusual in Greek shipping structures. But it is worth being clear about what it means for minority investors: you have essentially no say in how this company is run.  To be fair, Vafias also has genuine skin in the game. As of December 2025, he directly and indirectly owned 13.45 million common shares, representing 30.1% of the outstanding stock, and has not sold a single share since the company\u2019s inception. His economic interests are substantially aligned with outside shareholders. The issue is not that he has no incentive to grow the business. It is that his voting power far exceeds his economic ownership, and minority shareholders have no meaningful recourse if they disagree with how capital is being allocated.  The Stealth Group: manager, counterparty and supplier all at once  IMPP was spun out of StealthGas in December 2021, also controlled by the Vafias family. Since inception, all of IMPP\u2019s vessels have been managed by Stealth Maritime Corporation S.A., a private company controlled by the Vafias family. Stealth Maritime handles everything: crewing, day-to-day operations, maintenance, drydocking, insurance, chartering and back-office functions. For drybulk vessels, Stealth Maritime subcontracts to another Vafias affiliate, Brave Maritime.  The fee structure is disclosed in the 20-F. IMPP pays Stealth Maritime $440 per vessel per day for vessels on voyage or time charter, plus 1.25% of all gross freight, demurrage and charter hire collected, plus 1.0% of the contract price on every vessel bought or sold. On a 20-vessel fleet generating $161 million in revenue, those fees add up to a meaningful ongoing transfer to related entities. The 1% sale commission alone means Stealth Maritime earned approximately $480,000 on the Suez Enchanted sale.  Beyond management fees, a significant portion of IMPP\u2019s vessel acquisitions have been purchased directly from Vafias family affiliates. In August 2025, IMPP agreed to acquire three drybulk carriers from Brave Maritime, a Vafias family entity, for $51.6 million, with 10% of the purchase price payable in IMPP shares at the 30-day VWAP and the remaining 90% interest-free for up to one year. The transaction was described as having been approved by independent directors, but the counterparty was unambiguously a family affiliate. The $164.7 million in seller financing repaid by IMPP during 2025 largely flowed to related parties under similar arrangements across nine vessel acquisitions.  Whether these vessels were acquired at fair market value is impossible to verify from public filings. Independent valuations are not disclosed.  The December 2025 equity raise  On November 28, 2025, IMPP raised $60 million through a registered direct offering at $6.30 per share, placing stock with just two institutional investors. The sole placement agent was Maxim Group, the same firm that is the only sell-side analyst covering IMPP with a Buy rating and that has provided investment banking services to the company. The offering announcement noted proceeds would be used for working capital and general corporate purposes, at a time when the company already held approximately $179 million in cash.  Alongside the shares came two warrant tranches, Class F and Class G, each giving the holders the right to buy an additional 9.5 million shares at $6.30, immediately exercisable and valid for five years. That is 19 million warrants in total, representing roughly 42% potential dilution on today\u2019s share count if fully exercised.  The stock today trades at $4.80. The offering was done at $6.30, which is 31% above the current price. Existing shareholders were diluted at a premium that has since fully evaporated. The warrants are out of the money for now, but they represent a structural ceiling. Any sustained rally toward $6.30 means those two institutional holders can issue new shares into the market at zero effective cost, creating natural resistance at that level.  A $10 million share buyback was announced on February 9, 2026. As of March 2026, $0.9 million had been deployed.  What to make of all this  None of this is illegal. Most of it is disclosed in the SEC filings. Greek shipping families have operated this way for decades and the Vafias group has a long track record of building and running ships profitably.  But investors should go in with eyes open. The related-party fee flows are structural and permanent. Vafias cannot be removed by a shareholder vote. And the capital structure decisions, taken together, suggest that minority shareholder interests are not the primary consideration in how this company is managed.  The question is not whether these governance issues exist. They clearly do. The question is whether the current share price already reflects them and whether the earnings power of the fleet at current rates is large enough to make the stock compelling regardless. That is what the next two sections attempt to answer.  Part 4 \u2014 What Could IMPP Actually Earn in 2026?  This is where the article moves from context to numbers. The estimates below are my own, built from the ground up using publicly available fleet data, Baltic Exchange rate fixings, and broker research. They are not consensus estimates, not investment advice, and not guarantees. The rate assumptions in particular involve significant uncertainty and anyone who tells you they can predict tanker rates six months out with precision is selling something. What follows is a structured attempt to frame the range of outcomes.  Rate assumptions  For the tanker side, Suezmax spot rates as of late April sit at approximately $155,000 per day per Deutsche Bank. Evercore ISI\u2019s Q2 blended estimate for the segment is $165,000 per day. I have used a more conservative $130,000 per day Q2 blended assumption to account for the fact that the Suez Protopia was not necessarily fixed at peak rates for the full quarter and that some days will have been lost to repositioning. For H2 I assume a significant but not catastrophic reversion: $60,000 per day in Q3 and $55,000 per day in Q4, consistent with Evercore\u2019s scenario of gradual normalisation rather than an immediate return to pre-war levels.  For MR product tankers, Atlantic spot rates peaked above $100,000 per day in early April and currently sit around $64,600 per day as of April 20. Evercore\u2019s full year 2026 MR estimate is $40,100 per day, which I consider too conservative given the structural tonne-mile gains Pareto has identified in the product tanker market. I have used a blended $50,000 per day full year average, reflecting a strong Q2, a moderating H2, but a higher floor than Evercore assumes. The two MRs on time charter are assumed fixed at approximately $28,000 per day.  For drybulk, I have anchored to Baltic Exchange actuals from April 22: Supramax at $18,760 per day, Handysize at $14,050 per day, and Kamsarmax and Post-Panamax at approximately $17,744 per day using the Baltic Panamax Index as a proxy. These are rolling quarterly time charter rates, so the drybulk revenue line is relatively stable and predictable regardless of short-term market moves.  All utilisation assumptions are set at 90% to account for off-hire, positioning days and drydocking periods. The six new vessels contracted for delivery through 2026 are phased in from Q2 onward.  The P&amp;L  On those assumptions, IMPP\u2019s full year 2026 revenue comes to approximately $196 million, with EBITDA of approximately $84 million excluding the Suez Enchanted vessel sale gain. Including the estimated $30 million sale gain, net income reaches approximately $89 million, or roughly $1.98 per share on the current 45 million share count.                                      Q2 2026 is the standout quarter. Two Suezmaxes earning elevated rates for the full period, five MR tankers on spot in the strongest market in years, and an estimated $30 million vessel sale gain all landing in the same three months. Evercore described its Q2 expectations for the tanker sector as eye-watering. For IMPP, Q2 alone could deliver net income approaching $51 million, more than the company earned in all of 2025.  H2 is where the conservatism bites. With Suez Enchanted sold and Suez Protopia assumed to earn significantly lower rates as the market normalises, tanker revenue drops sharply in Q3 and Q4. The drybulk fleet partially cushions this, growing as new vessels deliver and providing a stable base from rolling quarterly charters. But the H2 numbers are unambiguously softer. This is not a company that will sustain Q2 economics through year-end.  The cash position  What makes the earnings story genuinely interesting is the balance sheet context. Starting the year with $179 million in cash, adding approximately $48 million from the Suez Enchanted sale, generating roughly $84 million in operating cash flow across 2026, and spending approximately $78 million on remaining vessel delivery commitments, IMPP should end 2026 with cash and time deposits of approximately $233 million. On 45 million shares, that is roughly $5.18 per share in cash at year-end, against a current stock price of $4.80.  The company will, in other words, finish with more cash this year than its entire current market capitalisation, while simultaneously delivering six new vessels and maintaining a zero-debt balance sheet.  The sensitivity                                      The table above shows how EBITDA moves across a range of Suezmax and MR rate scenarios, holding drybulk constant. The base case sits at $84 million. Even in the most bearish scenario modelled, with Suezmax averaging $80,000 per day for the full year and MR at $35,000 per day, EBITDA still comes to approximately $62 million. In the most bullish scenario, with rates staying elevated, EBITDA approaches $120 million.  The point of the sensitivity table is not to predict which scenario will occur. It is to illustrate that even in a significant rate reversion scenario, IMPP generates substantial cash relative to its entreprise value. The margin of safety is wide.  Part 5 \u2014 What Is It Worth?  This is the section that matters. We have established what IMPP does, why the market context is exceptional, what the governance risks are, and what the company could plausibly earn in 2026. Now we need to ask whether the current stock price makes sense given all of that.  The answer, across every framework I can apply, is no.  First: the EV situation  Let us start with the most striking number. IMPP\u2019s market capitalisation at $4.80 per share is approximately $216 million. As of today, the company holds an estimated $246 million in cash and time deposits, after adding the roughly $48 million in Suez Enchanted sale proceeds to the $179 million year-end 2025 balance and before accounting for remaining vessel delivery commitments of approximately $78 million. Net of those commitments, committed net cash stands at roughly $168 million. Subtracting that from the market cap gives an enterprise value of approximately $48 million.  That means you are buying the entire operating business \u2014 a 20-vessel fleet generating roughly $84 million in EBITDA this year, with six more ships arriving through year-end \u2014 for less than $50 million. A single Suezmax tanker just sold for $48 million.  Put another way: IMPP\u2019s EV/EBITDA on our base case is approximately 0.57x. Evercore\u2019s coverage universe trades at a group average of 2.9x for 2026. Even applying a 50% governance discount to the low end of the peer range still implies an EV around $150 million and a stock price in the range of $7 to $8.  Second: an updated NAV  In December 2025, management published a formal NAV estimate of $508 million, or $11.38 per basic share. That was calculated on September 30, 2025 financial data, before the Hormuz conflict, before the Q4 rate surge, and before six new vessel deliveries. It also explicitly excluded the seven vessels contracted in August and December 2025. In other words, it was a conservative snapshot of a company that has since become significantly more valuable in both earnings and asset terms.  I have attempted to update that NAV using recent transaction data. The inputs below are my own estimates and should be treated as indicative rather than precise.  For the Suezmax fleet: Suez Enchanted sold for $48 million in late April 2026, a confirmed market transaction. Suez Protopia is a 2008 Korean-built sister vessel. Using the same $48 million as a reference gives a combined remaining Suezmax value of approximately $48 million for the single vessel still owned.  For the MR fleet: Compass Maritime\u2019s weekly report from April 27, 2026 provides a useful age curve. A 2006-built Korean MR recently changed hands at $13.3 to $16.5 million. A 2014-built Korean MR with scrubber sold for $35 million. A 2015-built scrubber-fitted vessel also achieved $35 million. IMPP\u2019s seven MRs are 2007 to 2011 Korean-built without scrubbers. Based on the age gradient implied by those transactions, and applying a modest discount for the absence of scrubbers, I estimate the fleet value at approximately $22 to $25 million per vessel depending on build year, with a blended average of roughly $23 million across the seven vessels. That gives a total MR fleet value of approximately $161 million.  For the drybulk fleet: the eleven vessels on the water are predominantly 2009 to 2013 Japanese-built handysizes, supramaxes and kamsarmaxes. Using a conservative $15 million for handysizes, $22 million for supramaxes and $28 million for the kamsarmaxes gives a blended drybulk fleet value of approximately $215 million across the eleven vessels. The six contracted vessels not yet delivered are excluded as they are offset by remaining payment obligations.  Adding those fleet values together and netting against the preferred share liquidation value of approximately $20 million and the remaining vessel delivery commitments of $78 million gives an updated estimated NAV of approximately $571 million, or roughly $12.70 per basic share on 45 million shares outstanding.  At $4.80, the stock trades at approximately 0.38x this updated NAV estimate. Even the fully diluted NAV per share, assuming all 19 million warrants are exercised at $6.30 and adding the $120 million in warrant proceeds to the balance sheet, implies a diluted NAV of approximately $691 million on 64 million shares, or roughly $10.80 per diluted share. At $4.80, that is still 0.44x.  For context, Evercore\u2019s coverage universe of tanker stocks trades at P/NAV multiples ranging from 0.66x to 1.39x, with the product tanker names at 0.66 to 0.84x. IMPP at 0.37x to 0.44x represents a discount of 40 to 55% to even the cheapest comparable.  The warrant wall and what it means for the share price  Here is where the valuation argument gets more complicated, and where intellectual honesty requires acknowledging a structural constraint that most bull cases on IMPP tend to underplay.  In December 2025, IMPP placed $60 million of stock at $6.30 per share with two institutional investors, accompanied by 19 million warrants at the same $6.30 strike price, valid for five years. Those warrants are currently out of the money. But they represent approximately 42% of the current share count, and they create a well-defined ceiling for the stock.  As the stock approaches $6.30, the warrant holders can exercise and sell into any rally, effectively capping the price. There is no structural reason for the stock to sustain a price above $6.30 in the near term while those warrants remain outstanding, because the dilutive supply they represent would absorb any buying pressure near that level. This is not speculation, it is simple mechanics.  $6.30 therefore functions as a realistic near-term price ceiling, not a fair value floor. From the current price of $4.80, that represents approximately 31% upside. Not a bad return. But investors expecting a re-rating to full NAV of $11 to $13 will have to wait for one of two things: either the warrants are exercised and the overhang is cleared, at which point the company holds an additional $120 million in cash; or enough time passes that the market begins to look through the dilution risk. Neither is imminent.  Year-end cash per share: the simplest argument  Stripping away all of the above, the simplest version of the bull case is this. On our base case estimates, IMPP ends 2026 with approximately $233 million in cash and time deposits. On 45 million shares that is $5.18 per share. The current stock price is $4.80.  You are being asked to pay $4.80 for a company that will end the year holding more cash per share than the stock costs today, while simultaneously operating a fleet of 26 vessels and having generated the equivalent of the entire market cap in operating cash flow over the course of the year. The entire operating business is, at that point, free.  Verdict  Let me bring the threads together.  Imperial Petroleum is a 20-vessel Greek shipping company trading at $4.80 per share with an enterprise value of approximately $48 million. Its fleet generates an estimated $84 million in EBITDA this year. Management\u2019s own NAV, calculated before the Hormuz conflict and before six new vessel deliveries, stood at $11.38 per basic share. An updated estimate using recent transaction comparables puts that figure closer to $13.00. The company will likely end 2026 holding more cash per share than the stock costs today.  The tanker market is in the middle of an extraordinary dislocation driven by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, but the rate strength predates the conflict. The MR product tanker orderbook is the most constrained of any major shipping segment. The drybulk fleet, overlooked by most commentators focused on the tanker story, is quietly benefiting from a structural bull market anchored in Simandou iron ore volumes and record Chinese steel and vehicle exports, with the Supramax segment directly exposed to the same Chinese industrial production story that drives Capesize demand.  The governance is genuinely problematic. Vafias controls the vote with a $200,000 investment in Series B shares, Stealth Maritime earns recurring fees as manager and counterparty simultaneously, and the December 2025 equity offering diluted shareholders at $6.30 with 19 million warrants attached. Those warrants now function as a structural ceiling on the stock price. The realistic near-term upside is 31%, to $6.30, not a re-rating to NAV.  None of this makes IMPP uninvestable. A 60% discount to updated NAV, sub-1x EV/EBITDA, zero debt and $5 of year-end cash per share against a $4.80 stock price is a genuinely compelling setup. But it requires accepting that you are a minority passenger in a vehicle driven by someone who cannot be removed and who earns fees regardless of how the stock performs.  The margin of safety is wide. Eyes open.  ---  A note to readers  This is my first piece on this platform, and I want to be transparent about what it is and what it is not.
