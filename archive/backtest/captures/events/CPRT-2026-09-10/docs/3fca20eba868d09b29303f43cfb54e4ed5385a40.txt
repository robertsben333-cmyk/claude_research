Key Elements      Copart    Jeff Liaw    Leah Stearns    Purple Wave    Blue Car Commercial    Copart Dealer Services    United Kingdom    Germany    Canada    Middle East    CCC Intelligent Solutions    ISS Fast Track                                                                                           Copart Inc. notched a record for insurance auction prices in its fiscal third quarter even as U.S. insurance unit volumes declined, illustrating the salvager’s unique ability to turn structural repair-cost inflation into higher returns while a cyclical consumer pullback on coverage weighed on raw claims volumes. Total loss frequency – the share of claims resulting in a vehicle being declared a total loss – reached 23.6% in the first calendar quarter of 2026, up nearly five full percentage points over four years, a trend that powered the online auction giant’s results. 
 “U.S. insurance ASPs increased 4.1% year-over-year for the quarter, reaching a seasonally adjusted all-time record high for Copart insurance ASPs in the third quarter,” Chief Executive Officer Jeff Liaw said on the earnings call. “We are very much not passive beneficiaries of an increase in total loss frequency. We have helped drive it upwards, and we view it as our ongoing responsibility to drive ever-better auction returns.” 
 Financial Overview

 Revenue of $1.24 billion rose modestly as a 4.6% increase in global average selling prices more than compensated for the 2.4% dip in unit sales. The bottom line benefited from cost discipline and ongoing share buybacks, pushing diluted earnings per share up 2.4% to $0.43. 
 U.S. Insurance: Record Prices Offset Volume Slide 
 The core U.S. insurance business saw units fall 4.2% (or just over 3% excluding prior-year catastrophic volumes). Liaw attributed the shortfall largely to consumer behavior as elevated premiums led many drivers to reduce coverage. He cited a mid-2025 survey showing one in six policyholders had scaled back – moving from collision to liability-only or raising deductibles – and noted that ISS Fast Track data indicated earned car years fell 4% even as vehicles in operation grew 1.4%. “We believe this divergence … is clear evidence of the consumer pullback on insurance coverage,” Liaw said. 
 Yet higher average selling prices, fueled by a global buyer network spanning more than 160 countries, fully neutralized the volume weakness. U.S. insurance ASPs climbed 4.1% to a third-quarter record. International buyers now account for more than one-third of U.S. auction volume and nearly half of auction proceeds, a diversification that cushioned the platform when direct participation from certain Middle Eastern markets dipped amid geopolitical conflicts. “As certain corridors moderated, others expanded to fill the gap, including parts of Central Europe, West Africa, Central America and the Caribbean,” Liaw said. 
 That return strength has encouraged U.S. insurance carriers to place an ever-greater share of vehicles in pure-sale auctions. “Today, for U.S. insurance sellers at Copart, the mix of pure sale units is at all-time highs,” Liaw said, noting that the pure-sale volume at Copart is “literally an order of magnitude higher” than at other platforms. Pure sales attract more bidders and accelerate sales, a virtuous cycle that Copart has deliberately fostered. 
 International Momentum

 The international segment was a standout. Revenue jumped 14.1% to $234.2 million, driven by broad-based strength. Service revenue surged 17.9% as fee revenue per unit rose 10.5% and unit volumes climbed 5.9%. Gross profit expanded 21.9%. Chief Financial Officer Leah Stearns said the U.K., Germany and Canada led the charge, while Germany continues to benefit from carriers embracing Copart’s total-loss process. “We continue to see carriers be open-minded about how they’re approaching the total loss process, and that’s a market where we’ve seen some meaningful progress from a unit volume as well as a profitability perspective,” she said.     
 Non-Insurance &#x26; Purple Wave Gain Traction 
 Beyond the insurance auction core, Copart’s non-insurance channels showed momentum. Dealer Services and powersports units grew 1%, and the Blue Car Commercial consignment channel expanded more than 4%. Combined fleet and finance seller volumes grew at a healthy double-digit pace, partially offset by lower rental-company volumes as those fleets kept cars in service longer for repairs. Copart Direct volumes fell 26.3%, reflecting a deliberate shift of lower-value units into a direct-buy channel. 
 Purple Wave, the heavy-equipment auction platform acquired in 2022, posted gross transaction value growth of more than 25% over the last 12 months, fueled by territory expansion from the Central time zone toward the coasts and deepening relationships with enterprise accounts. Stearns said the team is now 2.5 to 3 times the size it was at acquisition and still has room to achieve full nationwide coverage. 
 Capital Returns and Liquidity 
 Copart’s capital allocation remained aggressive. Year-to-date, the company repurchased 43.4 million shares for approximately $1.6 billion. Liquidity stood at $5.5 billion, including $4.2 billion in cash and equivalents and held-to-maturity securities, with no debt. Free cash flow grew 12% year-to-date, giving management firepower for opportunistic investments and continued buybacks. 
 Management’s Long-Term Playbook 
 Pressed by analysts on growth drivers, Liaw outlined several levers beyond the core insurance business: the ongoing rise in total loss frequency, which Copart actively accelerates by generating superior auction returns; the capture of non-insurance liquidity from rental companies, dealers, corporate fleets and financial institutions; international expansion into markets that share characteristics conducive to total loss; and ecosystem services such as logistics and financing that reduce buyer friction. 
 He highlighted the role of “crossover buyers” – members who first discover Copart through non-insurance vehicles and then start bidding on insurance inventory. “Of the more than 30,000 buyers who first entered the Copart ecosystem by virtue of those non-insurance vehicles, a strong majority would bid on an insurance vehicle within the first 90 days,” Liaw said. 
 On the call’s Q&#x26;A, analysts probed the impact of fuel costs, the outlook for claims, artificial intelligence adoption and the newly launched domestic long-haul delivery service. Stearns disclosed that the long-haul product contributed about $15 million of year-over-year incremental costs in facility operations but is generating a positive margin. On AI, Liaw said carriers are “both excited and terrified,” requiring auditable and accountable decision-making, and that Copart is deploying AI to support front-end total-loss decisions and claims processing. 
 Despite near-term volume headwinds from consumer insurance pullback, Liaw conveyed confidence that the trend is cyclical. “When consumers feel pocketbook pressure, they dial back their coverage. The same has been true in reverse,” he said. With total loss frequency still climbing, non-insurance channels gaining share and international markets accelerating, Copart’s growth algorithm appears firmly intact.
