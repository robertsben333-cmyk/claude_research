  William Martin Keating      Jun 12, 2026   ∙ Paid                  2                         Share            On June 11, 2026, after the close, Adobe   reported   its second quarter of fiscal 2026.     Record revenue of $6.62 billion, which represents 13% year-over-year growth, or 11% in constant currency. This was also nicely above the $6.48 billion high end of guidance. However, that guidance explicitly    excluded    any contribution from the then-pending Semrush acquisition integration, which was officially completed on April 28 last.      Diluted earnings per share was $4.25 on a GAAP basis and $5.96 on a non-GAAP basis.     Total Adobe Annualized Recurring Revenue (“ARR”) exiting the quarter was $27.10 billion, including approximately $480 million from Semrush.     GAAP operating income in the second quarter was $2.24 billion and non-GAAP operating income was $2.95 billion. GAAP net income was $1.71 billion and non-GAAP net income was $2.40 billion.     Cash flows from operations were $2.17 billion.     Exiting the quarter, Remaining Performance Obligations (“RPO”) were $22.27 billion, and Current Remaining Performance Obligations (“cRPO”) were 67%.     Our strategy to deliver ground-breaking innovation for Business Professionals and Consumers, and Creative and Marketing Professionals is delighting customers and we are pleased to raise Adobe’s FY25 revenue target,” said Shantanu Narayen, chair and CEO, Adobe. “Adobe’s AI innovation is transforming industries enabling individuals and enterprises to achieve unprecedented levels of creativity.”   So far, so good. Looking ahead, Adobe forecasted current quarter revenues of $6.695 billion at the midpoint, up 1.1% sequentially and up 14% YoY. The company also increased their original FY2026 guide from $26 billion to $26.55 billion.  Adobe share price had traded down 6.25% during the session and went on to lose a further 5.6% in after hours trading.                                       All told, Adobe is now down 47% over the past year, making it the poster child for the ongoing SaaSPocalypse. In this particular case, it’s a little easier to understand the market reaction  Firstly, there was a reset of a very important growth metric, despite the addition of the Semrush acquisition into the mix during the second quarter   Our FY26 Total Adobe ARR growth target of 10.2% now reflects both the addition of the Semrush book of business as well as the strategic choice to accelerate MAU freemium growth and defer previously planned Creative Cloud line optimizations.   Then there was the surprise announcement of a rather abrupt departure of CFO Dan Durn:   Dan Durn, executive vice president and CFO of Adobe, is departing the company on June 15, 2026 to pursue a new professional opportunity. Steve Day, SVP of Corporate Finance and CFO of Adobe’s Customer Experience Orchestration Business Unit, will serve as interim Chief Financial Officer, effective June 15, 2026. Mr. Day brings 20 years of financial leadership experience at Adobe to the Interim Chief Financial Officer role. Day will report directly to CEO and Chair Shantanu Narayen.    Mr. Durn is moving to Marvell, details   here  . Somewhat curiously, Mr. Durn was previously on Marvell’s board, a position he resigned from on June 10, just one day before his CFO role was announced.    You may recall that back in March, Adobe’s CEO announced his transition out of the CEO role which he has held for eighteen years, details   here  .     SAN JOSE, Calif. — March 12, 2026 — Adobe (Nasdaq: ADBE)   — the global technology leader that unleashes creativity, productivity and customer experiences through innovative tools and platforms — today announced that Shantanu Narayen, who has served as CEO of Adobe for eighteen years, has decided to transition from his position as CEO after a successor has been appointed. Narayen will remain as Chair of the Board.    There’s a lot going on at Adobe. Let’s dig in…     Semicon Alpha is a reader-supported publication.
