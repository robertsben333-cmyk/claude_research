 Oracle Earnings Preview: What to Watch Thursday as Wall Street Tests the  AI  Backlog Story 
 
Oracle reports fiscal first-quarter results Thursday after the close. Wall Street wants confirmation of the $638 billion AI cloud backlog behind its rally. Analysts expect $1.74 in earnings per share and $19.14 billion in revenue.
 
 
 
 by  Gene Paul Jones  
 | 
 September 6, 2026 
 
 
 
 
 
 Oracle reports fiscal first-quarter earnings Thursday, and Wall Street has one number in mind: the backlog. 
  Oracle  is scheduled to post its first-quarter fiscal 2027 results after the market close on  Thursday, September 10 , with a conference call at 5 p.m.  ET . Analysts polled by Wall Street estimate earnings of  $1.74 per share  on revenue of roughly  $19.14 billion , according to consensus figures compiled ahead of the release. Oracle&#8217;s own guidance, issued when it reported fiscal fourth-quarter results in June, called for revenue growth of 27% to 29% in  US  dollars and non- GAAP  earnings between $1.72 and $1.76 a share. 
 Why This Report Matters More Than Usual 
 The number that will move the stock is not  EPS . It is Remaining Performance Obligations, or  RPO  — the contracted revenue Oracle has booked but not yet delivered. In its fiscal fourth quarter, Oracle said  RPO  jumped $85 billion in three months to a record  $638 billion , up 363% from a year earlier, according to the company&#8217;s earnings release filed with the  Securities and Exchange Commission . Total revenue for the quarter was $19.2 billion, up 21%, and cloud infrastructure revenue nearly doubled, up 93% to $5.8 billion. 
 Much of that backlog traces back to a single relationship:  OpenAI &#8217;s roughly $300 billion, multiyear cloud and compute agreement with Oracle, part of the Stargate data-center buildout that is meant to add 4.5 gigawatts of capacity. &ldquo;Our cloud infrastructure growth demonstrates our ability to meet the increasing demand for  AI  workloads,&rdquo; Oracle chief executive  Safra Catz  told analysts on the June earnings call. Investors on Thursday will be listening for whether that demand is broadening beyond OpenAI or whether Oracle&#8217;s fortunes remain tied to one customer&#8217;s spending plans.

 The Stock That Has Investors on Edge 
 Oracle&#8217;s own shares tell a more nervous story than its backlog does. The stock trades in the  $156 to $160  range heading into the report — down roughly 25% for the year — even after  surging 40% in a single session last September  on the strength of an earlier  RPO  jump. The pullback reflects a familiar worry: building  AI  data centers at this scale requires enormous capital spending and financing, and margins have come under pressure even as revenue climbs. Analysts have not abandoned the stock — the average 12-month price target of $242.69 implies more than 50% upside from current levels — but the gap between that target and where shares actually trade shows how much skepticism has crept into the  AI  infrastructure trade generally, the same skepticism that has periodically hit  Nvidia&#8217;s own valuation debate  and fueled arguments that  a single disappointing cloud quarter from any major player could rattle the whole group . 
 What to Watch When the Numbers Land      RPO  growth : another jump toward or past $700 billion would reinforce the backlog story; a slowdown would revive concentration-risk concerns tied to OpenAI.    Cloud infrastructure revenue : the 93% growth rate set in the fourth quarter is the bar. Anything materially below it invites questions about capacity constraints or demand.    Capital spending and cash flow : Oracle has been funding data-center buildouts with a mix of cash flow and new debt. Guidance on capex and free cash flow will show how sustainable that financing pace is.    Full-year guidance : Oracle&#8217;s fiscal 2027 targets call for 34% constant-currency revenue growth and non- GAAP   EPS  of $8.05. Any reiteration or revision Thursday will set the tone for  the broader  AI  infrastructure trade  heading into the fall earnings season.
