				  Broadcom flags 2026 chip supply squeeze as TSMC capacity tightens under AI demand  
			  
				
				
				
				
				   
			  
				
				
				
				
				
  Foundry bottlenecks and memory shifts reshape electronics sourcing timelines  



 Broadcom has warned that constrained foundry capacity at TSMC will limit chip supply into 2026, as demand for AI accelerators continues to outpace manufacturing expansion. The company said the bottleneck is already affecting delivery visibility and order fulfilment, raising pressure on component sourcing and production schedules. 



 According to  Reuters , Broadcom executives identified TSMC&rsquo;s advanced-node capacity as a limiting factor for high-performance chips. &ldquo;We are seeing supply constraints driven by TSMC capacity,&rdquo; the company said, pointing to persistent tightness in leading-edge process nodes used for AI and networking silicon. The report adds that the imbalance is expected to continue through next year, affecting shipment volumes and lead times. 



 
  We help you source memory stock  
 



 The same issue was highlighted in coverage by  Economic Times , which noted that &ldquo;AI chip demand is surging faster than supply can keep up.&rdquo; That demand concentration in advanced nodes is forcing suppliers to prioritise high-margin products, limiting availability for other applications and increasing procurement competition across sectors. 



 TrendForce data indicates that memory markets are also adjusting to the AI-driven shift, with production capacity being redirected toward high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and server DRAM. In its March update,  TrendForce  reported that &ldquo;DRAM suppliers are reallocating capacity toward HBM and server applications,&rdquo; tightening supply for conventional DRAM segments. This reallocation is expected to affect pricing and availability for consumer and industrial electronics components. 



 The combination of foundry constraints and memory reallocation is already feeding into longer procurement cycles. Advanced packaging, particularly CoWoS capacity at TSMC, remains a secondary bottleneck, compounding delays for AI processors that require complex integration. Lead times for certain high-performance components now extend beyond typical planning windows, forcing manufacturers to revise production timelines and buffer inventory strategies.

 Cost structures are also shifting. Limited wafer starts at advanced nodes are sustaining elevated pricing for 5nm and below, while HBM pricing remains firm due to tight supply. This dynamic is increasing bill-of-material costs for systems integrating AI acceleration, networking silicon, and high-speed memory. 



 Broadcom&rsquo;s warning signals that capacity additions scheduled for late 2025 may not fully relieve pressure in the near term. Foundry expansion cycles, including new fabs and packaging lines, require extended ramp-up periods before yielding meaningful output. 



 Attention is now turning to how quickly TSMC and memory suppliers can bring new capacity online, and whether demand growth moderates. Until then, constrained supply at both logic and memory levels is set to define sourcing decisions and production planning across the electronics sector.
