Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) has published its 11th annual Living Progress Report, outlining the company's Fiscal Year 2025 (FY25) progress under its Living Progress strategy. The report details how HPE is supporting customers as rising demand for compute-intensive workloads—particularly AI—places increased pressure on energy resources, regulatory compliance, and digital trust. 
 Business Growth Alongside Reduced Carbon Footprint 
 A central finding of the FY25 report is HPE's ability to grow its business while improving its environmental performance. Since 2020, HPE increased net revenue by 21% while decreasing its total carbon footprint by 5%, demonstrating that business expansion can be achieved alongside greater operational efficiency. 
 On the commercial side, demand for sustainability-linked offerings continued to grow. The following table summarizes key sustainability and business performance highlights from the report:

 In FY25, $4.4 billion of total net revenue reflected customers' propensity to purchase products with sustainability attributes after learning about HPE's IT sustainability offerings and expertise. 
 Advancing Responsible AI and Digital Governance 
 As AI adoption accelerates across industries, HPE strengthened its internal product and governance frameworks during FY25. The company continued to mature its AI Governance model through internal risk assessments, an AI Governance and Enablement Hub, and targeted team member training, including on the company's AI Ethical Principles. 
 HPE also advanced customer-facing solutions designed for secure and regulated environments, including HPE Private Cloud AI and HPE Private Cloud Enterprise air-gapped solutions, which support secure, disconnected deployments for sensitive workloads. 
 A notable deployment involves Siemens Energy, which is deploying a dedicated global platform through HPE's GreenLake platform to support engineering-driven high-performance computing across sites in the United States and Germany. The platform is designed to accelerate AI-enabled simulation and digital twin workflows while helping Siemens Energy maintain oversight of data and systems under strict export control requirements. 
 "AI is transforming enterprises around the world at a pace unlike any technology before it," said Antonio Neri, president and CEO of HPE. "The success of the next phase of AI adoption will depend on organizations' ability to use creativity and focus to deploy AI securely, efficiently, and responsibly at scale." 
 Energy-Efficient Infrastructure and Cooling Innovation 
 HPE expanded its portfolio of energy-efficient compute, storage, and networking solutions in FY25. New generations of compute and storage, such as HPE ProLiant Compute Gen12 servers, can reduce data center footprints by up to 96% and use up to 87% less power compared to earlier generations. HPE Alletra Storage MP B10000 has also been shown to deliver up to a 45% reduction in energy consumption. 
 HPE also expanded the use of direct liquid cooling (DLC) across its portfolio, including HPE ProLiant Compute and HPE Cray systems, helping minimize cooling-related carbon footprint and costs for customers by up to 86% annually. In high-performance computing, HPE had four systems ranked in the top 10 Green500 list of the world's most energy-efficient supercomputers. 
 One of those systems is Isambard-AI, built by HPE for the University of Bristol. Ranked fourth most energy-efficient in the world as of 2026, Isambard-AI was delivered through an HPE modular data center that achieved an estimated 72% reduction in facility construction emissions compared with traditional construction methods.
