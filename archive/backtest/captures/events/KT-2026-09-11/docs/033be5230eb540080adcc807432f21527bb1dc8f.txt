Key Elements      KT    LG Uplus    SK Telecom    Hyundai Motor Group    MVNO    M2M    connected car    AIDC    SK Hyper    KT Cloud    Paju AIDC                                                                                           KT has reclaimed the top spot in mobile virtual network operator (MVNO) lines from LG Uplus after two years. The decisive factor was winning the contract for Hyundai Motor Group's vehicle telematics lines last year. 
 According to disclosures from South Korea's three major mobile carriers on the 13th, MVNO lines using KT's network totaled 9.048 million as of the first half of this year. This represents an 18.3% increase from 7.649 million in the same period last year, and a 3.4% increase from the previous quarter. 
 In contrast, MVNO lines on LG Uplus's network grew only 0.4% to 9.025 million, approximately 23,000 lines fewer than KT. MVNO lines using SK Telecom's network totaled 2.637 million, the lowest among the three carriers. 
 This marks the first time KT has surpassed LG Uplus in MVNO line count since the first quarter of 2024. The gap between the two companies, which had widened to approximately 1.33 million lines in the second quarter of last year, narrowed for four consecutive quarters before the rankings ultimately flipped. 
 The primary factor determining the divergent outcomes was connected car lines from automakers. The MVNO lines disclosed by telecom operators include not only mobile phones but also machine-to-machine (M2M) lines such as vehicle telematics and remote monitoring. In particular, MVNO lines have been steadily increasing due to growing demand for connected services in vehicles. 
 KT's network MVNO lines posted a net increase of 390,000 in the second quarter of last year, a fivefold jump from the previous quarter. This was followed by net additions of over 300,000 lines for four consecutive quarters through the second quarter of this year. Conversely, LG Uplus had been adding approximately 350,000 lines per quarter until the second quarter of last year, but the figure dropped to 68,000 in the third quarter, followed by two consecutive quarters of net declines. 
 KT secured exclusive supply rights for Hyundai Motor Group's new vehicle lines last year. The company supports vehicle telematics and telematics services including Hyundai's Blue Link. With contracts spanning millions of lines, a single supply agreement can shift the rankings. LG Uplus had supplied Hyundai Motor lines for two years starting in 2023, but the transition to KT beginning last year resulted in LG Uplus ceding its MVNO leadership position. 
 Vehicle lines command superior per-line pricing compared to other M2M lines due to higher data usage and value-added service revenue. As autonomous driving expands and the data requirements per vehicle increase, the three major carriers are intensifying efforts to launch vehicle-specific eSIM rate plans and secure telematics supply contracts. 
 KT, which is competing with LG Uplus for the No. 2 position in total telecom lines, is aggressively expanding M2M and Internet of Things (IoT) lines in addition to existing mobile phone subscribers. The company previously won the sixth-phase contract for Korea Electric Power Corporation's Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) remote meter reading project. However, KT also exposed limitations in that the growth was concentrated in IoT lines with lower average revenue per user (ARPU), failing to translate into improved wireless profitability. 
 A KT representative stated, "We will continue to sustain MVNO line growth based on competitive quality and services."     
 Wireless Revenue Stagnates as AI Drives Growth 
 Apart from MVNO line competition, the three major carriers' second-quarter results showed mixed outcomes in the wireless segment. According to the Financial Supervisory Service's electronic disclosure system, SK Telecom recorded revenue of 4.3591 trillion won (approximately $3.1 billion) and operating profit of 566 billion won (approximately $397.7 million) this year. Revenue grew only 0.5% year-over-year, but operating profit surged 67.3%. Profitability improved as one-time costs from last year's USIM hacking incident disappeared this year. 
 KT posted revenue of 6.6799 trillion won (approximately $4.7 billion) and operating profit of 648.3 billion won (approximately $455.6 million), down 10.1% and 36.1% year-over-year, respectively. This was due to the fading of the temporary performance boost from last year's real estate sales. LG Uplus recorded revenue of 3.6949 trillion won (approximately $2.6 billion), down 3.9% year-over-year, but operating profit rose 13.1% to 344.5 billion won (approximately $242.1 million). 
 Results in the core wireless business were mixed. SK Telecom's wireless revenue fell 1.9% to 2.5634 trillion won (approximately $1.8 billion) due to declining subscriber numbers. KT's wireless service revenue also declined 1.8% year-over-year to 1.6749 trillion won (approximately $1.2 billion), with ARPU falling 2.3% to 34,412 won (approximately $24.18) over the same period. LG Uplus recorded mobile segment revenue of 1.6526 trillion won (approximately $1.2 billion), up 0.9%, driven by an increased proportion of 5G handset subscribers and net additions in subscriber lines. 
 AIDC Emerges as New Revenue Source for the Three Carriers 
 With wireless revenue stagnating, artificial intelligence data centers (AIDC) are establishing themselves as a new growth engine for telecom operators. SK Telecom's second-quarter AIDC revenue reached 136.2 billion won (approximately $95.7 million), up 92.5% year-over-year. AI B2B and B2C revenue excluding AIDC also grew 24.5% to 61.3 billion won (approximately $43.1 million), driven by increased cloud orders. 
 KT also continued its growth trajectory in AI transformation (AX) and AIDC, including AI and cloud services. KT's second-quarter AX revenue rose 22.3% year-over-year to 367.8 billion won (approximately $258.5 million). KT Cloud posted revenue of 265.4 billion won (approximately $186.5 million), up 19.8%, driven by rising utilization rates at the Gasan data center that opened in November last year and expansion of new design-build-operate (DBO) projects. 
 LG Uplus also saw AIDC business revenue grow 28.9% year-over-year to 124.1 billion won (approximately $87.2 million). Growth was particularly notable in the colocation (data center construction and operation) business. Solution segment revenue, which includes the AI Contact Center (AICC), also rose 6.8% to 134.6 billion won (approximately $94.6 million). 
 Divergent Approaches to AIDC 
 Each company's AIDC business strategy and execution approach differs markedly. SK Telecom established "SK Hyper," a dedicated business development organization, and set plans to build 5 gigawatts (GW) of AIDC capacity in phases by 2029. The company secures customers first, then utilizes project financing (PF) and external investment for construction, minimizing direct investment burden. 
 KT plans to invest approximately 5 trillion won (approximately $3.5 billion) over the next five years to build AIDCs at approximately 25 additional locations. Through this, the company aims to secure a total of 1GW of AIDC capacity. KT has adopted a "dual-track" strategy: aggressively expanding in the Seoul metropolitan area focusing on locations with resolved permitting issues, while securing customers first before supplying capacity in non-metropolitan regions. 
 LG Uplus plans to expand data center capacity to 400 megawatts (MW) by 2030. The company is currently building a 200MW-class AIDC in Paju, and will invest 1.3489 trillion won (approximately $947.9 million) in the second phase of the Paju AIDC to construct additional buildings 2 and 3 following the first computing building. Rather than raising separate external funding, LG Uplus plans to reduce its burden by combining DBO and leasing models within its mid- to long-term target range.
