Key Elements      Broadcom    AVGO    AI semiconductor revenue    VMware    S&amp;P 500 Information Technology Index    golden cross                                                                                           Broadcom (AVGO) enters its most consequential earnings report of the year carrying a $60 billion reason for investors to believe in its AI trajectory: a bookings and backlog pipeline that has swelled beyond $30 billion entering the fiscal third quarter, with management guiding AI semiconductor revenue to $16 billion for the period. That figure would represent year-over-year growth exceeding 200% and account for more than half of expected total revenue, underscoring how thoroughly the Palo Alto, California-based company has pivoted toward artificial intelligence infrastructure. 
 The company reports fiscal third-quarter 2026 results on Sept. 2 after market close, with analysts projecting earnings per share of $3.24 on revenue of $29.36 billion. The stakes are unusually high: Broadcom's last earnings beat in the fiscal second quarter was followed by a 12.6% selloff, driven by management's decision not to raise its long-standing full-year 2027 AI semiconductor target of "in excess of $100 billion." Whether executives revise that figure upward this time will likely determine whether the stock can break out of its current consolidation phase. 
 In the fiscal second quarter of 2026, Broadcom reported revenue of $22.2 billion, up 48% year-over-year and surpassing analyst estimates of approximately $22.1 billion. Non-GAAP diluted EPS reached $2.44 versus consensus near $2.40, while GAAP net income hit $9.3 billion. AI semiconductor revenue soared to $10.8 billion, up 143% year-over-year, powering semiconductor solutions to $15 billion in revenue. Infrastructure software contributed $7.2 billion, up 9% year-over-year, reflecting the continued integration of VMware into Broadcom's enterprise portfolio. 
 Technical Setup Shows a Golden Cross Under Pressure 
 From a technical perspective, Broadcom shares are trading at roughly $354, down 9.1% below the 20-day simple moving average of $393.63 and 7.7% below the 50-day SMA of $387.46. The stock also sits 3.1% below the 200-day SMA of $369.22, a level that has become a "line in the sand" for bulls attempting to stabilize the longer-term picture. The moving-average structure remains mixed: the 20-day SMA still holds above the 50-day SMA, and the golden cross that triggered in April—when the 50-day crossed above the 200-day—remains technically intact. However, with price currently below all three major averages, the market is effectively asking Broadcom to prove it can reclaim those levels before the uptrend narrative reasserts itself. 
 Momentum indicators lean softer. MACD sits below its signal line with a negative histogram, suggesting fading upside pressure compared with the prior upswing. Key resistance stands at $407.50, a nearby ceiling where rebounds have stalled and which aligns closely with the stock's mid-term moving averages. Key support sits at $356.50, a tight downside reference just below the current price where buyers have previously emerged.     
 The $100 Billion Question 
 The central question for investors goes beyond the immediate quarter. Broadcom's management has maintained a full-year 2027 AI semiconductor revenue target of "in excess of $100 billion," a figure that has anchored the bull case since it was first articulated. The decision not to raise that target last quarter—despite a beat—was widely interpreted as a signal that near-term AI growth, while explosive, may be approaching a plateau in terms of forward guidance. Analysts will scrutinize commentary on AI bookings and backlog visibility, which stood above $30 billion entering the quarter, along with margin trends amid a shifting customer mix. 
 The company's fundamentals remain formidable. Broadcom has invested more than $11 billion in research and development in fiscal 2025 alone, employs more than 33,000 people, and holds thousands of patents. The stock has delivered exceptional long-term returns, surging more than 600% over the past five years and more than 1,900% over the past decade, with a one-year total return near 19%. Trading at approximately $354 per share with a market capitalization of roughly $1.7 trillion, Broadcom benefits from robust free cash flow generation and consistent dividend payments. 
 Year-to-date, however, the stock has recorded a gain of only about 2%, reflecting market volatility after prior explosive rallies amid the AI boom. Compared with the S&#x26;P 500 Information Technology Index, Broadcom has frequently outperformed over multiyear periods through superior AI-driven growth, though recent YTD returns have trailed the sector's stronger advances amid broader tech rotation and valuation adjustments in semiconductor leaders. 
 For investors, the Sept. 2 report represents a critical juncture. A raised AI target for 2027 could reignite the rally that stalled earlier this year, while another quarter of unchanged guidance—even with a beat—could extend the consolidation that has kept the stock range-bound. The $60 billion in cumulative AI bookings and backlog provides a tangible foundation for optimism, but the market's reaction will ultimately hinge on whether management signals that the growth runway extends further than previously communicated.
