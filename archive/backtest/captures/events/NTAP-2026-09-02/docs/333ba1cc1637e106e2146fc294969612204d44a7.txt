May 2026  LAYOFF EVENT   NetApp Cuts 77 Jobs in Strategic Workforce Reduction

NetApp, the leading data storage and management company, laid off 77 employees on May 26, 2026, as part of a broader restructuring initiative aimed at optimizing operations and aligning resources with evolving market demands. The workforce reduction affects the company&#x27;s San Jose headquarters and represents approximately 0.3% of NetApp&#x27;s global workforce of roughly 25,000 employees.

The layoffs come as NetApp navigates shifting customer preferences toward cloud-native solutions and artificial intelligence-driven data management platforms. Industry sources indicate the company is reallocating resources to accelerate development of its hybrid cloud portfolio and AI-integrated storage solutions.

## Context of the Decision

The NetApp layoffs reflect the company&#x27;s strategic pivot toward high-growth areas including cloud data services and AI infrastructure. The data storage giant has been under pressure to modernize its offerings as enterprises increasingly adopt cloud-first strategies and seek intelligent data management capabilities.

NetApp&#x27;s decision follows a comprehensive operational review conducted throughout early 2026, focusing on eliminating redundancies and streamlining product development cycles. The company aims to reduce operational costs while investing heavily in research and development for next-generation storage technologies.

Market analysts note that traditional storage hardware revenues have declined industry-wide as customers migrate to software-defined storage solutions and cloud-based alternatives. NetApp&#x27;s workforce reduction positions the company to compete more effectively in the rapidly evolving data services landscape.

## Impact on Operations

The layoffs primarily affected NetApp&#x27;s engineering and administrative divisions, with the majority of cuts concentrated in legacy product lines and support functions. Approximately 45 positions were eliminated from the company&#x27;s traditional storage hardware division, while 32 roles were cut from administrative and operational support teams.

NetApp&#x27;s San Jose facility, which serves as the company&#x27;s primary research and development hub, bore the brunt of the workforce reduction. The company confirmed that core engineering teams focused on cloud integration and AI-powered data analytics remain intact and continue hiring specialized talent.

The restructuring will not impact NetApp&#x27;s customer support operations or field sales teams, ensuring continuity of service for existing clients. The company emphasized that product roadmaps for key offerings including ONTAP cloud software and StorageGRID object storage solutions remain on schedule.

## Company Financial Background

NetApp reported revenue of $6.3 billion in fiscal year 2025, representing a 2% decline from the previous year as the company transitioned from hardware-centric to software-focused business models. The company&#x27;s cloud revenue segment grew 18% year-over-year, now accounting for approximately 35% of total revenue.

The data management company has invested over $800 million in cloud infrastructure and AI capabilities since 2024, including strategic acquisitions of several cloud-native startups. NetApp&#x27;s stock price has remained relatively stable, trading around $95 per share, as investors recognize the long-term potential of the company&#x27;s transformation strategy.

NetApp maintains a strong balance sheet with $4.2 billion in cash and short-term investments, providing financial flexibility to weather the transition period while funding growth initiatives in emerging technology areas.

## Industry Outlook

The data services and management sector continues experiencing significant consolidation and transformation as traditional storage vendors adapt to cloud computing trends. Major competitors including Dell Technologies, HPE, and Pure Storage have implemented similar workforce reductions while pivoting toward software and services offerings.

Industry research indicates the global data storage market will grow at a 13% compound annual growth rate through 2028, driven primarily by AI workloads and edge computing applications. Companies successfully transitioning to software-defined storage and cloud-native solutions are positioned to capture disproportionate market share.

NetApp&#x27;s restructuring aligns with broader tech industry trends toward operational efficiency and strategic focus on high-growth segments. The company joins dozens of technology firms that have implemented workforce reductions in 2026 while simultaneously increasing investments in artificial intelligence and cloud infrastructure.

## Conclusion

NetApp&#x27;s strategic workforce reduction demonstrates the company&#x27;s commitment to operational excellence while positioning for long-term growth in cloud data services and AI-powered storage solutions. The 77 job cuts, while affecting dedicated employees, represent a necessary step in NetApp&#x27;s evolution from traditional storage hardware vendor to comprehensive data management platform provider.

The company&#x27;s focus on retaining core engineering talent and maintaining customer-facing operations suggests confidence in its transformation strategy and market positioning for sustained growth in the expanding data services sector.             Apr 2025  LAYOFF EVENT   NetApp Cuts 700 Jobs in Major Workforce Reduction Amid Storage Market Pressures

NetApp eliminated 700 positions on April 29, 2025, representing 6% of its global workforce as the data storage and management company restructures operations to address shifting market demands and competitive pressures. The layoffs affect employees across multiple divisions and geographic locations as NetApp adapts to evolving enterprise storage needs and economic headwinds.

## Context of the Decision

The workforce reduction reflects NetApp&#x27;s response to declining demand for traditional storage solutions and the accelerated shift toward cloud-native data services. Enterprise customers have increasingly moved workloads to public cloud platforms, reducing demand for on-premises storage infrastructure that has historically driven NetApp&#x27;s revenue. The company faces intensified competition from hyperscale cloud providers and emerging software-defined storage vendors.

NetApp&#x27;s decision comes as the broader technology sector continues to recalibrate workforce levels following pandemic-era hiring surges. The company expanded headcount significantly during 2021 and 2022 to capitalize on digital transformation trends, but current market conditions have forced a strategic reassessment of operational efficiency and resource allocation.

## Impact on Operations

The layoffs primarily target sales, marketing, and administrative functions, with engineering and product development teams experiencing more selective reductions. NetApp&#x27;s Sunnyvale headquarters and major facilities in Research Triangle Park, North Carolina, and international locations including Bangalore and Cork, Ireland, are affected by the workforce reduction.

The company is consolidating certain product lines and streamlining go-to-market operations to focus resources on high-growth areas including hybrid cloud services and data analytics platforms. Customer-facing support teams remain largely intact to maintain service levels during the transition period.

NetApp&#x27;s channel partner network will assume expanded responsibilities for certain customer segments previously managed by direct sales teams. The company is providing affected employees with severance packages, career transition services, and extended healthcare benefits.

## Company Financial Background

NetApp reported mixed financial results in recent quarters, with traditional storage hardware revenue declining while software and cloud services revenue showed modest growth. The company&#x27;s stock has underperformed broader technology indices over the past 18 months as investors questioned its ability to compete effectively in the cloud-first enterprise market.

Annual revenue of approximately $6.3 billion has remained relatively flat as NetApp transitions from a hardware-centric to software-focused business model. The company has invested heavily in developing cloud-native data management solutions and artificial intelligence capabilities, but these emerging revenue streams have not yet offset declines in legacy product lines.

NetApp&#x27;s gross margins have compressed due to competitive pricing pressure and the costs associated with product portfolio transformation. The workforce reduction is expected to generate annual savings of approximately $180 million, which the company plans to reinvest in research and development for next-generation data services.

## Industry Outlook

The data services and management sector faces significant disruption as enterprises accelerate cloud adoption and embrace software-defined infrastructure. Traditional storage vendors including Dell Technologies, HPE, and Pure Storage have similarly restructured operations to address changing customer requirements and market dynamics.

Cloud-native data management platforms are experiencing rapid growth, with companies like Snowflake and Databricks capturing increasing market share from established storage providers. The rise of artificial intelligence and machine learning workloads is creating demand for specialized data infrastructure that traditional vendors are racing to address.

Industry analysts expect continued consolidation among storage vendors as companies seek scale advantages and complementary technologies. NetApp&#x27;s workforce reduction positions the company to operate more efficiently while investing in strategic growth areas including multi-cloud data services and AI-powered analytics platforms.

## Conclusion

NetApp&#x27;s layoffs represent a decisive step in the company&#x27;s evolution from traditional storage hardware provider to cloud-centric data services company. While the workforce reduction creates near-term challenges, it enables NetApp to reallocate resources toward high-growth opportunities in hybrid cloud and data analytics markets. The company&#x27;s ability to successfully execute this transformation will determine its competitive position in the rapidly evolving enterprise data management landscape.
