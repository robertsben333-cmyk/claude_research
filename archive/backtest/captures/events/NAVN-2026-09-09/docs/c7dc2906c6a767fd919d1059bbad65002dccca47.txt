 Perk has raised $550 million in primary venture funding, meaning new cash invested into the company for newly issued shares, as opposed to investors buying stakes from earlier backers.&nbsp;

                             Key Points 
                 
                                             Perk is holding off on an IPO despite exploring one last year, citing volatile markets and Navan's shaky post-listing performance; with ~$400M annualized revenue (48% growth), near breakeven cash flow, and no debt covenants, it says it faces no pressure to list. 
                                             The U.S. is Perk's fastest-growing and strategically prioritized market (20-25% of revenue), with the company targeting mid-market American firms that have European operations and need cross-border tax and compliance support. 
                                             AI and automation are central to Perk's strategy—driving gross margins from 40% to the mid-70s, powering a voice bot making ~10,000 weekly hotel calls, and underpinning a new U.S. expense product (Perk Spend, via the Yokoy acquisition) launching in September to challenge Navan. 
                                     
            
                             Summary 
                 Perk (formerly TravelPerk), the SoftBank Vision Fund 2-backed travel and expense platform, has confirmed it has no immediate plans to IPO despite having explored a listing last year and reportedly hiring Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and Jefferies. President and COO JC Taunay-Bucalo cited volatile equity markets—and the rocky post-IPO performance of rival Navan—as reasons to hold off, adding that with roughly $400M in annualized revenue (up 48% in 2025), near cash-flow breakeven, $550M raised, and a $300M credit facility, the company faces no structural pressure to go public. Perk is doubling down on the U.S., its fastest-growing market (20-25% of revenue), targeting mid-market companies (200-5,000 employees) with European operations that need cross-border tax and compliance handling. The company is scaling AI throughout its operations—including a voice bot that places ~10,000 calls a week to hotels to ensure virtual credit cards are charged—and is expanding into corporate expense management via its Yokoy acquisition, with the combined Perk Spend product launching in the U.S. in September to compete with Navan. AI-driven automation has lifted gross margins from 40% to the mid-70s, with 96% of bookings and 90% of expense reports now fully automated.
