 The most notable pre-earnings move came from B of A Securities, which raised its price target to $34 from $26 on September 2, keeping a Buy rating intact. That follows Oppenheimer lifting its target to $35 from $30 just days earlier, and TD Cowen pushing to $34 from $29 in mid-August. The direction of travel across the Street is uniformly higher: every recent change in the dataset is a target raise, none a cut, and the consensus mean sits at $30.80 — roughly 11% above the current $27.78 close. The bulls&#x27; core argument centres on corporate travel recovery momentum, the strength of Navan&#x27;s partnership strategy, and a valuation they describe as discounted relative to peers. Bears counter that the loss of government contracts has already dented U.S. RevPAR growth, and that the platform&#x27;s ancillary products — expense management and adjacent software — have shown limited attach rates beyond the core travel booking use case. 
 Short interest has continued edging higher since the previous note published on September 2, reaching 4.2% of the free float on roughly 9.8 million shares — up about 25% over 30 days. That build remains the clearest bearish data point in the setup. Yet the borrow market still tells no stress story: availability is extraordinarily loose at over 3,600% of shares on loan, meaning the pool of shares available to borrow dwarfs what bears have actually borrowed, and cost to borrow is a negligible 0.46%. The short build is deliberate pre-earnings hedging, not a crowd being squeezed into a tight market. The ORTEX short score, at 41.7, has drifted modestly higher over the past two weeks but remains well clear of elevated territory. Options positioning has nudged slightly more cautious — the put/call ratio moved to 0.33 from the 0.23 reading flagged earlier this week — but still sits below its 20-day average of 0.30, keeping the options market in net bullish territory.
