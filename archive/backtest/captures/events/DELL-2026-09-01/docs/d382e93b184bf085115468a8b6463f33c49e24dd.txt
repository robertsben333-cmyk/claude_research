a record  $43 billion  AI server backlog and guided to roughly  $50 billion  in AI-optimized server revenue for fiscal 2027, according to Seeking Alpha&#x27;s coverage of the print, with $64.1 billion in cumulative AI orders and $25.2 billion shipped for the year. The company also raised its dividend 20% to $2.52 annually and continued aggressive buybacks, per Seeking Alpha and TIKR. Notably, Dell&#x27;s own investor relations release for the subsequent quarter (Q1 FY27, reported May 28, 2026) shows the backlog and guidance have already moved higher, to a  $51.3 billion  backlog and a raised $60 billion AI revenue outlook, underscoring how fast enterprise AI server demand is accelerating.         For procurement and infrastructure teams, the headline takeaway is less the specific dollar figures and more the trend: Dell&#x27;s AI server backlog and revenue guidance have been revised upward in consecutive quarters, signaling that enterprise and hyperscaler AI hardware demand continues to outpace supply.      What happened   Dell Technologies closed fiscal 2026 with a record  $43 billion  AI server backlog and  $64.1 billion  in cumulative AI orders for the year, with  $25.2 billion  in AI server shipments, according to Seeking Alpha&#x27;s coverage of the results (also cited by Zacks and Yahoo Finance). At that time, Dell guided to roughly  $50 billion  in AI-optimized server revenue for fiscal 2027. Seeking Alpha and TIKR also note Dell raised its annual dividend  20%  to  $2.52  per share and continued share buybacks. CNBC&#x27;s coverage of Dell&#x27;s following quarter (Q1 FY27, reported May 28, 2026) reports the company then closed that quarter with a new record  $51.3 billion  AI backlog after booking  $24.4 billion  in new AI orders and recognizing  $16.1 billion  in AI server revenue, and raised its full-year AI server revenue guidance to approximately  $60 billion  - an update from Dell&#x27;s own investor relations materials that supersedes the earlier  $50 billion  figure.       Financial context   Seeking Alpha and TIKR flag margin risk from the mix shift toward AI servers, which the outlets describe as often lower-margin, pass-through hardware, along with potential memory (DRAM) cost inflation as a pressure point. Dell&#x27;s own Q1 FY27 release states demand continues to outpace supply, with memory availability cited as the primary constraint on faster conversion of backlog to revenue. Zacks reporting also states Dell now serves over 4,000 AI customers (with more recent reporting on the following quarter citing over 5,000), alongside product initiatives such as liquid-cooled PowerEdge XE9685L systems, NVIDIA HGX-equipped platforms, and a reported Vera Rubin GPU platform and Deskside Agentic AI offering.       For practitioners   As a general industry pattern, large OEM-scale AI server backlogs combined with liquid-cooling adoption and turnkey lifecycle services tend to reduce integration friction for enterprises deploying large training or inference clusters, while increasing dependence on GPU and memory supply chains. Procurement teams should treat backlog-to-shipment conversion timelines, not just order totals, as the more informative signal of actual capacity coming online.       What to watch   Quarterly shipment conversion against the backlog, memory price trends, and margin trajectory in Dell&#x27;s ongoing earnings commentary. CNBC&#x27;s Jim Cramer, commenting on Dell&#x27;s strong quarter, framed it alongside Nvidia&#x27;s Computex keynote as a potential signal for broader AI-stock sentiment, an analyst opinion rather than an operational fact about Dell.               Key Points    1  Dell closed fiscal 2026 with a  $43B  AI server backlog and guided to  $50B  in fiscal 2027 AI revenue, per Seeking Alpha&#x27;s coverage.    2  Dell&#x27;s own subsequent Q1 FY27 release shows the backlog already grew to  $51.3B  and guidance was raised to  $60B , reflecting accelerating demand.    3  Analysts flag margin risk from lower-margin AI server mix and memory cost inflation even as Dell increases dividends and buybacks.                  Scoring Rationale  Dell&#x27;s reported $43B AI backlog and $50B revenue guide, now confirmed superseded by the company&#x27;s own Q1 FY27 release showing a $51.3B backlog and $60B guidance, materially affect enterprise AI server supply, pricing, and procurement planning. Score held at 7.8: a major, well-corroborated OEM earnings story with broad practitioner relevance, though not a frontier-model release or industry-defining event.
