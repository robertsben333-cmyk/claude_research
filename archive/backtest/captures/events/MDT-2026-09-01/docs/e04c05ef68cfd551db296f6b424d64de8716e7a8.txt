		 Medtronic plc (ISIN IE00BTN1Y115) stock is trading in the low-$90 range as of late August 2026, with a recent quote of $92.02 highlighting a modest premium to its 52-week low of $73.31 but still below a 52-week high of $106.33. Per a fresh analyst update dated August 27, 2026, a new $114 price target implies upside of more than 20 percent from the current level, underscoring investors' interest in the company’s earnings trajectory and upcoming fiscal 2027 guidance. At the same time, a newly issued US Food and Drug Administration Early Alert regarding one of Medtronic’s esophageal pH monitoring capsule delivery devices introduces a regulatory challenge that investors must weigh against the growth story.   Analyst raises price target and sees upside  A detailed analyst report published on August 27, 2026 outlines a decision to raise Medtronic’s target price to $114 from a prior $101 while maintaining a buy rating on the shares.  This report  cites an early-stage product cycle and expectations that Medtronic will beat consensus revenue and earnings per share estimates when it reports first-quarter fiscal 2027 results on September 1, 2026. With the stock recently trading at $92.02 as of August 27, 2026, the $114 target represents mathematically more than $21 per share in potential upside and signals confidence in Medtronic’s ability to accelerate growth.  A separate same-day analysis of the company’s valuation framework shows that, as of a recent market snapshot, Medtronic is assigned an intrinsic value estimate of $100.57 versus a spot price of $91.17, yielding a quantified undervaluation gap of 9.3 percent.  This valuation work  links the discount to the company’s dividend strength, suggesting that the shares may offer a combination of income and capital appreciation potential. For investors, the spread between the mid-$90 market levels and both the $100.57 intrinsic value estimate and the $114 analyst target crystallizes a key debate around how quickly Medtronic can convert its product pipeline into sustained earnings growth.  Consensus figures compiled in an August 27, 2026 overview show that Medtronic currently carries a moderate buy rating profile, with a collection of buy and hold recommendations and an average target price that sits close to $99-$100 per share.  One ratings summary  notes that the stock opened at $92.16 in the latest session, providing another concrete market reference point against which investors can compare target prices. The resulting picture is one of a widely followed large-cap medical technology issuer where valuation remains a central theme.  Recent earnings and guidance context  The same ratings summary highlights that Medtronic’s most recently reported quarterly results modestly exceeded expectations, with earnings per share of $1.55 compared with a consensus estimate of $1.54 and revenue of $9.81 billion versus analyst projections of $9.62 billion.  The overview  specifies that revenue grew 9.9 percent year over year, illustrating that the company is managing to expand its top line while delivering a marginal earnings beat. The nearly $0.19 billion revenue outperformance relative to consensus, and the one-cent EPS beat, point to operational execution rather than a dramatically surprising quarter, but they nevertheless help underpin the bullish analyst stance.  Looking at estimates for the current fiscal year, an earnings-trend dashboard dated August 27, 2026 shows that for prior interim periods Medtronic has tended to report results slightly above consensus.  This earnings history overview  lists a recent quarter with EPS of $1.31 against a $1.36 estimate and revenue of $9.0 billion against a $9.0 billion estimate, as well as an earlier period with EPS of $1.23 compared with a $1.26 estimate and revenue of $8.4 billion versus $8.6 billion estimated, both accompanied by small positive surprise percentages. This mixture of slight beats and tight variances signals that expectations are reasonably calibrated and that any meaningful acceleration in organic revenue growth could translate quickly into a re-rating of the stock.  Forward-looking commentary in the August 27, 2026 analyst piece indicates expectations that Medtronic will raise its full-year fiscal 2027 revenue and earnings guidance after its upcoming first-quarter release.  The report  points to new product launches, stable market growth, and relatively conservative prior guidance as reasons why management may have room to lift targets. From an investor’s standpoint, the possibility of guidance increases is significant because it can influence both near-term share price momentum and the longer-term valuation narrative.  FDA Early Alert adds regulatory pressure  While the analyst community highlights valuation upside, Medtronic simultaneously faces new regulatory attention. On August 26, 2026 the US Food and Drug Administration published an Early Alert focused on esophageal pH monitoring capsule delivery devices associated with Medtronic and its Given Imaging unit.  The FDA alert  describes concerns around potential device malfunctions and advises healthcare providers and patients on reporting adverse events, while also noting that the investigation into the issue is ongoing.  A financial news summary updated on August 27, 2026 references the same FDA Early Alert in the context of Medtronic’s share performance, listing a quote of $91.17 for MDT and a daily percentage change of -0.92 percent in intraday trade.  This market report  points out that the Early Alert centers on Medtronic’s Bravo CF esophageal pH monitoring capsule delivery device, which is used to evaluate acid exposure in the esophagus. For investors, the combination of a single-day share decline of just under 1 percent and the regulatory development suggests that while the alert has not triggered a sharp selloff, it is a factor to monitor in assessing potential liability, remediation costs, or reputational effects.  The FDA’s communication includes instructions for US customers who experience adverse reactions or quality problems, providing a dedicated contact number for Medtronic support and emphasizing the importance of reporting device issues.  The alert  fits into a broader pattern in which medical device manufacturers face rigorous post-market surveillance and must respond quickly to emerging safety concerns. In Medtronic’s case, investors will be watching for any follow-up actions such as voluntary device recalls, design changes, or enhanced training for clinicians that could influence near-term margins or longer-term growth in affected product lines.  Product cycle and portfolio developments  The August 27, 2026 analyst report frames Medtronic as being in the early stages of a strong product cycle led by multiple new technologies.  This analysis  highlights the Affera pulsed field ablation system, the Symplicity renal denervation system, and the Hugo surgical robot as key drivers of faster organic revenue growth over the coming years. The commentary notes that Medtronic’s revenue expanded 8.4 percent over the last twelve months, a figure that aligns with the 9.9 percent year-over-year quarterly growth and reinforces the view that these innovations are beginning to translate into tangible financial performance.  Beyond internal product development, Medtronic has been active in portfolio reshaping. The analyst piece details the completion of a $650 million acquisition of SPR Therapeutics, paid in upfront cash, which brings peripheral nerve stimulation therapies for short-term pain management into the Medtronic portfolio.  This acquisition summary  explains that the deal is expected to strengthen Medtronic’s positioning in non-opioid and non-surgical treatment options at a time when healthcare systems and regulators are focused on reducing opioid dependence. For investors, the $650 million purchase price provides a concrete measure of the company’s capital deployment toward growth adjacencies.  The same reporting notes that Medtronic recently experienced a legal setback, with a federal jury ordering the company to pay $88 million in a hernia mesh trial involving its Covidien unit.  This legal update  states that the jury found that Covidien failed to adequately warn about risks associated with the mesh device, resulting in significant complications for the plaintiff. The $88 million award emphasizes that, alongside growth investments, Medtronic faces litigation and regulatory exposures that can influence cash flows, earnings volatility, and investor perceptions of risk.  Representative product: Symplicity renal denervation system  One representative technology from Medtronic’s extensive portfolio that is central to the current growth narrative is the Symplicity renal denervation system, which targets treatment-resistant hypertension by using catheter-based radiofrequency energy or related modalities to disrupt nerve signaling in the renal arteries. Industry coverage around August 27, 2026 places renal denervation within Medtronic’s broader cardiovascular solutions segment and notes that reimbursement and adoption trends are key determinants of its commercial potential.  The same analyst commentary  indicates that renal denervation, along with Medtronic’s cardiac ablation solutions, is expected to significantly drive company growth by fiscal 2027.  For US investors, the Symplicity system exemplifies how Medtronic integrates advanced interventional technology into its cardiovascular franchise while navigating reimbursement frameworks and clinical data requirements. As payers and providers increasingly seek therapies that can reduce long-term cardiovascular risk and hospitalizations, the success of renal denervation can have meaningful implications for Medtronic’s revenue mix and margin structure. The company’s ability to scale such products globally, while addressing regulatory, clinical, and economic hurdles, is one of the levers that could justify the higher earnings multiples envisioned in various analyst models.  Stock level and market context  Market data as of the latest completed session in late August 2026 show Medtronic shares trading at $92.02, with another intraday reference at $92.16 and a separate quote at $91.17 reflecting normal day-to-day fluctuations.  The earnings-trend dashboard  lists $92.02 with a daily change of $0.88, or 0.97 percent, while other coverage notes $91.17 and a decline of 0.92 percent. In combination with the 52-week low of $73.31 and the 52-week high of $106.33 reported in the August 27, 2026 analyst summary, these figures place Medtronic’s current trading band roughly in the middle of its one-year range.  The same analyst summary indicates that Medtronic’s market capitalization stands at $117.96 billion based on the $92.16 opening price, yielding a price-to-earnings ratio of 24.71 and a price-to-earnings-growth ratio of 2.44.  This valuation overview  also notes a beta of 0.55, suggesting that Medtronic shares have historically exhibited lower volatility than the broader equity market. For investors, the combination of a large-cap market value, mid-20s earnings multiple, and below-market beta underlines Medtronic’s role as a core holding in many healthcare portfolios, with defensive characteristics balanced against specific regulatory and legal risks.  From a balance-sheet perspective, the same data show that Medtronic has a quick ratio of 1.62, a current ratio of 2.13, and a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.52.  This snapshot  suggests that the company maintains solid short-term liquidity and moderate leverage, which can support ongoing investment in R&D, acquisitions like the $650 million SPR Therapeutics deal, and shareholder returns via dividends. In the context of the FDA Early Alert and litigation costs such as the $88 million hernia mesh award, these metrics help investors gauge Medtronic’s capacity to absorb shocks while pursuing its product cycle strategy.  Read more  For a deeper overview of Medtronic’s business segments and investor materials, including updates on cardiovascular, neuroscience, and medical-surgical portfolios, readers can consult Medtronic’s own investor relations resources, which offer presentations and detailed financial reports that complement the market data and analyst commentary summarized here.  Medtronic stock and investor takeaway  Medtronic’s recent trading in the low-$90s as of late August 2026, against a 52-week range of $73.31 to $106.33 and an analyst price target of $114, presents investors with a familiar healthcare-equipment trade-off between valuation upside and event risk.  The analyst report  emphasizing a strong product cycle, possible fiscal 2027 guidance increases, and recent 8.4 percent trailing twelve-month revenue growth provides a quantitative basis for the bullish case. Conversely, the FDA Early Alert for the Bravo CF esophageal pH capsule delivery device and the $88 million hernia mesh jury award illustrate the type of regulatory and legal exposures that can occasionally pressure margins and sentiment.  For US retail investors evaluating Medtronic on the New York Stock Exchange, the key decision centers on whether the company’s diversified portfolio, ongoing innovation in areas such as renal denervation and surgical robotics, and balance-sheet strength will enable it to close the perceived undervaluation gap highlighted by the $100.57 intrinsic value estimate and the higher $114 price target. As of late August 2026, the data suggest that Medtronic has delivered consistent, if modest, earnings beats, maintained solid liquidity, and continued to invest in growth, all while managing adverse events and regulatory scrutiny that are part of operating a global medical device franchise.
