Avient stock has gained 20.4% year to date, yet the latest valuation checks and an intrinsic value estimate based on a Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) model suggest the share price may still sit at a discount to what the underlying cash flows imply.
Year to date, Avient has returned 20.4%, which puts fresh focus on whether the recent share price level already reflects its fundamentals.
Future cash flow generation and margin resilience can support the current valuation. In contrast, any setback in cash conversion or higher capital needs may weigh on what investors are willing to pay.
On Simply Wall St's broader valuation checks, Avient is assessed as undervalued in 5 of 6 areas. This tilts the overall picture toward the stock looking cheap rather than fully priced.
The stock's next move may depend on whether Avient's current price still leaves enough room between market value and this intrinsic value estimate to compensate investors for the risks involved.
The Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) approach here focuses on the cash that Avient can generate for shareholders over time. Based on the latest twelve-month free cash flow of about $200.3 million and projections that assume growing cash generation, the model points to an intrinsic value of about $60.71 per share.
Set against the current market price, that intrinsic value implies the stock trades at roughly a 37.1% discount. The cash flow path used in the model is relatively steady rather than aggressive, which gives some support to the idea that Avient may be pricing in more caution than the cash flows alone suggest.
On this DCF view, Avient stock currently appears undervalued relative to its estimated intrinsic value.
The P/E ratio is a useful cross check for Avient because it directly links what you pay today to the earnings the company is already producing. Avient currently trades on a P/E of about 22.2x, compared with an industry average of roughly 27.0x and a peer group average near 31.5x within chemicals.
The model based fair P/E for Avient, which blends its earnings profile, industry, size and risk, is about 20.6x. That is close to the current 22.2x, so the stock does not appear clearly cheap or stretched on this measure despite sitting below the broader peer averages. For an investor, this suggests the cash flow upside indicated by the DCF is neither strongly contradicted nor strongly reinforced by the earnings multiple.
Overall, Avient appears to be priced at roughly a fair level on its P/E multiple when set against its tailored fair ratio and sector benchmarks.
