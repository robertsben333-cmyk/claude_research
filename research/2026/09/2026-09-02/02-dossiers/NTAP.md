# NTAP — NetApp, Inc.

**What this print is about.** NetApp reports FQ1 FY2027 (quarter ended 31 July 2026) after the close on 2 September 2026 [1][2]. This is not a quarter about headline EPS — the near-term bar is trivially low (consensus $2.12 vs a company guide midpoint of $2.10 [3][12]). It is about two things that collide in the same release. First, **FY27 guidance**: the Street's published FY27 model ($9.02 EPS on $7.54B revenue, 20 analysts, as of 31 Aug 2026 [15]) already sits at or above the **top** of NetApp's own FY27 guide ($8.70–$9.00 on $7.325–$7.575B [12][13]), so management has to *raise* the year simply to validate consensus. Second, **product gross margin**: management pre-announced on the Q4 call that the July quarter is "more or less the trough" for product GM (56.1% in Q4) as NAND and DRAM inflation runs ahead of NetApp's price increases, which take roughly three quarters to flow through [11][13]. The reported quarter is therefore the ugliest margin quarter of the year by design, and the guide-up has to carry the stock. The set-up is loaded: shares are +28.6% since the last print (from a $142.40 pre-earnings close to $183.16) but **−12.4% from the $209.06 52-week high set on 13 Aug 2026** [8][21], options price a ±12.1% move against a six-quarter median realised move of 3.25% [7][8], and the closest pure-play peer — Everpure (ex-Pure Storage) — beat, raised FY27 revenue guidance by ~$590M, and **fell ~14% on the week on gross-margin concerns** [26][27].

My read: the triage rationale ("history of blowing through its own implied move") is **half wrong** and I say so in §2. NetApp has exceeded its implied move in only 3 of the last 8 prints [4], and at a ~12% implied the option is expensive relative to base rates. The directional edge, such as it is, is mildly negative — not because the quarter will be bad, but because the FY27 raise required to clear a 12% bar is larger than what a margin-trough quarter usually permits.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**It is the FY27 guidance revision, read jointly with product gross margin.** Not headline EPS.

Why:
- Management explicitly told the market in May that **"July quarter is more or less the trough" for product gross margin**, driven by elevated NAND and component costs, with gradual improvement as price increases take effect over "about 3 quarters" [11][13]. Q4 product GM was 56.1% [13]. The quarter being reported is therefore *pre-announced* as the margin low. The information content is not the level — it is whether management **confirms the trough** or pushes it out.
- The macro has moved against them since May. TrendForce: conventional **server DRAM contract prices +13–18% QoQ in 3Q26** [29], NAND +10–15% QoQ [29], and enterprise SSD contract prices rose ~80% in Q1 alone [29]. If the trough moves from Q1 to Q2 or Q3, the FY27 GM guide (68.5–69.5%) breaks and the $8.85 EPS midpoint goes with it.
- **What the market expects for it.** Consensus FY27 EPS of $9.02 [15] sits at the very top of the $8.70–$9.00 guide — i.e. the Street is underwriting *both* a guide raise *and* the margin trough being confirmed. That is how I know the expectation: the published annual consensus is arithmetically incompatible with a reiterate.
- Secondary metric to watch, same release: **AI/data-preparation win count and AFX commentary.** NetApp did ~500 AI wins in Q4 alone and 1,100+ in FY26 vs ~400 in FY25 [10][13]; Q4's 500 were all on-premises, split ~50% data prep, ~25% training/fine-tuning, ~25% inference [11]. AFX is now certified for NVIDIA DGX SuperPOD [24]. A sequential *deceleration* in wins would undercut the entire AI-storage narrative that took the stock from $142 to $209.

**What changed since the last print — and the quality-of-revenue problem (my analysis)**
- The CFO attributed the Q4 product revenue beat specifically to "the execution of a **multi-year agreement with Google Cloud** to deliver secure, AI-ready data infrastructure to Google Distributed Cloud environments," and noted Support revenue was "**partly driven by a one-time item**" [13]. Two of the three revenue lines in the quarter that caused a +22.4% stock move were flattered by non-recurring or lumpy items. That is the comparison NetApp now has to lap, and it is the strongest argument that the May reaction over-extrapolated.
- CEO Kurian did push back: he said "on the face of the Q4 P&L, the impact of pull forward or accelerated decision-making was minimal," attributing Q4 to large deals previously flagged for H2 FY26 [13]. Management nonetheless wrote into FY27 guidance that they "recognize the potential for pockets of demand driven by accelerated purchasing" [13] — i.e. they themselves flagged pull-forward risk.
- **Inventory built** both YoY and QoQ, and inventory turns fell sequentially to 12 [13]. My inference: consistent with pre-buying memory ahead of price rises — supportive of near-term GM, a working-capital drag, and a signal management expects costs to keep climbing.
- M&A since the print: **DataPelago** (announced 16 Jul 2026, GPU-accelerated data processing at the storage layer, terms undisclosed) [24] and **JetStream Software** (6 Aug 2026, VMware DR/migration) [24]. Neither is sized publicly; both add small dilution risk to the FY27 opex line that guidance may not have contemplated.
- FY26 R&D $991M; ~11,700 employees at 24 Apr 2026 [23].
- **Customer concentration is severe**: two distributor customers accounted for **43% of net revenues in FY26**, each >10% [23]. (FY25 split: Arrow 21%, TD Synnex 24% [23].) Distributor ordering patterns can shift a quarter by several points.

## 9. Bull / bear / base

**Bull case.** The demand backdrop is the best NetApp has ever faced and the sell side is not yet fully positioned for it. Dell just printed a $95B AI backlog with ISG margins expanding 490bp [33], Nvidia's non-hyperscaler half of data centre is the exact customer NetApp sells into [34], and NetApp's own AI wins nearly tripled to 1,100+ in FY26 with 500 in Q4 alone, all on-premises [10][13]. AFX is DGX SuperPOD-certified [24], DataPelago and JetStream broaden the stack [24], unbilled RPO is +88% and total RPO +14% — forward visibility is improving faster than revenue [13]. The quarter's bar is low (consensus $2.12 vs $2.10 guide mid, ESP +3.77%, 4/4 beats [3]), the memory cost squeeze is exactly what management pre-flagged as a Q1 trough with price increases arriving over the following three quarters [11][13], and the stock has already given back 12.4% from its high with short interest halved [8][9][21]. If NetApp raises FY27 revenue to $7.7B+ with EPS above $9.00 and says the trough is behind it, there is nothing on the tape stopping a repeat of the May gap — the 2.2:1 call skew says some flow is already positioned for it [17].

**Bear case.** The May +22.4% was built on two things that do not repeat: a lumpy multi-year Google Distributed Cloud agreement inside product revenue and "a one-time item" inside Support [13], amplified by a 12.2%-of-float short base [9] that has since collapsed to 5.47% [9]. Strip the extra week from the Q1 guide and underlying growth is ~13%, not 17% [13]. The Street's FY27 model ($9.02 / $7.54B [15]) is already at the top of company guidance, so anything short of a raise is a de facto cut — and Q1 is the *worst* quarter of the year to raise a full year into, because it is the pre-announced product-GM trough with server DRAM still inflating 13–18% QoQ [11][13][29]. Everpure just ran this exact experiment: a 38% growth print with a ~$590M guidance raise, sold 14% on the week on gross-margin concerns [26][27]. NetApp also carries 43% of revenue through two distributors [23], has an inventory build and falling turns [13], and has seen its price targets marked up 14–39% by four brokers in three weeks [18][19][20][21] — the sell side has already spent the good news. At a 12.1% implied move, the market is demanding perfection to break even.

**Base case (mine).** NetApp beats the quarter modestly — EPS in the $2.15–$2.25 range, revenue in the upper half of the $1.75–$1.90B guide — because the ESP is positive, the extra week is a near-certainty, and the demand data from Dell, Nvidia and Everpure all point the same way. Management confirms that product gross margin troughed in Q1 and nudges FY27 up, but **modestly** — to roughly the top of the existing range rather than materially through it — because the memory cost curve has steepened since May and because they built pull-forward caution into the guide language deliberately [13][29]. That combination is a fine result and probably not a 12% result. With consensus already at the guide's high end [15], a modest raise reads as "in line" and the stock does something between −6% and +5%. The distribution is genuinely two-humped: the credible tail is a large FY27 raise on AI-driven bookings, and the credible left tail is a margin-trough push-out. **Realised move likely inside the ±12.1% implied; direction mildly negative.**

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal of my mildly-negative lean is **an FY27 guidance raise driven by AI/AFX bookings rather than by price increases**. Concretely: FY27 revenue lifted to **≥$7.70B** (i.e. above the current $7.575B high end) with EPS **≥$9.20**, accompanied by (a) a Q1 AI/data-prep win count comfortably above 500 — meaning the Q4 run-rate accelerated rather than pulled forward — and (b) AFX revenue described as *material* rather than "gradual," and (c) unbilled RPO growth sustained near +88%. That combination would prove the May quarter was not a Google-deal one-off, would make the 43%-distributor concentration irrelevant to the narrative, and would justify a re-rate above 22x FY27 — a $200+ handle. The tell will be in the first two minutes of the CFO's guidance section, and specifically whether the FY27 gross-margin range of 68.5–69.5% is **raised, held, or cut**: a cut to that range with a revenue raise is the bear outcome dressed as a beat, and is exactly what Everpure delivered on 26 August.

The mirror-image flip: management pushes the product-GM trough from Q1 to Q2 or Q3 while reiterating FY27. That takes consensus from $9.02 toward $8.60 and, with the stock at 20.3x, is worth well more than the implied move to the downside.
