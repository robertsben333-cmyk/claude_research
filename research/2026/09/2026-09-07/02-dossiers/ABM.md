# ABM — ABM Industries Incorporated

**What this print is about.** ABM is a $2.8bn-cap, low-beta, 58-consecutive-year dividend-raiser
that sells janitorial, engineering, parking, aviation-ground and technical/energy services. It is
not a growth story and it is not really an EPS story — it is a *margin-ramp credibility* story.
Management guided FY2026 adjusted EPS to $3.85–$4.15 [S12] but delivered only $1.73 in the first
half, exactly flat against H1 FY25's $1.73 [S6][S9]. Every cent of the year's growth is therefore
parked in the second half, and this print is the first of the two quarters that has to deliver it.
Consensus of $1.01–$1.04 [S16][S17][S19] implies roughly an 8.0% consolidated segment operating
margin (my arithmetic, §4) against 7.3% in the year-ago quarter, 7.3% last quarter and 7.1% two
quarters ago [S6][S9][S10]. ABM has missed the adjusted-EPS consensus four quarters running
[S17] — and yet the stock rose on three of those four prints [computed, §2], because the market
has been trading the revenue line, the bookings line and the reaffirmation of the guide rather
than the headline. So the question tomorrow is narrow: **does the promised H2 margin inflection
show up in Q3, and does the $3.85–$4.15 range survive?**

**Event confirmed: yes.** Date, session and call time are confirmed from the company's own
press release of 2026-08-25 [S1]. One data provider (TipRanks) carries the session as "After
Close" [S18] and MarketBeat labels the date "estimated" [S19]; both are wrong against the
company source and against Finviz's "Sep 08 BMO" [S23]. Treat the company release as controlling.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Consolidated segment operating margin for FQ3 FY26 — and whether it prints an 8-handle.**

Not EPS, and not revenue. Revenue is the most predictable line ABM has: it has beaten the revenue
consensus in every one of the last nine reported quarters [S17], and StockStory's framing is that
"ABM rarely misses Wall Street's revenue estimates" [S20]. Margin is where the four consecutive
EPS misses came from, it is what management explicitly promised would inflect in H2, and it is
the single number that determines whether the FY guide survives.

**What the market expects for it, and how I know.** No broker publishes a segment-margin
consensus I could source, so I reverse-engineered it from the EPS consensus. Working from Q3 FY25
actuals [S10] — segment operating profit of $170.4M (B&I $73.8M + M&D $36.4M + Aviation $19.7M +
Education $21.1M + ATS $19.4M) on ~$2.22bn revenue = 7.66% on the segment-sum basis, 7.3% as
reported consolidated — and using 58.8M diluted shares (implied by Q2 FY26's $52.9M adjusted net
income / $0.90) and a 29.5% tax rate:

- $1.01 adjusted EPS ⇒ ~$59.4M adjusted net income ⇒ ~$84.2M adjusted pre-tax, versus $73.3M in
  Q3 FY25: a **+$10.9M pre-tax delta**.
- Add roughly **+$5M/quarter of interest headwind** (FY26 interest now guided ~$110M versus FY25's
  actual run-rate implied by the original $95–105M guide) [S6][S12].
- ⇒ segment/corporate profit must rise ~**$15.9M** to ~**$186M** on $2.32bn revenue = **≈8.0%
  segment operating margin.**

Against 7.1% (Q1 FY26), 7.3% (Q2 FY26) and 7.3% (Q3 FY25) [S6][S9][S10]. **The consensus requires
a ~70bp YoY and ~70bp sequential step-up in one quarter.** It is not unprecedented for this
company — Q4 FY25 printed **8.2%** [S12] — but Q4 is seasonally the strongest quarter and Q3 has
never carried an 8-handle in the period I can source. This is the number to read first in the
release. Label: the 8.0% figure is **my inference**, built from company-sourced inputs; it is not
a published estimate.

Secondary reads, in order: (a) whether the **$3.85–$4.15 range is reaffirmed, narrowed, or
trimmed**; (b) **ATS/Technical Solutions revenue growth and margin** — the segment ran +27.2%
revenue at 6.3% margin in Q2 [S6] and management promised mix improvement, so a margin that
does *not* improve there kills the H2 thesis; (c) **free cash flow against the $250M full-year
guide** — H1 FCF was only $71.2M [S13], so H2 needs ~$179M.

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

**Segment mix.** FQ2 FY26 (quarter ended 2026-04-30) [S6]:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Business & Industry | $1,015.8M | flat | $76.7M | 7.6% | 7.5% | 7.1% |
| Manufacturing & Distribution | $463.8M | +16.5% | $40.6M | 8.8% | 8.6% | 8.9% |
| Aviation | $310.8M | +19.5% | $16.3M | 5.2% | 4.2% | 6.8% |
| Education | $232.2M | +1.9% | $16.4M | 7.1% | 9.4% | 9.0% |
| Technical Solutions (ATS) | $267.3M | +27.2% | $16.8M | 6.3% | 3.7% | 7.8% |
| **Consolidated segment margin** | | | | **7.3%** | **7.1%** | **7.3%** |

B&I is still ~44% of revenue and is the drag; ATS and M&D are the growth engines and carry the
acquisition overlay (WGNSTAR, LMC).

**What changed since the last print.**

- **Revenue and organic growth accelerated.** Q2 revenue $2.30bn, +8.4%, organic +6.1%,
  acquisition +2.3% — a Q2 record [S6][S8]. First-half new sales bookings a record **$1.2bn**
  [S6][S13]; FY25 bookings were a record $1.9bn [S12].
- **ATS inflected on AI/data-centre and battery storage.** +27.2% revenue in Q2, with management
  citing data-centre construction growing at a double-digit global pace and US battery storage up
  52% nationally in 2025 [S6][S14].
- **Margin did not.** Consolidated segment margin fell to 7.3% from 7.9% a year earlier [S6];
  adjusted EPS fell to $0.90 from... rose from $0.86, but adjusted *net income* fell to $52.9M
  from $54.1M — the EPS increase came entirely from the lower share count [S6].
- **Interest expense guidance was raised** from $95–105M to ~$110M at Q2 [S6][S8][S12].
- **Leverage rose on the WGNSTAR deal.** Total debt $1.7bn / 2.9x at Q1 FY26 [S9] → **$1.9bn /
  3.2x at Q2 FY26**, with management targeting **below 3.0x by fiscal year-end** and naming debt
  repayment the near-term capital priority [S6][S14]. Available liquidity $613.8M [S6]. The 10-Q
  shows ~$1,866.8M outstanding including **$1,045.0M drawn on the revolver** — floating rate —
  against covenants of 5.00x total net leverage and 1.50x minimum interest coverage, in
  compliance [S21].
- **The buyback is running out of room.** H1 FY26 repurchases were 2.13M shares at an average
  $44.17 for $94.1M [S21] — about 3.6% of shares out, a material H1 EPS tailwind. **Remaining
  authorisation is only $89.0M** [S21], down from $233M at Q3 FY25 [S10], and management has said
  debt paydown comes first [S14]. My inference: the share-count tailwind that carried H1 EPS
  largely disappears in H2, which makes the operating margin ramp carry all the weight.

**Unit economics and balance sheet.** Gross margin 11.35%, operating margin 4.13%, net margin
1.75%, ROE 8.87%, debt/equity 1.13, current ratio 1.46 [S23]. Goodwill was **$2,738.4M** at
2026-04-30, up $147.3M on WGNSTAR ($146.0M) [S21] — i.e. goodwill alone slightly exceeds the
entire $2.76bn market cap, so tangible book is deeply negative. No impairment indicated; a $1.9M
ROU-asset write-down sits inside the restructuring charge [S21].

**Free cash flow and the ERP overhang.** This is ABM's live scar. A botched ERP rollout in B&I and
M&D drove billing and collection delays and **negative $123M free cash flow in Q1 FY25** [S11].
The recovery has been real: Q3 FY25 operating cash flow $175.0M (+120%) and FCF $150.2M (+134%)
[S10]; FY25 FCF $155.1M [S12]; Q1 FY26 OCF $62.0M / FCF $48.9M [S9]; Q2 FY26 OCF $66.2M / FCF
$22.4M [S6]. H1 FY26 OCF $128.2M and FCF $71.2M, against a *use* of $73.9M and −$107.8M in H1 FY25
[S13]. Three of five segments are now live on the platform [S14]. Receivables at 2026-04-30 were
$1,263.5M billed + $277.2M unbilled = **$1,517.2M**, versus $1,471.1M at FY25 year-end [S21] —
roughly 60 days on the quarter's revenue (my calc), so the working-capital build has stabilised
but not reversed. The bad-debt provision was **−$0.1M** in Q2 FY26 versus $2.1M a year earlier
[S21], which is a genuine collections-quality improvement.

**Customer concentration.** One customer at **10.40% of revenue** per SEC filing data [S30] —
snippet-only and I could not verify the exact fiscal period, so treat with caution. Otherwise
diversified across ~113,000 employees and five segments [S31].

**Contract headwinds management has already flagged for H2** [S14]:
- B&I revenue "will likely moderate" on client exits **including a large UK client (TfL)**, which
  alone cuts B&I growth by **~300bp in the second half**.
- Aviation organic growth will "remain strong, but moderate from Q2" as ABM anniversaries several
  large contracts, with management watching fuel costs at airline clients.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The H2 setup management described actually arrives, and the market pays for it.
The evidence: ATS grew +27.2% in Q2 with management promising volume ramp *and* mix improvement
[S6][S14], and the peer complex (FIX +73% backlog, EME +44% RPO, MYRG record) plus Dell'Oro's
H2-acceleration call say that end market is wide open [S42][S36]. Fiscal Q3 is Aviation's
seasonal peak and summer 2026 was the busiest on record — 2.99M screened on 2026-06-23, 8 of the
10 busiest days ever in June [S35] — into a segment already compounding +19.5% [S6]. The $35M
restructuring is at full run-rate from early FY26 [S10]. The self-insurance landmine that
destroyed Q4 FY25 by $(0.26) appears to be a Q2/Q4 review item and Q2 FY26 came back clean [S21]
[S15], so the year-ago Q3 comp is not booby-trapped. The definitional change to adjusted EPS is
an implicit raise nobody has marked to market [S8][S12]. And the stock is 10.8x forward [S23]
with a sell side whose consensus target is *at* the spot price [S25] and short interest of 3.2%
and falling [S2][S23] — nobody is positioned for good news. Precedent: the last two prints where
ABM missed EPS but held the story were +5.49% and +6.67% [computed].

**Bear case.** The margin ramp is a promise ABM has broken four quarters in a row and the
arithmetic of the guide is now the problem. Consensus at $1.01–$1.04 requires roughly **8.0%
segment operating margin** against 7.1%, 7.3% and 7.3% in the three most recent quarters
[S6][S9][S10] — a 70bp sequential step-up in a quarter that has never carried an 8-handle. The
bar was *raised* into the print ($0.98→$1.01, 3 up / 0 down) [S16], not lowered. The H1 EPS
tailwind from repurchasing 3.6% of the shares [S21] largely disappears because only $89.0M of
authorisation remains and debt paydown is the stated priority [S21][S14]. Management has already
pre-flagged two revenue headwinds for exactly this half — the TfL/UK exit costing B&I ~300bp and
Aviation growth moderating on contract anniversaries [S14] — and raised interest expense
guidance to ~$110M [S6]. Meanwhile the macro turned against a 3.2x-levered floating-rate borrower
three sessions ago [S41][S21], industrials are −7% QTD [S41], and the two worst prints in ABM's
recent history (−8.67%, −9.07%) both came from exactly this pattern: revenue fine, margin light,
guide questioned. If the range is trimmed rather than reaffirmed, the implied 8.4% [S27] is the
right order of magnitude for the downside.

**Base case (mine).** Revenue beats — it has for nine straight quarters [S17] and Aviation and
ATS seasonality both point the right way. Adjusted EPS lands at or slightly below the $1.01–$1.04
consensus, because a 70bp one-quarter margin step-up is more than this management team has
executed all year. The FY range is **reaffirmed rather than raised**, most likely with continued
"toward the low end" language on segment margin [S6], and Q4 is left carrying $1.08–$1.41. On the
reaction function I documented in §1 — three of the last four EPS misses produced flat-to-up
sessions, and Vestis just did the same thing on 2026-08-11 [S28] — a reaffirmed guide plus record
bookings plus visible ATS momentum is more likely than not to be rewarded, but modestly, and by
less than the 8.4% the options are charging for. **I lean marginally positive with low
conviction: direction score +12, prob_up 54.** Honest characterisation: this is close to a coin
flip with a slight tilt, and the sharper observation in this dossier is about *magnitude* — the
implied 8.4% sits above the 5.81% mean and 6.08% median of the last six realised moves, and the
front-month IV of 49.0% sits at ~1.95x RV30 of 18.7% [S27][S26]. If there is an edge here it is
more likely in selling the event premium than in picking the direction.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal, stated concretely: **ABM trims the FY26 adjusted EPS range — say to
$3.85–$4.00 or $3.80–$4.00 — while pointing at the TfL/UK exit, moderating Aviation comps and
~$110M of interest expense.** That is not a tail scenario; it follows directly from the
arithmetic in §2, because the low end alone needs +24.7% H2 growth on a reported basis after H1
was exactly flat [S6][S9][S10]. The trigger to watch in the release is a **segment operating
margin that prints with a 7-handle** — 7.4%–7.6% would be a solid sequential improvement in
absolute terms and would still be a clear miss against what the $1.01 consensus embeds, and it
would make the Q4 residual ($1.11–$1.41 versus $0.88 a year ago) visibly unreachable. That is the
2026-03-10 template: revenue fine, margin light, guide questioned, stock −4.62% [S17][computed].

The mirror-image flip, if you come in bearish: **ATS margin recovers toward its 7.8% Q3 FY25 level
from 6.3% in Q2** [S6][S10] *and* management raises the low end of the FY range on the strength
of the bookings backlog. Given the peer backlog data [S42], that is a live possibility rather
than a courtesy scenario, and it would likely take the stock through the 52-week high at $50.12.

The lower-probability but high-impact wildcard: **an unexpected prior-year self-insurance
adjustment in Q3.** The Q2 10-Q language implies the review is a Q2/Q4 exercise [S21], so I do
not expect one — but if a $(0.15)–$(0.26)/share charge appears the way it did in Q4 FY25 [S15],
the print misses badly, the guide almost certainly moves, and the −8% to −9% precedents apply.
