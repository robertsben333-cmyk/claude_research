# ORCL — Oracle Corporation

**What this print is about.** Oracle reports fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended 31 Aug 2026) after the close on Thursday 10 September 2026 — confirmed by the company's own 2 September press release [1]. This is not an EPS event. Oracle has already told the Street exactly what the P&L will look like (non-GAAP EPS $1.72–$1.76, revenue +27–29%, total cloud +58–64% USD) [2], and consensus sits obediently inside the guide at ~$1.74 on ~$19.13bn [11][13]. What the print actually trades on is whether the **$638bn RPO backlog converts** — specifically the sequential direction of RPO and whether OCI accelerates from +93% to the ~+116% the guide implies — and, on the other side of the ledger, whether the FY27 capex/financing envelope ($90–95bn gross, ~$70bn net, ~$40bn of new debt+equity) gets **worse** [2][38][39]. The market's reaction function for this name changed a year ago: the last three reports were decided on capex, free cash flow and financing, not on revenue. Oracle is 53% below its September 2025 all-time high [4], carries a BBB– rating from S&P with Moody's on negative outlook and a 5-year CDS near an 18-year high [20][21], and has just rallied **+14.4% in six sessions into the print** [5]. Options price ~11.5%. The setup is a genuinely two-sided, high-variance event where the fundamental numbers will probably be fine and the stock's direction is decided by capex, RPO optics and how much of the good news the six-session run-up already bought.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**The sequential change in RPO, read alongside the FY27 capex/financing envelope.**

Not EPS. Not revenue. Oracle pre-committed the P&L in June [2], and the market has spent three consecutive quarters demonstrating that it will look through a beat. In December 2025 the stock fell ~11% on a $150m revenue miss *and* a $15bn capex-guide raise [44]. In June 2026 it fell 8.5% on a top- and bottom-line **beat** because FY26 free cash flow printed at **−$23.7bn** and management guided to ~$70bn of net capex plus ~$40bn of new debt and equity for FY27 [2][22][38].

**What the market expects for it:**
- RPO was **$638bn** at 31 May 2026, up **$85bn** sequentially from $553bn and **+363% YoY**; ~$75bn of that is prepaid / customer-supplied GPU hardware on AI contracts [2].
- Management's own conversion schedule: ~**12% within 12 months (~$76.6bn)**, 34% in 13–36 months, 34% in 37–60 months, 20% beyond [40] *(snippet_only)*.
- Oracle delivered >1.2 GW in all of FY2026 and told investors it expects **nearly 1 GW in Q1 FY2027 alone**, with Abilene reportedly ahead of schedule [40] *(snippet_only)*. That is the physical claim underneath the OCI acceleration.
- Consensus focus per the previews: "whether OCI growth remains within guidance and whether **sequential RPO expands**, confirming Oracle's position as a viable third hyperscale AI platform" [40]; and "how quickly this backlog converts into recognised revenue" [13].

**How I know, and my read:** every preview I sourced — IG [13], SpotGamma [7], Pomegra [40], the Q4 post-mortems [26][22] — converges on RPO conversion and capex as the decision variables, and none of them leads with EPS. The asymmetry I see is this: RPO has to absorb ~$19bn of revenue recognition this quarter, and **I found no announced Oracle mega-contract between 10 June and 9 September 2026**. FY26's $85bn and $183bn sequential adds came from a small number of named megadeals (OpenAI, xAI, Meta, Nvidia, AMD [45]). If Q1 FY27 bookings are lumpy-low, RPO could print roughly flat or **down sequentially for the first time**, and "backlog shrinks" is a headline the tape will not forgive at an 11.5% implied move. Conversely, a fresh megadeal disclosed on the call is the single fastest route to the +11% tail. Note also the 28 October **Analyst Day**, where Oracle is expected to update its FY2030 targets [17] — management has an incentive to hold new disclosure back for that event, which mildly biases this call toward being informationally thin.

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

**Segment mix and unit economics (FY2026, year ended 31 May 2026)** [2]:

| Line | FY26 | Growth | Q4 FY26 | Q4 growth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Total revenue | $67.4bn | +17% | $19.2bn | +21% |
| Total cloud | $34.0bn | +39% | $9.9bn | +47% |
| — Cloud infra (OCI/IaaS) | $18.1bn | +77% | $5.8bn | **+93%** |
| — Cloud apps (SaaS) | $15.9bn | +11% | $4.1bn | +10% |
| Software (licence + support) | $24.5bn | **−1%** | $6.8bn | **−2%** |
| Services | $5.7bn | +10% | $1.5bn | +13% |
| Hardware | $3.1bn | +5% | $0.9bn | +9% |

- **Margin trajectory:** FY26 GAAP operating margin 31%, non-GAAP 43%; Q4 GAAP 32%, non-GAAP 45% [2]. Q1 FY26 was 29% / 42% [3]. Margins are holding up despite the OCI mix shift, which is the bull's core evidence. Jefferies flags "115% OCI growth and a 41% operating margin" as the KPIs it is watching [18]; Morgan Stanley raised numbers specifically on an **improved GPUaaS gross-margin trajectory** [18]. GPUaaS gross margin is the swing factor: it is structurally far below Oracle's legacy software margin, so OCI mix growth mechanically dilutes group gross margin unless pricing and utilisation improve.
- **Free cash flow:** FY26 operating cash flow $32.0bn (+54%, a record) but capex $55.7bn (from $21.2bn in FY25), giving **FCF of −$23.7bn** versus −$0.394bn in FY25 [2][39][22]. This is the number that broke the stock.
- **Balance sheet:** total debt **$167.43bn**, cash $31.89bn, **net debt $135.54bn** ($47.05/share); EV/EBITDA 19.71 [6]. S&P estimates the FCF deficit could widen to ~$42bn if spending continues at the current pace [20].
- **Capital raising / dilution:** FY26 raised $43bn debt + $5bn equity; FY27 plans ~**$40bn** more including a **$20bn at-the-market common equity programme** [2][47]. The February 2026 notes deal alone was **$25bn** across eight tranches with coupons ranging 4.55% (2029) to **6.85% (2066)** [26]. There is also **Mandatory Convertible Preferred Stock** outstanding paying $1,625/share [25]. Shares outstanding are up 1.68% YoY [6] and the **buyback is effectively dead: $95m repurchased in all of FY2026** [2]. Dividend $0.50/quarter, 1.24% yield [2][6].
- **Customer concentration:** S&P estimates OpenAI is **about half** of Oracle's backlog; BofA puts it above 50% [42] *(snippet_only)*. S&P explicitly named OpenAI a "key credit risk" in the downgrade [20]. The contract is ~$300bn of compute over five years starting in 2027 [31][45].
- **Multi-cloud database:** Ellison reported MultiCloud database revenue from AWS/Google/Azure grew **1,529%** in Q1 FY26 [3] — a smaller but very high-margin line and a genuine bull differentiator versus pure GPU rental.

**What changed since the last print (10 June 2026):**
1. **S&P downgraded Oracle to BBB–/A-3 from BBB/A-2 on 2026-07-09**, one notch above speculative grade, naming OpenAI concentration as a key credit risk; Moody's outlook negative [20][24]. Oracle's 5-year CDS reached ~**203bp, an 18-year high** [21].
2. **Project Jupiter (Santa Teresa, NM) — the New Mexico Supreme Court granted a unanimous temporary stay on 2026-08-23/24**, pausing the air-quality permit hearing and challenging the well-drilling authorisation [30]. Does not cancel the project but slows the schedule at the moment Oracle needs ~1 GW delivered in Q1 alone.
3. **The 600 MW Abilene expansion talks between Oracle and OpenAI ended in March 2026** [31] — earlier, but the same theme: contracted backlog is running ahead of deliverable power.
4. **OpenAI's liquidity event slipped to 2027** [41], and WSJ reported (2026-04-28) that OpenAI missed internal user and revenue targets [42] *(snippet_only)*.
5. **Headcount is falling.** Revelio Labs puts Oracle at ~143,406 employees as of March 2026, **−1.6% YoY**, with hiring velocity down to 3,965 roles/month from 4,829 in 2025 and 6,473 in 2024; a second 2026 layoff round reportedly ran before September, touching OCI engineers, Cerner and enterprise sales [34] *(snippet_only, and a low-tier source — treat as colour)*.
6. **The stock.** From the 10 June pre-earnings close of $201.26 [8] to a low near $141.32 on 1 September [5], then a violent +14.4% six-session rally into the print [5].

**What is at stake:** Oracle is mid-way through converting itself from a 43%-non-GAAP-margin, cash-generative software company into a capital-intensive, debt-funded infrastructure utility with a single dominant, loss-making counterparty. This print is one of the first data points on whether the conversion produces revenue at acceptable margin, or just more capex.

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Regime.** Nasdaq Composite ~26,584, up 1.4% in early September [32] *(snippet_only)*. The important macro fact is a **hawkish repricing**: strong August payrolls (162k, unemployment 4.1%) and CPI near 3.4% YoY have pushed futures to roughly **60% odds of a 25bp Fed HIKE at the 15–16 September FOMC** [33] *(snippet_only — I could not verify this against a tier-1 source and it is unusual enough that it should be treated with caution)*. If even directionally right, this is a specific and material headwind for ORCL: a BBB–, $167bn-debt issuer funding a −$23.7bn FCF deficit with 30-to-40-year paper at 5.7–6.85% coupons [26][6][20] is about as rate-sensitive as large-cap tech gets. The Fed meets **three trading days after this print**, which is also a reason the monthly straddle prices wider than the weekly.

**FX / commodity:** Oracle's guidance is given in both USD and constant currency and the two are within 1pt for Q1 FY27 (revenue +27–29% both; cloud +58–64% USD vs +57–63% cc) [2], so FX is close to a non-factor this quarter. The relevant "commodity" is **memory and GPU cost inflation** — Amazon explicitly raised its 2026 capex guide from ~$200bn to ~$220bn *because of the higher cost of memory* [35]. That is the mechanism by which Oracle's $90–95bn gross capex could get revised up on this call.

**Peers who already reported:**
- **Broadcom (AVGO), Q3 reported 2026-09-02/04.** Revenue $29.59bn (+86%), AI semiconductor revenue **+221% YoY to $16.7bn**, Q4 AI guided to $21.7bn (+236%), and a multi-year AI frame of $58bn (2026) → $115bn (2027) → $230bn (2028). The stock **fell ~3%** — a textbook sell-the-news, because the Q4 total revenue guide of ~$34.8bn came in just under the ~$35bn modelled and the stock had run into the print [28]. **This is the most directly instructive precedent available for ORCL this week.**
- **CoreWeave and Nebius, reported 2026-08-12.** CoreWeave Q2 revenue $2.6bn (+112%), backlog **$104.2bn** (from $99.4bn), >$25bn of net new commitments already in the current quarter; Nebius revenue +514% to $575m, 2026 contracted power target raised to 5 GW, total customer commitments >$40bn. **CRWV +19%, Nebius +34%** [29]. Demand for AI infrastructure is not the constraint; supply and financing are.
- **Hyperscaler capex.** Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet and Meta spent $301bn in H1 2026 and have guided to roughly **$725–732bn for 2026, up ~77% from ~$410bn in 2025**; Amazon raised to ~$220bn, Alphabet to $195–205bn, Meta lifted its floor to $130–145bn [35]. The capex cycle is accelerating, not rolling over.

**Read-through, my synthesis:** the peer tape is unambiguously supportive of Oracle's *demand* narrative and unhelpful for its *stock* narrative. CoreWeave and Nebius show that neocloud backlogs are still compounding. Broadcom shows that in September 2026 a spectacular AI print into a run-up produces a flat-to-down stock. Oracle's differentiator versus all of them is that it is the only one funding the build with investment-grade-marginal debt while burning $24bn a year of free cash flow. **Customer read-through** is the weak link: Oracle's dominant counterparty (OpenAI, ~50%+ of backlog [42]) is private, loss-making (reported ~$20.9bn operating loss in 2025 [23-adjacent] *snippet_only*), and has pushed its liquidity event to 2027 [41]. There is no peer print that can de-risk that.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The guide itself implies a step-change: total cloud +58–64% against +47% last quarter, and OCI accelerating from +93% toward the ~+116% TD Cowen models — off a physical claim of nearly 1 GW delivered in Q1 alone versus 1.2 GW in all of FY26 [2][43][40]. If that lands, the "backlog is just paper" bear thesis takes a real hit, and it lands into margins that have so far *held* (Q4 non-GAAP operating margin 45%, up from 42% a year earlier [2][3]) and a Morgan Stanley note explicitly raising numbers on a better GPUaaS gross-margin trajectory [18]. Sentiment is where Jefferies says it is — near peak-negative, with the stock 53% off its high and a forward P/E of 20.05 against a 33% FY27 revenue growth target [19][4][2]. Short interest is negligible so there is no forced-cover mechanic, but there is also nobody left to sell short. The peer tape is loud: CoreWeave +19%, Nebius +34%, Broadcom AI +221%, hyperscaler capex guided to ~$725bn [29][28][35]. Zacks ESP is +2.08% with 30-day revisions positive [14]. And ORCL's own base rate is that it exceeded the priced move on three of the last five prints [10] — the fat right tail is real and was worth +35.95% one year ago to the day [10].

**Bear case.** The market's reaction function for this name is now capex, FCF and financing, and Oracle beat on both lines in June and still fell 8.5% [10][22][2]. Since then S&P has cut it to **BBB–** with OpenAI named as a key credit risk, Moody's outlook is negative, and the 5-year CDS is at an 18-year high near 203bp [20][21]. FY27 asks for ~$70bn net capex, $90–95bn gross, and ~$40bn of fresh debt and equity including a live $20bn ATM that is already diluting (+1.68% share count YoY) while the buyback is dead at $95m [2][38][47][6]. Amazon has just shown that memory cost inflation forces capex guides *up* [35] — the most likely direction of surprise on the capex line is the wrong one. Physical delivery is under strain: the New Mexico Supreme Court stayed Project Jupiter's permits on 23–24 August, and the 600 MW Abilene expansion talks ended in March [30][31]. RPO must absorb ~$19bn of recognition and I found **no announced megadeal since 10 June**, so a flat-or-down sequential RPO print is a live risk with no good spin. And the stock is **+14.4% in six sessions and +11.1% over 20 days into the event** [5] on a product headline from its customer and a $3 price-target raise [16][18] — with Broadcom's 2 September sell-the-news as the freshest template [28]. Zero Sell ratings and a consensus target ~50% above spot mean the marginal post-print analyst action is another target cut [46][11].

**Base case.** Oracle prints inside its own guide — EPS $1.74–$1.78, revenue $19.0–19.3bn, total cloud at or near the top of 58–64%, OCI somewhere between +100% and +116% — because the guide was set only three months ago against contracts already signed and capacity already energising [2][43]. Free cash flow is again deeply negative and capex is reaffirmed or nudged up. RPO prints somewhere between $620bn and $680bn; the *sign* of that sequential change, not the level, sets the tape. Given the +14.4% six-session run-up, a market pricing 11.5% and a company with an incentive to save its FY2030 reset for the 28 October Analyst Day [17], I think the distribution is **wide, slightly fat on the downside, and roughly a coin flip on sign with a modest negative tilt**. My working numbers: preliminary direction score **−22**, probability of an up move **41%**, conviction **Low**. I want to be explicit that "Low" is the honest label — an 11.5% implied move on a name whose last five reactions were −8.5%, +9.2%, −10.8%, +35.9%, +13.3% is not a set-up in which anyone should carry high conviction on direction.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal, stated concretely: **a new named multi-billion-dollar contract disclosed on the call that pushes RPO decisively above $700bn, paired with a reaffirmed — not raised — $70bn net capex number and an explicit statement that the ~$40bn FY27 financing plan is unchanged or smaller.** That combination attacks the bear thesis at both ends simultaneously: it proves bookings did not stop with the FY26 megadeal cluster, and it removes the "every good quarter costs more money" reflex that has driven the last three selloffs. It would also implicitly de-risk the OpenAI concentration by diluting it with a second name. Oracle has done exactly this before — Catz pre-announced in Q1 FY26 that RPO was "likely to exceed half-a-trillion dollars" and the stock went +35.95% [3][10].

The mirror-image flip on the bull side: **RPO printing below $638bn sequentially, or FY27 gross capex guided above $95bn.** Either alone is probably a high-single-digit down day; together they would test the $145.75 straddle breakeven [7] and the $141 September low [5].

A third, lower-probability flip: management pulling forward any of the FY2030 OCI ladder ($32bn → $73bn → $114bn → $144bn [2][3]) rather than deferring it to 28 October.
