## 3. The one metric that matters

**It is the CooperSurgical strategic-review language, and second to it the fiscal-Q3 buyback dollar figure.** Neither is a P&L line.

Why: JANA Partners built a stake in October 2025 and pushed to break the company up, including floating a combination of CooperVision with Bausch + Lomb [24]. Cooper responded on 4 December 2025 by naming Colleen Jay board chair and launching a "formal and comprehensive strategic review… including through partnerships, joint ventures, divestitures, mergers, business combinations and other transactions," while committing capital to a $2bn buyback [25]. On the 4 June call White confirmed "robust interest" from multiple parties in the *whole* of CooperSurgical and said a "significant portion" of proceeds "certainly will be used for share buybacks" [8]. Wells Fargo has been cited at a ~$4.1bn deal value for the unit [14] — about 31% of Cooper's current $13.2bn market cap [7]. Stifel's post-Q2 framing was that a post-divestiture, pure-play CooperVision is worth ~$80/share [26].

**What the market expects, and how I know:** the options tape has been positioned for the deal — a Market Rebellion pre-market IV report on 11 August recorded a **13.5:1 call/put ratio in COO with the focus in February $85 calls** [13], strikes ~26% above spot with a five-month horizon, which is deal-speculation structure, not earnings structure. Meanwhile the equity has faded 11.8% since 19 August [22]. **Inference (mine):** the market is holding cheap long-dated call optionality on a deal while letting the cash equity drift, which means the *equity* is probably not pricing near-term deal news. That asymmetry is the single most important thing about this setup.

Secondary metric: **CooperVision Asia-Pacific organic growth.** It was −6% in Q2 [16], management guided it to decline again in Q3 with continued hydrogel rationalisation "all through 2027" [8]. A sequential improvement here would validate the "stabilise by Q4" story; a deeper decline would raise the question of whether the FY CVI cut in June was deep enough.

Tertiary: **gross margin versus the ~66% guide** [8][27]. Q2 printed 68% [16]; a 200bp step-down is already in the number, so the risk is that FX and tariffs overshoot it.

Cooper's beta is 0.80 [19], so the print is mostly idiosyncratic.

**Rates / FX / commodities.** Cooper is a genuine FX story. On 4 June, CFO Andrews said FX turned **"decently negative"** in Q3 and Q4 after contributing roughly **$0.08 per share of benefit in Q2** [8]. What actually happened in fiscal Q3 (1 May – 31 Jul 2026): USD/JPY moved from about **156.66 to 159.15 during May** and reached **163.87 on 29 July**, and the DXY advanced 0.9% in May [40]. EUR/USD sat around **1.15–1.16** in late August/early September and DXY was **98.85 on 8 September**, having been near 99.9 on 1 August [41]. **Inference (mine):** yen weakness through the quarter was substantial and is a double hit — it compresses reported Asia-Pacific revenue in a region already declining organically, and it lands in the same line management already flagged. Euro strength partially offsets it for the larger EMEA book. Net, I would not be surprised by a small revenue miss with EPS held by cost control, which is precisely the historical Cooper pattern. Rates matter through the $2.46bn debt stack and the $550M December 2026 maturity [29], and commodity exposure is limited to freight and resin, both named in the margin bridge [8].

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull.** The EPS bar is the lowest it has been all year: $1.11–1.12 against $1.10 a year ago [4][10][12], already reset down from $1.17 in June [12], against a company that has beaten ten quarters running [16]. Management pre-guided the ugly parts — 66% gross margin, negative FX, APAC down [8] — so the bad news is in the number, and the two closest peers just grew contact lenses 4–5% and raised guidance [42][43][44]. Positioning is washed out: −11.8% in fourteen sessions into the print [22], RSI 41.5, below all three moving averages [19], PTs already cut [15], no crowded short. And there are two free options: a strategic-review update on a process where multiple parties submitted significant interest in a unit Wells Fargo values near $4.1bn versus a $13.2bn market cap [8][14][7], and a buyback that management explicitly promised to accelerate with $860.8M of authorisation against a stock that spent the whole quarter 5–17% below the $81.21 they paid in H1 [8][16][29][22]. The precedent is June: Cooper *cut* CooperVision guidance and the stock still rose 8.6% because the strategic-review narrative dominated [6][8][14].

**Bear.** Four of the last six reactions were negative, two of them worse than −12% [5], and over the most recent three years only 36% of one-day reactions were positive [5]. The yen went from ~157 to ~164 through the exact quarter being reported [40], which is a bigger FX headwind than the "$0.08 benefit reverses" framing implied in June, and it lands on a region already at −6% organic [16]. Gross margin is guided to fall 200bp sequentially on FX, tariffs and deliberately lower production [8][27] — a 100bp miss on a 66% guide is roughly $11M of gross profit, about 4–5 cents, enough to break the beat streak. FY26 revenue guidance was already cut once in June; a second cut two quarters running would be the thing that finally forces the "structural, not cyclical" conclusion, and the sell side has been drifting that way — Rothschild's downgrade, UBS initiating Neutral at $75, JPMorgan at $71, Citi Neutral [15][32]. On the M&A leg: no 8-K since 4 June and no deal press release in July or August [20][23] means the process has taken more than a quarter with nothing to show, and if White's update is a repeat of "advancing discussions," the long-dated calls that have been supporting the story lose their nearest catalyst. The stock's 11.8% slide since 19 August may be information rather than noise.

**Base case (mine).** A small in-line-to-slightly-better non-GAAP EPS print ($1.12–$1.15) helped by cost control and possibly the $15M tariff-refund upside [27], revenue at or a touch below $1.10bn with CooperVision Asia-Pacific still negative but less bad, gross margin at or fractionally under 66%, FY26 EPS guidance held at $4.58–4.66 or narrowed to the upper half, a materially larger buyback disclosure for the quarter, and a strategic-review update that advances the language without naming a buyer. That combination is worth a modest positive reaction from a de-risked base — call it +2 to +5% — but the distribution is fat on both sides and the realised move has exceeded 8% in three of the last six quarters. My preliminary read is **+12 direction score, 55% probability of an up move**, with **low conviction**, because the largest single driver — what management says about CooperSurgical — is not forecastable from public information.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **a second consecutive cut to fiscal 2026 revenue guidance driven by Asia-Pacific, delivered alongside a strategic-review update that does not advance beyond June's language.** Concretely: CooperVision organic growth printing below 3.5% for the quarter, the FY26 revenue range moving below $4.285bn, and White saying only that discussions "continue." That converts Cooper from "cheap sum-of-the-parts with a catalyst" into "structurally decelerating contact-lens business whose activist-driven break-up has stalled," and it removes the one thing that let the stock rise 8.6% in June on a guidance cut. In that scenario I would expect a move worse than the −8.08% implied, because the two 2025 double-digit declines came from exactly this combination [5].

The credible reversal in the other direction is narrower but sharper: **a signed or exclusivity-stage CooperSurgical transaction disclosed on the call.** At the $4.1bn figure cited [14], against a $13.2bn market cap and $2.46bn of debt [7][29], that is a step-change in the capital structure and would likely be worth more than the implied move on its own — and it is exactly what the February $85 call open interest is positioned for [13].
