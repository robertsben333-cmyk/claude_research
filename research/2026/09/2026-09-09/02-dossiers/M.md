# M — Macy's, Inc.

**Prepared:** 2026-09-09 (UTC) · **Event:** 2026-09-10, before market open · **event_confirmed: true**

## What this print is about

This is not an EPS print — it is a **quality-of-beat print**. Macy's almost certainly reports a
large headline earnings beat, because the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February
2026, the refund portal opened 2026-04-20, Macy's is publicly identified as eligible for roughly
**$320 million** of refunds, and its fiscal Q2 (13 weeks ended ~2026-08-01) is exactly the window in
which every comparable retailer — TJX ($219M net), Target ($994M), Ross ($253M), Kohl's (~$150M),
Dillard's ($37.2M) — booked theirs. Macy's own Q2 guidance of **$0.29–$0.34 adjusted EPS explicitly
excluded refunds** ("timing remains uncertain," CFO Tom Edwards, 2026-06-03 call). Sell-side
consensus sits at **$0.35–$0.37**, i.e. *above the top of the company's own guide* on an ex-refund
basis, which tells you the Street has partially pencilled something in but not the full windfall.

The trap is that the market has already learned to look through refund-driven beats. Kohl's booked
its ~$150M, printed $1.28 versus a $0.57 consensus, raised full-year EPS guidance from $1.00–$1.60
to $1.80–$2.40 — **and the stock fell 5%**, because comps were −0.9% and sales missed. So the number
that decides Thursday is not adjusted EPS. It is (i) the **owned+licensed+marketplace comparable
sales** line against a guide of flat to +1%, after a Q1 that did +3.0%, and (ii) the **ex-refund**
component of any FY26 guidance raise. Offsetting that: the stock has already de-rated roughly 10%
from its early-August high into the print, short interest is ~11.9% of float at 6.4 days to cover,
and the published event-implied move has compressed to ~5.2% against a five-quarter mean absolute
realised move of ~7.1%.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Owned + licensed + marketplace comparable sales, company-wide — and the ex-refund half of any FY26
guidance raise.**

*Expectation:* the company guided **flat to +1%** for Q2 [10]. Q1 FY26 printed **+3.0%** company-wide,
the strongest first quarter in four years, with Macy's nameplate +1.6%, Reimagine 200 +2.4%,
Bloomingdale's +10.2% and Bluemercury +6.4% [9][38]. So the guide itself embeds a 200–300bps
deceleration. The year-ago base is Q2 FY25 comps of +1.9% company-wide (Macy's nameplate +1.2% O+L+M,
Bloomingdale's +5.7%, Bluemercury +1.2%) [12][37].

*How I know this is the metric:* every Q2-2026 department-store and off-price print this season was
adjudicated on the underlying comp, not the refund-inflated EPS. Kohl's: EPS $1.28 vs $0.57
consensus, FY guide raised by $0.80, **stock −5%** on a −0.9% comp and a sales miss [16]. Ross: FY EPS
raised to $8.61–$8.77 including ~$0.60 of refund, stock touched an intraday high then **settled −2.0%**
[Ross coverage via 18]. Dillard's beat by 54.7% on $37.2M of refunds plus a $104.1M interchange
settlement gain — a beat built almost entirely on non-operating items [17]. Coresight's 2026-09-08
scorecard documents the split explicitly: Walmart and Target ran refunds *through* earnings and
margins, while a second cohort excluded them from forward guidance to avoid overstating prospects
[15]. The buy side is doing that adjustment itself.

*Secondary metric:* the **gross margin rate**. Q2 FY25 was 39.7%, already down 80bps [12]; Q1 FY26 was
38.9%, down 30bps [9]. Guidance assumes a further 20–40bps tariff/fuel hit [10]. A third consecutive
quarter of GM erosion would confirm that the comp is being bought with markdown dollars.

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

**Q1 FY26 (quarter ended 2026-05-02), the last hard datapoint** [9]:

- Net sales $4.68–4.7B, +1.8% y/y; company comps +3.0% O+L+M.
- Gross margin 38.9%, **−30bps**; SG&A $1.952B = 39.9% of revenue.
- GAAP diluted EPS $0.23; **adjusted diluted EPS $0.13**.
- Real estate gains $15M (a low-quality but recurring line).
- Credit card revenue $172M, **+12%** — CFO cited a healthy portfolio and controlled net credit losses [10].
- Cash $1.294B; total debt $2.4B; merchandise inventory $4.833B, **+3.6%** y/y — roughly in line with
  comp growth, which management framed as deliberate "increased newness across price points" [9][10].
- Operating cash flow $292M; capex $88M PP&E + $89M software → **FCF ≈ $115M** [9].
- Capital return: **$50M buyback (2.6M shares) + $50M dividends** in the quarter [9]. At $50M/qtr the
  buyback is ~3.4% of market cap annualised — real, but not a dominant support.

**Segment mix and the engine.** The growth is not in the Macy's nameplate. Bloomingdale's is on a
seven-quarter comp streak and delivered its highest Q1 sales volume on record [9]; Bluemercury is on an
18-quarter streak as of Q2 FY25 [12]. Reimagine 200 — now ~60% of the store base and ~75% of sales —
posted positive comps in 8 of the last 9 quarters [10]. The bull thesis is entirely a mix story: shrink
the unproductive nameplate, grow luxury and beauty.

**What changed since the last print:**
1. **IEEPA refunds became collectable.** The Supreme Court ruled 6–3 on 2026-02-20 that IEEPA does not
   authorise the tariffs; CIT ordered CBP to begin refunding on 2026-03-04; the CBP portal opened
   2026-04-20 against ~$166B of refunds. Macy's is named at **$320M** [13][14][45]. On 263M shares that
   is roughly **$0.90/share pretax** — about three times the entire guided quarter.
2. **Bluemercury got a new CEO.** Alexandre Choueiri joined 2026-08-03, reporting to Bloomingdale's CEO
   Olivier Bron [40]. Mid-quarter leadership change in the highest-comping small banner.
3. **Real estate monetisation slowed by design.** Proceeds guided to ~$175M versus $283M in 2024; the
   150-store closure programme was stretched out to 2028 to protect pricing, with ~65 locations left [41].
   Lower asset-gain contribution to EPS.
4. **Fuel got worse.** National average gasoline hit $4.09/gal on 2026-08-28 [18], against a guide that
   assumed elevated but offsettable fuel.

**At stake.** Macy's trades at ~9.2–9.9x earnings against a multiline-retail peer median near 14.9x and
industry forward P/E near 15.4 [20][22]. Estimates of the owned real estate run $5–15B against a ~$6B
market cap [42]. The equity is a re-rating story: the discount only closes if the comp is durable and
the margin stops leaking. This print is a direct test of both.

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Regime.** Consumer discretionary is bifurcated. August US retail sales rose **+0.6% m/m** against
+0.2% expected and **+5% y/y**, with **apparel +1% m/m and +8.3% y/y** [19] — a genuinely good tape for
Macy's category. But the composition is hostile: US consumer sentiment fell to a **two-year low**, top-
decile households now account for **49.2% of all spending** (highest since 1989), and August spending
grew **+2.2% for high-income versus +0.3% for low-income** households [19]. Unit volumes were flat-to-
down across every retail segment for the four weeks to 2026-08-30, meaning the headline is price, not
demand [19].

That split cuts **for** Bloomingdale's and Bluemercury and **against** the Macy's nameplate — which is
precisely the shape Q1 already showed (+10.2% / +6.4% / +1.6%) [9].

**Commodity/rate sensitivity.** Gasoline averaged **$4.09/gal on 2026-08-28** [18]; Macy's guidance
carried a −$0.03/−$0.04 EPS fuel-and-tariff assumption for the quarter and −$0.10/−$0.20 for the year
[10]. Walmart flagged ~$2B of incremental annual fuel cost [18]. FX is immaterial for Macy's. Rate
sensitivity runs mainly through the credit-card income line ($172M, +12% in Q1 [10]) and through
mall-asset cap rates on the real-estate programme.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull.** Macy's books a meaningful slice of its **$320M** IEEPA refund [13][14], prints adjusted EPS far
above both the $0.29–$0.34 guide [10] and the $0.35–$0.37 consensus [11][22], delivers comps at or above
the +1% top of the guide on continued Bloomingdale's/Bluemercury/Reimagine-200 strength (+10.2%/+6.4%/
+2.4% in Q1 [9]) and a supportive apparel tape (+8.3% y/y in August [19]), and raises FY26 EPS above
$2.20 **with a visible ex-refund component**. Into that, the setup is unusually clean: the stock has
already given back ~10% from $24.90 (2026-08-10) to $22.45 (2026-09-08) [3][21], sits below its 50-DMA
[4], carries **11.93% short interest at 6.43 days to cover that did not shrink into the print** [4], and
the event is priced at only ~5.2% versus a 7.14% five-quarter mean absolute realised move [4][5]. Add
Berkshire building a position [33][34] and a 9.2x multiple against a 15.4x industry [22], and a
double-digit squeeze is entirely available — Macy's did exactly that on 2025-09-03 (+21.9% [5][37]).

**Bear.** The bar is genuinely above the guide: consensus revenue $4.82B exceeds the $4.75–4.80B guide
and consensus EPS $0.35–0.37 exceeds the $0.29–0.34 guide [10][11]. A print *inside* guidance is
therefore a *miss*. The Q2 comp guide (flat to +1%) already implies a 200–300bps deceleration from Q1's
+3.0% [9][10], gross margin has fallen for consecutive quarters (39.7% → 38.9%, −80bps then −30bps
[9][12]) with a further 20–40bps tariff/fuel drag guided [10], real-estate gains are being deliberately
reduced ($175M vs $283M [41]), and inventory is up 3.6% [9] into a quarter where gas hit $4.09 [18] and
consumer sentiment hit a two-year low [19]. Above all, **Kohl's is the template**: same category, same
quarter-end, huge refund-driven beat, an $0.80 FY guidance raise, and the stock still fell 5% on a
−0.9% comp [16]. Ross raised FY EPS and settled −2.0% [18]. Placer.ai had Macy's foot traffic −10.2% in
Q1 [36]. Consensus PT of $21.33–$23.23 [3][28] sits at or below spot, and 76% of the sell side is at
Hold [28].

**Base (my read).** Macy's recognises a tariff refund, prints a large headline beat, comps land in the
**+0.5% to +1.5%** band, and management raises FY26 with the refund clearly identified. The stock's
first move is up on the headline; the durability of that move depends entirely on whether the comp
prints above +1% and whether the ex-refund guidance component is positive. I lean modestly bullish on
direction — the ~10% de-rating into the event, the ~12%-of-float short base at 6.4 days to cover, the
turned-up revision breadth (Zacks #1/#2 through the summer) and the compressed 5.2% implied move
together create a better-than-coinflip risk-reward — but I hold **low-to-medium conviction**, because
the two closest analogues both faded and because Macy's has demonstrably refused to pay for beats
before (Q1 FY26: beat, raised, **+0.6%**). The fat-tailed history (median |move| 2.8%, mean 7.14%, max
21.9%) argues the modal outcome is small and the tail is violent.

**Direction score +18 · prob_up 56 · conviction Med.**

**On the triage rationale.** The hint was "universe at or below the triage threshold — all eligible
names carried forward." Having done the work, I think carrying M forward was **right, for a reason
triage would not have seen**: the IEEPA refund creates a mechanical, near-certain, very large deviation
between reported EPS and guided EPS, and the peer set has already established that the market
adjudicates these on the comp instead. That is a well-defined, testable structure — far more tractable
than a generic department-store print. The name deserves the panel.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **Macy's excluding the refund from adjusted EPS and from guidance
entirely.** Kohl's, TJX, Ross, Target and Dillard's all ran theirs through the P&L, so the Street's
working assumption is that Macy's will too. If Macy's instead follows the Lululemon/Adidas/Levi's
cohort [15] and treats the $320M as a non-recurring GAAP-only item, then: (a) the headline adjusted EPS
prints at $0.29–$0.34 versus a $0.35–$0.37 consensus — a clean **miss**; (b) there is no FY guidance
raise; (c) the entire bull structure I have described evaporates in the first ten seconds of the
release, and the 5.2% implied move is revealed as far too cheap to the downside. This is not a tail —
it is a live accounting-policy choice with recent precedent on both sides, and I have found **no
disclosure indicating which way Macy's will go.**

The second, lower-probability reversal runs the other way: management pre-signals a **Q3/Q4 comp
inflection** on the back of the 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade campaign and a holiday apparel forecast of
+5% — the strongest since 4Q21 — which would let the market underwrite the FY guide as conservative and
squeeze the ~30M-share short base hard.
