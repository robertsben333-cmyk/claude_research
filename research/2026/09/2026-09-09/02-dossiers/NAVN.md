# NAVN — Navan, Inc.

**Event confirmed: true.** Navan reports Q2 FY2027 (quarter ended 31 July 2026) after the
US close on Wednesday 9 September 2026, confirmed by the company's own BusinessWire/IR
release of 25 August 2026 [1] and independently by MarketBeat, which flags the date as
"confirmed" [30], Benzinga [12] and Ortex [9].

**What this print is about.** Navan is a ~10-month-old IPO (listed 30 Oct 2025 at $25,
closed its first day down ~20% [34][35]) that has already given the market a violent
education in how to trade it: three prints, three completely different regimes — a
‑7.8% one-day move on the first, a **+42.5%** move on the second, and a **‑1.0%** move
on the third [7]. The third is the one that matters. On 10 June 2026 Navan beat revenue
by 7.3% ($220.2M vs $205.3M Street [36]), swung non-GAAP EPS to +$0.08 against a
consensus loss, and raised FY27 revenue guidance by ~$40M to $907–913M — $25M above the
Street's $884M [36] — and the stock popped ~19% after hours [22] and **closed the next
session down 1.0%** [7]. That round trip is the single most important fact in this
dossier: the market has already learned to fade Navan's headline beat. Since then the
stock ran to an all-time-high close of $30.58 on 27 August [24] and has given back
12.9% to $26.64 [6]. So the print is not really about whether Navan beats — its
guidance has been systematically conservative and Amex GBT, Sabre, Expedia and Booking
all confirmed a strong corporate-travel tape [23][31][32] — it is about (a) whether
**take rate / usage yield**, which compressed ~45bp year-over-year last quarter (my
computation, §4), keeps compressing while GBV growth is paraded as the headline, and
(b) whether a company that has explicitly guided its own non-GAAP operating margin
**down from 11% to 6%** this quarter [2] can hold a 6.8x EV/sales multiple in a tape
where the 10-year is at its highest since late 2023, the Fed's September meeting is a
coin flip on a *hike*, and enterprise software sold off for two straight sessions on
OpenAI GPT-6 "Astra" agent fears [26][27][28].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Take rate — specifically usage revenue as a percentage of Gross Booking Volume — and
whether GBV growth is still converting into revenue growth.**

I am not guessing at this. Benzinga's week-ahead names exactly three things the market is
pricing for Navan: "**revenue growth, take rate and the path to profitability**" [12].
Oppenheimer's preview is framed around Street **bookings** of $2.86B alongside revenue
of $220.5M [13] — the sell side is explicitly modelling the two together, which is what
a take-rate debate looks like.

### Why it matters — the numbers (my computation from sourced inputs)

Navan's revenue is ~92% usage-based, effectively a yield on booking value [2][29]. So
GBV growth and revenue growth should track. In Q1 FY27 they did not: **GBV +50%, revenue
+40%, usage revenue +41%** [2]. That 9–10 point wedge is take-rate compression.

| Period | Revenue | GBV | Total take | Usage revenue | **Usage yield** | Source of inputs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 FY26 | $157.3M *(derived)* | ~$2.07B *(derived)* | 7.61% | ~$143.3M *(derived)* | **6.93%** | [2] |
| Q4 FY26 | $177.9M | $2.3B | 7.73% | $161M | **7.00%** | [5] |
| FY26 (full year) | $702.3M | $9.1B | 7.72% | $640M | **7.03%** | [5] |
| **Q1 FY27** | **$220.2M** | **$3.1B** | **7.10%** | **$202.1M** | **6.52%** | [2] |

Usage yield fell from ~6.93% (Q1 FY26) and ~7.03% (FY26 average) to **6.52%** in Q1 FY27
— a **~45–50bp year-over-year compression**. Sacra's independent description of the model
as a "usage yield of approximately 7%" [—via 19-adjacent] corroborates the ~7% baseline.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** GBV is disclosed rounded to one decimal in billions, so each
take-rate figure carries roughly ±0.15pp of rounding error. The *direction* survives the
rounding at both extremes; the exact magnitude does not. Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4 also carry real
seasonality (Q2 FY26 total take computes to 8.07%, the series high). Treat this as a
directional finding with a wide error bar, not a precise 45bp.

### What the Street's numbers imply — and this is the sharp bit

Street is at **$2.86B GBV and $220.5M revenue** [13]. That combination implies a take
rate of **7.71%** — i.e. the Street is modelling take rate **snapping all the way back to
the FY26 level**, reversing the entire Q1 compression in one quarter. My derived Q2 FY26
GBV of ~$2.1B (from FY26's $9.1B less the three known quarters) makes $2.86B only **+34%
YoY GBV growth**, a sharp deceleration from Q1's +50% and a ~8% *sequential decline*.

That looks conservative on volume and aggressive on yield at the same time. The scenario
grid (my computation):

| Q2 GBV | YoY | Revenue $220.5M | Revenue $225M | Revenue $230M |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $2.86B (Street) | +34% | **7.71%** | 7.87% | 8.04% |
| $3.00B | +41% | 7.35% | 7.50% | 7.67% |
| $3.10B | +45% | 7.11% | 7.26% | 7.42% |
| $3.20B | +50% | 6.89% | **7.03%** | 7.19% |
| $3.30B | +55% | 6.68% | 6.82% | 6.97% |

**The trap:** if Navan does in Q2 exactly what it did in Q1 — GBV blowout, revenue
beating by a few points — it prints something like GBV $3.1–3.2B (+45–50%, a 8–12% beat
vs Street) with revenue ~$225M (+2% vs Street). The headline reads "GBV +50%!", the
press release leads with it, the after-hours algos buy it — and the take rate lands at
**7.0–7.3%, still 40–70bp below where the Street modelled it**. That is precisely the
shape that produced +19% after hours and ‑1.0% at the close in June [7][22].

**The bull resolution:** GBV ≥$3.1B *and* revenue ≥$230M, i.e. take rate ≥7.4% and
stabilising. That would kill the compression thesis outright and is the single cleanest
upside trigger.

**Secondary metric to watch:** non-GAAP operating income versus the $13.5–14.5M guide
[2]. Because "path to profitability" is the third thing Benzinga says is being priced
[12], and because the guide itself embeds a 500bp sequential margin *reversal*, an
in-line margin print is a visibly negative optic even if revenue is fine.

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

### The business

AI-powered corporate travel + expense + payments platform (formerly TripActions,
Palo Alto) [3]. ~**12,500 customers**, **no customer >2% of revenue**, and the 10-Q
explicitly states "no customers accounted for 10% or more of revenue" in Q1 FY27 — down
from one payment partner at 10% in the prior-year quarter [4][29]. Customer
concentration is genuinely low and improving.

### Dilution and the GAAP problem

- FY26 GAAP net loss **$398.0M** on $702.3M revenue; basic/diluted loss per share
  **$(4.07)** [19]. The Q3 FY26 quarter alone carried a $225.4M net loss, driven by
  IPO-related SBC [18][19].
- SBC at $38.4M/quarter (~$154M annualised, 17.4% of revenue) [4] is the structural
  reason GAAP and non-GAAP diverge so violently. **There is no buyback.** Share count
  goes one way.
- Trailing-twelve-month: revenue $765.0M (+34.8%), net income ‑$357.3M, EPS ‑$2.40 [6].
  Forward P/E 101x [6]; SaaStr independently pegs it at "6x forward sales, 100x forward
  earnings" [29].

### What changed since the last print

1. **Smartrips (Brazil) acquisition** — definitive agreement announced 18 June 2026,
   Navan's **first acquisition as a public company**, expected to **close in Q2 FY27**
   i.e. *inside this reporting quarter*. Terms undisclosed. Brazil is ~40% of Latin
   American corporate travel spend; Navan frames it against a $185B market opportunity
   [16]. TD Cowen cited it as upside to numbers when raising to $34 [15]. **This is the
   most likely mechanism for a guidance raise that is not organic** — and a reason to
   scrutinise any FY27 raise for how much is Smartrips versus core.
2. **Three partnership announcements in August alone:** Insight Enterprises (4 Aug,
   deployment across 26 countries), DataVisor (10 Aug, real-time fraud), OpenTable
   (14 Aug, restaurant booking inside Navan Edge) [—via search cluster, see §9 sources].
3. **AI product cadence:** Cognition platform, integration with Google Gemini /
   Gemini Enterprise ("Navan Anywhere"), MCP launch; proprietary AI usage rose from 20%
   to 30% [20][24][29].
4. **Share gain from the incumbent is documented:** **38% of Q1 wins came from the Amex
   GBT cohort**; RFP volume +200% YoY; Fortune 500 customers **28 → 45** [29].

### What is at stake

The bull case is entirely an *operating leverage* story: 74–75% gross margin, non-GAAP
margin going 0% (Q4 FY26) → 11% (Q1 FY27) → 6% (Q2 guide) → 9% (FY27 guide). Navan is
asking the market to look through a guided sequential margin reversal on the way to a
higher full-year number. It got away with that framing in June because revenue blew out.
If revenue merely meets, the margin optic is the story.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

### Bull case

Navan beats again, and this time the beat is big enough and *clean* enough to break the
"fade the headline" reflex. The evidence for this is substantial and specific: management
has beaten its own guide and the Street in both prior prints by ~10% and 7.3% on revenue
[30][36]; the Q2 revenue guide of $219–221M is essentially *flat sequentially* against a
quarter that just grew GBV 50% [2]; Oppenheimer explicitly sees upside to Street's
$2.86B GBV / $220.5M revenue and raised its target to $35 two weeks ago [13][14]; the
Street's GBV number implies only +34% YoY growth versus Q1's +50%, and an ~8% sequential
*decline* that Navan's own H1 Business Travel Benchmark (+13.5% YoY, record 189.1) does
not support [37]. Amex GBT's Q2 (TTV +57%, transactions +45%, 95% retention) confirms the
demand tape, and Navan is documented as taking 38% of its wins directly out of GBT's
cohort [23][29]. Smartrips closes inside this quarter and gives a clean, TD-Cowen-flagged
reason to raise FY27 again beyond organic [15][16]. The implied H2 growth embedded in the
current FY27 guide is only ~26%, below the Q2 guide's 28% — visible sandbagging. Add a
4.2–5.4% short base with a 0.46% borrow that is cheap to buy back [9], a stock already
12.9% off its high [6][24], and a sell side clustered at $34–35 [9][14][15], and a clean
beat-and-raise with take rate ≥7.4% could produce a move toward the top of the ~14%
implied range — the Q4 FY26 precedent (+42.5%) proves the right-tail exists.

### Bear case

The bar has been re-rated out of reach while the operating story stayed the same. On
10 June Navan beat revenue 7.3%, swung EPS positive against a loss consensus, and raised
FY27 guidance $25M above Street — and the stock **closed down 1.0%** [7][36]. It has
since gone to $30.58 and back to $26.64 [6][24], so a repeat of June's performance is now
the *neutral* case. Underneath the headline, the monetisation is deteriorating: usage
yield fell from ~7.03% (FY26) to **6.52%** in Q1 FY27 as GBV (+50%) outran usage revenue
(+41%) [2][5, my computation] — and the Street's $2.86B/$220.5M pair implies take rate
**snapping back to 7.71%**, which the trend does not support [13]. Meanwhile management
has itself guided non-GAAP operating margin **down from 11% to 6%** [2], so the "path to
profitability" — one of the three things Benzinga says the market is pricing [12] —
visibly reverses this quarter even on an in-line print. GAAP is still catastrophic
($398M FY26 loss, $(4.07) EPS, SBC at 17.4% of revenue, no buyback) [4][19]. All of that
lands into the worst possible factor regime: the 10-year at its highest since late 2023,
a coin-flip **hike** at next week's Fed, CPI two days later, and enterprise software
sold for two straight sessions on GPT-6 Astra agent fears that took Intuit ‑4%,
ServiceNow ‑5% and NAVN itself ‑4.10% on 8 September [26][27][28]. NAVN is +63.2% YTD
against a sector down 20%+ [12][28] — it is the obvious source of funds. Positioning is
one-sided long (put/call 0.11–0.33, unbid puts, retail "extremely bullish", every broker
at $34–35) with no short base to squeeze at a 0.46% borrow [9][10][21]. And the CEO's
10b5-1 plan sold ~$11.9M across himself and three family trusts at $30.14 on the exact
day of the all-time high [40], while the finance organisation has turned over three times
in four months post-IPO [17][19].

### Base case (mine)

Navan beats revenue modestly (~$225–232M vs $220.5M Street), beats GBV substantially
(~$3.05–3.20B vs $2.86B), delivers non-GAAP operating income at or slightly above the
$14.5M high end, and raises FY27 revenue guidance to roughly $925–940M with Smartrips
contributing part of it. Take rate lands at **7.0–7.3%** — better than Q1's 7.10% only at
the margin, and well below the ~7.7% the Street's pair implies. The release pops the
stock after hours on the GBV headline, and then the same three questions that flattened
June's move reassert themselves: is the yield compressing, is the margin step-down real,
and why is a 6.8x-sales GAAP-lossmaking software asset up 63% YTD with the 10-year at
4.80% and the Fed possibly hiking. I expect a fade, but not a rout — and I hold this view
loosely, because with a ~14% implied move and a three-observation history containing a
+42.5% outlier, the magnitude uncertainty swamps the directional edge.

**Preliminary read: direction score ‑18, prob_up 43, conviction Low.**

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

**The single most credible reversal is take-rate stabilisation disclosed explicitly.**

Concretely: if Navan prints **GBV ≥ $3.1B (+45%+) *and* revenue ≥ $230M**, the implied
take rate is **≥7.42%** — which would not merely beat the Street's revenue line, it would
*reverse* the Q1 yield compression and destroy the core bear mechanism in this dossier.
Combined with a non-GAAP operating income print above $16M (i.e. management revealing
that the 6% margin guide was pure sandbag rather than a real mix/investment step-down)
and an FY27 revenue guide taken to **$940M+** with Smartrips quantified as *additive*
rather than as the source of the raise, that package would make my base case wrong and
justify a move toward the upper half of the implied range. The Q4 FY26 print
(+42.5% on a 9.3% implied move [7]) is the existence proof that this name can pay a
4.5x-implied move when the surprise is qualitative rather than quantitative.

**The second, non-fundamental reversal:** the macro is the swing factor and it is
external to the company. If Friday's CPI is anticipated soft and Fed hike odds collapse
between now and the 10 September close, the entire high-duration software complex rallies
and NAVN — as the sector's momentum leader — outperforms into it regardless of the print
[26][27]. Conversely a third straight session of GPT-6 Astra de-rating would swamp even a
clean beat. **The panel should treat the macro tape on 10 September as roughly co-equal
in weight to the release itself**, which is unusual and is a direct consequence of this
being a 6.8x-sales, +63%-YTD name in a hiking scare.

**What would flip me bearish beyond my base case:** any deceleration in GBV below ~+40%,
or an FY27 guide *maintained* rather than raised. Given a 4th consecutive raise is close
to consensus expectation, a mere reiteration would read as a guide-down.
