# AEO — American Eagle Outfitters, Inc.

**What this print is about.** AEO reports fiscal Q2 2026 (13 weeks ended August 1, 2026) after the close on Wednesday, September 9, 2026, call at 4:30pm ET [1][2]. On the surface this is a "revenue up, profit down" quarter: consensus is ~$1.37bn of revenue (+6.5–6.8% y/y) against EPS of $0.21–$0.22, versus $0.45 a year ago, because management guided Q2 operating income to $45–50m versus $103m last year on a 150–200bp tariff hit to gross margin [3][4][5][6]. But the print has two features the headline numbers hide. First, the 10-Q states in black and white that the $108.3m of IEEPA tariff refunds AEO had already received from CBP's CAPE system "will be recorded as a reduction of cost of sales for the 13 weeks ending August 1, 2026" — and that company guidance explicitly *excludes* IEEPA refunds [7][8]. So the GAAP gross margin and GAAP operating income for this quarter are mechanically going to look enormous relative to a $45–50m guide, and the market will have to decide what to capitalise. Second, the fiscal-2026 operating income guide of $390–410m, minus Q1's actual $28m and Q2's guided $45–50m, requires $312–337m — 80–86% of the full year — to land in the back half, at a tariff rate that steps from 10% to 15% [7][8][9]. This is a credibility print for a guide, delivered by a CFO who started 37 days ago [10]. The stock is -34.7% year-to-date and the last two reports produced -13.9% and -11.8% one-day reactions [own calc from 11].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**It is not EPS. It is underlying (ex-IEEPA-refund) Q2 operating income versus the $45–50m guide, read together with whether the FY26 $390–410m operating income guide survives.**

Why:

- The 10-Q's Note 14 (Subsequent Events) is unambiguous: *"Beginning on May 12, 2026, the Company began to receive refunds of its tariff claims submitted through CBP's CAPE system. As of the date of this Quarterly Report, the Company has received refunds of $108.3 million. **This amount, plus any additional refunds received will be recorded as a reduction of cost of sales for the 13 weeks ending August 1, 2026.** The timing and ultimate availability of any additional refunds remains uncertain."* [7]
- AEO had paid ~$192m of IEEPA tariffs and has submitted claims of **$189.8m** [7]. The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs on 2026-02-20 and CBP's CAPE refund system went live 2026-04-20 [7][26].
- Guidance excludes all of this [8]. So the *reported* GAAP gross margin, operating income and EPS for Q2 will be far above guide and far above the $0.21–0.22 consensus, for a reason that has nothing to do with the run-rate business. My inference: if the underlying business merely hits the guide, GAAP operating income lands somewhere around **$153–158m+**, roughly 3x the guide, and GAAP EPS well north of $0.60. Any headline-EPS-driven algorithmic reaction in the first minutes is therefore close to meaningless.
- The metric the humans will trade is what management calls "adjusted", plus the FY guide. The two most recent prints were both beats on the headline that fell 12–14% because the *quality* of the number disappointed [16][17][18][19]. That is the pattern to expect.
- Secondary metric, closely watched: **American Eagle-brand comparable sales.** In Q1 FY26 AE comps were -2% against +3.1% expected, even after the Sydney Sweeney relaunch, while Aerie comped +25% [18][19]. Segment data from the 10-Q makes the dependence stark [7]:

Aerie is now producing more segment operating income than the entire company earns. The market expectation for Q2, per management, is Aerie/OFFLINE comps "high teens to low twenties" and AE "flat to negative low single digits" [9].

- Third: whether the ~$140m anticipated net cash benefit from refunds (management's number on the Q1 call, excluded from guidance) [9] gets deployed into buyback at ~$17 or is simply banked.

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Tariff regime — the dominant macro variable for this name.** The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs 6-3 on **2026-02-20** [26][7]. The CIT ordered CBP to process unliquidated entries without the tariffs and to build a refund plan; CBP's CAPE system went live 2026-04-20 [7]. As of 2026-07-31, per a CBP declaration filed with the CIT on 2026-08-04, more than 75,000 CAPE declarations had been submitted, 17.69m validated entries liquidated without IEEPA tariffs, and ~$128.68bn of potential and certified refunds accepted for processing [26]. Refunds go to the importer of record [26]. AEO has claimed $189.8m and received $108.3m [7]. Counter-risk flagged by counsel: retailers face emerging consumer class-action exposure alleging tariff costs were passed through and should be returned [26]. ## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The quarter contains a large, mechanically certain, positive item that is not in guidance and appears not to be in consensus: at least $108.3m of IEEPA tariff refunds is recorded as a reduction of cost of sales in the 13 weeks ended August 1, 2026, per the 10-Q's own words, on top of a company that guided $45–50m of operating income [7][8]. Management flagged ~$140m of anticipated net cash benefit on the Q1 call, also excluded from guidance [9]. On a $2.89bn market cap [13], that is ~5% of the equity in cash, arriving at a share price 39.5% below the 52-week high and 34.7% below the 2025 year-end, into an active buyback [8][11][13]. Underneath, Aerie is compounding — +33.6% revenue and $96.3m of segment operating income versus $1.0m a year earlier, more profit than the whole company earns [7] — and management guided Aerie to high-teens-to-low-twenties comps for Q2 [9]. The tape is friendly: ANF +11.9% and GAP +14% after-hours on beat-and-raise prints in the same weeks [31][44], apparel card spend +9.5% in July [40], options flow 2.1:1 calls with put skew softening [14], 11% of float short at a 0.45% borrow [29][30], sentiment at 0/100 [33], and 11 of 14 analysts parked at Hold with an average target above spot [23]. That is what a low bar looks like.

**Bear case.** AEO has fallen on six of its last eight prints, including -13.9% and -11.8% on the last two, and both of those were headline beats that the market rejected on earnings quality — Q4 FY25 beat EPS and revenue but missed adjusted EBITDA by 34.2% with operating margin down to 5.4% from 8.9% [16][17]; Q1 FY26 beat EPS and grew revenue 10% but AE-brand comps came in at -2% against +3.1% expected [18][19]. The core American Eagle banner is shrinking (-2.2% revenue, segment operating income down y/y) after two of the most talked-about ad campaigns in retail, and management has already said it will "recalibrate" marketing spend [7][18]. The full-year guide is arithmetically extreme: $312–337m of operating income, 80–86% of the year, has to land in H2 while the tariff rate steps from 10% to 15% [7][8][25]. Consensus of $0.21–0.22 sits at or above the top of the $45–50m guide on my arithmetic, so there is no cushion. Q2 SG&A was guided +mid-teens against mid-to-high-single-digit comps — negative operating leverage by design [8]. And the person delivering the guide is a CFO who has been in the seat 37 days, with every professional incentive to rebase expectations at his first opportunity [10]. Estimates have not moved in 30 days and the Zacks Earnings ESP is -4.00%, meaning the most accurate estimate sits *below* consensus [3]. Finally, the refund is a one-off that sophisticated investors will strip out immediately — it improves cash, not the multiple.

**Base case.** Revenue beats modestly (Aerie carries it), underlying operating income lands in or near the $45–50m guide, and the headline GAAP number is enormous and confusing because of the ≥$108.3m refund. The stock's direction then hinges almost entirely on what happens to the FY26 $390–410m operating income guide in the first ten minutes of the 4:30pm call. My base case is that the guide is trimmed or heavily caveated rather than reaffirmed cleanly — because 80–86% of a year in the back half is a very hard promise to repeat, and a new CFO is the natural person to reset it — but that a chunk of that is already in a stock down 34.7% year to date with 11% of the float short and sentiment at zero. Net: a move of roughly the implied 13% magnitude is likely (realised mean is 12.9%), with direction close to a coin flip and a mild tilt up on the refund-plus-positioning asymmetry. I do not have high conviction here and say so.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The consensus view going into this print is "melting core brand, heroic back-half guide, avoid" — 11 of 14 analysts at Hold, PT $19.55, stock -34.7% YTD [23][11].

The single most credible reversal, stated concretely: **management reaffirms or raises the $390–410m FY26 operating income guide *and* explicitly folds the IEEPA refunds into a larger, accelerated buyback** — for example announcing a new authorisation or an ASR funded by the ~$140m net cash benefit [9], while showing American Eagle-brand comps back to flat-or-better and Aerie comps at or above +20%. That combination would convert three separate bear arguments at once (guide credibility, brand decay, capital return) and, against 10.9% of float short at a 0.45% borrow [29][30] and a 2:1 call-heavy options tape [14], would be the setup for a move well beyond the 13% implied — AEO has printed +37.96% and +15.07% reactions inside the last twelve months when the guide surprised to the upside [11][20].

The mirror-image reversal, equally concrete: **the new CFO rebases FY26 operating income below $390m** on H2 tariff step-up and marketing recalibration. Given the last two prints delivered -13.9% and -11.8% on far milder disappointments, I would expect that outcome to exceed the implied move to the downside.
