# GTLB — GitLab Inc.

**Event confirmed: yes.** GitLab reports Q2 FY2027 (quarter ended 31 July 2026) **after the US close on Tuesday 1 September 2026**, with the call at 4:30 p.m. ET / 1:30 p.m. PT — confirmed from the company's own Business Wire release of 18 August 2026 [1]. No date change and no pre-announcement of results.

**What this print is about.** This is not an EPS print. GitLab has beaten its own EPS guide every quarter for two years and been sold anyway — the last five reactions were −2.8%, −6.2%, −12.8%, −7.4% and −10.6% [8][9][12][14]. What has changed is the setup. In March 2026 GitLab guided FY27 to 15–17% revenue growth (from 26%) and $0.76–0.80 EPS against a $1.03 consensus, and the stock was destroyed [5][6]. In May–June it cut 14% of staff, exited 22 countries and took $30–35M of charges [17][18][30]. The shares bottomed at $25.83 on 22 June [10]. Then GitLab introduced a brand-new disclosure — **paid Consumption Run Rate (CRR)** for the Duo Agent Platform — restated it to ~$15M at 30 April and told the market on 8 July that it had already **surpassed $20M by 30 June** [23][24]. That single number, plus a sector-wide "AI is additive to software" re-rating (Snowflake, Elastic), has driven GTLB from $25.83 to $46.54 in ten weeks (+80%) with +34.9% in August alone [10][3]. The stock now trades ~15% **above** the average sell-side target of $40.26 against a **Hold** consensus (18 Holds, 3 Sells) [26][27]. So the question on 1 September is narrow and brutal: *does the CRR number and the FY27 guide justify a price the sell side does not yet justify, in a quarter that contains the operational disruption of a 14% RIF?* Options price ~14% either way [4][20].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Paid Consumption Run Rate (CRR) for the GitLab Duo Agent Platform / Flex — and whether management puts a number on it that implies it is compounding, not decaying.**

The evidence that this is the metric:
1. CRR did not exist before June 2026. It was first disclosed on the Q1 FY27 call as "nearly $20 million" of paid consumption run rate in DAP's first quarter [11][24].
2. GitLab then filed an **Item 7.01 8-K on 8 July 2026** for the sole purpose of updating it — restating Q1 CRR **down to ~$15M** after excluding one-time credit incentives, and disclosing that CRR **surpassed $20M as of 30 June 2026**, now including the newly launched Flex consumption program [23][24]. Companies do not file a Reg FD 8-K to update an immaterial metric.
3. The 8-K itself concedes "CRR is not material to the Company's financial performance at this time" and that "its definition and calculation methodology [is expected] to continue to evolve" [23]. That is a metric being built in public, in real time, for investors.
4. The August price-target wave was explicitly built on it. Cantor's $50 (from $35) cited "AI driving higher platform engagement, with usage across CI/CD workflows accelerating as more code is generated" and "paid recurring revenue now exceeding $20 million"; DA Davidson's raise to $45 was titled on "consumption trends"; BTIG went to $52, TD Cowen to $42 from $29 [13][32][33]. The stock is now trading the consumption line, not the seat line.
5. FY27 guidance assumes **no material DAP contribution** [30]. So every dollar of CRR is optionality that is not in the guide — which is exactly why the market will price the trajectory rather than the P&L.

**What the market expects for it (my inference, flagged):** the anchor is >$20M at 30 June. Two months of further compounding plus the first full quarter of Flex agreements implies a market expectation somewhere in the **$28–40M range at or shortly after 31 July**, i.e. roughly a doubling off the restated ~$15M Q1 base within one quarter. I have **no sourced consensus for CRR** — no analyst published number was findable — so this range is my own read and should be treated as such. The asymmetry is nasty: a number that merely says "above $20M" again, or a further definitional restatement of the kind that already happened once, would strip the entire August thesis.

Secondary metrics that carry the print: **DBNRR** (117% in Q1, down from 122% a year earlier [21]); **cRPO** (+24% to $724.1M in Q1 [7]); **$100K+ customers** (1,519, +18% [7]); and any FY27 revenue raise.

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Regime.** 2026 opened with the "SaaSpocalypse": ~$1T wiped off software by February, ~$2T by April, on the thesis that AI agents would collapse seat-based pricing [47]. That narrative inverted violently from late April into a June–August melt-up as investors re-underwrote AI as *additive* consumption for entrenched platforms [47]. GitLab is a pure expression of the flip: it fell to $18.73 at the trough and has round-tripped to $46.54 [3]. **The single largest macro risk to this trade is not GitLab-specific — it is that the sector re-rating is the crowded thing, and any high-profile disappointment breaks the whole complex.**

**Peers who already reported:**
- **Elastic (ESTC)** — reported 27 Aug 2026: adj. EPS $0.70 vs $0.58; revenue $478.1M (+15%) vs $469.7M; FY27 guidance raised; **37% of $100K+ customers using Elastic for AI use cases**; stock **+~22% after hours**, spiked to new highs [48]. Strongly positive read: a ~15%-growth consumption-adjacent infrastructure name got paid for AI attach. This is the closest structural analogue to GitLab's CRR story and it is bullish.

- **MongoDB (MDB)** — reports **the same session, 1 September AMC**, consensus ~$1.61 EPS / ~$735M revenue [50]; MDB rose 8.49% on 27 Aug on a +35.2% month [35]. Two mid-cap data/dev-infra names printing into the same after-hours tape means GTLB's move will be partly a sector move, and a bad MDB print could contaminate GTLB's reaction independent of GitLab's own numbers.

**Rate / FX / commodity sensitivity.** GitLab is zero-debt with $1.36B of cash and investments [21], so it is a net beneficiary of higher short rates on interest income and a duration-sensitive long-multiple equity on the discount-rate side. Commodity: none. **A specific Fed policy expectation for September 2026 is unavailable** — I could not source it and will not guess.

**Customer/supplier read-throughs.** GitLab's customers are enterprise engineering organisations. The Q1 call flagged a "tale of two cities": enterprise resilient, **SMB and a price-sensitive cohort (~20% of ARR) under pressure from customer layoffs and M&A contraction**, with public sector outperforming in Q1 after the prior-year federal shutdown drag [30][12]. ## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The consumption flywheel is real and accelerating, and Q2 is the quarter it shows. CRR went from a restated ~$15M at 30 April to >$20M at 30 June [23][24], and Flex only became available to customers *after* the June call — so Q2 captures the first Flex agreements plus two more months of compounding, and FY27 guidance contains **none of it** [30][24]. GitLab has beaten its own revenue guide by ~4pts [11], DAP attached to 4 of the top 10 deals with new-logo growth +30% y/y [13], and the restructuring converts ~$30–35M of one-time cost into permanent R&D reallocation with margins already up 200bps y/y [7][18]. Elastic's 27 August print — a same-profile, ~15%-growth infrastructure name that beat, raised, quantified AI attach at 37% of large customers, and gained ~22% [48] — is the template. Add 13.79% of float short with 6.65 days to cover [28] and a company sitting on $350M of unused buyback at prices it last exercised at $21 [21], and a strong CRR print plus an FY27 raise squeezes this through $52 toward the OI wall at $60.

**Bear case.** Every element of the bull case is already in the price and none of it is in the sell-side numbers. The stock is +80% in ten weeks, +34.9% in August, 62.5% above its 200-DMA [10][27], trading ~15% **above** a $40.26 average target on a **Hold** consensus with 18 Holds and 3 Sells [26][27], and Zacks records **zero** revision to the Q2 EPS consensus in 30 days [19] — the estimates have not followed the price. GTLB has printed a *negative* one-day reaction in each of the last five quarters while beating EPS every time [8][9][12][14]; the mechanism is always the same, guidance conservatism meeting elevated expectations, and expectations have never been more elevated than now. Q2 is the quarter that absorbs the 14% RIF and the exit from 22 countries [17][30] — sales disruption shows up in bookings and cRPO before revenue. DBNRR is already down 500bps y/y to 117% [21] and ~20% of ARR sits in a cohort management describes as under pressure [30]. And the metric the whole thesis rests on has **already been restated downward once**, five weeks after it was invented, with the company saying its definition will keep evolving [23]. A second definitional wobble, or a CRR figure that only says ">$20M" again, removes the entire reason the stock is at $46.54. Options are 1.9:1 calls with $55/$60/$75 call spreads bought [20] — the marginal buyer needs a gap, and if he does not get one the unwind is the trade.

**Base case (mine).** GitLab beats the Q2 revenue guide by the usual 3–4pts (~$281–285M) and beats on EPS, nudges FY27 revenue up modestly, and discloses a CRR number in the high-$20s to $30s millions with continued caution about materiality in FY27. That is a *good* quarter that is not clearly good enough for a stock that has already re-rated 80%, and the five-quarter pattern of guidance conservatism meeting a raised bar reasserts itself. I expect a negative-skewed reaction of roughly the historical median magnitude (~8%) rather than the full 14% implied, consistent with GTLB underrunning its implied move in 6 of 8 prints [4]. But the distribution is genuinely two-sided: the sector tape (Elastic +22%, Salesforce +22%, Snowflake) is the strongest positive read-through available, and a clean CRR acceleration plus an FY27 raise is a live path to a squeeze through $52. Hence a modest negative tilt with medium conviction, not a high-conviction short.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

Concretely: **a Q2 CRR disclosure at or above roughly $35M with an explicit statement that consumption revenue will be material to FY28, accompanied by an FY27 revenue raise to ≥$1.13B and DBNRR flat-to-up at 117%+.** That combination does three things at once — it converts CRR from a hand-waved "new metric" into a forecastable revenue line, it proves the restructuring did not break go-to-market, and it forces the 18 Hold-rated analysts sitting at $40 targets to move to numbers with a 5-handle. With 13.79% of float short and 6.65 days to cover [28] and open interest stacked at $60 [20], that is a gap-and-go, not a drift, and my negative tilt would be plainly wrong.

The mirror image, which would confirm the bear case in one line: **CRR restated or redefined a second time**, or a Q2 number that management again characterises as "not material" without a growth figure. Given the 8 July precedent [23] and the Q3 FY26 press-release correction [12], this is not a tail risk — it is a demonstrated failure mode at this company.
