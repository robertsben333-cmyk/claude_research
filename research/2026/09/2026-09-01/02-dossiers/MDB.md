# MDB — MongoDB, Inc.

**What this print is about.** MongoDB reports Q2 FY2027 (quarter ended 31 July 2026) after
the close on Tuesday 1 September 2026, and it does so as a stock that has just repriced
violently in its own favour: +34% in the month of August alone, from ~$337 on 31 July to
$453.37 on 31 August, within 4.2% of a 52-week high of $473.10 and ~79% above the $252.73
close it printed the day after February-quarter guidance disappointed in March [1][7][19].
The debate is not whether the quarter was good — three independent sell-side channel-check
notes in the two weeks before the print say Atlas consumption and Q2 sales attainment
improved [15][16] — it is whether *anything* management can say still clears a bar that the
market has already lifted to meet it. Consensus revenue ($733.6–735.1M) and EPS
($1.60–1.62) both sit at or above the **top** of the company's own Q2 guide ($729–734M,
$1.58–1.61) [3][8][14], the average sell-side price target ($458.65) is now ~1% above spot
[9], and retail sentiment is pinned at "extremely bullish" [26]. MongoDB's two double-digit
*down* prints in the last six quarters (-26.9% in Mar-2025, -22.2% in Mar-2026) were both
guidance events, not quarter events [6][10][19] — and this is the quarter where the full-year
guide gets raised or does not. The print trades on the Atlas growth rate and the size of the
FY27 raise, and almost nothing else.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**The Atlas year-over-year growth rate printed for Q2, and the implied Atlas growth rate
embedded in the revised FY27 guide.** Not EPS, and not headline revenue.

Why I am confident this is the metric:
- Atlas is **75% of total revenue** and rising (was 73% in FY26) [4][27]; the $512.5M of
  Atlas-related subscription revenue in Q1 FY27 grew 29.4% and added a record $117M of YoY
  dollars [4].
- Every violent MDB reaction of the last two years has been an Atlas-rate event. March 2025:
  guidance implied Atlas deceleration, −26.9% [19]. March 2026: Q4 Atlas grew 29% but the
  guide took Q1 to 26% and FY27 to 21–23%, and the stock lost 22.24% despite beating EPS by
  $0.19 and revenue by $28M [10]. December 2025 and August 2025: Atlas held ~29% and the
  guide went up, +22.23% and +37.96% [8][22].
- Sell-side previews are explicitly framed around it: RBC models $550.8M Atlas (+26%) [16];
  Cantor's whole thesis is that "implied Atlas deceleration into H2 FY27 to the 21% range,
  down from 26% Q2 guidance, is conservative," noting Atlas ran 29–30% over the prior four
  quarters [16]; Investing.com's initiation says the H2 guide implies ~15% total revenue
  growth and ~21% Atlas growth and should prove conservative [24].

**What the market expects for it.** Consensus Atlas revenue of **$552.44M, +25.9% YoY** [14]
— i.e. exactly the guided ~26% and a *further* deceleration from Q1's 29.4%. **My inference:**
because the Street has modelled Atlas to the guide rather than above it, the asymmetry sits in
the print itself: Atlas at 28–30% would be a genuine upside surprise against a positioned
number; Atlas at 26–27% is "in line" against a stock that has already rallied 34% in a month;
Atlas at ≤25% with an unchanged H2 shape is the March-2026 setup.

**Secondary signals that will move the stock in the same direction:** the net ARR expansion
rate (121% in Q1, up from 119% [24][28]), the FY27 Atlas growth band (23–25% today), and any
quantification of AI/agentic revenue, which management has so far explicitly refused to size
("early, but I am seeing very encouraging signs" — CEO Desai) [28].

end of guidance (20% growth + 20% margin) [28], FCF nearly doubled YoY, GAAP profitability
achieved, no debt, $2.4B of cash. What is not established is that Atlas can *reaccelerate*
rather than merely decelerate slowly. At 72x forward earnings the equity is priced for
reacceleration.

positioned on the other side of that trade** — as AI *infrastructure* rather than an AI
*victim* — which is exactly why it is +34% in a month while the group is down for the year.
That framing is the thing most at risk on this print. MDB has no debt and $2.4B of cash [29],
so rate sensitivity is duration-of-cash-flow only; there is no material commodity exposure and
FX is a second-order item.

**Competitive read-through.** The structural bear case is well documented and has not gone
away: PostgreSQL leads DB-Engines and developer-survey adoption and pgvector 0.8 is credible
for vector workloads [37][47]; every major relational engine now has native binary JSON,
JSONPath and multi-document ACID, with Oracle accepting unmodified MongoDB drivers [47]; and an
open-source **DocumentDB** project is backed by AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft and Snowflake
[47]. Against that, management's Q1 commentary cited displacement *wins* from Postgres and
first-party databases where customers hit scale ceilings (ElevenLabs named) [28], and the 10-K
confirms no customer concentration [30]. **My read: the competitive threat is real and slow;
it is a multiple story, not a next-quarter revenue story.**

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Atlas prints 28–30% rather than the guided/consensus 26%, and management takes
the FY27 revenue guide to ~$3.0B with Atlas to 26%+, invalidating the implied ~21% H2 Atlas
deceleration that Cantor and Investing.com both call conservative [16][24]. The evidence for
this is unusually specific for a pre-print: Citizens' 15 diligence data points came back 14
positive with explicit evidence of **improved Q2 sales attainment** [15], and Cantor's partner
checks describe **sustained consumption growth with improving pilot conversions** [16]. The
mechanism is visible in the numbers already reported — RPO +88% YoY and cRPO +69% [4] are the
strongest forward-booking signals MongoDB has ever printed, NRR ticked from 119% to 121%, and
45% of $100k+ customers now use two or more platform features versus 37% [28]. Elastic just
demonstrated that a cloud-acceleration-plus-raise gets paid 23% in this tape [45]. Structurally,
MongoDB's non-Q4 prints are 4-for-4 up, all of them by ≥3% and three by ≥12% [8][19][20][22],
and the options market is pricing only 0.76x the historical mean absolute move [5]. Add the
8/13 Build Fest shipping a Managed MCP Server that wires Atlas into Claude Code, Codex and
Devin [31], and the AI-native narrative has product substance for the first time.

**Bear case.** The stock has already been paid for the good quarter. +26.6% within August,
+34% from 31 July, 23.6% above its 50-DMA and 32.7% above its 200-DMA, four days off a 52-week
high [7][23]. Consensus revenue and EPS both sit at the **top** of guidance [14][4], Atlas
consensus is exactly the guide [14], and the average sell-side target ($458.65) is now ~1%
above spot after ten raises in nineteen days [9] — the targets chased, they did not lead.
Retail sentiment is 97/100 extremely bullish [26], institutions own ~90% and were accumulating
into the print [25], and short interest is 3.6–8.4% with under 2.5 days to cover [23][33] —
there is no under-positioned marginal buyer and no squeeze fuel. The precedent is exact and
recent: on 2026-03-02 MongoDB **beat** EPS by $0.19 and revenue by $28M and fell 22.24% because
the forward Atlas rate decelerated [10]; Datadog beat on 2026-08-06 and fell 15.6% [45].
Management's own stated guidance philosophy is conservative — they forecast only closed or
high-probability enterprise deals and explicitly warned against expecting "large swings versus
guidance" now that Atlas is "more predictable" [28] — so the raise may be structurally smaller
than the price action demands. At 71.7x forward earnings and ~11.6x EV to the FY27 revenue
guide [23], with SBC at ~20% of revenue and 5.06M unvested RSUs [29], there is no valuation
cushion. And the only discretionary insider trade in the window was the former CEO selling
40,000 shares at ~$383 with no 10b5-1 footnote [38].

**Base case.** MongoDB beats — the channel checks are too consistent and MongoDB has beaten its
own guide in every one of the last six quarters [8] — with revenue in the $745–760M range,
Atlas at 27–29%, EPS around $1.75–1.90, and an FY27 revenue guide lifted to roughly $2.98–3.02B
with Atlas taken to 25–26%. Against a stock at $453 that has already moved 34% in a month with
consensus at the top of the guide and price targets at spot, that outcome is *good but not
clearing*, and the distribution is genuinely two-tailed: a wide beat with a visibly
non-conservative H2 Atlas guide gets +12% to +20%; a solid beat with the H2 shape left intact
gets −5% to −15% as the run-up unwinds. I put a modest tilt to the up side on the strength of
the channel checks, the 4-for-4 non-Q4 base rate and the fact that the straddle is priced below
MDB's own historical mean move — but the conviction is medium at best, and the reversal risk is
higher than the direction score alone suggests.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The single most credible reversal is **an Atlas print at or below 26% combined with an FY27
Atlas band left at 23–25%.** Concretely: Atlas revenue of ≤$552M and a full-year revenue guide
of ≤$2.97B. That is not a bad quarter in absolute terms — it is a beat on total revenue and EPS
— but it would confirm exactly the deceleration path management guided in March, meaning the
entire August re-rating was a multiple expansion with no numbers behind it. In that state,
the March-2026 mechanism runs in full: the stock has ~$200 of ungiven-back gain since 3 March
[10][1], the marginal holder bought above $400 in the last three weeks, price targets are
already at spot, and a −15% to −22% session is well inside the six-quarter empirical
distribution.

The mirror-image flip, for anyone bearish on valuation: **Atlas ≥29% with the FY27 Atlas band
taken to 26%+ and any quantified AI/agentic revenue disclosure.** Management has so far refused
to size the AI contribution [28]; the first quarter they put a number on it re-bases the entire
argument, because the bear case rests on the claim that MongoDB's AI positioning is narrative
rather than revenue. Elastic's +23.1% on 8/27 shows what this tape pays for that [45].

A third, lower-probability flip: **a downward revision or removal of the FY27 non-GAAP
operating income guide ($571–591M)** in favour of AI/federal investment. MongoDB's whole
2026 defence has been Rule-of-40 at the high end [28]. Trading margin for growth would be read
as an admission that growth has to be bought.
