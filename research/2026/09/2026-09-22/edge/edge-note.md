## Read this before the table: the hunt very nearly restated the free control

**`-run_up_20d_pct` ranks this day at ρ = +0.857 against the key.** That is the highest
agreement between the hunt and the free control in the record, and it is the single most
important thing on this page. The free number is available off the sealed baseline before
a single subagent is spawned; twenty subagents produced an order that is almost the same
order. The two disagree materially in exactly one place — the control puts WOR last and
AYTU seventh, the hunt puts AYTU last and WOR seventh — and CBRL/MANU swap at the top.

It is not an accident, and three hunters said so unprompted. PAYX: *"two-thirds of my
direction is already given away by the sealed file."* KBH: *"my net sign agrees with the
−2.4 skew and with the free control, which by this stage's own standard means it is
already in the price — so the honest answer is a near-zero."* CTAS declined to convert a
−5.26% run-up into a long for the same reason. Those are the hunters doing the right
thing and the aggregate still coming out nearly collinear with the control.

Over the six originally-resolved runs the control ranked at ρ=0.335 against the hunt's
0.407, a gap whose CI spans zero; on the 105-event sample the control **returns −0.42%
per trade** and has stopped working, while the hunt above the floor pays +3.37%. So the
control is not a rival that is winning — it is a rival that is unstable. A day this
collinear with it tells you very little either way, and nothing here should be read as
the hunt having beaten anything.

sweep established this from the company's own release of 2026-09-16 and published it to

only the name that scores better is how a scorer gets quietly tuned.

## What this table does not say

**Separate the order from the sign.** Below the conviction floor the sign is a coin flip on
the evidence so far — 53% over 38 events. Above it, the rank of conviction predicted
sign-correctness at ρ=+0.514. **Five of the eight rows here are below the floor**, so a
reader treating CTAS's +0.00 or KBH's +0.40 as a directional view is reading the table
wrong. The whole middle of this table is noise by construction.

**`impact_sum` is not a forecast of the move.** It ranks; it does not size. The same fact
often appears in two findings from two sources and adding both double-counts it — the
cluster-max was built to stop that and the measurement demoted the cluster-max. Nothing
collapses duplicates now. Three rows here carry an explicit `flags` entry saying the
findings split both ways and the sum *nets* opposing theses rather than resolving them:
MANU (2/2), KBH (3/2), WOR (2/2).

**Sign balance: 5 positive, 2 negative, 1 zero.** That leans *positive*, which is the
opposite of this stage's usual artefact — six of eight leaned negative on 2026-08-31 and
the standing worry is that asking hunters to find what the market has missed generates
pessimism. It is worth recording that on a day when six of eight names went into the print
already down double digits over 20 sessions, the hunt came out net long. That is a
plausible-looking pattern and it is also exactly what collinearity with `-run_up_20d_pct`
would produce mechanically.

**Nothing checked the findings.** There is no adversary pass and no second hunter. A
factually wrong finding entered the key at full size and nothing in this run would have
caught it. That is the accepted cost of hunting every name; the adversary was the only
thing that ever caught a misread covenant date or a claim contradicted by its own source.

**The key is not reproducible to better than its own size.** When the stage still
double-hunted, twelve paired names came back with a median gap of 2.40 points and **four of
the twelve had opposite signs**, on a key whose typical magnitude is about 5. Today's
entire table spans 8.8 points and six of its eight rows sit inside a 4.5-point band.
Nothing re-measures that reproducibility any more.

**The lessons file moved 7 of 8 names.** `pre_lessons` against the key: MANU +1.5 → +4.5,
CBRL +3.5 → +2.5, PAYX +1.7 → +0.9, GIS +1.2 → +0.8, KBH +1.9 → +0.4, WOR −3.2 → −3.4,
AYTU −6.5 → −4.3, CTAS 0.0 → 0.0 (the only one unmoved, and it says so honestly). The file
shrank six names and enlarged one. `edge_resolve.py` ranks both sums against the same
realised move, so whether that was worth doing is measurable — but not on one day.

**How much of the baseline was measured rather than inferred: 7 of 8 names carry a live
option chain.** That is the best-instrumented day this stage has had; 18 of the first 43
resolved names took the fallback, and here only AYTU does. So `priced_lean_pct` is a real
priced lean for seven rows rather than −0.05 × the run-up. Two caveats survive: MANU's
chain is 1,201 contracts with a 67%-of-mid ATM spread and CTAS's is 1,637 with 19.4%, so
"has a chain" is not "has a liquid chain", and `edge_resolve.py` still normalises by the
expected move — a normalised correlation on this day is only as good as those two quotes.

**What the day would cost to trade.** Six of the eight names are genuinely reachable at
size: PAYX, GIS and CTAS trade $300m+ a day, CBRL $41m, KBH $65m, WOR $22m. Two extremes
are not symmetric. MANU at $6.96m/day clears the floor but is the thinnest name in the book
and carries a 38m-share float. **AYTU at $0.06m/day is unreachable outright** — and it is
the day's top conviction. So the tradable book tests the *long* end of this ranking and one
short, and cannot test the bottom of it at all.

## One day is an anecdote

Eight names cannot produce a meaningful rank correlation, and the ranking here agrees with
a free control at ρ=+0.857, which makes it less informative still. The pooled figure across
many days is the result. The stage has not been shown to beat `-run_up_20d_pct`; on the
larger 105-event sample that control has itself stopped working, which makes the comparison
unstable rather than settled. Nothing on this page is a finding.
