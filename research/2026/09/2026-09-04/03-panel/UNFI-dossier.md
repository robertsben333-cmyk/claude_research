## 2. What the market cares about

Two things, in order: whether Q4 (fiscal year-end) delivers on an easy comp against a cyber-attack-depressed year-ago quarter, and — more importantly — the initial FY2027 adjusted EBITDA guidance. UNFI has only ever described a "framework" off the $695M FY26 midpoint pointing toward roughly $800M by FY28; a first FY27 number at or above ~$740–750M would validate that path, while anything with a $7.2–7.3 handle reads as a walk-down. Layered on top: this is the first quarterly guide from CFO Alfredo Luchini, who started 2026-08-10, four weeks before the print, after a broader five-person C-suite reshuffle in the seven weeks prior.

## 5. Thesis

**Bull.** The quarter itself is close to a lay-up: Q4 laps a cyber-crippled comp (adjusted EPS −$0.11, adjusted EBITDA $116M a year ago) against an implied guide of $156–176M EBITDA this year, and UNFI has beaten consensus by an average of ~30% over the trailing four quarters. The structural turnaround is verifiable in the filings — gross margin up, opex down, net leverage from 3.3x to 2.5x, net debt at its lowest since FY2018, active buybacks. The stock is 23.5% off its 52-week high with only 4.4–4.5% of float short and essentially no options crowd — a washed-out setup, not a crowded one. Both prior quarters with a comparably negative pre-print run-in printed up days.

**Bear.** Consensus EPS of $0.62 sits near the top of management's own implied Q4 band ($0.45–0.65), so the Street already needs a top-of-range print. Management has pre-flagged that it pulled productivity benefits into Q3 and pushed technology/supply-chain investment into Q4 — a mechanical margin compressor for the reported quarter — against a fuel cost line up roughly 50% YoY. The bigger issue is the guide: a brand-new CFO must set FY27 against that cost backdrop, a natural-segment growth rate that has decelerated three quarters running, and a $40M FY26 cyber-insurance cushion that does not repeat. Two of the last three prints were down, and June 2026 gapped nearly 17% lower intraday before closing down 10.3% on a profit beat but a revenue miss and a guide that was only narrowed, not raised.

**Base.** UNFI likely beats on Q4 EPS and EBITDA — the comp is too easy and the sandbagging habit too consistent for it not to — and the outcome is decided almost entirely by the FY27 guidance range and the first minutes of the call. The panel's own aligned-near-zero read matches a "good quarter, cautious first guide" outcome: exactly the combination that has produced this stock's violent gap-and-reverse pattern in prior quarters, which is why reversal risk sits so much higher than the direction score would suggest on its own.

## 6. What flips it

The red-team case (reversal risk 72, the day's highest across both names) is specific: a first FY2027 guide from a four-week CFO, a non-repeating $40M cyber-insurance cushion sitting in the FY26 base, and a directly analogous June 2026 precedent where a beat plus a merely-narrowed guide produced a double-digit decline. It does not go unanswered — two personas (options-positioning at +16, sentiment at +12) independently read the identical new-CFO fact as a sandbagged-bar setup that favors upside, using different evidence (inverted options skew and de-risked positioning versus technical de-risking and the Whole Foods demand signal). That is a real disagreement about which way one known catalyst cuts, not a case that went unchallenged. The clearest trigger either way: an initial FY27 adjusted EBITDA guide at or above ~$750M with an explicit statement that reported wholesale sales grow (even including the project-work drag) points toward the +18–30% template; a midpoint below ~$720M, or a refusal to guide FY27 sales growth at all, points toward the −10% to −17% template already seen twice this year.

## 8. Insider / communication / alt-data findings

No insider buying since January 2026; two discretionary (non-10b5-1) officer sales near the highs in May–June, then silence into a 15% drawdown, and the only 2026 open-market buy (a director, ~$573k) is eight months stale and was not added to on the way down — a one-sided but modest signal. The more material forensic finding is organizational: five C-suite changes in the seven weeks before this print, including the CFO transition, with the outgoing Chief Corporate Affairs Officer's departure disclosed only via a transaction-free Form 4 rather than an 8-K. Communication-wise, the Q3 call already hedged the quarter explicitly — fuel and technology-investment pressure loaded into Q4, working-capital benefits flagged as non-repeating — and guidance was narrowed with midpoints held, a neutral signal rather than a pre-announcement in either direction. As with ABM, "no unusual insider buying/selling pattern beyond routine sales" is itself a reportable finding, distinct from the separately material leadership-turnover finding.
