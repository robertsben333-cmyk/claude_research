## 5. Thesis

**Bull.** Revenue beats comfortably on WGNSTAR and a full quarter of Technical Solutions growth (which ran +27% in Q2 on data-centre, BESS and microgrid demand); estimate revisions have moved up with zero cuts; the peer set (EMCOR, Comfort Systems, Aramark) is corroborating strong facilities-services demand emphatically; and the stock trades at an undemanding 11.5x forward earnings against a $52.43 consensus target. The June 2026 print — a headline EPS miss plus a revenue beat plus record bookings — produced +6.67%, and is the template bulls are pricing.

**Bear.** The FY26 guide requires H2 adjusted EPS of $2.13–$2.43 (+25% to +42% YoY) after H1 grew roughly 0%, and management has already conceded the low end of the margin range at this exact point in the calendar last year (5-Sep-2025, before landing FY25 below its own reaffirmed range). Interest expense has been marked up to ~$110M, mix is shifting into lower-margin segments, and cluster insider selling by the CEO, CFO, COO and CAO in June–July sits awkwardly against a story that is supposed to inflect in Q3.

**Base.** ABM likely beats on revenue and lands adjusted EPS close to but probably a touch under consensus, extending a four-quarter miss streak whose magnitude keeps shrinking. Segment margin improves sequentially but probably lands at the bottom of what the FY bridge requires; management reaffirms the range but leans verbally toward the lower half. The panel's own read — a genuinely coin-flip direction with an asymmetric downside tail if the margin bridge visibly breaks — matches this base case closely.

## 6. What flips it

The red-team case (reversal risk 70, the panel's highest) is specific: it rests on H2 EPS math that requires +25–42% YoY growth after flat H1, a UBS price-target cut explicitly citing margin-ramp risk, and a documented cluster of insider sales by four separate officers in June–July. It is well-corroborated — three other personas reached similar scores from different angles (KPI arithmetic, options/vol pricing, disclosure-language forensics) rather than citing the same sources. It does not go unanswered: the bull case (strong facilities-services peer read-through, an uncrowded stock with no run-up to unwind) is equally specific and sourced. The clearest reversal trigger either way: an 8.0%+ Q3 segment margin with an unqualified guidance reaffirmation flips this bullish fast (thin options liquidity means no dealer flow to absorb the move); a narrowing to the low end of $3.85–$4.15, a margin print below 7.66% (last year's Q3 level), or a new self-insurance charge would rerun the September 2025 script.

## 8. Insider / communication / alt-data findings

The forensics persona found a genuine null on insider activity — every 2026 Form 4 traces to a pre-existing 10b5-1 plan or tax withholding, with no cluster and no buying anywhere in the visible record (the one partly discretionary sale, the CAO's ~19%-of-holding trade in July, is too small in dollar terms to read as signal on its own, though the red-team persona weighted the broader four-officer selling pattern more heavily). The more material finding is a communication shift: FY26 guidance originally excluded "any potential positive or negative prior year self-insurance adjustment," but after an SEC staff comment ABM stopped excluding that item — the cushion that softened prior guidance resets no longer exists. At Q2, revenue guidance narrowed toward the top of its range while segment margin guidance was pushed toward the low end, and CEO Salmirs added a hedge ("the broader macroeconomic environment continues to evolve") not present in earlier calls. "Nothing unusual" on the insider side and "a real disclosure change" on the guidance side are both genuine findings, and they point in different directions.
