**What this print is about.** This is not an EPS print. Chewy has already told the Street what Q2 looks like: net sales $3.30–3.33bn and adjusted EBITDA margin 6.3–6.4% [12], and consensus sits at $3.32bn / $0.36 adjusted — i.e. the top of the revenue guide and dead-on the margin guide [8][9]. The stock trades on three things instead. First, **whether the fiscal-2026 net sales guide of $13.40–13.55bn holds**, after management already cut it in June from $13.6–13.75bn on a consumer that "softened as we moved through the quarter" [12][13][16]. Second, **active-customer net adds and NSPAC** — the two series that decide whether the growth reset is bottoming; management guided adds to the *low* end of a 150k–250k/quarter range in June [12]. Third, **gross margin**, which faces a self-flagged Q2 headwind from lapping non-recurring MAP pricing benefits plus a mid-single-digit-million fuel surcharge [12] — and gross margin/opex is precisely what has triggered Chewy's two worst earnings-day reactions in the last two years. Layered on top: a brand-new $600m secured term loan signed 2026-06-23 [23], the Modern Animal acquisition closed inside the quarter [6][7], and a stock that has lost 44% over a year but has recovered 36% off its June low. The options market is asking ~10.7% for the one-day move [3][4].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Active-customer net adds, read together with NSPAC — and specifically whether the FY net-sales guide survives them.**

Why this and not EPS: Chewy's Q2 adjusted EPS is effectively pre-announced (guide implies $0.36, consensus is $0.36 [8][12]), the top-line range is 90bp wide, and the company has beaten revenue four quarters running [11]. The only genuinely unknown numbers are the customer-file numbers and whether they force a third revision to the year.

- **What the market expects:** active customers **21.662m** (+~165k sequentially, +3.6% y/y) and **NSPAC $603.42** (+2.1% y/y) [8]. Both are visible, both are printed in the release table, and both are what the sell side has publicly said it is watching — "active-customer growth and NSPAC are the two numbers analysts will be watching closest for signs that growth is finally bottoming" [10][20].
- **How I know it is the swing factor:** management explicitly re-based this metric in June — adds now expected "towards the lower end of our previously stated range of approximately 150,000 to 250,000 net adds per quarter" — and attributed the FY cut to "incremental pressure on premiumization and product attach rates amongst our current customer base," i.e. an NSPAC problem, not a customer-acquisition problem [12][13]. The Q1 print delivered "nearly 200,000" adds and NSPAC of $597 (+2.4% y/y, decelerating) [5]. The bull case that carried the stock 36% off its June low is that this bottoms; the metric is the test.
- **Secondary metric with real optionality: gross margin.** Q1 GM was 30.1% (+50bp y/y) [5]; Q2 FY2025 GM was 30.4% (+90bp) [21]. Management pre-flagged Q2 GM headwinds (lapping non-recurring MAP pricing benefits, mid-single-digit-million fuel surcharge) [12]. A y/y GM decline is arguably in the guide; a *sequential and y/y* GM decline with SG&A deleverage is the exact combination that produced the −16.6% print on 2025-09-10 [21].
- **Third: the share count and what the $600m term loan bought.** See §5 and §8.

**Unit economics and mix.** Autoship at 84.4% of net sales is the highest on record [5] and is overwhelmingly consumables/health — the resilient half of the pet wallet. The corollary, which bears press, is that the non-Autoship half is shrinking: 84.4% share on +10.5% Autoship growth against +7.7% total growth implies the remaining ~15.6% is roughly flat to down. Consensus embeds exactly this shape for Q2: consumables +4.9%, hardgoods +11%, "other" −34.4% [8]. Margin levers management is leaning on are sponsored ads, private brands, automation and category mix, plus a stated "low tens of millions of dollars" AI efficiency benefit in FY2026 [25].

**Balance sheet — materially changed since the last print.** At 2026-05-03: cash $485.2m (down from $860.1m at FY-end), marketable securities $34.9m, inventories $1,007.9m (+$143m q/q, a deliberate hardgoods build), total assets $3,303.7m, total liabilities $2,879.5m, **no funded debt**, equity $424.2m [5]. Then on **2026-06-23** Chewy signed a **$600m seven-year senior secured term loan** with JPMorgan at SOFR+175 (base rate +75), amortising 1%/yr, guaranteed by domestic subsidiaries and secured by substantially all assets, and extended its ABL maturity to 2031 [23]. That is Chewy's first real leverage. The 8-K says proceeds are for fees/expenses "and, to the extent not so applied, for general corporate purposes and/or working capital" [23] — it does not name a use. The obvious candidates are the Modern Animal purchase and buybacks.

**Capital returns and dilution.** $200.0m of stock repurchased in Q1 [5]; Class A count fell 238.6m → 233.4m [5]. Board added **$500m** to the authorisation on 2026-04-07, leaving **$550m** available at 2026-05-03 [24]. Against that, SBC ran $66.9m in Q1 cash-flow terms and $73.4m including related taxes [5] — buybacks are currently doing more than offsetting dilution, but adjusted EPS excludes SBC entirely, so the GAAP/non-GAAP gap ($0.23 vs $0.43 in Q1) is wide and permanent.

**M&A — two deals inside twelve months.** SmartPak (equine) closed in Q1: $174.8m cash, $73.9m goodwill, ~$88m intangibles, $9.8m transaction costs, ~$80m FY2026 revenue contribution [5][12][15]. **Modern Animal** — announced 2026-04-08, **$400m** for 100% of the equity, closed 2026-05-21, 29 owned clinics taking Chewy Vet Care from 18 to 47 locations, >$125m annualised run-rate revenue, ~$70m contribution inside FY2026, management says **EBITDA-dollar-neutral in FY2026** and accretive to EPS within a year of close [6][7][12]. Read that carefully: ~$70m of revenue at zero EBITDA is *dilutive to the reported margin* even if it is strategically sound. Part of the sequential margin step-down from 7.5% (Q1) to 6.3–6.4% (Q2 guide) is arithmetic, not deterioration.

**Customer concentration:** not applicable in a meaningful sense — Chewy is a direct-to-consumer retailer with ~4,000 brand partners and ~190,000 SKUs [5]. Supplier concentration is not disclosed as a risk-quantified item in the release.

**What is at stake on 2026-09-09:** the margin story (Adjusted EBITDA margin has expanded every quarter and is the entire bull case at a compressed multiple) versus the growth story (two consecutive quarters of decelerating NSPAC and a guide already cut once). A print that holds both is the re-rating; a print that trades one for the other is not.

## 7. Forensics

- The one open-market sale in the window was CEO Singh's **2026-08-03 sale of 43,244 + 6,233 shares at $23.01**, and the Form 4 footnote states it was "effected pursuant to a Rule 10b5-1 trading plan adopted by the filing person on January 16, 2026" (spouse's plan same date) [35]. Press coverage of this as an $1.9–2.24m "CEO sells" story [36] conflates it with 32,364 tax-withheld shares. **It is a scheduled plan sale adopted seven months earlier and carries no signal.** Singh retained ~886,630 direct + 125,404 indirect shares after it [35].
- **Executive change, already digested.** Christopher S. Deppe was appointed CFO effective **2026-02-23**, ending a search during which CAO William Billings was interim principal financial officer; the 8-K states Billings' removal as interim PFO "was not a result of any disagreement with the Company on any matter related to its operations, policies, practices, financial disclosures, or accounting matters," and he remains CAO [37]. Deppe was Chewy's VP Finance from Aug-2022 and before that a Director of Finance at Amazon [37]. **2026-09-09 will be Deppe's second earnings call as CFO** — the first, on 2026-06-10, was the one where guidance was cut. That is a real, if soft, risk factor: new CFOs have an incentive to re-base a year early rather than defend it.
- **Auditor / restatement: clean.** The SEC's Office of Trade & Services closed its review of Chewy's FY2024 10-K with a no-further-comment letter dated **2026-02-04** [38]. No restatement, no material-weakness disclosure found.
- **Litigation resolved.** An 8-K filed 2026-04-28 (event 2026-04-14) disclosed a Stipulation and Agreement of Compromise, Settlement and Release in the Gilbert derivative matter involving BC Partners/Argos entities and certain directors [39]. Settled, disclosed under Item 7.01.
- **8-K cadence is normal and low.** Since the last print: 2026-06-10 (results), 2026-06-24 (term loan + ABL amendment), 2026-07-13 (annual meeting vote results, Item 5.07) [22][23][40]. **No 8-K in August or September 2026** — no pre-announcement, no guidance update, no negative surprise pre-signalled.
- **The capital-structure change is the forensic item that deserves the most weight.** A debt-free retailer with $485m of cash taking a $600m *secured* seven-year term loan, and disclosing the use of proceeds only as "general corporate purposes and/or working capital," is a decision management will be asked to justify on the call [23]. Benign reading: cheap term funding for the $400m Modern Animal deal plus buyback capacity against the $550m remaining authorisation [24]. Less benign: financing repurchases with first-lien debt at a point when free cash flow was $70.8m in the quarter [5]. **Watch the Q2 balance sheet and diluted share count.**
- **Filing-language shift.** The Q1 release replaced the prior tone with hedged framing — "despite a more dynamic consumer backdrop" in the CEO quote [5] — while the call added "a modest level of incremental pressure on premiumization and product attach rates" [12]. That language, not the numbers, is what the tape traded on in June.

**Synthesis (my inference).** The pet complex in this reporting season splits along exactly the line Chewy's mix sits on. **Consumables, essentials and daily-feeding demand held** (Freshpet +15.5%, Petco comps positive, IDEXX raising). **Discretionary premiumisation and elective vet spend cracked** (Zoetis cutting a full-year guide by ~$600m of revenue on lower clinic visits and price sensitivity). Chewy is 84.4% Autoship [5] — structurally on the resilient side — but its *incremental* growth engine (NSPAC via premiumisation and attach) and its newest asset (Chewy Vet Care + Modern Animal clinics) sit on the pressured side. **Petco's positive comp five trading days before Chewy's print is the single most directly comparable, most recent datapoint, and it is supportive of the top line.** Zoetis is the counterweight and it is specifically bearish for the vet-clinic and premium-health narrative.

**Industry backdrop.** US pet spending is projected at ~$165bn in 2026 vs $158bn in 2025; pet ownership ticked down (67% of surveyed consumers vs 69% in 2024); average vet visits fell to under two in six months from just over two in 2024 — a cautious-consumer signature concentrated in routine and elective care. Food and treats remain the largest category at 43.2% of spend; healthcare has been the most resilient [45]. Morgan Stanley's own 2026 pet-industry work frames growth as slowing on rising costs [46].

**Macro/factor regime.** Consumer discretionary into September 2026 is being supported by rate-cut expectations and a consumer-sentiment reading at a four-month high, against depleted excess savings and credit-card delinquencies at post-2019 highs [47]. **I am deliberately down-weighting this section** — the search results returned a mix of current and stale content that I could not date-verify cleanly, so treat the macro read as directional only, not as a sourced anchor. Chewy has negligible direct FX exposure (US-centric) and a modest commodity/freight exposure that management already quantified as a mid-single-digit-million fuel surcharge headwind in Q2 [12]. Rate sensitivity is now non-zero and new: $600m of floating-rate SOFR+175 term debt means every 25bp of policy easing is worth ~$1.5m of annualised interest [23], immaterial to EPS but directionally helpful.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The bar has been cut to the floor and positioning is not long. Guidance was already reduced once in June ($13.6–13.75bn → $13.40–13.55bn) [12][14], the stock is −28.4% YTD and −44.1% over a year [2], forward EV/EBITDA has compressed from ~24× to ~10.8× [20], and consensus revenue of $13.49bn now sits comfortably *inside* the reduced range rather than above it [10]. Chewy has beaten revenue estimates in four consecutive quarters [11] and has hit or exceeded the top of its own quarterly guide repeatedly. The most directly comparable recent datapoint — Petco's second consecutive positive comp on 2026-09-02, which sent that stock up ~9–10% [41][42] — says the consumables demand base is stable, and Freshpet's +15.5% agrees [43]. Chewy's own mix is 84.4% Autoship [5], the resilient half. Meanwhile the margin engine is intact: adjusted EBITDA margin +130bp y/y in Q1 to a record 7.5% [5], with sponsored ads, private brands and an AI efficiency program worth "low tens of millions" in FY2026 [25]. 9.6% of float is short at a 0.46% borrow with shorts *re-adding 13% in the last week* [26] — a reaffirmed FY guide plus in-line adds gets a violent covering bid. And the historical analogue fits: the last two times Chewy printed after a large drawdown rather than into strength (2026-03-25 at $23.45 → **+13.30%**; 2024-08-28 at $25.86 → **+11.06%**), it went up double digits [2].

**Bear case.** Growth is decelerating on a metric the company has already had to re-base twice, and the quarter has a self-inflicted margin comp. NSPAC growth slowed to +2.4% y/y in Q1 [5] and management attributed the FY cut to exactly this — "incremental pressure on premiumization and product attach rates" [12] — while conceding net adds would land at the *low* end of 150k–250k [12]. Q2 gross margin laps non-recurring MAP pricing benefits and absorbs a fuel surcharge [12], and gross-margin/opex disappointment is precisely the mechanism of Chewy's worst reaction on record (**−16.60% on 2025-09-10** on a quarter where it beat EPS and raised the year, purely on opex deleverage [21]). Zoetis just cut a full year by ~$600m of revenue citing lower companion-animal clinic visits and price sensitivity [43][44] — a direct negative read for Chewy's newest and most-promoted growth vector, the 47-clinic vet network. Modern Animal adds ~$70m of revenue at zero FY2026 EBITDA [6][12], optically diluting the margin line the bull case rests on. Zacks carries a **Rank #4 (Sell)** on negative revision momentum [8]. And the base rate is bearish: **4 of the last 6** earnings days were down, with a mean absolute move of 7.59% [2]. A second FY guidance cut in two quarters, from a CFO in his second quarter on the job [37], is the scenario that takes this back toward the June low.

**Base case (mine).** Chewy prints revenue at or just above the top of its guide (~$3.32–3.35bn), adjusted EPS at or a cent above $0.36, adjusted EBITDA margin inside 6.3–6.4%, gross margin down modestly y/y on the flagged MAP/fuel comp, active-customer adds of roughly 150–180k landing near the 21.66m consensus, and NSPAC growth of ~2%, still decelerating. The FY2026 net sales range is **narrowed rather than cut** — most likely to something like $13.45–13.55bn — and the EBITDA margin range is held or raised at the low end. That is a "steady, not exciting" quarter. Because expectations, valuation and positioning have all already been reset, and because the recent-drawdown-into-print pattern has twice produced double-digit up-moves, I think that outcome is modestly more likely to be rewarded than punished — but the distribution is fat-tailed in both directions and the straddle at 10.65% is expensive relative to a 7.59% mean realised. **Preliminary direction score +16, prob_up 56, conviction Medium.**

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **a second consecutive cut to the FY2026 net sales guide**, and the specific, observable trigger is the customer file. If Chewy prints **active-customer net adds below ~150,000** (i.e. below 21.65m total) **and** NSPAC growth decelerating to ~1% or lower, the guide almost certainly comes down again — the arithmetic of 6.3–7.5% full-year growth does not survive a Q2 exit rate near 6% with a softening back half. That single combination converts the story from "growth is bottoming at a 10.8× EBITDA multiple" into "growth is still falling and the multiple was not cheap after all," and it invalidates the bull case's core premise directly rather than by degrees. On my read of the 2025-09-10 precedent — a quarter that beat EPS, raised the year, and still fell 16.6% on opex [21] — the market would take that print down by more than the implied move, not less.

The inverse flip, less likely but cleanly specified: **net adds ≥ 200k with NSPAC growth re-accelerating above +3% and gross margin flat-to-up y/y despite the MAP comp**, plus an FY guide raised at the low end. That combination would confirm bottoming on both axes at once, and against 9.6% short interest that gets a covering move well beyond the +13.3% of March 2026.

Two lower-probability wildcards that are genuinely uncertain and worth watching on the tape: (a) the **share count and balance sheet** — if the $600m term loan funded a large Q2 buyback, the diluted count drops and adjusted EPS beats mechanically, which the market may read as either shareholder-friendly or as levering up to buy a decelerating business; (b) any **change to the vet-clinic expansion plan** (currently 10–12 openings in 2026, exiting with ~60 clinics [12]) in the wake of Zoetis's clinic-visit warning [44].
