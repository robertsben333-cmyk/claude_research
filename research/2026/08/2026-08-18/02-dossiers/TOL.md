# TOL — Toll Brothers, Inc.

**Event confirmed: true.** Toll Brothers announces FY2026 third-quarter results **after the market close on Tuesday, 18 August 2026** (session: `amc`), with the conference call the following morning, **Wednesday 19 August 2026 at 8:30 a.m. ET**, hosted by Executive Chairman Douglas C. Yearley, Jr. and CEO Karl K. Mistry [1]. A third-party calendar puts the release at 4:30 p.m. ET [24]. Note a live sourcing hazard: several syndicated previews (Zacks, Alphastreet) describe the report as "before market open on August 19" [5][8]. That is wrong — the company's own release says results come out after the close on the 18th [1]. Anyone reading the secondary previews will have the timing off by one session.

**What this print is about.** Toll's fiscal Q3 covers May–July 2026 — precisely the window in which the rest of the public builders reported that demand softened. D.R. Horton's orders came in flat against an +8% forecast and management cited buyers turning hesitant in May and June; Lennar called traffic "inconsistent"; KB Home orders fell 4% [10][18]. Toll is the one builder that raised full-year guidance in May while most peers cut [21], on the argument that its buyer — 23% all-cash, 69% average LTV on the mortgaged remainder — is structurally insulated from the affordability squeeze [21][23]. This print is the test of that argument. The bar has already been cut hard: consensus EPS is down 15.6% in 90 days to roughly $2.90–$2.93, a 21–22% year-on-year decline [5][8]. So the headline EPS number is not where the risk sits. The risk sits in Q3 **net signed contracts** — did the luxury cohort hold while the entry-level builders wobbled — and in whether the company can still stand behind its FY26 adjusted gross margin and delivery guide when it has to sell and settle roughly 2,000 spec homes in the back half to hit it [23]. Options price a 5.67% move against a 4.25% eight-quarter mean realised move [12][6], and the last eight prints have gone down six times out of eight — but the two up moves were the big ones [6].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Q3 net signed contracts (units and dollars), read together with the FY26 gross-margin reaffirmation and the back-half spec sell-through requirement.**

Headline EPS is close to mechanical this quarter: deliveries come out of a backlog that was already fixed at 5,394 homes / $6.32bn at the end of Q2 [2], and the company has guided the margin and the SG&A rate. What is *not* fixed is demand in the May–July window and what it implies for FY27.

Why this is the metric:

1. **The backlog is shrinking and the spec burden is rising.** Q1 FY26 backlog was down 20% in units YoY [17]; Q2 FY26 backlog was 5,394 units versus 6,063 a year earlier (−11% units, −7.6% in value) [2]. HousingWire reported that Toll expects to close roughly 4,100 homes out of backlog in the back half of FY26, which implies it must **sell and settle roughly 2,000 spec homes** in H2 to reach the 10,400–10,700 delivery guide [23]. Specs were already 51% of Q2 deliveries and 41% of home sales revenue [21]. If Q3 orders were weak, the FY26 delivery guide is at risk *and* the margin is at risk, because unsold completed specs are where the incentive dollars go.
2. **Incentives are the cleanest tell.** Incentives on new contracts were flat at **8% of gross sales price for the fourth consecutive quarter** through Q2 FY26, with higher incentives on completed specs offset by lower ones on build-to-order [21]. That 8% is the number to watch. Peers' incentives are running 10–13% (Lennar 12.9%, Pulte 10.4%, Century ~12%) and had just begun to tick *down* in their June/July prints [18]. A break above 8% at Toll would say the luxury insulation thesis is cracking; a flat 8% would confirm it. Independent third-party previews converge on the same two items — backlog and incentive spend [24], and net signed contracts, community count, ASP, cancellation rate and spec levels [8][5].
3. **The comparison is unusually clean.** Q3 FY25 delivered 2,959 homes at a $973,600 ASP and $3.73 of EPS [8][5], so the YoY declines are already in the model. The surprise can only come from orders and from forward guidance.

**What the market expects for it, and how I know.** No published consensus for Q3 net signed contracts could be sourced — this is a real gap (see §12). What can be sourced is the *directional* expectation from the peer group that already reported the same calendar quarter: Zelman's tally of nine public builders puts aggregate Q2-calendar order growth at **+1% versus a +4% estimate**, with move-up demand explicitly stronger than entry-level and D.R. Horton alone accounting for ~90% of the aggregate unit shortfall [18]. NAHB's August HMI adds that **custom builders continue to report stronger conditions than spec builders, reflecting better conditions at the higher end** [15]. My read of that: the market's implicit expectation for Toll's Q3 orders is roughly flat-to-modestly-positive in units against a Q2 that ran +7% units / +8% dollars [2], with anything positive treated as validation and anything clearly negative treated as the luxury thesis breaking. *(This is my inference from peer data, not a sourced consensus number.)*

### Mix and unit economics
- **Luxury move-up is the engine:** 62% of home sales revenue in Q2 FY26, up from 59% in Q1 [21].
- **Buyer quality:** 23% of Q2 customers paid all cash; mortgage buyers averaged a 69% LTV [21]. HousingWire's separate write-up cites ~20% all cash and 71% average LTV, and notes Toll's backlog cancellation rate has been the lowest in the industry for decades [23].
- **Spec strategy:** specs were 51% of Q2 deliveries and 41% of home sales revenue; management's stated approach is to sell specs early in the construction cycle to protect margin and preserve design-studio attach [21].
- **Incentives:** flat at 8% of gross sales price for four consecutive quarters [21].

### Margin trajectory — the thing that has actually deteriorated
Adjusted gross margin has stepped down through FY26: 26.5% in Q1 (25bp better than guide) [17] → 26.2% in Q2 (70bp better than guide) [20] → **guided 25.25% for Q3** [2], against 26.10% for the full year [2]. The Q3 guide is a ~225bp YoY contraction [8]. The offsets management is leaning on are ASP mix (ASP delivered up 8% YoY in Q2) and construction cost deflation, which Zelman measured at **5–7% YoY across the group** [18].

### Balance sheet, cash return, dilution [2]
- Cash $1.11bn (down from $1.26bn at FY-end); $2.24bn available of a $2.38bn revolver.
- Debt/capital 24.7%; **net debt/capital 15.4%**; stockholders' equity $8.48bn.
- Lots owned 32,025; optioned 44,779; **76,804 total controlled**.
- Q2 buyback: ~1.2m shares at $143.72 = $175.4m; FY26 YTD $226m against a **$650m FY26 target**, with management explicitly saying most of it falls in H2 when operating cash flow is higher [2][20][26].
- Dividend raised 4% to $0.26/quarter, announced 2026-03-10 [2].
- Share count down 4.93% YoY [3] — buyback is a genuine EPS tailwind, not offsetting dilution.

**What is at stake.** With net debt/capital at 15.4% and 93.5m shares against a $650m repurchase target, the buyback alone is ~4.5% of the market cap; the balance sheet is not the question. The question is whether a 25.25% adjusted margin is a floor or a waypoint, and whether the 76,804-lot land bank and +9% community count growth become a fixed-cost problem if orders soften. Community count growing 9% YoY while backlog units fall 11% is, in my read, the single most under-discussed tension in the story: more stores, less order book.

MBA forecasts 6.5% for Q3 and Q4 2026; Fannie Mae's June forecast has 6.4% for the balance of 2026 [38]. **Direct relevance to TOL is lower than for peers** — 23% of its Q2 buyers paid all cash and mortgage buyers averaged 69% LTV [21] — but the *stock* trades as a rate-sensitive asset regardless of the customer's balance sheet, and the September-expiry implied move of 9.35% versus 5.67% for the earnings week [12] is the option market saying exactly that: a chunk of the near-term variance is FOMC, not Toll.

Toll also sits at 26%-ish adjusted gross margin against a 20.3% group aggregate [2][18]; its margin premium is the whole equity story, and it is the number contracting fastest.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The bar has been cut to the floor and Toll keeps clearing its own guide. Consensus fell 15.6% in 90 days and then stopped moving for 30 days [5][8], so the de-risking is done. Toll beat its adjusted-margin guide by 25bp in Q1 and 70bp in Q2 and beat consensus EPS both times [17][20][9]; the same construction-cost deflation of 5–7% that let seven of nine peers beat on margin applies to Toll [18]. Its buyer is the least rate-exposed in the industry (23% all cash, 69% LTV, lowest backlog cancellation rate in the sector for decades) [21][23], and both NAHB and Zelman independently say the high end and the move-up buyer held up best in exactly this quarter [15][18]. Toll was the only major builder to *raise* guidance in May while peers cut [21]. If Q3 orders are flat-to-up and the FY26 delivery and 26.10% margin guide are reaffirmed, the setup — stock 13.6% below its 52-week high, −4.85% on the week, RSI 43.6, 11x trailing, average sell-side target 15.6% above spot with fresh $180/$185 reiterations from BofA and Wells Fargo in the last week [13][16] — is one where a clean print gets bought, with a $650m FY26 buyback weighted to H2 as the mechanical bid [26].

**Bear case.** The peer template for this exact calendar quarter was an order miss: aggregate +1% versus +4% expected, with D.R. Horton flat versus +8% and management citing buyers turning hesitant in May and June [18][10]. Toll's fiscal Q3 *is* May–July. The backlog has been eroding for three quarters (units −20% YoY in Q1, −11% in Q2 [17][2]) while community count grew +9% — more selling communities producing fewer orders is negative operating leverage, and it shows up in SG&A already 80bp higher YoY at 10.3% [2]. To hit the FY26 delivery guide Toll must sell and settle ~2,000 spec homes in the back half [23], and specs are exactly where incentives go; the 8%-of-price incentive rate that has been flat for four quarters [21] is the thing most likely to break. The adjusted margin has already stepped 26.5% → 26.2% → guided 25.25% [17][2], and Zacks flags a mix shift toward lower-margin product [8] which the mid-$400k community openings corroborate [31]. Against that, the sell side is stacked bullish at an average $168.20 target [16], meaning a guidance trim forces target cuts. And insiders have sold $13.3m in three months with no purchases [7], including a **discretionary, non-10b5-1** $12.2m sale by the Executive Chairman near the highs [34].

**Base case (my read).** Toll delivers roughly in line to modestly above the $2.90–$2.93 consensus, with adjusted gross margin at or slightly above the 25.25% guide — because that is exactly what it has done for two straight quarters and because the whole peer group beat on margin on shared cost deflation [17][20][18]. Orders are the coin flip: I lean flat-to-slightly-positive in units given that the higher end demonstrably outperformed [15][18], but that lean is inferential, not sourced. FY26 delivery guidance is most likely reaffirmed rather than raised; management is unlikely to give FY27 numbers on an August call, so the FY27 question gets answered in tone, not digits. Reaction: a modest positive drift, well inside the 5.67% implied move [12], with option premium probably a touch rich versus the 4.25% eight-quarter mean realised move [6]. The tail risks are asymmetric though: the down-tail (order roll-over plus an incentive break above 8%) is fatter than the up-tail, because the sell side's $168 average target has more room to fall than to rise from here [16], and because six of the last eight reactions were negative [6].

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **an incentive break, disclosed on the call rather than in the release**: incentives on new contracts moving off 8% of gross sales price — say to 9–10% — combined with Q3 orders down year-over-year in units and a Q4 adjusted gross margin guide below ~25%. That single combination would say the luxury-insulation thesis is not a structural moat but a lag, and that Toll is simply arriving at the discounting phase later than Lennar (12.9%) and Pulte (10.4%) [18][21]. It would invalidate the specific premise the stock is priced on: a ~26% adjusted gross margin against a 20.3% peer aggregate [2][18]. Because the sell side is clustered at an average $168.20 target with fresh $180 and $185 reiterations in the last week [16], the target cuts would be large and fast, and the stock would not be defended by short covering — short interest is only 4.7% of float and falling [14].

The mirror-image reversal, less likely but cleanly identifiable: **orders up year-over-year in units with incentives held flat at 8% and the FY26 adjusted gross margin guide raised above 26.10%**. That would restore the "Toll is a different asset class from the builders" narrative in a quarter when D.R. Horton's orders went flat, and would justify a re-rating off 11x trailing rather than merely a relief bounce.

One second-order flip worth naming: the September-expiry implied move is 9.35% versus 5.67% for the earnings week [12]. If the print is quiet, the residual variance in this name over the next month is FOMC, not Toll. A directionally correct earnings read can still be washed out by the rate path — that is a reason to keep conviction on the event low even if the fundamental read is right.
