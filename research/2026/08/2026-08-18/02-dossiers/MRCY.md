# MRCY — Mercury Systems, Inc.

**What this print is about.** Mercury reports Q4 and full-year FY2026 after the close on Tuesday 18 August 2026 (call 5:00 p.m. ET) [1][2]. Mercury does not publish quantitative guidance in its press release — it frames the year verbally on the call — so the print is really two events: a Q4 that management has *already told the Street will be weaker than it looks*, and the first FY2027 framing. On the May call management pulled forward roughly **$25M of revenue, $15M of adjusted EBITDA and $25M of cash from Q4 into Q3** and simultaneously raised the FY26 guide to "approaching mid-single-digit" revenue growth with a "mid-teens" adjusted EBITDA margin, while warning of "more modest margin expansion into the fourth quarter" [14][15]. Since then the stock has gone from $90.17 (29 Jul) to $113.36 (17 Aug), +25.7%, on the Palantir factory-automation announcement and defense-sector momentum [8][21]. It now trades at ~84x forward earnings and ~7.4x EV/sales, **above** the average sell-side price target of $102–106 [5][6]. The bar is no longer the Q4 number; the bar is whether the FY27 framing can justify a multiple the analysts themselves will not underwrite. The triage read ("small-cap defense-electronics turnaround with program-execution and margin questions… track record of sharp post-print gaps") is directionally right on the gap history — six-quarter mean absolute move is 14.4% — but it understates the change since: this is no longer a cheap turnaround, it is a fully re-rated momentum name where the turnaround is the *consensus*, not the debate.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**The FY2027 outlook framing on the call — specifically the adjusted EBITDA margin trajectory — not Q4 EPS.**

Why I am confident it is this and not the headline:
1. Mercury withheld FY27 guidance at Q3 but reaffirmed a **long-term target of low- to mid-20% adjusted EBITDA margin and 50% free-cash-flow conversion** [14]. Q4 is where the year gets framed: last August the company gave a full qualitative FY26 frame ("low single-digit revenue growth, relatively flat first half, full-year adjusted EBITDA margin approaching mid-teens, Q4 margin the highest of the year") [33]. It will do the same for FY27.
2. The Street is modelling FY27 revenue $1.05B (+9.1%) and EPS $1.51 (+40.3%) [6]. That is a step from ~15% FY26 EBITDA margin to something in the high-teens. The 12-month backlog at Q3 was **$891M** [10] — roughly 85% coverage of the FY27 revenue consensus, which is normal for Mercury but leaves nothing spare.
3. Jefferies' Hold thesis is explicitly a margin-timing call: "Mercury's low-margin backlog is diminishing quickly, and may be out of the base by fiscal 2027," with margin upside plus "increasing budgets and Golden Dome opportunities" as the offsets [26]. The market's disagreement with Jefferies is entirely about *when* the low-margin legacy work clears.
4. Headline Q4 EPS is a near-worthless signal here: Zacks' consensus was $0.06 against a $0.27 actual last quarter (+350% "surprise") [18]. A headline beat is close to automatic on that basis and will not by itself move the stock.

**Secondary metrics, in order:** (a) Q4 bookings / book-to-bill and total backlog against Q3's record $348M / 1.48x / ~$1.6B [10]; (b) full-year FY26 free cash flow — 9M was $39.5M (OCF $60.2M less $20.7M capex) [13] versus FY25's record $119.0M [12], and $25M of Q4 cash was already harvested in Q3 [14][15]; (c) whether the Airborne platform stops shrinking (9M Airborne revenue $262.7M, −8.9% YoY; Q3 alone $83.8M, −17.5% YoY) [13].

**Auditor / restatement.** No material weakness, restatement or auditor change disclosed in the Q3 FY26 10-Q; the company reports compliance with all revolver covenants [13].

**Filing-language shift.** The one I can point to concretely: Q4 FY25 headlines led with "record free cash flow of $119.0 million" [12]; the Q3 FY26 release leads with bookings, backlog, revenue and adjusted EBITDA, and reports Q3 free cash flow of **$(2)M** [10]. ## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull.** Mercury has now put four consecutive quarters of margin expansion and record bookings on the board, and Q4 is structurally its biggest quarter. Q3 bookings of $348M at a 1.48 book-to-bill produced a record ~$1.6B backlog (+17.9%) with the 12-month component jumping to $891M, +10.3% sequentially [10][14] — that is the leading indicator for FY27 and it accelerated. Every prime that matters to Mercury has raised guidance: RTX (17% of revenue) and Lockheed (11%) both lifted 2026 forecasts on restocking [29b], Kratos beat and raised hard [27], HII and RTX gapped +16.4% and +9.2% on their prints [16]. Jefferies' own Hold thesis concedes that low-margin backlog "may be out of the base by fiscal 2027" [26] — meaning FY27 is the year the margin math inflects, exactly when management would frame it. Mercury has beaten 4 of 4 and the Zacks ESP is +6.67% [18]. The two prior August prints delivered +26.95% and +17.65% [8][9]. Positioning supports upside: P/C open interest is 0.35, the largest position in the chain is 8,608 Sep-$130 calls, Sep skew is if anything call-rich, and 4.5–5.0M shares (8.9% of float, ~8 days to cover) are short [3][5]. A Q4 with $270M+ revenue, ~19% adjusted EBITDA margin, a strong FY26 FCF finish and an FY27 frame of high-single-digit growth plus high-teens margin clears the 13% straddle to the upside.

**Bear.** The stock has re-rated to 83.8x forward earnings, 7.37x EV/sales and 74.4x TTM EV/EBITDA [5] — for a business with a 9M GAAP operating **loss** of $14.2M [13] and a 14.7% EBITDA margin. It trades **above** both published consensus price targets ($102.44 and $106.22) [6][20], so the sell-side has to chase merely to justify the current price. Management itself removed $25M of revenue, $15M of adjusted EBITDA and $25M of cash from this quarter [14][15] and guided to "more modest margin expansion into the fourth quarter." My arithmetic off the guide puts implied Q4 revenue at $254–267M against consensus of $265–271M — consensus is at the *top* of the band with no cushion. Free cash flow is the quiet problem: 9M FCF of $39.5M [13] versus FY25's record $119.0M [12], with the Q4 cash already partly pulled forward, against a stated long-term target of 50% conversion [14]. The largest platform, Airborne, is shrinking 8.9% YTD and 17.5% in Q3 [13]. There is no buyback bid ($15M used of a $200M authorisation, share count rising) [13][14]. Supply is real: JANA sits on 4.11M shares and has sold in the days after each of the last three prints [24d]; a director liquidated his entire position at $99 in May and there has not been a single insider purchase all year [24][25]. And the 10-Q carries an unquantified new legal overhang — Starboard funds representing 14% of the class opted out of the $32.5M settlement on 21 April and intend to sue separately [13]. Into a +25.7% three-week run-up, a soft or merely in-line FY27 frame is a −15% day.

**Base case.** Mercury beats the (stale) headline EPS consensus and reports Q4 revenue in the $260–272M band with adjusted EBITDA margin in the 16–19% range — good absolute numbers, but flattered-by-comparison ones because the pull-forward is now in the base. Bookings stay strong and backlog holds ~$1.6B or better; the company confirms FY26 revenue growth of ~5% and a mid-teens FY26 adjusted EBITDA margin, with FY26 free cash flow well below FY25's $119M record. On FY27, management repeats the long-term profile — above-market growth, low-to-mid-20% adjusted EBITDA margin, 50% FCF conversion — without committing to a year, and points to Golden Dome/LTAMDS as upside not in backlog [14]. That is a fine print for the business and an ambiguous one for a stock at 84x that has already run 25.7% in three weeks. My centre of gravity is a move of roughly the implied size with a **modest downward skew**: the direction depends almost entirely on how numerically specific and how ambitious the FY27 margin framing is, and management's demonstrated pattern is to under-commit at Q4 and raise later in the year [33][14].

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The single most credible reversal of my mild bearish tilt: **management gives a numeric FY2027 adjusted EBITDA margin frame of "high-teens" or better, together with a quantified free-cash-flow target, and pairs it with a Q4 book-to-bill above 1.2 and 12-month backlog above ~$950M.** That combination would validate Jefferies' "low-margin backlog out of the base by FY27" observation [26] a year early, hand the Street the +40% FY27 EPS it already models [6], convert the 4.5–5.0M short position (8 days to cover) [5] into fuel, and give the 8,608 Sep-$130 calls a reason to exist. In that scenario the analyst targets clustered at $102–106 all move to $130+ within 48 hours and the stock clears the upper straddle break-even near $128.

The mirror image, and the most credible path to a large down move: **management declines to frame FY27 margins numerically, or frames FY27 revenue growth as "mid-single-digit,"** while FY26 free cash flow lands below ~$70M. That reads as the pull-forward having borrowed from the future rather than demonstrated better conversion, and at 84x forward there is no valuation floor beneath it — the July episode showed the stock can lose 28.6% in four weeks with no company-specific news at all [8].

A third, lower-probability flip: an unexpected Golden Dome or LTAMDS award quantification, or a disclosed Starboard claim large enough to matter. The first is upside, the second downside; both are tail events.
