# BILL — BILL Holdings, Inc.

**What this print is about.** This is not an EPS print. BILL's fiscal Q4 (June-quarter) result is close to pre-ordained: the company has beaten its own non-GAAP EPS guidance by roughly $0.07–$0.11 in each of the last three quarters [17][18][19], and consensus for Q4 ($0.71 EPS, $430.5M revenue) [10][14] sits almost exactly on the midpoint of guidance issued in May ($0.69–$0.72, $425–435M) [3]. What actually trades is the **first fiscal-2027 guide**, delivered against a stock that has rallied ~56% off its 52-week low of $31.41 [26] and ~19% off its 23 July close of $41.26 [12], and against a company that has just cut up to 30% of its workforce — including, reportedly, disproportionately from sales and customer support [30] — installed a brand-new Chief Revenue Officer on 6 July [7], and promised the Street a "roadmap to Rule of 40" on this specific call [21]. Sitting behind all of it is a live governance overhang: three activists (Starboard ~8.5% with two board designees, Elliott ≥5%, Barington), a formal sale process reported by Reuters in November 2025, and Bloomberg reporting in February 2026 that Hellman & Friedman engaged with the company's advisers [6][8][20]. The market is being asked to price a margin story that is already largely in the numbers (FY26 EPS estimates +22.4% over 90 days [10]) against a revenue story that has been decelerating and just had its go-to-market org rebuilt mid-flight.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**The initial FY2027 *core* revenue growth guide, and whether the "Rule of 40 roadmap" management explicitly promised for this call is bridged by growth or purely by cost.**

Why this and not EPS:
1. Truist wrote on 19 May 2026 that "Bill.com management will provide a roadmap to reaching the Rule of 40 on next quarter's call," and that Street estimates only get to **Rule of 37 in fiscal 2028** [21]. This call is that call. Management has pre-committed to a framework and the Street has a number to score it against.
2. The margin half of the equation is already banked and already modelled. Management quantified the restructuring on the Q3 call: **~$110M of gross annualised savings, with ~$20–30M reinvested in FY2027**, i.e. ~$80–90M of net drop-through [4]. That is why FY26 EPS estimates rose 22.4% in 90 days [10] and FY27 EPS consensus is +27.9% [23]. There is limited *incremental* surprise left on cost.
3. The growth half is the contested half. On the Q3 call management guided net new customer adds to **"tend below 4,000 in the near term"** (from ~4,100 in Q3) as it shifts upmarket [4]; Truist cut its target in May specifically on "a slower pace of take rate expansion for the AP/AR business" [21], then **downgraded BILL from Buy to Hold on 10 June 2026** citing slowing revenue growth and heightened competition [31]; and Q3 same-store-sales TPV grew only **4% YoY** [4].

**What the market expects for it (my synthesis, from [23] + [3] + [4]):** consensus FY27 total revenue of $1.85B against FY26 of ~$1.647B implies ~+12.3% total growth. Backing out float — FY26 float revenue guided at ~$145.7M [4] — implies FY27 **core** revenue of roughly $1.70B, i.e. **~13% core growth**, versus FY26 core of +15–16% [3]. So the Street is already modelling ~250bp of core deceleration.

**The scoring rule I would apply:** an initial FY27 core-revenue growth guide of **13%+** is a clean pass and pairs with a margin guide that gets FY27 to Rule of ~36–37 with a visible FY28 path to 40. A guide of **10–12% core** — plausible for a company that just cut 30% of headcount out of sales and customer support [30] and whose CRO started 6 July [7] — is the bear case, because it says the Rule of 40 is being bridged entirely by subtraction. Watch also for the **float revenue assumption**: see §9, where I think the Street may be modelling this too conservatively.

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Rates — the most underrated variable in this print, and I read it as a *tailwind*.**
The FOMC held the fed funds target at **3.50%–3.75% on 29 July 2026**, its fifth consecutive hold, on a **9–3 vote with three dissents (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) arguing for *higher* rates** to address inflation that has run above 2% for more than five years. Officials' year-end projections span **3.6%–4.1%** — i.e. the central case is flat-to-higher, not lower — and the next meeting is 16 September 2026 [39][40].

Why this matters for BILL: float revenue is interest on customer funds and was **$161.8M in FY25**, guided to **~$145.7M in FY26 — a figure management *raised* by $4.2M on the Q3 call "driven by higher expected yields on customer funds"** [4][29]. FY26 float declined because of cuts already delivered. With the funds rate now expected flat-to-up and funds-held-for-customers of ~$4.0B growing with TPV [29], **FY27 float revenue can plausibly be flat to up rather than down.** My inference: consensus and much of the bear commentary still frames "declining interest rates" as a BILL headwind [31] — that framing is a year stale, and a FY27 guide with flat-or-growing float would be a genuine positive surprise to a chunk of the Street. This is the single most differentiated fundamental point in this dossier.

**Sector regime.** Software has been under pressure from budget reprioritisation toward AI infrastructure — clients "exhausting IT budgets to secure supply-constrained AI servers and high-bandwidth memory instead of software" [31] `snippet_only`. BILL is additionally exposed to SMB spending, which is the softest end of the demand curve.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** BILL beats Q4 comfortably — the last three quarters overshot the top of guidance by $0.07–$0.09 [17][18][19] — and pairs it with an FY27 guide of ~13%+ core revenue growth and a non-GAAP operating margin near 23–24%, i.e. a credible Rule-of-37 FY27 with an explicit FY28 bridge to 40 [21]. The margin arithmetic supports it: ~$110M gross savings less $20–30M reinvestment is ~$80–90M of clean drop-through onto a FY26 base of ~$306M non-GAAP operating income [3][4]. The revenue side gets help the Street is not modelling: with the Fed on hold at 3.50–3.75% and three dissents pushing for hikes [39][40], float revenue — already guided *up* $4.2M in May on higher yields [4] — can be flat-to-growing in FY27 rather than the decline embedded in the "falling rates" bear framing [31]. Add Supplier Payments Plus suppliers doubling sequentially [4], subscription growth accelerating to +9% [3], joint customers +39% [4], a $1.0B authorisation equal to ~20% of the market cap [5], 11.56% of float short with 4.33 days to cover [15], and an activist-installed board with a live sale process behind it [6][8][46]. Peer precedent this season is explicit that this tape pays for beats in SMB-adjacent software: Paycom beat revenue by 3.5% and gained 23.6% [25]. BILL has risen on four consecutive prints [11][16].

**Bear case.** The margin story is already paid for. Q4 EPS estimates are +22.4% in 90 days [10], Zacks has it at #1 Strong Buy [22], the stock is +56% off its low and +19% off its 23 July close [12][26] — and the sell-side has *not* followed, leaving only 11.0% to a $54.57 average target with a $42 low [23]. Into that, BILL must issue an initial full-year guide, and its house style is to sandbag by 250–350bp (initial FY26 was +9–11% versus a +12–13% landing) [3][19]. This year the sandbag lands on a company that just removed up to 30% of its workforce with sales and customer support bearing the brunt [30], whose CRO started six weeks ago [7], whose three most senior product/payments/customer executives are leaving [6], whose net customer adds are guided **below 4,000** [4], whose same-store TPV grew just 4% [4], and whose AP/AR take-rate expansion Truist called out before downgrading to Hold on competition and slowing growth [21][31]. Competition is intensifying in real time — Ramp, Brex, Melio, Tipalti [44], plus 1TCH knocking 5.7% off the stock on 30 July [33]. And BILL has the worst guide-down reaction record in its cohort: **−34.8% in February 2025 against a 6.0% implied move** [11]. A 10–12% core FY27 guide says Rule of 40 is being reached by subtraction, and this stock does not survive that at $49.

**Base case (mine).** BILL beats Q4 on both lines — I would expect non-GAAP EPS in the high-$0.70s to mid-$0.80s against $0.71 consensus, and revenue $435–445M against a $425–435M guide, purely by extrapolating three quarters of identical behaviour [3][17][18][19]. It then issues a characteristically conservative initial FY27 guide, most likely **core revenue growth in the 11–14% range with a non-GAAP operating margin step to roughly 22–24%**, and delivers the promised Rule-of-40 roadmap as a multi-year bridge rather than an FY27 achievement — which is what Truist already told clients to expect (Street at Rule of 37 in FY28) [21]. Because the quarter's beat is near-universally anticipated and the cost savings are already in consensus, the entire reaction rests on the revenue line of that guide and on the float assumption. I lean **modestly positive** — the four-print winning streak, the mechanical beat, an 11.56% short base with 4.33 days to cover [15], a hawkish Fed that has quietly turned float from headwind to tailwind [39][40], and an activist/M&A floor [6][8][46] outweigh the crowding — but only modestly, and with genuinely fat tails in both directions given a 15.7% median realised move against a 12% implied [11][13].

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The single most credible reversal is **an FY2027 total revenue guide with a midpoint below ~$1.80B** (versus $1.85B consensus [23]), particularly if the shortfall sits in *core* rather than float and management attributes it to go-to-market disruption from the restructuring or to competitive pricing. Concretely: if the initial FY27 core-revenue growth guide comes in at **10–12%** — decelerating from FY26's +15–16% [3] — while the operating-margin guide steps to 23%+, the market will correctly read that as **Rule of 40 by subtraction**. That is the print where the entire bull thesis (AI-native platform re-accelerating growth while costs fall) collapses into a cost-cutting story on a decelerating asset, and where the 22.4% of 90-day EPS revisions [10] and the 56% rally off the June low [26] both become liabilities at once. Given BILL's precedent of **−34.8% on a guidance disappointment in February 2025** [11], a move materially beyond the 12% implied is entirely plausible.

The mirror-image flip, less discussed but real: **an FY27 guide that keeps core growth at 15%+ while stepping margin above 24%** — i.e. Rule of 40 reached in FY27, a year ahead of the Street's FY28 Rule-of-37 [21] — would invalidate the "growth is dead" thesis outright, and would land on a 11.56% short base [15]. The most likely mechanical source of that upside is a **float revenue assumption that is flat-to-up on a hawkish Fed** [39][40], which most bear notes are not carrying.

A third, discontinuous flip: **any confirmation, denial or new development on the sale process** [46]. A stated conclusion that the board has ended strategic alternatives would strip out deal optionality independently of the operating result.
