# WOLF — Wolfspeed, Inc.

**What this print is about.** Wolfspeed reports fiscal Q4 2026 (June quarter) and full-year FY26 after the close on Wednesday 19 August 2026 — its fourth report as a post-Chapter-11 successor entity and the first one that lands squarely on the deadline management set for itself. In October 2025 the company told investors it would deliver "a comprehensive financial update in the first half of calendar 2026, but will not otherwise be updating its long-term model or guidance before then" [S18]; as of the Q3 call (5 May 2026) no forward model had been given [S12], and the only H1-CY26 disclosure was a set of unaudited pro-forma *historical* statements filed 9 June 2026 [S24]. So this call is the natural venue for the first FY2027 framework. The revenue print itself is near-mechanical — the company guided $140–160M and hit the midpoint of its last guide — but the stock trades on (a) the September-quarter guide, (b) whether management reaffirms or quietly buries the pre-bankruptcy targets of "$800 million of annual revenue" for adjusted-EBITDA breakeven and "positive levered free cash flow in fiscal 2027" [S19], and (c) the cash-burn arithmetic now that the $698.6M of Section 48D refunds that funded FY26 are largely spent [S13]. All of that sits on top of an equity where 24.5M shares — 47.1% of shares outstanding and 80.7% of a 30.4M-share float — are sold short [S8], and where a large part of that short is, in my read, convertible-arbitrage hedge rather than directional bears.

**Event confirmed: yes.** Date, time and session are confirmed from the company's own IR release [S1] and the BusinessWire original [S2].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**The fiscal-Q1-2027 revenue guide, read together with whether management reaffirms or retires the "$800M revenue = adjusted-EBITDA breakeven / positive levered FCF in FY2027" framework.**

Why, and how I know:

- Management explicitly withheld a long-term model until "the first half of calendar 2026" [S18] and had still not provided one at the Q3 call, where the CEO offered only a qualitative objective: "to return to above-market revenue growth driven by a more diversified customer base and to achieve EBITDA and cash flow profitability," with no timeline [S12]. H1 CY2026 is over. The Q4/full-year call is where it has to appear.
- The standing public targets, set 28 March 2025, are: adjusted-EBITDA breakeven at **"$800 million of annual revenue"**; **"positive levered free cash flow in fiscal 2027"**; FY2027 capex of **$30–50M** [S19]. FY2026 is landing at ~$665M and *falling*. Getting to $800M requires ~+20% growth off the June-quarter run-rate ($150M × 4 = $600M) — i.e. +33% off run-rate. Either management re-times those targets (bearish, and the most likely honest outcome) or reaffirms them (very bullish if paired with a real order book).
- The last three guides went $150–190M, then $140–160M, then $140–160M. A fourth guide at or below $160M says the AI-datacenter narrative is not yet moving the revenue line; a guide above $160M says it is.
- Secondary metric with real signal value: **a dollar figure for AI-datacenter revenue.** Management has given only percentages — +50% q/q in Q2 FY26, +30% q/q in Q3 FY26, described as "a moderate portion of our business today" [S12][S14][S15]. Every AI-adjacent move in the stock (LITEON on 6 Aug, +9.53% and +19.14% on 6–7 Aug; the Mizuho Vera-Rubin note on 17 Aug, +9.97%) has been narrative, not quantified [S16][S17][S20][S6]. Putting a number on it is a two-sided event: it converts a story into a fact.
- Third: **underutilisation charges**, $46M in Q3 alone, with the company stating it "expect[s] to continue to incur significant underutilization costs until market demand for our products meets or exceeds our production capacity" [S12][S13]. This is the single largest gross-margin swing factor.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **a quantified AI-datacentre revenue disclosure attached to a September-quarter guide above $165M.** Concretely: if management says something like "AI datacentre was $X million in Q4, up Y% sequentially, and we expect fiscal Q1 revenue of $165–180M," that single sentence does three things at once — it breaks a four-quarter sequential revenue downtrend, it converts the LITEON/GE/10 kV narrative [S16][S17] from qualification to revenue, and it makes the $800M EBITDA-breakeven target [S19] arithmetically reachable within FY27 rather than aspirational. Against 24.50M shares short at 5.81 days to cover [S8], with upside option skew already bid [S30], that is the setup for a move well beyond the 15.4% implied — the name did +45% over four sessions after the May print [S6]. The mirror image, equally credible: management explicitly withdraws or re-times the "positive levered free cash flow in fiscal 2027" target [S19] and guides Q1 FY27 below $145M. That would confirm the share-loss thesis against growing peers [S38][S39][S40] and, on a −5.4% SOX tape [S33], would plausibly break the stock through the 200-DMA at $28.42 [S8] toward the $22.50–$25.00 put strikes where Tuesday's flow was concentrated [S30].

A third, lower-probability flip: because provider consensus is demonstrably contaminated [S9][S11], the first algorithmic headline off the wire may be wrong by a wide margin in either direction. An initial knee-jerk move that reverses within the first 30 minutes of after-hours trading is a live scenario and the panel should not treat the first tick as signal.
