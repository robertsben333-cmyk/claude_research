# ROST — Ross Stores, Inc.

**Event confirmed: YES.** Q2 fiscal 2026 results release Thursday **2026-08-20 at ~4:00 p.m. ET (AMC)**, conference call 4:15 p.m. ET, confirmed by the company's own scheduling release [1][2]. No date change and no pre-announcement found.

**What this print is about.** Ross has just delivered the three most violent upside quarters in its history — comps of +7%, +9% and **+17%** against guidance of 2–3%, 3–4% and 7–8% respectively [20][21][23] — and the stock has re-rated from ~$137 to an all-time high of $255.23 on 2026-08-07, +60.7% over 52 weeks [3][4][5]. That re-rating changed the nature of the trade. The Q2 EPS number is now close to a non-event: management guided $1.85–$1.93, published consensus is $1.92–$1.94 (i.e. *at the top of the guide*), and the buy-side bogey is $2.03–$2.05 on a 9–10% comp [18][27][29]. The variable that actually prices the stock is **the Q3 comparable-sales guide**. Ross lowballs it every single quarter; it did so from a 2–3% base and got away with it. Doing it now — UBS expects "low-single-digit," StreetSignal puts published consensus near 3% and the buy-side bogey at 5–6% [27][29] — lands into a 30x forward multiple and a fresh, unhelpful precedent: TJX beat, raised the full year, guided Q3 light, and was sold 3.3% one day earlier [31][32][33]. The bull's counter is hard and quantified: Placer.ai measured Ross Dress for Less same-store visits **+16.4% YoY across Q2 2026** while T.J. Maxx sat at or below flat [26], and the year-ago quarter carried an $0.11/share tariff drag that has since been struck down by the Supreme Court [24][22]. This is a genuine two-sided print where a large beat is likely and the guide decides the tape.

## 3. The one metric that matters

> **The Q3 FY2026 comparable-store-sales guide.**

Not Q2 EPS. Not Q2 comps. Here is how I know:

1. StreetSignal frames it explicitly: a Q3 guide of **5–6% "would validate durability," 6–7% is "success versus 3% consensus," and 3–4% is "disappointing"** even off a strong Q2 [29].
2. UBS frames the same variable as the risk: they expect management to guide Q3 to **low-single-digit** growth, and write that "the bigger post-earnings risk can be a valuation reset tied to the Q3 guide and what it implies for longer-term earnings power, not just whether Q2 landed a few cents high or low" [27].
3. The arithmetic of the current FY guide makes it unavoidable. FY26 guidance is comps +6–7% [20][21]. Q1 delivered +17%. With Q1 and Q2 each roughly a quarter of the year and Q2 guided 6–7%, the residual H2 comp embedded in the full-year guide is **only ~2%** *(my calculation from company figures, not a sourced figure)*. Ross therefore has to either raise the FY guide meaningfully or admit to a sharp H2 deceleration. There is no neutral option.
4. The precedent is one day old and it cut the other way: TJX beat Q2 ($1.22 adj vs $1.19), raised FY27 EPS guidance to $5.31–$5.36, and **still fell 3.3% to $145.85 because the Q3 guide of $1.30–$1.32 missed the $1.35 Street** [31][32][33].

**Secondary metric to watch:** the **tariff-refund disclosure.** The Supreme Court ruled the 2025 IEEPA tariffs unauthorized; Ross has filed refund claims with CBP but as of 2026-05-02 had **recognised no receivable or gain** because realisation was not assured, and management said on the Q1 call: *"Given ongoing uncertainties related to the timing and ultimate amount of the reimbursement, we have excluded potential refunds from our forward guidance"* [22][25]. TJX booked its refund in Q2 — GAAP EPS $1.36 vs adjusted $1.22, i.e. **~$0.14/share of tariff-refund benefit** [31][33]. If Ross does the same, headline GAAP EPS could print far above $1.92 on a one-time item. The market should look through it; a naive tape may not, for a few minutes.

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what's at stake

**Q1 FY2026 (13 weeks ended 2026-05-02), the quarter that reset the story** [20][21][22]:

| | Q1 FY26 | Q1 FY25 | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Total sales | $6,010M | $4,985M | **+21%** |
| Comparable store sales | **+17%** | — | highest in company history |
| Diluted EPS | **$2.02** | $1.47 | +37% |
| Operating margin | **13.4%** | 12.2% | +120 bps |
| Net income | $650M | $479M | +35% |
| COGS / SG&A | $4,231M / $976M | — | — |

Guidance for that quarter had been comps +7–8% and EPS $1.60–$1.67 [23]. They beat the comp guide by ~9 points and the EPS guide by 21%.

**Composition of the 17%** [25]: "primarily driven by a growth in transactions"; average basket grew but by a much smaller proportion; **units per transaction were flat**. Press coverage puts the split at roughly **+11% traffic / +6% basket** with a double-digit increase in customer count across income levels, ethnicities and age groups. Management attributed "a portion of this growth to the increase in tax refunds, versus last year" while insisting "the underlying fundamentals of our growth were extremely healthy" [25]. **The tax-refund flatter does not repeat in Q2 — that is the core bear objection to extrapolating Q1.**

**Margin bridge.** Merchandise margin improved **85 bps** in Q1, the largest contributor to the 120 bps of operating-margin expansion [25]. Marketing spend was flat YoY in dollars, producing leverage [25]. Year-ago Q2 op margin was 11.5% including tariff costs [24]; the Q2 FY26 guide of 12.8–13.0% therefore embeds **~135 bps of expansion**, much of which is simply the tariff drag disappearing.

**Balance sheet and cash (as of 2026-05-02)** [22]:
- Cash & equivalents **$4,131M**; total debt **$1,018M** (LT $777M) → large net cash
- Stockholders' equity **$6,305M** vs $5,576M a year earlier [21]
- Operating cash flow **$836M**; capex **$209M**
- Merchandise inventory **$2,977M, up 11%** ( +$307M ). Consolidated inventories **+12%**; **packaway 36% of total inventory vs 41% a year ago** [25]
- ROE **38.98%**; profit margin **9.74%** [4]

**Inventory read (my inference):** +12% inventory against +21% sales is *clean* — inventory turns improved. But packaway falling from 41% to 36% means less forward-buy cover, which is the mechanism Ross uses to defend margin in a volatile buying market. Wells Fargo cited "rising inventory levels" among its downgrade reasons [43]; I read the mix shift as the more meaningful item and the absolute level as unconcerning.

**Capital return** [23][22]:
- New **two-year $2.55B repurchase authorisation** for FY26–27, **+21%** vs the $2.1B repurchased across 2024–25
- Q1 FY26: 1.5M shares for **$319M**; on track for ~$1.275B in FY26
- FY25: 7.1M shares for $1.05B; quarterly dividend raised **10% to $0.445**
- Dividends paid Q1 FY26: $144M
- Net effect: ~1.7%/yr share-count reduction at the current run-rate plus 0.76% yield (my calculation from the above)

**Unit growth.** 2,282 stores at Q1-end (1,917 Ross + 365 dd's) [22]. ~110 new stores planned for FY26 (~85 Ross, ~25 dd's) ≈ 5% unit growth [25][46]; 47 opened across June–July across 15 states/territories, announced 2026-08-18 [42].

**Customer concentration:** none — Ross is a mass-market off-price retailer. The relevant concentration is *sourcing*: more than half of merchandise sold originates in China, the reason the 2025 tariff episode was so damaging [—May 2025 coverage, see §9].

**Strategy under the new CEO.** James Conroy (ex-Boot Barn) was named CEO in December 2024 [49]. His programme — brand modernisation, store refresh, improved marketing, a more entrepreneurial merchant culture, better branded-vendor access — is the stated cause of the customer-count surge [26][10]. He has also signalled openness to **pushing into higher price points / taking retails up** [50], which is the strategic risk: Ross's franchise is extreme value, and the traffic surge is currently concentrated at the *value end* of off-price [26].

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**Macro.** July 2026 US retail and food-services sales were **$763.6B, −0.6% m/m** (steepest drop since May 2025) though **+5.0% YoY**; the weakness was concentrated in nonstore (−2.2%, distorted by Amazon moving Prime Day into June), autos (−1.8%), gasoline (−0.9%) and electronics (−0.5%) [35][36]. **Clothing & accessories stores were the best category at +1.9% m/m**, rebounding from −0.4% in June [37]. Consumer confidence is deteriorating [36]. This is close to the ideal macro for Ross: a soft aggregate consumer with intact apparel demand drives trade-down into off-price, which is exactly the mechanism Ross cited when it guided FY26 up.

Rate/FX/commodity sensitivity is second-order for Ross: net-cash balance sheet ($4.1B cash vs $1.0B debt) [22], domestic-only revenue, beta 0.88 [4]. The live policy sensitivity is **trade policy**, and it has just swung from headwind to tailwind-plus-refund-option [22].

**Peers who have already reported.**

- **TJX (2026-08-19, one session before ROST)** — the dominant read-through [31][32][33][34]:

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Placer.ai measured Ross Dress for Less same-store visits **+16.4% YoY across the reported quarter, accelerating through June and July**, while T.J. Maxx traffic sat at or below flat [26]. In Q1, roughly 11 points of traffic produced a 17% comp [25]. If Q2 traffic is genuinely +16.4%, a comp materially above the 6–7% guide — plausibly low-double-digit — is very hard to avoid, which puts EPS at or through the $2.03–$2.05 buy-side bogey [29]. The margin math is on the same side: the year-ago quarter carried an $0.11/share tariff hit and an 11.5% operating margin [24], the IEEPA tariffs have been struck down [22], and merchandise margin was already +85 bps in Q1 [25]. Ross has beaten its own comp guide by 4–10 points four quarters running [20][21][23] and the stock has gone +8.4%, +8.0%, +8.1% on the last three [14][12][11]. Positioning is defensive (2.3 puts per call [9], UBS and Morgan Stanley targets below spot [10], stock −8% from its high [5]), so a big beat plus even a merely-not-terrible Q3 guide clears a low sentiment bar. The tariff-refund gain excluded from guidance [22][25] is free optionality that TJX has already demonstrated is bookable [31][33].

**Bear case.** The bar is no longer the guide, it is the whisper: $2.03–$2.05 EPS on a 9–10% comp [29], versus UBS's below-consensus $1.87 and channel checks showing demand "lighter than analysts expected" [27][28]. Q1's 17% was flattered by tax refunds, which management itself conceded [25] and which do not repeat in Q2. UBS's Google-search proxy is **−31% YoY in August** [27] — the quarter Ross must guide. The FY26 guide of 6–7% comps mathematically embeds an H2 comp near 2% *(my calculation)*, so management must either raise or confirm a sharp deceleration; their entire history says they will guide Q3 conservatively, and StreetSignal says 3–4% would be "disappointing" [29]. Ross trades at ~30x forward after +60.7% in 52 weeks [4], Wells Fargo has already called the easy money made [43], and two bulge-bracket targets sit below spot [10]. The precedent is one session old and unambiguous: TJX beat, raised the year, guided Q3 light, and lost 3.3% [32][33]. Ross's own single down print in the last five (−9.8%, May 2025) was caused by exactly this — a fine quarter and a bad forward guide [15][16]. Insiders have sold eight times and bought zero in six months [30].

**Base case.** Ross beats Q2 comfortably — comp high-single to low-double-digit, EPS at or above $2.00, possibly with a separately-disclosed tariff-refund gain — because the Placer traffic evidence is too large and too directly measured to square with a 6–7% comp [26][25]. Management then guides Q3 conservatively, most likely in the 4–6% range, and raises the full year to something around $7.80–$8.00 rather than the $8.20+ a straight-line extrapolation of Q1 would imply. Whether that is bought or sold is close to a coin flip and depends on the exact Q3 comp digit. Two things tilt me modestly positive rather than neutral. First, the de-risking has already happened: the stock is 8% off its high with six down closes in eight sessions and put-heavy event positioning [5][9], so a good deal of the "guide disappoints" scenario is pre-paid — unlike TJX, which went into its print near its highs. Second, ROST fell only 0.71% on the day TJX fell 3.3% [5][33], evidence that the market is already differentiating the share gainer from the share donor. Realised moves have averaged 7.27% against a 6.9% implied [6][7][8], so the event is roughly fairly priced to marginally cheap; I do not think there is edge in the straddle, only a modest directional tilt up.

**My preliminary read: direction score +18, probability up 57%, conviction Medium.** Reversal risk is high and concentrated in one sentence of the press release.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal, stated concretely: **Ross prints a Q2 comp of only 6–8% — at or barely above the top of its own guide — and guides Q3 to +3% to +4% comps while leaving the FY26 EPS range essentially unchanged at $7.50–$7.74.**

That combination would do three things at once. It would validate UBS's channel checks over Placer's foot-traffic reading [27][26], forcing the market to conclude that visit growth is no longer converting into comp dollars — that Conroy's traffic engine is buying customers who spend less, consistent with flat units-per-transaction in Q1 [25]. It would confirm that Q1's 17% was a tax-refund artefact rather than a durable step-change, exactly as management hedged [25]. And it would make the 30x forward multiple [4] indefensible, since that multiple capitalises a mid-to-high-single-digit comp algorithm, not a 3% one. Against a buy-side bogey of 9–10% comps and $2.03–$2.05 EPS [29], the de-rating would not be bounded by the 6.9% implied move; the May 2025 analogue (−9.8% close-to-close, −12.8% intraday, on a quarter that beat) [15][16] is the right template, and the TJX tape one day earlier proves the mechanism is live [32][33].

The lower-probability mirror image that would flip the bears: a **12%+ comp with a Q3 guide of 6–7% and an FY26 EPS raise above $8.00**, which would confirm the share shift from Marmaxx [34][26] as structural and re-rate rather than de-rate the multiple.
