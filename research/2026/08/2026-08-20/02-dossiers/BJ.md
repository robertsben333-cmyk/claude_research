# BJ — BJ's Wholesale Club Holdings, Inc.

**Event confirmed: YES.** Q2 fiscal 2026 results release **Friday 2026-08-21, prior to market open (bmo)**, conference call the same day at **8:00 a.m. ET**, confirmed by the company's own scheduling release dated 2026-07-23 [1][2][3]. No date change, no pre-announcement, no negative or positive pre-release 8-K found. Fiscal period is the **13 weeks ended 2026-08-01** [1][4].

**What this print is about.** BJ's has a headline-revenue problem that is the opposite of its actual problem. Total revenue is growing ~10% because gasoline is up ~26% year-over-year at the pump [5][6], while the number the market actually trades — **comparable club sales excluding gasoline** — printed **+1.5% in Q1 FY26 against a full-year guide of +2–3%** [7][8]. The stock fell **8.3% that day despite a $0.06 EPS beat** [9][10][11], which is the third time in the last five prints that BJ has beaten on EPS and been sold. Management then did the one thing that removes ambiguity: CFO Laura Felice explicitly told the Street that **"we see Q2 through Q4 in that range"** for the 2–3% ex-gas comp [12]. That converts Friday's ex-gas comp into a pass/fail test with a published threshold. The alt-data going in is unhelpful — Placer.ai measured BJ's Q2 2026 visits at only ~+5.0% overall with **"the widest quarterly gap in the sector between overall and same-store visits,"** swinging from +7.1% in May to +3.2% in June, and attributed the softness to BJ's Northeast-heavy footprint and unfavourable summer weather [13][14]. Against that, the stock has already de-rated hard (−27% over twelve months, 19.7x forward against Costco's ~42x [15][16]), it fell 6.8% in the three weeks straight into the print [17], short interest is unusually heavy for a staple at 6.4–7.5% of float [18][19], and there are two identifiable, management-flagged P&L tailwinds that did not exist in Q1: **gasoline prices fell sequentially through the quarter** (May $4.48 → July $3.93 [5]) after having *risen* through Q1 and compressed fuel margin [12], and **additional tariff refund dollars were explicitly guided into Q2** [12][20]. This is a genuinely two-sided print where an EPS beat is likely and the ex-gas comp decides the tape.

## 3. The one metric that matters

> **Q2 comparable club sales excluding gasoline, versus the +2–3% range management said applies to Q2 through Q4.**

Not EPS. Not total revenue. Not total comps. Here is how I know:

1. **Management pre-committed to the threshold.** CFO Laura Felice, on the Q1 call: *"We continue to expect comparable club sales, excluding gasoline, to grow in a range of 2% to 3%, and adjusted earnings per share in the range of $4.40 to $4.60,"* and on the cadence, **"We see Q2 through Q4 in that range,"** clarifying Q1 was expected to sit outside it [12]. There is no ambiguity left about what a pass looks like.
2. **The last print proves the reaction function.** Q1 FY26 delivered EPS $1.10 vs $1.03–$1.04 and revenue $5.66B vs $5.41B — a clean double beat — and the stock fell **8.3%** because ex-gas comps were **+1.5%** and merchandise gross margin was **−10bps** (and −60bps excluding the tariff benefit) [9][10][11][41]. The total comp of +6.3% was dismissed as gasoline [7][8].
3. **Total revenue is contaminated by fuel.** US retail gasoline averaged **$4.48/gal in May 2026, $4.05 in June, $3.93 in July**, roughly **+26% YoY** [5][6]. Consensus revenue growth of +9.5% is therefore not a demand signal. Costco's July disclosure makes the same point in the same channel: **US comps +10.3% as reported but +6.9% excluding gasoline and FX** [42].
4. **The alt-data points the wrong way.** Placer.ai (published 2026-08-14): BJ's Q2 2026 overall visits ~+5.0% YoY, but **"the widest quarterly gap in the sector between overall and same-store visits"** — i.e. new clubs, not existing ones, drove the gain. Monthly: **+7.1% May, +3.2% June**. Placer attributes the softness to BJ's Northeast-heavy footprint and unfavourable summer weather. Costco by contrast ran +8.2% overall visits with **+6.0% same-store** [13][14].
5. **The macro tape for the merchandise line is weak.** July 2026 US retail sales fell **0.6% m/m** (+5.0% y/y), **grocery store sales rose only +0.8% y/y**, CPI is +3.3% y/y, SNAP funding was cut, and economists described "a more discerning consumer" with softness in gasoline stations, electronics, recreational goods and groceries [43].

**Secondary metrics, in order:**
- **Merchandise gross margin direction.** Q1 was −10bps reported / −60bps ex-tariff-benefit, because BJ deliberately handed tariff refunds back to members as price, producing "roughly 0.5 point of deflation" [12][20][41]. Felice said the laps "get easier as we progress through the year" [12]. A second consecutive negative merchandise margin print, with the price investment now annualised, would be read as structural.
- **Whether the $4.40–$4.60 FY EPS guide holds, is raised, or is trimmed.** BJ raised FY guidance at the Q3 FY25 print [38]; it has never cut mid-year in this cycle. A trim is the single most damaging outcome available on Friday.
- **The tariff refund line.** Felice: *"There will be a little bit of additional tariff dollars that flow into Q2, certainly that we have line of sight to"* [12]. Q1's benefit was ~**50bps of merchandise margin, closer to $20M** [12][20]. Peers have booked large one-offs here — Target's Q2 GAAP EPS of $4.11 included **$1.65/share of tariff-refund benefit** [44]. If BJ books a comparable one-time gain, headline EPS could clear $1.20 on a mechanically inflated basis and the first five minutes of the tape may not distinguish it.
- **Membership fee income.** Grew **+9.9% to an all-time-high $132.4M** in Q1 [7][8]; management has guided growth to **moderate** as the January-2025 fee increase annualises [37][45]. MFI is the highest-quality line in the model and a deceleration below ~8% would be a real negative.
- **Gasoline profit dollars.** Q1 comparable gallons **+8%** chain-wide against an industry down ~4%, but *"margins were pressured early in the period as prices moved higher"* [12]. **My inference, not a sourced claim:** Q2's sequential price *decline* ($4.48 → $3.93 [5]) is the mirror image of Q1 and is normally a fuel-margin-dollar tailwind. This is the most underdiscussed upside variable in the print.

### Balance sheet, cash, capital return [7][8][37]

- Total debt **$774.2M** (current $375.0M / long-term $399.2M); cash $27.8M; **net debt $746.3M**; **net debt / LTM adj. EBITDA 0.6x**. Leverage is not a risk factor here.

### Footprint and unit economics [7][12]

- **267 clubs, 205 gas stations, 22 states** at Q1 end. Twelve club openings planned for FY26: 1 / 3 / 3 / 5 by quarter — **three opened during the quarter being reported.**
- Clubs opened in the last five years are comping **>6%, more than four times the chain average**; newer markets (Tennessee, Alabama, Indiana, Pittsburgh) are comping >10%; two-thirds of clubs opened in the last two years are expected to exceed their five-year sales plan in year one.
- **Texas is the debate.** First DFW club opened in Q1 and was described as "the best openings that we've had to date," membership **33% ahead of plan**, ~100,000 members in DFW; three more opened in Q2 [12]. UBS confirms four Texas stores "had been among its strongest openings to date" but says "the longer-term outlook in the highly competitive market remains a key point of debate" [40]. Jefferies' downgrade (Buy → Hold, PT $120 → $90) rests precisely here: limited white space in core regions, execution and margin risk in Western/DFW expansion, and comps trending nearer **2% through 2028 vs a 3% consensus** [47].
- Announced new locations continue: Kentucky, Florida and Indiana [48]; a Frankfort KY membership centre opened 2026-08-18 ahead of a fall club opening, with founding-member offers at $20/$50 [49].

### Membership economics [7][34][45][50]

- ~8 million members as of Q2 FY25; **90% tenured member renewal rate** in fiscal 2025.
- Fee structure: **$60 Club / $120 Club+**, increased effective 2026-01-01 [51][52] — the first increase in seven years. FY25 MFI $499.8M vs $456.5M in FY24 (+9.5%).
- Sam's Club raised its own fees to $60/$120 effective **2026-05-01**, i.e. *during* BJ's Q2 [50] — this removes part of BJ's price-of-membership advantage against its closest structural competitor.

6. **The stock de-rated:** from $98.45 on 2026-07-30 to $91.79 on 2026-08-19, **−6.8%** [17].

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

### Bull case
The stock has already paid for a bad quarter. BJ is **−27% over twelve months**, sits 14.7% below its 52-week high, trades at **19.7x forward against Costco's ~42x** [15][16][21], and drifted **−6.8% in the three weeks straight into the print** [17]. Positioning is defensive and thin: short interest is **6.4–7.5% of float** on a 0.19-beta staple [18][19], the event straddle is put-heavy at 1 : 1.1 [25], put/call *volume* is 1.40 [55], two long-only holders (Victory, Select Equity) cut or exited in Q2 [33], and retail chatter has collapsed 88% [60]. Into that, the P&L has two management-flagged tailwinds Q1 did not have: **gasoline prices fell sequentially through the quarter** ($4.48 → $3.93 [5]) after having *risen* through Q1 and explicitly pressured fuel margin [12], and CFO Felice pre-announced **"additional tariff dollars that flow into Q2, certainly that we have line of sight to"** [12][20], with Target having just demonstrated how large that line can be [44]. Felice also said merchandise-margin **"laps certainly get easier as we progress through the year"** [12]. Fundamentals underneath are intact: MFI at an all-time-high $132.4M growing ~10% on a **90% tenured renewal rate** [7][45], digital comps +28% on a +63% two-year stack [7], 17 consecutive quarters of traffic growth [12], new clubs comping >6% and DFW membership **33% ahead of plan** [12], and a buyback that shrank the share count 2.6% with $545M still authorised [7]. Zacks ESP is **+0.39%** positive with the Most Accurate Estimate above consensus [31], and Gordon Haskett upgraded to Buy on 2026-07-29 explicitly arguing the concerns are well understood [16][59]. A 2.5–3% ex-gas comp with flat-to-positive merchandise margin and a maintained $4.40–$4.60 FY guide, into short interest this heavy, is a squeeze setup.

### Bear case
The one metric that matters is the one the alt-data says is broken. Placer.ai measured BJ's Q2 2026 visits at ~+5.0% overall but with **"the widest quarterly gap in the sector between overall and same-store visits"** — three Texas openings inside the quarter did the work — and a monthly cadence that **fell from +7.1% in May to +3.2% in June** amid an unfavourable Northeast summer [13][14]. That maps onto a macro backdrop where **July grocery-store sales grew just +0.8% y/y** and total retail sales fell 0.6% m/m [43], and a competitive one where Costco is compounding US ex-gas comps at **+6.9%** [42] and Sam's Club raised fees to parity with BJ's on 2026-05-01 [50]. Management has already committed to +2–3% ex-gas for Q2–Q4 [12], so there is nowhere to hide: Q1's **+1.5%** [7] plus a June air-pocket makes a second consecutive miss plausible, and the Q1 miss cost **8.3% in a day despite a $0.06 EPS beat** [9][10][11]. The reaction function is documented — BJ beat EPS in each of its last four quarters and the stock fell on three of them (−8.5%, −1.5%, −8.3%) [26][27][28][29]. Consensus has drifted *up* into the event ($1.16 → $1.20 across sources, +0.09% over 30 days [31][33]) even as UBS cut its target from $117 to $109 [40], Deutsche Bank sits at Hold/$94 essentially at spot [39], and Jefferies models comps at **2% through 2028 vs a 3% consensus** while flagging DFW/Western execution and margin risk [47]. Q1 already showed the shape of the problem — revenue +9.9%, operating income +2.1%, **net income −4.7%**, adjusted FCF **−$42M** on $182M of capex [7][37] — growth is being bought with margin and cash. And insiders have sold twelve times and bought zero in six months, including the CEO seven sessions before the print [33][68].

### Base case
BJ beats the headline EPS — the Zacks ESP is positive, the beat streak is intact, gasoline margin mechanics reversed favourably, and tariff refunds were pre-flagged into the quarter [12][31] — and the market spends the day trading the **ex-gas comp** and the **FY guide** instead. My central expectation is an ex-gas comp printing at or just under the low end of the 2–3% commitment (call it 1.5–2.5%), merchandise gross margin roughly flat with a tariff-refund assist, MFI up high-single-digits, and the $4.40–$4.60 FY EPS range **maintained but not raised**. Historically that combination has been sold, but far less has been sold in advance this time: the stock is near its 52-week low with heavy short interest, put-skewed event positioning and a 6.8% run-down into the print [15][17][19][25]. So I expect a smaller-than-implied realised move with a modest downward tilt, and a wide right tail if the comp clears 2% cleanly and the guide moves up. Conviction is genuinely low: the two most decision-relevant inputs — Walmart/Sam's Club's same-morning print and any July BJ visit data — are both unavailable to me.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The consensus view going into Friday is "another gasoline-inflated revenue line covering a low-single-digit merchandise comp; fine business, no catalyst, $100-ish price target." **The single most credible reversal is an ex-gas comparable club sales print at or above 3.0% combined with a raised FY26 adjusted EPS guide above $4.60.**

Concretely, that would require: ex-gas comp ≥3.0% (vs +1.5% in Q1 and management's 2–3% Q2–Q4 commitment [7][12]); merchandise gross margin **up** year over year rather than −10bps [7]; membership fee income still growing ~10% despite the flagged moderation [7][37]; and the FY EPS range lifted off $4.40–$4.60 [7][8]. That combination would prove three things the bears deny simultaneously — that the Q1 comp was a weather/timing artefact rather than a trend, that the price investments funded by tariff refunds are buying share rather than just giving margin away, and that DFW/Texas is accretive rather than a margin drag [47]. Into 6.4–7.5% short interest [18][19], a 19.7x forward multiple [15], put-skewed event positioning [25][55] and a −6.8% run-in [17], the mechanical response is violent: BJ's median *positive* earnings reaction is **+7.3%** and its largest is **+12.2%** [26].

The mirror-image reversal — the one that would break the "cheap defensive compounder" thesis rather than confirm it — is an **ex-gas comp below 1.5% accompanied by a trim to the $4.40–$4.60 FY guide**. BJ has not cut mid-year in this cycle; doing so would validate Jefferies' 2%-through-2028 structural call [47] and would likely take the stock through the $83.21 52-week low [15].
