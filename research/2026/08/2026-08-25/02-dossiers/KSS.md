# KSS — Kohl's Corporation

**What this print is about.** Kohl's reports Q2 FY2026 (13 weeks ended ~1 Aug 2026) before the open on Wednesday 26 August 2026, with the call at 9:00 a.m. ET [1][2]. This is the fifth print of a turnaround under Michael Bender, who was made permanent CEO in November 2025 after the company's fourth CEO change in four years [3][4]. The stock does **not** trade on headline EPS — the last four reactions (+24.0%, +42.5%, −1.5%, +20.6%) line up almost perfectly with the comparable-sales trajectory and the full-year guidance revision, not with the size of the EPS beat (the March 2026 print beat EPS by 24% and the stock still fell) [own calc from 5][6]. Two things make this quarter unusually two-sided. First, the Street's line-item consensus implies net sales of $3.33bn, −0.5% y/y — i.e. comps at roughly −0.5%, an improvement from Q1's −1.1% — and Placer.ai puts Q2 store visits at −1.4% y/y versus −8% in Q1, the biggest sequential traffic improvement of the turnaround [7][8]. Second, Kohl's paid roughly $190m of IEEPA tariffs and has filed roughly $140m of refund claims, had received **nothing** as of the Q1 10-Q, and its FY2026 guidance explicitly excludes any refund — while Target booked $994m, Amazon $640m and Nike $302m of the same refunds in the same window [9][10][11]. Against that, Walmart's 20 August print (US comps +2.6% vs ~3.5% expected) took KSS down 10.3% in a single session and signalled that the low-and-middle-income consumer Kohl's depends on is deteriorating [12]. The options market prices a ~13.9% move; the realised eight-quarter mean absolute move is 16.3% and the median 18.8% [own calc from 13][14].

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Q2 comparable sales — specifically, whether Kohl's prints its first non-negative comp of the turnaround — read together with the FY2026 comp/EPS guidance revision.** The tariff refund is the wildcard that determines whether a headline beat is believed.

Why comps and not EPS: the four-print pattern is unambiguous. March 2026 delivered a $0.21 EPS beat and the stock fell 1.5% because comps were −2.8% and the FY guide was cautious. May 2026 delivered a −$0.13 loss and the stock rose 20.6% because comps were −1.1%, "our best comparable sales performance in over four years" [6][31]. November 2025 rose 42.5% on a surprise profit *plus* a guidance raise [30]. The market is trading the slope of the comp line and the credibility of the FY guide, nothing else.

**What the market expects and how I know:**
- Derived Street comp: **~−0.5% to 0%** (Zacks net-sales line item of $3.33bn, −0.5%, on a flat store base) [7].
- Company's own FY frame: −2% to flat for the year, reaffirmed with Q1 [6].
- Comp sequence: Q2 FY2025 −4.2% → Q3 FY2025 −1.7% → Q4 FY2025 −2.8% → Q1 FY2026 −1.1% [35][30][20][6]. The Q2 lap is the easiest of the year.

**The differentiated evidence:** Placer.ai foot traffic for Kohl's was **−1.4% y/y in Q2 2026 versus −8% in Q1 2026** (Retail Dive, 19 Aug 2026) [8]. In Q1, −8% traffic produced a −1.1% comp; a ~660bp traffic improvement, if ticket and conversion merely hold, argues for a comp comfortably above the Street's derived −0.5% and plausibly positive. This is the single most load-bearing non-consensus datapoint in this dossier.

**The tariff refund overlay.** Target booked $994m of pretax IEEPA refunds *within gross margin* in its Q2 (same calendar window) and reported EPS of $4.11 vs $2.05 [40]. Ross booked 405bp of operating margin from refunds [41]. Kohl's has $140m of claims filed and $190m paid, none received as of Q1, and guidance excludes it [9][10]. $140m pretax on 113.4m shares is roughly **$1.23/share pretax, ~$0.93 after tax** — against a $0.55 consensus. If any of it landed in Q2, the reported gross margin and EPS could be spectacular and the market's job on the morning becomes separating operating comps from a one-off refund. If none landed, the cash-flow question rolls to Q3.

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

**Q1 FY2026 (quarter ended 2 May 2026), the most recent hard data [6]:**
- Net sales $3.0bn, −1.7%; comps −1.1%; net sales fell faster than comps on prior-year store-closure timing
- Gross margin 39.9%, +4bp y/y
- SG&A $1.1bn, −1.6% in dollars but **+15bp as a percentage of revenue** to 36.2% — deleverage
- Operating income $46m (down from $60m); net loss $14m, −$0.13/share (vs −$0.21 Street) [1]
- Inventory $2.9bn, **−8% y/y** — "one of the cleanest inventory positions we have been in a while" [31]
- Operating cash flow −$74m; FCF −$158m, adjusted FCF −$184m (seasonal) [37]
- Cash $429m (vs $153m); **revolver drawn $0**, down $545m y/y; long-term debt $1.387bn; $50m of debt repurchased at a $9m discount [6][37]
- Rolling-12m adjusted EBITDA $1.195bn; company-framed net debt + leases / EBITDAR **2.2x** [37]. Against that, StockAnalysis computes EV $8.18bn on $1.20bn EBITDA (6.8x) — the leverage picture depends entirely on lease treatment [22]

**Mix and unit economics [31]:**
- Juniors +10%; women's sportswear flat; accessories flat; Home +400bp better than Q4; **proprietary brands +6%** (SO, LC Lauren Conrad, Sonoma)
- Men's and footwear underperformed; management guided to sequential improvement from Q2 (Nike and Skechers newness into back-to-school)
- **Sephora at Kohl's ran down low-single-digits**, dragged by makeup and skincare; fragrance and haircare strongest. M·A·C, Rare Beauty and Korean skincare (Beauty of Joseon, Aestura) roll out later in 2026
- Credit: Kohl's-card customers were **flat on comps, a 600bp sequential improvement**; CFO Jill Timm noted payment rates up and loss rates down. But **Other revenue fell 8%** on lower receivable balances — a structural drag the Street models at −6.4% for Q2 [31][7]

**FY2025 for context [20]:** net sales $14.8bn (−4.0%), comps −3.1%, GAAP EPS $2.38, adjusted EPS **$1.62**, operating income $624m (vs $433m), operating cash flow $1.4bn. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $1.00–1.60 is therefore a **down year** at the midpoint.

**What changed since the last print:**
- 1 July 2026 — revolver amended: maturity extended to 30 June 2031, borrowing base widened to include in-transit inventory up to 15% of eligible collateral, a Debt Maturity Reserve added [42][43]
- 5 August 2026 — Google Gemini-powered AI shopping assistant launched into back-to-school [44][45]
- August 2026 — Babies R Us shop-in-shops opening across 200 stores through the fall [46]
- 18 August 2026 — Kohl's added to DoorDash Marketplace [1]
- 18 August 2026 — quarterly dividend held at $0.125, **not raised** [19]

**What is at stake:** with a $2.08bn market cap and $500–600m of guided FCF before refunds, the equity trades at a ~24–29% FCF yield on management's own numbers [37][21]. The bull case is that the comp line inflects and that yield gets re-rated. The bear case is that Other revenue keeps shrinking, SG&A keeps deleveraging on a shrinking sales base, and $6.1bn of EV-implied obligations makes the equity a levered option on a secularly declining format [22].

## 8. Macro & peer read-through

**The macro tape is actively hostile to Kohl's customer.**
- **20 Aug 2026:** Walmart fell ~10% despite a revenue beat and a raised full-year guide, because **US comparable sales grew 2.6% against a ~3.5% expectation**, Q3 guidance was 3–3.75%, and it flagged **>$2bn of incremental fuel-related cost headwinds** [12]
- **July 2026 retail sales −0.6%** — the first decline in nine months [12]
- **University of Michigan consumer sentiment fell ~8% m/m in August 2026**, ending two months of improvement, on fuel, energy and food prices [72]
- KSS fell **10.3%** that day, closing at $17.33 — a 30.3% drawdown from its December 2025 high at the time [12]

**But the value/off-price channel is booming, and Kohl's now positions itself as value.**
- **Target Q2 2026 (Aug 2026):** net sales +5.3%, comps **+3.8%**, comp traffic +3.6%, digital comps +8.7%, EPS $4.11 vs $2.05 — including **$994m of pretax tariff-refund benefit inside gross margin** [40]
- **Ross Stores Q2 FY2026:** total sales +13%, comps **+10%**, operating margin +610bp of which **405bp was tariff refunds**; raised Q3 comp guidance to +6–7% and Q4 to +4–5% [41]
- **TJX Q2 FY27 (19 Aug 2026):** net sales +5%, comps **+4%** (better than expected), **but Marmaxx — the largest division — softened** [73][74]
- **Macy's, the closest department-store comparable, does not report until 10 September 2026** — so there is no direct department-store read-through available before this print [75]

**Read (mine):** the divergence is the whole story. Traffic and dollars are flowing to off-price and to Target's value assortment, which is exactly where Kohl's is trying to reposition (value pricing, opening price points, proprietary brands). Whether Kohl's is a beneficiary or a donor in that trade is the fundamental question this print answers. Two things bias the answer positively: the Placer.ai traffic delta [8], and the fact that the entire retail complex just monetised tariff refunds that Kohl's has explicitly excluded from guidance [9][10][40][41]. One thing biases it negatively: Kohl's core low-and-middle-income customer is the exact cohort Walmart just warned about, and Kohl's carries $6.1bn of EV-implied obligations against $1.2bn of EBITDA [22].

**Rate/FX/commodity sensitivity:** limited direct FX exposure (domestic retailer). Fuel and freight are the live commodity input — Walmart's $2bn fuel headwind is a sector-wide flag [12]. Interest expense was $76m in Q1 FY2025 [76]; Kohl's is deleveraging opportunistically ($50m of debt repurchased at a $9m discount in Q1 FY2026 [37]), and the July revolver amendment extended maturity to 2031 [42][43]. The credit-receivable book is a rate- and credit-cycle-sensitive line running −6% to −8% y/y [7][31].

## 9. Bull / bear / base case

**Bull case.** Q2 comps print flat-to-positive — the first non-negative comp of the turnaround — on the back of Placer.ai traffic that improved from −8% to −1.4% y/y [8], an easy −4.2% lap [35], proprietary brands compounding at +6% [31], and the Sephora newness (M·A·C, Rare Beauty) plus 200 Babies R Us shops landing in the back-to-school window [31][46]. On top of that, some or all of the $140m of filed IEEPA tariff claims lands in the quarter, producing a gross-margin and EPS number far above the $0.55 consensus and a cash balance that accelerates deleveraging — precisely what Target ($994m) and Ross (405bp) just demonstrated is happening across the channel [9][40][41]. Management raises the FY2026 EPS guide meaningfully above $1.00–1.60 (UBS, a Sell-rated bear, already models $1.10–1.70 [34]). With 27.6% of the float short, ~9 days to cover, and an average price target at $18.23 that the sell-side would be forced to chase [22][38], the reaction resembles November 2025 (+42.5%) or May 2026 (+20.6%) rather than a polite +5%.

**Bear case.** Walmart just told the market that the low-and-middle-income consumer is cracking, July retail sales fell 0.6% and August sentiment fell 8% — and that cohort *is* Kohl's customer [12][72]. Consensus already models gross margin −40bp on promotional investment, digital shipping and transportation [2], and Q1 showed SG&A deleveraging 15bp on a shrinking base [6]; add a credit book running −6% to −8% and the operating-margin math only works if comps actually inflect. If comps come in at −1.5% or worse, the "best comps in four years" narrative dies, and the stock — up 41.5% since the last print, trading *at* its 14-analyst average price target with three bulge-bracket Sells outstanding — has nothing to fall back on [25][38][53]. Even a large EPS beat may not save it: March 2026 beat by 24% on EPS and still fell 1.5% because comps and the guide disappointed [5][20]. And if the beat is visibly manufactured by a one-off tariff refund that guidance already excluded, the market discounts it entirely and trades the underlying comp — which is the March 2026 script exactly.

**Base case (mine).** Comps land between −0.5% and +1.0%, modestly better than the Street's derived −0.5% bar, because the traffic data is too big a delta to ignore and the lap is easy. Kohl's discloses receipt of a material portion of the tariff refund and quantifies it separately, producing a headline EPS well above $0.55 that management explicitly frames as excluded from adjusted results. FY2026 guidance is raised, but modestly — to something like $1.15–$1.65 — whose midpoint barely clears the $1.43 Street number [38], so the guide is a "confirmed, not upgraded" event. Net reaction: positive but well short of the last two up-moves, in the +4% to +12% range, with meaningful tail risk in both directions given a 13.9% implied move that sits below this name's own 16.3% eight-quarter mean absolute realised [own calc from 13][25]. Direction score **+20**, probability of an up move **57%**, conviction **Medium** — the bullish evidence is genuinely differentiated (Placer, tariff refund, bear-side estimate raises) but the valuation and positioning headroom is gone and the macro is moving the wrong way in real time.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

**The single most credible reversal: the tariff refund arrives, is enormous, and the market treats it as the whole beat.** If Kohl's reports, say, $1.40 of EPS against a $0.55 consensus but the release shows that $0.90+ of it is a non-recurring IEEPA refund inside gross margin, *and* the underlying comp is −1.5% rather than flat, then every element of the bull case collapses at once: the headline beat is dismissed as one-off, the comp inflection is falsified, the guidance raise is revealed as a refund pass-through rather than an operating upgrade, and a stock trading at its average price target with 41% of gains banked since May has no defence. This is not hypothetical — it is exactly the March 2026 configuration (a 24% EPS beat, a −2.8% comp, a −1.5% stock) with a bigger, more obviously non-operating beat on top. The tell to watch in the first ninety seconds of the release: whether the comparable-sales line and the "adjusted" EPS line are given *before* the tariff-refund footnote, and whether the FY comp guide (−2% to flat) moves, not just the EPS guide.

**The mirror-image flip:** if the comp prints positive and the FY *comp* guide is raised to flat-to-positive — the first positive comp guide in years — then the 27.6%-of-float short base and a sell-side sitting at $18.23 with three Sells become the fuel, and the November 2025 (+42.5%) outcome is live regardless of what the tariff line says.
