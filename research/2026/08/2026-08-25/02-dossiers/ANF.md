# ANF — Abercrombie & Fitch Co.

**What this print is about.** ANF reports Q2 FY2026 before the open on Wednesday 26 August 2026 into a
stock that has gone almost vertical: +21.0% in one month and +55.7% in three months [22], closing at
$112.36 on 24 Aug [3]. The bar management itself set is low — Q2 EPS guidance of $1.80–$2.00 implies EPS
down ~18% YoY [6][16] — and the Street has parked itself at the midpoint ($1.90–$1.95) [16][17][20]. So
this is not a print about whether ANF clears its own guide; UBS, JPMorgan, Jefferies, Telsey and Barclays
have all effectively pre-announced that it will [12][24]. It is a print about whether a beat-and-raise is
still worth paying for after a 56% three-month move, in a tape where Walmart beat *and* raised guidance
five sessions ago and fell 9% [27][28]. Two things could break that frame: (a) comps, which turned
negative in Q1 (total −1%, Hollister −2%) [10][11] and which the Street models at ~0.0% for Q2 versus
+3.0% a year ago [17]; and (b) a genuine off-model wildcard — ANF has applied for roughly $100 million of
IEEPA tariff refunds that carry **zero** benefit in current guidance [30][31]. Options are pricing a
±15.5% move [4], the richest setup of the eight-quarter series.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**Comparable sales — specifically whether total company comps inflect back to positive and whether
Hollister comps stop going backwards.**

- Q1 FY2026 comps were **−1% total**, with Abercrombie brands **flat** and Hollister **−2%**; by region
  Americas +1%, APAC +15%, EMEA −11% [10][11]. That is the first negative company comp of the current run.
- Consensus for Q2 is **total comps −0.0%** against **+3.0%** last year, and **Hollister comps −0.7%**
  against **+19.0%** last year [17].
- Why it is the metric and not EPS: all of ANF's reported net sales growth in Q1 came from square footage
  (845 stores this year vs 807 [17]; 130 new "experiences" planned [11]), not from productivity. A second
  consecutive negative comp turns the "14 consecutive quarters of growth" narrative [10] into a
  new-store-driven optical, which is exactly the thing that de-rates a 10x retailer. Hollister carried the
  company through FY2025 (+19% in Q2 FY25 [36], +16% in Q3 FY25 [13]); Hollister rolling over removes the
  offset to a soft Abercrombie brand.

**Secondary signal with the largest tail: the IEEPA tariff refund.** ANF's 10-Q confirms it "applied for
refunds of IEEPA tariffs previously paid" and had recognised none as of 2 May 2026, treating them as an
ASC 450 gain contingency [31]. A call summary puts the amount applied for at **~$100 million, with no
benefit in guidance** [30]. For scale: $100M pre-tax on 44.4M shares at a ~32% tax rate is ~$1.53/share
against a $1.90 consensus (*my calculation*). Target booked $752M / $1.65 per share of tariff refunds in
its comparable quarter [28], and CBP had disbursed ~$100B of ~$166B collected as of end-July [40], so the
mechanism is live. Any Q2 recognition would produce a headline EPS blowout that is economically real but
non-recurring. Watch whether the market treats it as cash (bullish) or as noise (Target's stock closed
flat on the day it booked its refund [28]).

## 4. Fundamentals — what changed, what is at stake

**Q1 FY2026 (reported 27 May 2026)** [6][10][11]:
- Net sales $1.1B, +2% (Americas $899.9M +3%; EMEA $167.4M −10%; APAC $46.5M +24%)
- Abercrombie brands $564.7M +3%; Hollister brands $549.1M flat
- Gross margin 62.8%; operating margin 8.0%; operating income $88.8M; diluted EPS $1.47 on 45.7M shares
- Cash $594.1M; inventory $532.7M (−2% at cost, units up low single digits); **no ABL borrowings** [31]
- Operating cash flow $44.3M; capex $61.3M → **FCF ≈ −$17M in Q1** (*my calculation from [31]*)
- $105.0M of buybacks, 1.156M shares [31]; 834 company-owned + 62 franchise stores [31]

**Margin trajectory — the real story.** ANF's operating margin is compressing off a peak: 16.2% → 14.1%
in Q4 [14]; ~13.9% → ~10.1% expected in Q2 [16]. Q1's gross margin held only because 180bps of freight
tailwind exactly offset 180bps of tariff headwind [26]. **Freight flips to a headwind in H2** while the
tariff assumption steps from a 10% effective rate in Q2 to 15% on global US imports in H2 [11][26]. FY
guidance of 12.0–12.5% operating margin against $3.37 of H1 EPS ($1.47 + ~$1.90) versus a $10.20–$11.00
full-year range means ~$6.83–$7.63 has to come from H2 — an extremely back-half-loaded guide (*my
calculation*). That is the structural risk in the FY number.

**Capital returns.** ~$450M of FY2026 buybacks planned [6][11] against a $4.99B market cap [3] — roughly
9% of shares retired per year, the single largest support under EPS. Q1 delivered $105M [31]; Q2 guided
to "at least $150M" [6][11].

**Balance sheet.** Cash $594.1M, ~$1B of total liquidity including the ABL, no ABL drawn [31]. Enterprise
value $5.67B vs $4.99B market cap [5] — the ~$680M gap is essentially operating lease liabilities. Current
ratio 1.45 [5]. This is not a leverage story.

**What changed since the last print:**
1. **China / APAC strategic review went public.** Bloomberg (4 Aug 2026) reported ANF is working with an
   adviser and may sell a stake in its China unit, potentially worth several hundred million dollars;
   early stage, may not transact [18][19]. Jefferies sizes an APAC exit or royalty conversion at ~**$0.40**
   of FY2028 EPS, noting APAC operating income was **−$12M in 2025** on sales that grew $119M (2023) →
   $157M (2026) [19].
2. **NFL partnership materially expanded** (19 Aug 2026): distribution moves beyond ANF's own channels to
   NFLShop.com, stadium stores, team e-commerce and Fanatics.com, plus baby/toddler/kids extension [25].
   This is an H2/Q3 revenue item, not a Q2 one.
3. **Mary Fox (President, Lovesac) elected to the board** effective 18 Aug 2026 [2].
4. **Macro deteriorated:** US retail sales −0.6% in July, the first decline in nine months [29].

**Customer concentration.** Not applicable — direct-to-consumer retail. The Fanatics/NFLShop wholesale
channel is new and small.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** ANF trades at 10.28x forward earnings [5] against a specialty-retail industry at ~20x [22],
with ~$450M of annual buybacks against a $4.99B cap [3][6] retiring ~9% of shares a year. UBS models a
7-cent Q2 beat on better sales and gross margin and expects the FY guide raised toward the high end of
$10.20–$11.00 [12]; ANF has beaten by an average 8.1% over four quarters and by 16.7% last quarter [16].
July clothing-store receipts rose 1.9% despite a −0.6% overall retail print [29], so the back-to-school
window inside the quarter was fine. Then there are two off-model kickers: the **~$100M of IEEPA refunds
applied for and explicitly excluded from guidance** [30][31], worth ~$1.53/share pre-buyback on my
arithmetic; and the **China stake sale** [18][19], which Jefferies values at ~$0.40 of FY2028 EPS from
removing a −$12M operating drag [19]. If the print lands with positive comps, a raised FY guide, and any
refund recognition, a 10x stock with $153 and $135 targets on it [24] can move violently higher — as it
did by 37.5% in November [4].

**Bear case.** The stock has already been paid for all of that. It is +21.0% in a month, +55.7% in three
months, and +7% in the two sessions immediately before the print [3][22], against a consensus target of
$119.27 that implies just 6.1% upside [24] — and UBS itself, while raising to $153, wrote that the run
"causes the firm to see limited upside over the earnings event" [12]. The operating business is
decelerating, not accelerating: total comps went **negative (−1%) in Q1**, Hollister comps −2% [10][11],
and consensus has Q2 comps at ~0.0% versus +3.0% a year ago with Hollister at −0.7% versus +19% [17].
Growth is coming from 845 stores versus 807 [17], not productivity. Web traffic is **−13.2% over 90 days**
and job postings **−15.2%** [42]. Operating margin compresses 380bps YoY [16], and the FY guide is
absurdly back-half loaded — ~$6.83–$7.63 of H2 EPS against $3.37 in H1 (*my calculation*) — into a period
where freight flips to a headwind and the tariff assumption steps up to 15% [11][26]. The base rate is
hostile: the stock has finished higher after only **3 of the last 8** reports, with a 12.2% average
absolute move [4]. And the tape just took 9% out of Walmart for beating *and* raising [28], with Target
getting nothing for a $1.65-per-share tariff refund [28]. Shorts have already covered (4.90M → 3.60M
shares [5]), so there is no squeeze underneath. Raymond James downgraded to Market Perform on 18 Aug, into
the rally [24].

**Base case (mine).** ANF beats the $1.90 consensus — probably by $0.05–$0.15 ex-refund, consistent with
its surprise history and UBS's work [12][16] — and nudges the FY range up modestly. Total comps come in
around flat to slightly negative, with Hollister still soft and EMEA still impaired by the Middle East
conflict [11]. That is a "fine" quarter into a stock that needs a great one. With a ±15.5% straddle [4]
and IV percentile 82 [23], the realised move will be large in either direction, but I think the modal
outcome is a fade: initial pop on the EPS headline, then a give-back as the comp line and the H2 margin
bridge are digested. The genuine two-sided risk is the IEEPA refund — if ANF recognises a material slug of
that $100M in Q2 [30], the headline EPS could print near $3.00+ and the tape's *first* reaction would be
sharply positive even if the quality is poor.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal of my bearish lean is **a positive total comp combined with an FY EPS guide
raised to a range starting at $11 or above, and explicit recognition of IEEPA refund dollars.**
Concretely: total company comps ≥ +1% (versus consensus 0.0% [17]), Hollister comps back positive (versus
consensus −0.7% [17]), Q2 EPS ≥ $2.05 excluding any refund, and FY2026 EPS guided to ≥ $11.00 with
management quantifying refund recovery on the call. That combination would (a) kill the "growth is only
square footage" objection, (b) de-risk the back-half-loaded FY bridge that is my main structural worry,
and (c) convert the $100M refund from a gain contingency into cash that funds an above-plan buyback on a
9%-of-cap annual run rate [6][31]. At 10.3x forward [5] with a $153 street high [24], that print gets the
stock through $125 and makes the 56% three-month run look like the *early* part of a re-rating rather
than the whole of it. A second, lower-probability flip: a signed or credibly-reported China transaction
announced alongside the print [18][19], which converts a −$12M EBIT drag into cash and a royalty stream.
