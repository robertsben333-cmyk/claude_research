## 1. Why this name
DLTR came out of the same 32-name universe and 6-name shortlist at priority_score 67.7
(change_expectation 74, ai_edge 60). Stage 2's deep dive gave it evidence_completeness 84/100 and a
preliminary_direction_score of −10 (not shown to the panel). It ranked #2 by panel_priority (48.7)
under `panel.rank_by`, just behind OKTA (49.1).

## 2. What the market cares about
Whether the Q2 comp beat is traffic-led or ticket-led. Q1 FY26 comps of +3.5% came entirely from
ticket (+4.5%) with traffic down 1.0% — a tariff-pass-through-pricing story, not real demand. Q2 guide
is comps +2.5–3.5%, adj EPS $1.00–$1.15; the question the print answers is whether third-party
foot-traffic data (Placer.ai: Q2 rolling traffic +1.4%, July +4.5%, best in nine quarters) shows up as
a genuine traffic inflection in the reported comp, and whether the FY26 adj-EPS guide ($6.70–$7.10) is
raised again.

## 3. Expected move size
Implied ±10.51% (refreshed 2026-08-26) sits above both the 8-quarter realised mean (8.97%) and median
(7.39%) — priced richer than DLTR's own recent history, largely because the one −22.16% tail sits in
the sample. Several personas flag that tail as a Family-Dollar-era, pre-divestiture event that no
longer applies to a single-banner Dollar Tree. Blended expected move is 9.9%, with a right-skewed band
of 4.9%–23.3%. The implied move is a one-standard-deviation reference, not a ceiling — and here it may
be a generous one.

## 4. Directional consensus
Consensus score +5.4, P(up) 51.8%, disparity 21.7/100 (aligned). No persona scored beyond |18| in
either direction: Fundamental (+18) and Options & Positioning (+14) lean bullish on traffic data and a
put-heavy positioning setup; Sentiment (−11) and Base-Rates (−8) lean bearish on a rich implied move
and an expensive setup cohort; Macro/Peers, Red-Team and Forensics all land mildly positive (+8 to +9).
This is a genuine coin flip with real disagreement, not a suppressed lean. Reversal risk (59.7
consensus, 60 red-team, tier Med) sits separately from direction.

## 5. Thesis
**Bull case:** the Q2 comp beat is traffic-led, not ticket-led — Placer.ai and Jefferies both show a
genuine nine-quarter-best traffic inflection — landing DLTR in the "rewarded beat" bucket this
earnings season has shown for traffic-driven prints (Ross +8%), on top of a 32%-average four-quarter
EPS surprise and a freshly replenished $2.5B buyback.

**Bear case:** the bar sits at the top of guidance with no valuation cushion (spot above the average
sell-side target, Hold consensus), the ticket-driven pricing engine behind Q1's comp structurally rolls
off now that IEEPA tariffs were struck down, and a large but excluded tariff-refund windfall could
flatter the beat while the core comp merely meets guidance — the exact pattern that got Walmart sold
9% on its own beat-and-raise this cycle.

**Base case:** an in-line-to-slightly-better comp beat with a modest guide raise, landing inside the
implied move without resolving the traffic-vs-ticket debate cleanly either way.

## 6. What flips it
Red-Team's strongest reversal case: the bar is already at the top of the guide, Q1's comp was
ticket-only with traffic down, and the tariff-pricing tailwind is rolling off — a cautious Q3/H2 frame
on a headline beat is the most likely path to a double-digit down print, echoing DLTR's own history of
guidance-reset selloffs. It converges with Sentiment's and Base-Rates' independent bearish reads on the
same "already priced" logic. It does not overturn the panel: Fundamental and Options & Positioning
independently cite the same third-party traffic data as evidence of a real, not-yet-fully-recognized
inflection. It would be broken by a quantified tariff-refund recognition alongside an EPS raise above
$7.10 and positive traffic comps together.

## 7. Positioning & sentiment
- **Options/skew:** front weekly call/put near 1:1.7, 50-day put/call volume ratio at the 86th
  percentile — puts are the crowded side, hedges to unwind on an in-line print.
- **Short interest/crowding:** modest, ~5.2% of float per the freshest read (one stale Jan-2026 figure
  cites ~9.8%), 3.4 days to cover — enough for a de-risking pop, not a mechanical squeeze.
- **Run-up/momentum:** spot ($133.21) sits above the average sell-side target with a Hold consensus,
  having faded from an intraday $140 on 25 Aug — de-risking into the event, not fresh crowding.
  Dollar General reports the same morning, so the subsector re-rates jointly.
- **Social/retail tone:** Stocktwits reads roughly Neutral with unremarkable message volume — no
  meme/pump signature, unlike the "extremely bullish" spike that followed the Q1 print.

## 8. Insider / communication / alt-data findings
Forensics found no informative insider signal: zero open-market purchases in 12 months, only
tax-withholding RSU-vest sales, and the one large print (Mantle Ridge/Paul Hilal, ~$248M) is an
activist fund wind-down and LP distribution mechanic, not a view on the quarter — Hilal remains on the
board. Communication signal reads as confidence rising, not falling: Q1 was a guide raise with
disclosure tightening (explicitly excluding tariff refunds), and the board replenished its buyback to
$2.5B immediately after the company itself bought stock at $111. The alt-data leg (Placer.ai traffic)
corroborates a comp beat but is already public — Jefferies published the same read before the print.

## 9. Analyst & revisions read
Consensus sits at the top of the company's own $1.00–$1.15 Q2 EPS guide range ($1.11–$1.15 depending on
source) after a 32.1% average four-quarter surprise — the whisper number is higher than the guide.
Multiple sell-side upgrades (Goldman off Sell, Jefferies to Hold with PT $85→$135, Raymond James, Truist
to $138) have occurred into the print, yet spot still trades above the average target — capitulation
without upside room left in the numbers. FY26 adj-EPS consensus could not be cleanly resolved between
two conflicting reads (~$6.72 vs an implied ~$7.17); left unresolved by the fundamental persona rather
than guessed.
