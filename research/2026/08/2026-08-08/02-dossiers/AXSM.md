**How I know what the market expects for it:** the revenue consensus is directly published ($221.9m–$228.52m) [26][27]; the AUVELITY split is my arithmetic from that consensus less the SUNOSI/SYMBRAVO run-rates disclosed in the Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 releases [4][5]; the opex expectation is my back-solve from the published EPS consensus, cross-checked against management's own Q1-call language [8].

**The secondary, unmodelled swing factor:** the first quantified AUVELITY Alzheimer's-agitation launch metric. Approval was 2026-04-30; commercial launch June 2026; so Q2 contains roughly one month [9][10][31]. Management has disclosed the infrastructure (sales force expanded to ~630 reps; ~100% of Medicare and Medicaid lives covered, which is ~90% of the expected AD-agitation payer mix) but has explicitly said "it's difficult to predict exactly" how fast long-term-care adoption accelerates [8][10]. ## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** The bar has already been cut and the stock has already been sold. Zacks consensus EPS came down 14.78% in 30 days [26] and the stock is 16.6% below its June closing high [11][17], so the price no longer embeds a clean quarter. Meanwhile the sequential revenue snapback consensus requires (+16–17%, implying AUVELITY ~+19%) is *smaller* than the one AUVELITY actually delivered last year on the same gross-to-net normalisation (+24.3%, $96.2m → $119.6m) [19][20], and Harmony's Q2 confirms the CNS demand environment held up [49][51]. On top of that sits an entirely unmodelled positive: the first AUVELITY Alzheimer's-agitation launch datapoint, into ~100% Medicare/Medicaid coverage with a 630-rep field force and a $8bn peak-sales frame [8][10][31]. Operating cash burn is already down to $20.7m a quarter from $43.4m [13], so a flat SG&A print would let management credibly reiterate the path to cash-flow positivity. History supports the tape too: 5 of the last 7 reactions were positive, and the last one was +8.31% *on a $0.41 EPS miss* [5][17].

**Bear case.** This management has surprised the Street on cost twice in three quarters, and the one thing it must now prove — that SG&A "levels out" [8] — is unverifiable until the number prints. The consensus already assumes it (+7.2% sequential R&D+SG&A on my back-solve, versus +9.0% last quarter), and the tighter vendor consensus assumes only +3.2%; if SG&A instead repeats a $15m+ step-up for the June launch and the DTC campaign, the EPS line breaks again. The positioning offers no cushion: 20 of 21 analysts at Buy with a $282 mean target 33% above spot [14][27] means every rating is already at maximum and the only available revisions are downward — Zacks has already gone to Strong Sell [24][26]. Stockholders' equity has fallen to $54.6m against $439.2m of current liabilities [13], leaving no accounting slack for another Q1. And a fresh competitive overhang landed five days ago: Takeda's oveporexton approval directly threatens SUNOSI's ~18% revenue contribution and complicates the AXS-12 story [37]. Finally, the AD-agitation opportunity may simply be slow — Rexulti has taken only 4.6% share of this market since 2023 [52], and management itself declined to predict long-term-care adoption pace [8].

**Base case.** Revenue lands in or slightly above the $222–229m band on AUVELITY gross-to-net normalisation plus a token AD-agitation contribution; SG&A rises sequentially but by less than Q1's $15.7m step; EPS lands somewhere between the two vendor consensuses and is largely ignored; the stock's direction is set in the first ten minutes of the 8:00 a.m. call by whatever concrete number management attaches to the Alzheimer's launch and by whether the phrase "into cash flow positivity" survives unqualified. My central expectation is a **move smaller than the 10.58% priced** — the seven-quarter realised maximum is 9.35% and the mean is 4.67% [17] — with a modest upward tilt, because the numbers have already been cut, the stock has already de-rated, and the sequential revenue hurdle is below last year's comparable. Rests on: [11][14][17][26][27] for the setup, [4][5][19][20] for the sequential arithmetic, [8] for the opex commitment, [12] for the pricing.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal is **not** a revenue miss — it is a revenue *beat* accompanied by another SG&A step-up of $15m or more, i.e. Q2 SG&A at or above ~$200m against Q1's $185.0m. That combination would reproduce the Q4 2025 pattern exactly — a good top line the market refuses to pay for — and it would break the one forward commitment management has made ("level out shortly thereafter") [8]. Because 20 of 21 analysts are at Buy with targets averaging $282 [14][27], that outcome forces the first real wave of target cuts rather than rating changes, and the stock has 33% of sell-side optimism to unwind before it finds a supported level. Concretely: **SG&A ≥ $200m with revenue ≥ $225m would be the bearish outcome that the current bull consensus is least prepared for**, and I would expect it to produce a move at or beyond the lower bound of the implied range.

The symmetric flip on the upside is smaller and more specific: management quantifying AD-agitation patient starts or LTC formulary wins at a level that lets the sell side move the FY27 revenue line (currently $1.60bn [14]) higher. Because there is no consensus for that number, it is pure upside optionality.
