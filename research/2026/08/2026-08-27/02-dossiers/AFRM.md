# AFRM — Affirm Holdings, Inc.

**Event confirmed: TRUE.** Affirm reports fiscal Q4 2026 (quarter ended June 30, 2026) **after the close on Thursday, August 27, 2026**, with the shareholder letter posted to the IR site and a conference call at **2:00pm PT / 21:00 UTC**, hosted by Max Levchin (CEO), Michael Linford (COO) and Rob O'Hare (CFO). The date was pre-announced on August 6, 2026 and has not moved. [1][2]

**What this print is about.** It is not about the June quarter. Q4 is well-fenced: management guided GMV to $13.15–13.45B, revenue to $1.08–1.11B and RLTC to $535–550M back on May 7, and both Oppenheimer and TD Cowen have publicly said they expect GMV at or above the high end. [3][12][13] The print trades on the **first formal FY2027 guide**, which management is expected to give on the call. The Street models roughly **29% GMV growth** in FY27; Affirm's own medium-term framework laid out at the May 2026 Investor Forum is "**more than 25%**" GMV growth en route to a $100B GMV target. [12][13][14] That gap is the whole trade. It is also exactly the mechanism that has destroyed the BNPL cohort this reporting season — Klarna beat and cut its full-year volume guide and fell ~22% on Aug 18; Sezzle beat and *raised* full-year guidance but flagged second-half deceleration and fell ~28% after hours on Aug 6. [15][16][17] Affirm itself has now traded **down on the day after each of its last two prints (-4.0% in February, -5.0% in May) despite beating on both the top and bottom line.** [computed from 20] A beat is no longer sufficient; the FY27 number, and how Levchin frames "revert to the mean" GMV growth, is what the tape will price.

## 3. The one metric that matters

**FY2027 GMV growth guidance (and, secondarily, the FY27 RLTC-margin and adjusted-operating-margin framing).**

Why: Affirm's Q4 is a pre-guided, well-modelled quarter with two sell-side firms publicly calling for an above-high-end GMV beat, so the June-quarter print carries little information. The variable with genuine dispersion is the first FY27 number. Cantor Fitzgerald's July 22 note states the explicit setup: management is expected to guide **GMV growth above 25% versus the Street at 29%**, with revenue at 7.5–8.5% of GMV, RLTC margin 3.75–4.0% and adjusted operating margin 30–35% — all lifted directly from the medium-term framework Affirm presented at its May 2026 Investor Forum. [13][14] Oppenheimer independently says it expects management "to keep longer-term targets cautious, likely not lifting FY2027 guidance much above its medium-term view of more than 25% GMV growth." [12] Management has separately said GMV growth should "revert to the mean" as it laps a prior-year period that carried 40–45% growth. [8]

**How I know the market cares:** the two most recent AFRM prints both beat on revenue and EPS and both traded down (-4.02%, -4.97%) [computed from 20]; Klarna beat every line and fell ~22% purely on a full-year volume guide cut [15][17]; Sezzle beat and raised and fell ~28% after hours purely on second-half deceleration commentary [16]. Three consecutive data points in the same cohort say the market is trading the forward volume guide, not the quarter.

**Secondary metrics that can move the stock:** (a) 0% APR GMV mix — management guided this to be "stable on a year-over-year basis" in FQ4'26, and any rise is dilutive to revenue-as-a-percent-of-GMV [3]; (b) 30+ day delinquencies ex-Peloton, which were 2.8% in FQ3'26, up 29bp YoY, with management attributing roughly half the increase to a smaller denominator from tax-refund seasonality [3]; (c) average cost of funds, 5.8% in FQ3'26 and down 126bp YoY [3]; (d) Affirm Card GMV, $2.13B and +146% YoY [3][11].

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Affirm delivers GMV above the $13.45B high end — Oppenheimer says $13.5B and TD Cowen says a few points above the top of guidance [12][13] — with RLTC at or above $547M, a second consecutive quarter of GAAP operating profitability, and adjusted operating margin at the 29.5% top of the range [3][12]. Credit is a non-issue: Klarna's US 30+ DQ fell 20bp QoQ, all-card 60+ DQ is 2.69% vs 2.87% a year ago, and Affirm's own funding cost is 126bp better YoY [17][42][3]. Shopify's +32% June-quarter GMV corroborates the volume [40]. Management then gives an FY27 GMV number with a 28-or-29 handle rather than a vague ">25%", validating the Street, and layers in the Google/Gemini, Stripe agentic, Expedia-exclusive and international optionality unveiled since May [23][24][25][14]. With short interest already down 13.5% MoM and the stock 23.5% below its 52-week high, a clean guide re-rates the multiple; the two comparable "guide-and-raise" Q4/Q1 prints (Aug 2025, Nov 2025) produced +10.59% and +11.61% [computed from 20].

**Bear case.** The quarter is pre-solved, so the only variable is the guide, and both Cantor and Oppenheimer have already told clients management will anchor FY27 to ">25%" GMV growth against a Street at 29% [12][13][14]. Management itself has said growth should "revert to the mean" as it laps a 40–45% comp [8]. That is a guide-down in everything but name, into a cohort that has just been savaged for precisely this: Klarna -22% on a volume guide cut despite beating every line, Sezzle -28% on second-half deceleration despite beating *and* raising [15][16][17]. Affirm's own last two prints were beats that closed down 4.02% and 4.97% [computed from 20]. Positioning offers no cushion — 0 sell ratings out of 34, four PT raises in five weeks, short interest only 5.7% of float and falling, and the stock already +8.6% over three months and +82% off the March low [4][18][19][35][20]. Add the second-order risks: the 0% APR mix at 29% of GMV is dilutive to revenue-as-percent-of-GMV if it drifts up [3]; the 30+ day DQ is up 29bp YoY [3]; the $1.5B 0% converts mature November 15, 2026 [29][30]; and there is a ~4% diluted share overhang with no active buyback [31].

**Base case (my read).** Affirm beats Q4 on GMV, revenue, RLTC and EPS — that is the highest-probability single outcome and it is largely in the price. The FY27 guide comes in framed conservatively, at or modestly above ">25%" GMV growth, below the Street's 29%, with management pointing to the Investor Forum framework and macro caution rather than raising the medium-term bar. The tape then does what it has done for two consecutive quarters and for both BNPL peers this season: fades the beat. Magnitude is more likely mid-single-digit than double-digit, because the conservative-guide risk is *already publicly flagged by two brokers* and therefore partly discounted, and because realised dispersion has been compressing (last four reactions all inside ±12%). I therefore expect a modestly negative skew with a wide, roughly symmetric distribution around it, and I do not think the ±10.55% straddle is meaningfully mispriced.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The single most credible reversal is **management putting a hard, above-Street number on FY2027 rather than repeating the ">25%" framework.** Concretely: an FY27 GMV guide of **≥$63.5B (≈+28.5% on an FY26 base of ~$49.4B)**, paired with an RLTC-margin guide at or above the 4.0% top of the 3.75–4.0% band and an adjusted operating margin guide of 30%+ [3][13][14]. That single sentence would (a) invalidate the Cantor/Oppenheimer "conservative guide" framing that the buy side has pre-positioned around [12][13], (b) separate Affirm decisively from the Klarna/Sezzle deceleration narrative that has driven the entire cohort's derating [15][16], and (c) force upward FY27 revisions from a base of FY27 revenue consensus $5.29B [4]. In that scenario a double-digit up-move well beyond the implied ±10.55% is the realistic outcome — it is exactly what happened on 2025-08-28 (+10.59%) and 2025-11-06 (+11.61%) when Affirm guided above the Street [computed from 20].

A secondary, lower-probability flip: management discloses that the 0% APR / Pay-in-X mix *fell* year-over-year while RLTC-as-percent-of-GMV rose above 4.3%, which would break the "growth is being bought with promotional financing" bear argument outright [3].
