## 3. The one metric that matters

> **The Q3 FY2027 revenue guidance number, and specifically whether it is comfortably above $3.03B — with the FY2028 $16.5B target as the tie-breaker.**

How I know:
- Marvell's Q2 consensus is only **0.3% above the guidance midpoint** [S6]. There is nothing to beat *by* on the quarter itself.
- Management **already told the market** on 27 May that it saw ~$3B quarterly revenue in Q3, a full quarter earlier than previously planned [S26]. Consensus has simply adopted that number. So "in-line" Q3 guidance is, functionally, management repeating itself — a stall.
- TradingKey states it explicitly: "investors await Q3 guidance significantly exceeding the $3.03 billion consensus estimate and full-year FY2027 target revisions above $11.5 billion. If Q3 guidance merely aligns with or falls below consensus, even if Q2 revenue and earnings exceed expectations, the market may interpret it as a sign of slowing growth" [S6].
- The moomoo preview reaches the same conclusion from the options side: "A meaningful FY28 raise could matter far more for the stock than a modest Q2 beat" [S25].
- Precedent: on 28 Aug 2025 Marvell delivered record revenue but guided Q3 to $2.06B vs $2.11B expected and flagged "nonlinear," "lumpy" custom-chip demand — the stock fell 18% [S17].

**Secondary metric with real teeth: non-GAAP gross margin.** Marvell's GM has been on a long slow decline as custom silicon (structurally lower-margin) becomes the mix driver: 58.9% in Q1 FY27, −10bps q/q, guided flat at 58.75% mid for Q2 [S24][S5]. A GM guide with a 57-handle would, in my judgement, override a good revenue guide. This is the single most under-priced downside trigger in the setup.

**Third: Google-warrant accounting.** The 58,970,907-share warrant at $206.58 was issued 18 Aug 2026 [S19]. 1,360,867 time-based shares vest quarterly over the first year; the remaining ~57.6M vest in 240 tranches, one per $500M of Custom Products revenue, **beginning Q3 FY27** [S19]. This is ~6.7% potential dilution [S6] and will introduce a customer-consideration/contra-revenue or non-cash charge line that has not previously existed in the model. This is the first quarter the market will see how management presents it. Presentation risk is real and, so far as I can find, not widely modelled.

## 9. Bull case / bear case / base case

**Bull case.** Marvell prints ~$2.78B+ on revenue with EPS near $1.00, and guides Q3 to $3.20B+ while lifting FY28 above $16.5B on the back of the Google programme. The evidence for this path is strong and specific: Nvidia guided Q3 to $108B and said demand exceeds supply for 70% FY28 growth [S46]; hyperscaler capex is +77% to $725B in 2026 with Goldman at $1.01T for 2027 [S49]; Credo (+157%) and Astera (+104%) already confirm the interconnect ramp [S48]; Marvell's own interconnect line is running >70% growth versus a 50% plan and switching is tracking >$1B annualised into FY28 [S7]; the Google agreement covers accelerators, storage controllers, NICs, memory controllers and near-memory compute — five product families, not one socket — and warrant tranches begin vesting in Q3 FY27, meaning revenue starts now [S19]. Marvell claims 18 custom sockets and 20% target share [S37], and JPMorgan-sourced work says there has been **no ASIC share loss at either Microsoft or AWS**, with Trainium 3 ramping in CY2026, Maia Gen 2 on plan, and Maia Gen 3 already won for CY2027–28 production [S50]. Consensus is only 0.3% above guidance [S6] and Zacks Rank is #2 [S26]. If management pre-releases even a directional FY29 number ahead of the 6 October Investor Day, the stock clears $290 and the $325–$360 targets [S39][S27] start to look like the anchor. In this scenario the implied ±10.3% is materially too cheap and the move is +14% or better.

**Bear case.** The company beats a bar it set itself, guides Q3 to $3.05–3.10B — a raise, but not the *step-function* raise the buy side is demanding [S6] — and reiterates rather than raises FY28. Non-GAAP gross margin guides to 58.5% or below as custom-silicon mix bites, continuing the documented multi-quarter decline [S24]. The new CFO, five weeks from an Investor Day, declines to be pinned down on Google revenue timing and stresses "lumpiness" — the exact word that cost the stock 18% in August 2025 [S17]. Into that, the stock is at ~$259, up ~59% in four weeks and ~194% YTD, at 57–58x forward against AVGO's 20x [S9][S12], with an InvestingPro fair value of $178 [S12], 83.5% institutional ownership [S38], put/call at 0.53–0.56 [S9], call volume up to 50x normal [S14], Stocktwits "extremely bullish" [S13], and only 4.1% short interest at 1.2 days to cover [S15] — no shorts to squeeze, nobody left to buy, and everybody with a gain to protect. Marvell is positive on only 42% of its last 19 prints, with a median negative reaction of −8.0% and two of the last six at −18.6% and −19.8% [S16]. Add ~6.7% of warrant dilution [S6][S19] and an unfamiliar contra-revenue line making its debut, and a −12% to −18% reaction is entirely available.

**Base case (mine).** Q2 beats — revenue ~$2.74–2.78B, EPS ~$0.95–0.99 — because the quarter is effectively already known and the demand backdrop is exceptional (88% Polymarket beat probability is not wrong [S26]). Q3 guides to roughly $3.05–3.15B, at or slightly above the $3.03B consensus, and FY28 is reiterated at ~$16.5B with the big FY29 framing explicitly deferred to 6 October [S2][S6]. Gross margin guides flat, in the 58.5–59.0% band. That is a genuinely good quarter — and, from a base of ~$255–262 after this morning's Nvidia-driven gap [S20], not good enough. I expect an initial down move inside the implied ±10.3%, in the −3% to −9% range, with a real chance the Investor Day tease is enough to stabilise it. The single highest-variance branch is whether the Google contract is large enough and near-term enough to force an out-of-cycle FY28 raise tonight; if it is, I am wrong by a lot.

## 10. What would flip the consensus view

The most credible reversal, stated concretely: **management raises FY2028 revenue from ~$16.5B to $18B+ on the call, and attaches a dated, quantified Google custom-products ramp — e.g. "we expect Google custom products to contribute over $1B in FY28, beginning in the October quarter."**

Why this specifically flips it: the entire bear case rests on "management already told you $3B for Q3 and $16.5B for FY28, so the news is priced." The one thing nobody can have priced is an FY28 number that moves *again*, for the third consecutive quarter, on a contract signed 29 July that no analyst has modelled because the 8-K discloses vesting economics but not revenue timing [S19]. Warrant tranches vest at one per $500M of Custom Products revenue starting Q3 FY27 [S19] — if management uses the call to say how many tranches it expects to vest in FY28, it hands the market a hard revenue number for a business the Street currently carries at roughly $4B for FY28 [S3]. That converts the $120B headline from a 2033 abstraction into a near-term model input, justifies the 57x multiple on forward-forward earnings, and makes the stock's four-week 50% rally look early rather than late. In that case the reaction is +12% or more and my read is simply wrong.

The narrower version that would also flip me: **non-GAAP gross margin guided above 59.25%** — i.e. the top of the current band or better — because it would falsify the "custom silicon dilutes margin" thesis that is the structural bear argument [S24][S5], and would mean the mix shift is happening at better economics than anyone assumed.

The reverse-flip (what makes the bear case worse than I think): any explicit language about hyperscaler **digestion**, **non-linearity**, or **lumpiness** in the custom pipeline. That vocabulary cost the stock 18% on 28 August 2025 [S17] and would land on a much more extended chart this time.
