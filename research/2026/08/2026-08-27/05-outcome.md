## Where the process failed

**The panel is now 0-for-2 on High-certainty calls where it had strong sourced
conviction in a specific mechanism (beat-and-fall), and both times the market did the
opposite of the historical pattern the panel leaned on.** OKTA (08-26, High certainty)
missed because guidance was raised instead of hedged; ESTC (08-27, High certainty)
missed for the identical reason — a well-sourced "this company beats and still falls on
guidance optics" thesis that simply didn't repeat. Two High-certainty misses out of
three High-certainty calls scored to date, against a Med-certainty record of 2-for-2
(WOLF, AFRM), is now a pattern worth naming plainly: **High-certainty calls are hitting
*less* often than Med-certainty ones**, the opposite of what the tiering is supposed to
guarantee. The common thread in both High-certainty misses is the same failure mode —
strong conviction built on a specific historical repeat pattern (company X always
does Y after beating) that the panel treated as more reliable than it is. That is a
`scripts/synthesize.py` certainty-scoring question, not bad luck: certainty is being
driven by how *aligned* the panel is (ESTC disparity 8.7) or by a single red-team
reversal-risk score, not by whether the underlying thesis has actually held up
out-of-sample.

**The preliminary read is now 6-for-8 lifetime on non-panelled names (S, MRVL, RBRK
all hit today) and, cumulatively across every panelled name to date, still outperforms
the panel on raw direction** — though today's ESTC panelled call being a *shared* miss
with the preliminary read (both -20/Neutral-leaning-down, both wrong) is the first time
the preliminary read and the panel failed together rather than the preliminary read
correcting a panel hedge. That is worth tracking: it suggests the preliminary read's
edge so far has come from cases where the panel oversplits into Neutral on genuinely
divided evidence, not from the preliminary read having a systematically better model of
guidance dynamics than the panel.
