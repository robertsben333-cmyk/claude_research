Defence stocks were handed a boost on Monday morning following reports that Chancellor John Healey was set to borrow billions to invest in the military at the forthcoming Budget. Rolls-Royce was up nearly two per cent in early trading to 1,184.40p, while Melrose was up 1.4 per cent. Both are key players in aerospace and operate at different areas in the supply chain on flight technology. Shares in warship builder Babcock rose 2.6 per cent to 964p, while fighter jet maker BAE’s stock was up 1.7 per cent to 1,963p. It comes as Healey is tipped to announce extra funding for defence through the use of public finance institutions (PuFins) at the Budget. The chancellor is set to use the National Wealth Fund and British Business Bank to borrow cash for defence, without using breaching the government’s fiscal rules. While this isn’t the 1930s-style rearmament many say is needed, it should be a sign of more things to come as the UK looks to fulfil its role in NATO,” Chris Beauchamp, chief market analyst at IG, said. “Recent events in Ukraine have reminded the UK and others that outsourcing their security to the US is no longer the safe choice, and with that realisation comes more defence spending.” The funds generated are expected to be directed towards backing British defence manufacturers and military technology firms, while also seeking to boost domestic jobs, according to reports in The Times. Healey under pressure for Nato targets The use of borrowing through PuFins could also serve as a lightning rod for criticism, despite not breaking the fiscal rules. Healey has committed to reduce the UK’s deficit by bringing down borrowing and funding day-to-day public spending through tax receipts. Under the government’s updated fiscal framework borrowing done by these institutions to take financial stakes or extend loans does not count against core debt targets. Healey resigned as defence secretary under Sir Keir Starmer after a row over funds for the Defence Investment Plan. He had demanded a timetable to increase defence spending to three per cent of GDP by 2030, ahead of Nato’s wider target of 3.5 per cent by 2035. The Office for Budget Responsibility (OBR) has estimated that reaching three per cent of GDP would require an additional £17.3bn in 2029 to 2030. Shadow chancellor Andrew Griffith has called on Healey to resign unless Wednesday’s Budget sets out a fully funded plan to reach the three per cent target. “We are living in an era of extraordinary threats, and yet the Chancellor is dithering over finding the money to pay the country’s insurance premium,” he said.
