Group Chief Executive’s Review Overview The first half has marked an important step in creating a stronger, focused QinetiQ. We delivered a solid financial performance and sharpened the Group’s focus on the UK, European NATO and key allied defence markets where we have the strongest positions and see the most attractive opportunities for growth and returns. I would like to thank our highly skilled employees for their continued dedication and hard work and our customers and partners for their ongoing support. We delivered orders of £771 million, up 9% and revenue of £923 million, up 3%. Operating profit increased to £110 million, representing an operating margin of 11.9% and we delivered operating cash flow of £113 million. We ended the period with leverage of 0.8 times and a backlog of £4.4 billion, providing good multi-year visibility. Since the year-end, we concluded our review of the US business and as announced today agreed its sale to Serco plc. The US market has changed and, despite improvements to the business, we do not have the scale to meet our growth and returns expectations relative to the rest of our portfolio. The transaction will create a stronger, focused company and enable us to concentrate our resources on the UK, European NATO and key allied markets, which offer more attractive opportunities for sustainable growth and attractive returns. The transaction remains subject to regulatory approvals and is expected to complete in the first quarter of 2027. Our core business, excluding the US, delivered revenue growth of c. 5% in the first half and has leading positions underpinned by long-term customer relationships and differentiated capabilities aligned with growing demand for warfighting readiness. We are confirming our guidance for the full year: revenue growth of 3% to 5%; an operating margin of 11.0% to 11.5%; EPS growth of 8% to 10%; and cash conversion above 90%. We are increasing investment to capture the sustainable growth opportunities ahead. Following completion of the US sale, we expect to return £200 million of the proceeds to shareholders by increasing the existing FY28 and FY29 share buybacks by £100 million per year. A stronger focused company Following the agreed US sale, we are focusing the Group on core markets and the capabilities where we hold the strongest positions. Our role is clear. We develop, test, deploy and sustain critical defence systems, helping our customers improve their warfighting readiness. We help get capability into service and keep it effective throughout its operational life. As a sovereign, integrated defence and technology services company, we bring together the expertise and infrastructure needed to support customers across that lifecycle. Our strength lies not only in our individual capabilities, but in how we combine them to solve complex customer problems. We have deep technical expertise, trusted customer relationships, established positions on critical programmes and unique national infrastructure. Together, these strengths give us a clear right to win as customers invest in upgrading existing systems, introducing new technologies and improving warfighting readiness. Customer priorities play to our strengths Our customers are responding to an urgent need to improve warfighting readiness. This requires them both to accelerate the deployment of new platforms and technologies and to upgrade the systems they operate today. It also means increasing the use of affordable mass, autonomy and artificial intelligence, improving interoperability across allied forces and domains and building greater sovereign industrial resilience. These priorities play directly to our strengths. Customers need partners that can develop advanced technologies, integrate them into complex systems, test and assure them, and support them throughout their operational lives. Our four capabilities address these needs: developing advanced technologies; engineering complex systems; testing and evaluating their performance; and supporting live defence operations. Together, these enable us to help customers introduce new capability faster and get more from their existing platforms and systems, creating demand for our expertise both now and over the longer term. Clear focus on three core markets Our three core markets—the UK, European NATO and key allies—are increasing defence spending to improve warfighting readiness. The UK defence budget is set to reach £68 billion this financial year, rising by 5% annually over the next three years. Near-term budget pressures nevertheless continue as customers seek to meet increasing requirements, ahead of the anticipated acceleration in defence spending. European NATO and key allied markets are also increasing defence spending creating significant growth opportunities for our aligned capabilities. Countries operating the same platforms, or participating in shared programmes, need many of the same engineering, test, training and operational support capabilities that we already provide to the UK. We are building on that experience, supported by strong government-to-government and industry relationships, to develop and pursue new opportunities beyond our current customer base. Across the UK, European NATO and key allies, we see a £13 billion annual addressable market for our capabilities, including £6 billion in the UK. Our growth strategy in action Across our four capabilities, we see growth from platform upgrades, defence digitalisation, increasing readiness requirements and investment in innovation. Our trusted expertise helps customers bring complex capabilities into service faster, while our sovereign capabilities support operational advantage and the development of next-generation technologies. In engineering services, we help customers bring complex capabilities into service faster and support them throughout the platform lifecycle. Our role on the Global Combat Air Programme, where we are providing engineering services to support the acquisition and entry into service is a good example. Programmes of this scale create opportunities over several decades, with further growth as customers upgrade platforms and capabilities over time. In mission support and operations, customers need digital advantage to strengthen deterrence and improve operational effectiveness. We bring multi-domain network and systems integration expertise built over several years of delivery in developing and assuring key elements of the digital backbone for the UK Ministry of Defence. Existing programmes – such as the TacSys Resource Partner contract – that we have in this area already generate more than £100 million of revenue per annum and we see further opportunities as customers invest in defence digitalisation and faster decision-making. In test and training, the drive for warfighting readiness is increasing demand for our specialist expertise and unique sovereign facilities. We are already seeing higher utilisation by UK and allied customers, with trials revenue up 50% in the first half. Through Project SAFARI, we are transforming the speed and scale of delivery by enabling easier access to the UK's sovereign test and evaluation facilities. This will create an agile environment to prove and deliver new technologies at pace as well as bringing the next generation of military capability into service faster. We are opening up our infrastructure, equipment and expertise to key allies of the UK, major Primes, disruptors and SMEs – expanding our customer base and building on the long-term revenue visibility of our Long-Term Partnering Agreement (LTPA) programme. In research and development, we continue to see significant potential in disruptive laser technologies for counter-drone and air-defence. Over the next several years, we are investing £20 million in further product development and production capability for our Helena family of laser sources. This investment will support smaller, more powerful lasers, opening up opportunities for mobile and redeployable systems for battlefield and critical national infrastructure protection. Working with partners including MBDA and Rheinmetall, our ambition is to sell these products into UK and international allied markets, including maritime and land applications. Investing for sustainable growth and attractive returns Our established roles on long-term customer programmes provide a strong foundation for sustainable growth. Following the sale of US business around 70% of revenue will come from sole-source and long-term contracts, supported by a £4 billion backlog and a 5-year pipeline worth £12 billion. This more focused portfolio strengthens revenue visibility and predictability, giving us confidence to invest in the opportunities ahead. We are increasing investment in our people and engineering skills, product development and manufacturing, workplaces and secure facilities, and the greater use of digital technology and artificial intelligence. We are directing that investment towards markets and opportunities where we are best placed to grow and generate attractive returns. Capital allocation and shareholder returns Shareholder returns remain a clear priority. We have declared an interim dividend of 4.0 pence per share, an increase of 33%, consistent with our policy of targeting a payout ratio of 35–40% of underlying earnings. We are continuing our existing share buyback programme of £100 million per year through FY29. Following completion of the US sale, we expect to return a further £200 million of the proceeds to shareholders by increasing the FY28 and FY29 buybacks by £100 million per year. Our disciplined approach to capital allocation balances investment in organic growth with attractive and predictable returns to shareholders, while maintaining balance-sheet strength and financial flexibility. Investment case – positioned for sustainable growth and strong shareholder returns We are focused on the UK, European NATO and key allied markets, where increasing defence investment and the urgent need for warfighting readiness are driving demand for our capabilities. We hold leading positions across engineering services, mission support and operations, test and training, and research and development. Deep technical expertise, trusted customer relationships, unique national infrastructure and embedded roles on critical defence programmes underpin these positions. We have strong multi-year revenue visibility with attractive margins, high returns on invested capital and strong cash generation. This supports increased investment in growth and predictable returns to shareholders. Group Chief Financial Officer’s Review Overview of half-year results The Group has delivered a solid half-year financial performance against the backdrop of near-term budget pressures in each of our markets. Total orders were up 9% on last year at £771m (H1 FY26: £707m), excluding the £1.7bn LTPA five-year extension secured in FY26. The book-to-bill ratio remained robust at 1.0x, reflecting sustained demand across our markets. This was a good result in the near-term market conditions, which continue to impact the timing of certain awards. EMEA services increased 6% driven by awards across our engineering services framework and tasking under the LTPA framework. Global Solutions increased 14% driven by increased award levels on the Space Development Agency contract in the US and our aerial and maritime targets business. Order backlog of £4.4bn, which includes £0.3bn of US unfunded backlog, provides good visibility for future growth driven by our core long-term UK business frameworks. On 30 September 2026, approximately 84% (£1.7bn) of the Group’s FY27 expected revenue was under contract, compared to 89% (£1.7bn) of the forecast FY26 revenue at the same point last year. Organic revenue grew 3% to £918.2m (H1 FY26: £890.9m) despite challenging near-term market conditions. Reported revenue was £923m, 3% growth, reflecting the combined impacts of foreign exchange movements and the disposal of the US Federal IT Business in FY26. EMEA Services increased 4% driven by strong growth in our UK Defence operations in engineering services, test and training and tasking revenue. This was partially offset by the expected lower H1 performance in our UK Intelligence business following the loss of the Ace contract in the prior year. We expect a stronger H2 as new business wins increase to offset this loss. Global Solutions was down 4% reflecting the actions in the US business last year on divesting and exiting some business lines. The US operations are tracking in line with our guidance for the full year with the second half weighting driven by growth in the space development agency work and strong half year orders. Our targets business performed well on orders and revenue reflecting the high demand for warfighting readiness amongst our customer base. Operating profit from segments of £109.7m (H1 FY26: £96.0m) was up 14%. This represents an 11.9% operating margin (H1 FY26: 10.7%), driven by good consistent programme execution against our backlog especially in EMEA services 12.5% (H1 FY26: 11.5%), which allowed for a number of risk retirements in the half, along with timing of investments in the business. Operating margins in Global Solutions increased from 7.4% in H1 FY26 to 9.5% in H1 FY27, driven by an increase in profitability in the US due to actions taken in FY26. The statutory operating result was a loss of £79.1m (H1 FY26: profit of £64.3m), including the impact of specific adjusting items (see below) and recurring income on Research and Development Expenditure Credits (RDEC), which was £14.0m (H1 FY26: £14.8m). Underlying profit before tax increased 12% to £114.4m (H1 FY26: £102.2m), in line with the increase in underlying operating profit, with underlying net finance expense in line with the prior year at £9.3m (H1 FY26: £8.6m). Specific adjusting items The total impact of specific adjusting items on operating profit was a net charge of £202.8m (H1 FY26: £46.5m). These items are excluded from underlying performance because of their nature and the potential to distort the assessment of the Group’s underlying performance. In H1 FY27 costs associated with the Group’s discrete digital investment programme were £12.5m (H1 FY26: £12.1m). The programme continues to modernise the Group’s IT infrastructure and support its future growth ambitions. The non-recurring costs of the programme are reported as specific adjusting items in the income statement, while recurring operating costs, including licence costs and overhead, are included withing underlying operating costs. FY27 is expected to be the final year of the programme. Restructuring costs and other impacts resulted in a net credit of £4.1m in H1 FY27 (H1 FY26 net charge: £22.6m). The net credit principally arose from the reversal of provisions recognised in prior periods following the completion of efficiency programmes in the UK and Australia, together with the reversal of certain non-cash charges recognised in the US in the prior year. The impairment charge of £174.6m in respect of the US CGU comprised £166.4m relating to goodwill and £8.2m relating to acquired intangible assets. The impairment arose following a change in management’s business strategy and capital allocation priorities, which resulted in a reassessment of the CGU’s future cash flows. The recoverable amount of the CGU was determined on a fair value less costs of disposal basis, with reference to third-party bids received for the business. Further details of the impairment assessment, including the valuation methodology and key assumptions are set out in Note 12. Specific adjusting items also included net finance income relating to pensions of £1.6m (H1 FY26: £1.1m), amortisation and impairment of acquisition intangibles of £19.6m (H1 FY26: £11.6m) and disposal-related costs of £1.8m (H1 FY26 £nil). Tax The total tax charge was £24.4m (H1 FY26: £19.0m). The underlying tax charge was £27.7m (H1 FY26: £25.4m) and is calculated by applying the expected underlying effective tax rate at a geographical level for the year ending 31 March 2027 to the underlying profit before tax for the six months to 30 September 2026. The Group’s full year expected underlying effective tax rate is 24.3% which is marginally above the underlying effective tax rate of 24.2% (H1 FY26: 24.9%). The decrease on last year is due to the geographic mix of profits. In future we expect the effective tax rate to be in line with the UK statutory rate subject to the geographic mix of profits and the recognition of deferred tax in respect of overseas tax losses and excess interest deductions. The Group has assessed the impact on the tax charge of the OECD's Global Anti-Base Erosion Model Rules (Pillar Two). The Group believes it qualifies for one of the transitional safe harbours provided in the rules in all territories in which it operates. The Group has not accrued a Pillar Two top-up tax charge for FY27 or prior periods. The Group has applied the temporary exemption issued by the International Accounting Standards Board from the accounting for deferred taxes under IAS 12 and neither recognises nor discloses information about deferred taxes related to Pillar Two income taxes. The Group does not anticipate a material quantitative impact and is fulfilling its compliance obligations under the Pillar Two legislation. Cash management and capital allocation policy Working capital management and overall cash performance has remained good. Underlying net cash flow from operations was £112.9m (H1 FY26: £127.9m). Our cash conversion definition reflects our pre-capital expenditure cash flows as a proportion of EBITDA to demonstrate how we convert our profit (excluding interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) into cash flow. We achieved solid underlying cash conversion of 64% (H1 FY26: 85%) in line with expectations. It was lower reflecting the out performance at FY26 year end and higher levels of working capital. As at 30 September 2026, the Group had £273.6m net debt, compared to £180.9m at last half year and £159.1m at 31 March 2026. The increase since year end reflects shareholder distributions, comprising dividend payments of £41.1m and £47.9m of shares repurchased under the ongoing share buyback programme, which contributed to the higher level of net debt. The half year also included cash payments relating to restructuring activities that had been provided for in FY26. The net debt position results in a leverage ratio of 0.8x (31 March 2026: 0.5x). During H1 FY27 we have demonstrated our capital allocation policy in action: − Invested in our organic growth through CAPEX, R&D, digital and major competitive bids − Provided a progressive dividend to shareholders and changed the dividend policy to a pay-out ratio – year-on-year growth rate of 33% − Return of excess cash to shareholders – Approximately 75% of the £200m share buyback extension announced in March 2025 had been completed as at 30 September 2026. The further £200m extension announced in May 2026 will commence on completion of this tranche. The Group is not subject to any externally imposed capital requirements. Committed facilities The Group has a £333.6m Term Loan split into two tranches: GBP Term Loan £273m (Tranche A); and, USD Term Loan $80m (Tranche B), which will mature on 27 September 2027. Participating banks have lent on a two-tier basis, three banks at £67m and four banks at £35m. In line with Group policy, £270m (c.80%) of the floating rate debt has been fixed using SONIA interest rate swaps at a weighted average rate of 3.46%, maturing on 27 September 2027. The Group entered into a new £250 million amortising term loan facility in October 2026, comprising a £200 million UK Export Finance (“UKEF”) Export Development Guarantee-backed tranche and a £50 million commercial tranche, with a final maturity of October 2032. The facility refinances the Group’s existing term loan arrangements and further strengthens liquidity while extending the debt maturity profile. The Group has a £290m bank revolving credit facility with an ‘accordion’ facility to increase the limit up to £400m. The facility, which will mature on 22 April 2029, was undrawn at 30 September 2026. We adopt a strict policy on managing counterparty risk through a combination of diversification of investments and regular reviews of counterparty limits using credit rating assessments. Our debt sits with our key relationship banks, who have strong credit ratings and diverse portfolios. The banks have been selected for their capabilities in our home countries to support our business. Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) ROCE at a group level uses the calculation of: (operating profit from segments less underlying amortisation) / (average capital employed less net pension asset), where average capital employed is defined as shareholders’ equity plus net debt (or minus net cash). H1 FY27 ROCE was 41% (H1 FY26: 21%) driven by stronger profitability and the reduction in assets following the write-down of the US CGU. As we continue to invest in the business to drive our organic growth strategy and maintain the disciplined capital allocation agenda, ROCE is expected to remain high, at or above the 25-30% range. Sector Reviews UK Defence Overview UK Defence delivered a strong first-half performance, achieving good revenue growth and further strengthening its position as a trusted partner to the UK Ministry of Defence. Demand remained healthy across Test & Training, Engineering Services, Mission Support & Operations and Research & Development, reflecting sustained customer focus on readiness, modernisation and operational advantage. The period was characterised by strong execution across major long-term programmes and progress in transforming how defence systems are developed, tested and assured. Through our unique facilities, technical expertise and deep customer relationships, we remain well placed to address the UK’s defence priorities and leverage sovereign capabilities with allies and international partners. Operational highlights • Modernising UK Test & Training: During the period, we launched Project SAFARI, a major programme to transform the delivery of Test & Evaluation through the Long-Term Partnering Agreement. Working with the MOD, regulators and industry, SAFARI will increase the pace and productivity of testing through more accessible ways of working and greater use of digital technologies, modelling and simulation, and AI-enabled analysis. It will also make it easier for SMEs and new entrants to access the UK’s specialist facilities and expertise, enabling innovative technologies to be developed and fielded more quickly. • Accelerating counter-drone integration: We supported the MOD and BAE Systems in integrating and assuring the Advanced Precision Kill Weapon System (APKWS) for RAF Typhoon aircraft. Tested at our MOD Aberporth range and now operational in the Middle East, the programme demonstrates QinetiQ's role in accelerating the deployment of new capability to front-line forces. • Delivering advanced operational training: Our MCAST programme achieved initial operating capability on schedule and entered service delivery, with the successful completion of Exercise Virtual Warrior 26 marking an important milestone. • Advancing underwater test and evaluation: At BUTEC, we achieved a UK first by collecting in-water data using a new submerged electromagnetic signature measurement system, which will help assess and reduce the detectability of advanced naval platforms. We also enabled the first launch of a US Mk48 heavyweight torpedo from the Royal Navy’s Excalibur extra-large autonomous underwater vehicle. Together, these milestones demonstrate the strategic value of BUTEC and QinetiQ’s role in assuring increasingly complex and autonomous maritime systems. • Enabling future radar capability: We signed a statement of intent with the UK Ministry of Defence, the Australian Department of Defence and Australia’s CEA Technologies to support the potential adoption of advanced radar technology by the UK Armed Forces. The programme builds on QinetiQ’s role in integrating, testing and assuring complex defence systems and further strengthens our position within UK sovereign air defence and sensing programmes. • Scaling our directed-energy capability: In July, we approved a £20m investment in a new low-rate initial production facility at Farnborough to accelerate the development and manufacture of high-energy laser technology. The investment will support HELENA™, QinetiQ’s newly launched family of coherent beam-combination laser sources for use in systems designed to counter drones and other advanced threats. It will expand our proprietary product portfolio and production capacity, positioning us to address future laser-directed energy programmes in the UK and allied markets. Outlook UK Defence remains well positioned for growth, underpinned by increasing defence spending, a sustained focus on readiness and accelerating demand for the testing, integration and assurance of new technologies. While the timing of customer orders affected first-half order intake, strong revenue visibility from the existing backlog and robust programme execution give us confidence in the full-year outlook. We see good opportunities across all four core capabilities, with initiatives such as SAFARI expected to strengthen the long-term competitiveness and relevance of our offerings. UK Intelligence Overview UK Intelligence supports UK Government customers across intelligence, cyber, mission systems and operational capability development. Its specialist expertise in information advantage, data exploitation and advanced digital technologies positions it to address increasingly complex defence and national security requirements. UK Intelligence performance was solid in the half with good programme execution driving strong profitability contributing to the good EMEA Services profit Outturn. Revenue was lower principally reflecting the non-repeat of revenue from the ACE contract, reduced pass-through work and the timing of customer spending. While customer funding and procurement delays continue to affect the near-term top line growth, the focus in the second half is on converting the opportunity pipeline and mobilising new programmes to support our expectations for the full year. Operational highlights • Shaping future defence engineering: We continued to deliver mission-critical engineering services through the Engineering Delivery Partner contract, while working with the customer on the future delivery of engineering services. This includes exploring how digital engineering and AI can accelerate delivery, simplify SME participation and improve access to finance across the defence supply chain. • Delivering frontline operational capability: We successfully transitioned a major operational-support programme secured in FY26 to full operating capability, providing frontline digital-forensics and triage services for critical customer missions. • Expanding our role in critical national infrastructure: We increased the scope of our work on the Emergency Services Mobile Communications Programme, providing specialist certification and security assurance for the UK’s next-generation Emergency Services Network. • Enabling operations without satellite navigation: We achieved further milestones in resilient Positioning, Navigation and Timing, developing technologies that enable forces to operate when satellite navigation is jammed, spoofed or unavailable. During the period, new soldier-worn and high-sensitivity technologies entered operational evaluation. • Positioning for the future of battlefield intelligence: We were down-selected to bid for the British Army’s Land Intelligence and Targeting Programme, Project ZODIAC, leading a consortium including Accenture, Northrop Grumman UK and specialist SMEs. The programme would transform how battlefield intelligence is collected, processed and exploited, enabling faster and better-informed operational decisions. Outlook Although customer funding and procurement delays are likely to continue affecting near-term performance, we enter the second half with a good opportunity pipeline and a clear focus on conversion and programme mobilisation. Longer term, the sector is well positioned to benefit from increasing customer focus on information advantage, artificial intelligence, resilient navigation and mission-critical digital services. Australia Overview Our Australia sector comprises our specialist advisory, engineering and our threat representation businesses operating in the Australian market. Trading conditions remained challenging during the first half, reflecting continued changes in government defence investment priorities, procurement processes and programme timing. We remain focused on aligning the business to priority defence requirements while improving operational efficiency and positioning for future long-term growth. Operational highlights • New MASS contract signed: New Maritime Aviation Support Services contract extends and expands the scope of services previously provided. • Advancing directed-energy capability: We signed an MoU with Rheinmetall Defence Australia to progress our Laser Directed Energy Weapon capability towards a concept demonstrator. The programme builds on our established partnership with Australia’s Defence Science and Technology Group and draws on technology developed through the UK’s DragonFire programme. • Supporting priority defence programmes: We continued to deliver engineering support under the Major Support Provider Agreement, while engaging with the customer on the design of its next phase. Outlook Australia's defence sector is expected to remain in a period of adjustment as government investment priorities, processes and delivery timelines continue to evolve. While the long-term market outlook remains positive, we expect trading conditions to remain challenging through the second half and into next year as customer procurement activity and programme awards continue to be delayed and we position the business towards the longer term, higher scale opportunities. United States Overview Our US sector provides specialist engineering, systems integration and mission operations support to defence and homeland security customers across the United States, operating across priority mission areas including space and missile defence, maritime systems, persistent surveillance, sensing and autonomy. The US business remains focused on disciplined delivery and selectively pursuing opportunities aligned with our core capabilities.
