Brand Group Core holds its own in a difficult environment – special items significantly impact overall result

Wolfsburg. The Brand Group Core (BGC) closed the first nine months of 2026 with an operating result of 4.33 billion euros, compared with 4.72 billion euros in the previous year.
The operating margin therefore declined from 4.4% to 4.1%.

#Finance #Brands

Unit sales of 3.75 million vehicles and sales revenue of 106.4 billion euros were virtually on a par with the previous year.

Overall, operating performance was affected by a further deterioration in market conditions. Intensifying competition, higher expenditure on sales incentives, and negative mix effects put pressure on the operating margin across the entire brand group. Ongoing performance programs and initiatives to improve the cost base within the Brand Group Core are having a positive overall impact.

Driven by high fuel prices and government incentives, demand for battery-electric vehicles (BEVs) accelerated significantly, particularly in the German market.
However, this transition to BEVs is coming at the expense of volumes, and above all, to the detriment of the earnings contribution from higher-margin internal combustion engine (ICE) models.

In addition, earnings performance in the first nine months of 2026 was adversely affected by significant special items, particularly restructuring expenses, the planned sale of Volkswagen Osnabrück GmbH, and the discontinuation of ID.4 production in Chattanooga which has already been implemented.

This led in particular to a decline in the operating margin of the Volkswagen Passenger Cars brand to 1.3%, significantly below the original expectations. Excluding the special items mentioned above, the Volkswagen brand’s operating margin after Q3 would have been 2.6%, while the BGC’s operating margin would have stood at 4.9%.
The brand group’s result was bolstered by the continued strong performance of the Škoda brand, which recorded a convincing operating margin of 8.3%.

Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles achieved an operating margin of 3.8 %, while SEAT & CUPRA posted an operating margin of 0.1%.

Outlook

The Brand Group Core is continuing to push ahead with its course of cross-brand collaboration and systematic efficiency increases, thus directly addressing the challenging competitive environment.

A key lever in these efforts is the new BGC steering model: since the middle of the year, decisions have been made even more consistently from the perspective of the brand group as a whole. This fosters a culture of joint decision-making and creates additional scope for efficiency improvements.


At the same time, the Brand Group Core is currently stepping up initiatives to further improve its cost base: specialized task forces are optimizing the vehicle mix between ICEs and electric models, thereby further reducing product costs.
Additional measures include reducing advance expenditure to safeguard net cash flow and optimizing working capital.

In connection with the “Zukunft Volkswagen” agreement reached at the end of 2024, Volkswagen AG expects additional restructuring expenses arising from the expansion of early retirement arrangements.

Important momentum in the vehicle model portfolio is being generated by the new ID. Tiguan, which celebrated its world premiere a few weeks ago – as well as the market launch of the Electric Urban Car Family. Shortly after their debut, 150,000 orders for the Volkswagen ID. Polo and ID. Cross, Škoda Epiq, and CUPRA Raval had already been received. The jointly developed models are being produced under the project leadership of SEAT & CUPRA in Martorell and Pamplona. Over their entire product life cycle, they are expected to generate synergy potential of around 600million euros.

Overview of the brands in the Brand Group Core:

Volkswagen Passenger Cars

Volkswagen Passenger Cars unit sales totaled 2.24 million vehicles in the first nine months of 2026 (excluding China, including external manufacturing), a decrease of 1.7 % compared with the same period last year. While demand for electric vehicles in Germany was high, availability remained limited, at the same time as volumes for high-revenue ICE models fell. Overall, the Volkswagen brand’s delivery performance on international markets and in the regions was weak. At 61.95 billion euros, sales revenue was 2.9% lower than the level of the previous year. The operating result came in at 813 million euros (previous year: 1,476 million euros). This was primarily due to higher expenditure on sales incentives, negative mix effects, and high special items, especially in respect of expenditure in connection with restructuring measures, the sale of Volkswagen Osnabrück GmbH, and discontinuation of ID.4 production in Chattanooga. Excluding the above-mentioned special items, the Volkswagen brand’s cumulative operating margin after Q3 would have been 2.6%.

Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles

At 465 million euro (previous year: 220 million euros), Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles more than doubled its operating result in the first nine months of 2026, the operating margin improved to 3.8 % (previous year: 1.8 %). Key to this was the brand’s disciplined approach to costs. At 320,444 vehicles, unit sales remained largely stable (previous year: 323,900 units), there was a moderate decrease of 2.0% in sales revenue to 12.3 billion euros. In contrast, deliveries to customers increased by 3.2% to 286,100 vehicles, with growth in allelectric models as high as 23.4 %. Developments in the third quarter were particularly strong: this was the strongest quarter of the year, with an operating result of 190 million euros (previous year: 13 million euros), and an increase in unit sales (+4.8 %) and sales revenue (+53 %) compared with the same prior-year quarter.

SEAT & CUPRA

SEAT & CUPRA recorded an operating result of 6 million euros in the period from January to September 2026 (previous year: 16 million euros). Following the positive effects of the ELEVATE strategy and the performance program, particularly in the first half of the year, the economic environment became significantly more challenging in the third quarter. The accelerating pace of electrification in key European markets, growing competitive pressure, and geopolitical volatility impacted the company's performance. While the current market transformation confirms the electrification strategy of SEAT & CUPRA, it also underscores the need to further improve margins on all-electric vehicles. In response to these challenges, the company is tightening cost-control measures and rigorously driving forward its ELEVATE strategy, which is based on Martorell's growing role as an industrial powerhouse and the continuous growth of CUPRA.

Škoda Auto

Škoda Auto continued its growth path in the first nine months of 2026. Global unit sales rose by 3.4% to 899,055 vehicles, and sales revenue grew by 3.5% to 23.1 billion euros. All-electric models were particularly in demand, with deliveries surging by 41.3%, and the Škoda Elroq standing out as the best-selling electric vehicle for the Volkswagen Group. The operating result increased by 7.4% to 1.92 billion euros. With an operating margin of 8.3% (previous year: 8.0%), Škoda Auto underscored its position as a top-performing brand in terms of profitability. In the third quarter, the brand also increased its operating result by 10.3% to 557 million euros.
