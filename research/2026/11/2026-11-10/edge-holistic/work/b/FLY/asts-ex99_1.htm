EXHIBIT 99.1

 

 

 

AST SpaceMobile Provides Business Update and Third Quarter 2026 Results

Nationwide intermittent service activation in the United States and other key markets before year-end 2026

 

Announced Blacklift, AST SpaceMobile’s own 20-ton reusable rocket to deploy BlueBird satellites into low Earth orbit, starting in late 2028 using Miranda and Vira engines

 

Secured United States priority DPAS rating for dual-use government and commercial satellite program, with planned launches from SpaceX, Blue Origin and others to reach a total of over 100 BlueBird satellites deployed in aggregate during 2027 and 2028

 

$1.0 billion in non-dilutive funding for manufacturing and launch of midband satellites from the Japan Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications

 

MIDLAND, Texas, November 9, 2026 – AST SpaceMobile, Inc. (“AST SpaceMobile”) (NASDAQ: ASTS), the company building the first and only space-based cellular broadband network accessible directly by everyday smartphones, and designed for both commercial and government applications, is providing its business update and results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

 

“We advanced several important initiatives to support the global expansion of our space-based cellular broadband network and expect to work with all major mobile network operators in the United States and Europe, providing potential expansion of our commercial ecosystem of over 60 mobile network operators representing over 3 billion subscribers,” commented Abel Avellan, AST SpaceMobile's Chairman and Chief Executive Officer. “We expanded our international commercial footprint, strengthened mobile network operator partnerships, and secured additional spectrum and regulatory authorizations. We also announced Blacklift, our proprietary 20-ton rocket, which is intended to provide greater control over launch availability, deployment schedules, and attractive ownership economics.”

 

“On the manufacturing front, we are transitioning to full-scale ASIC integration beginning with BlueBird 34, while advancing production through BlueBird 51,” added Avellan. “We expect to achieve a production rate of six ASIC satellites per month in December of this year.”

 

“Looking ahead, our priorities are to advance service readiness across our initial markets, complete the transition to ASIC-enabled production, and scale our constellation deployment. Through our partner-first strategy, we are expanding our scope of service and positioning AST SpaceMobile to become the direct-to-device partner of choice for mobile network operators, technology leaders and the United States Government,

extending our total addressable market well beyond traditional direct-to-device cellular broadband. With additional launch capacity and manufacturing output expected to increase, we are focused on accelerating constellation deployment and capitalizing on this expanded opportunity.”

Third Quarter 2026 Financial Highlights

•
Third quarter revenue of $42.3 million driven by gateway deliveries and U.S. Government milestones met
•
Total operating expenses for the third quarter of 2026 were $211.0 million, including $77.4 million of depreciation and amortization and stock-based compensation expense. This represents a decrease of $118.1 million as compared to $329.1 million in the second quarter of 2026 due to a $125.9 million loss on involuntary conversion in the second quarter, and an $8.2 million decrease in general and administrative costs, partially offset by a $8.5 million increase in engineering services costs, a $4.4 million increase in depreciation and amortization expense, a $1.7 million increase in cost of revenues, and a $1.3 million increase in research and development costs
•
Adjusted operating expenses(1) for the third quarter of 2026 were $133.6 million, an increase of $14.5 million as compared to $119.1 million in the second quarter of 2026 due to an $11.0 million increase in Adjusted engineering services costs(1), a $1.3 million increase in research and development costs, a $1.2 million increase in Adjusted general and administrative costs(1), and a $1.0 million increase in Adjusted cost of revenues(1). Our Adjusted operating expenses, excluding Adjusted cost of revenues(1) for the third quarter of 2026 were $109.4 million, compared to $95.9 million in the second quarter of 2026

About AST SpaceMobile

AST SpaceMobile is building the first and only global cellular broadband network in space to operate directly with standard, unmodified mobile devices based on our extensive IP and patent portfolio, and designed for both commercial and government applications. Our engineers and space scientists are on a mission to eliminate the connectivity gaps faced by today’s five billion mobile subscribers and finally bring broadband to the billions who remain unconnected. For more information, follow AST SpaceMobile on YouTube, X (Formerly Twitter), LinkedIn and Facebook. Watch this video for an overview of the SpaceMobile mission.

We define Adjusted operating expenses as Total operating expenses adjusted to exclude amounts of stock-based compensation expense, loss on involuntary conversion, and depreciation and amortization expense. We define Adjusted cost of revenues, Adjusted engineering services costs, and Adjusted general and administrative costs, as cost of revenues, engineering services costs, and general and administrative costs, respectively, adjusted to exclude stock-based compensation expenses. We define Adjusted operating expenses, excluding Adjusted cost of revenues as Total operating expenses adjusted to exclude amounts of stock-based compensation expense, loss on involuntary conversion, depreciation and amortization expense, and Adjusted cost of revenues.

Adjusted operating expenses; Adjusted cost of revenues; Adjusted engineering services costs; Adjusted general and administrative costs; and Adjusted operating expenses, excluding Adjusted cost of revenues are non-GAAP financial measures that have no standardized meaning prescribed by U.S. GAAP, and therefore have limits in their usefulness to investors. Because of the non-standardized definitions, these measures may not be comparable to the calculation of similar measures of other companies and are presented solely to provide investors with useful information to more fully understand how management assesses performance.
