THIRD QUARTER 2026 HIGHLIGHTS
      Net sales were approximately $1.6 billion, down 2 percent from the prior-year period. Favorable currency
       translation partially offset slightly lower selling prices and a 2 percent decline in sales volume2.
      Net earnings were a loss of $13 million, or $0.08 per share, while adjusted earnings reflected a loss of $15
       million, or $0.09 per share. Adjusted EBITDA was $234 million, earnings before income taxes was $34 million,
       and segment operating profit was $139 million.
      Results were below the prior-year period primarily due to net price pressure and an unusually high tax rate.
       Earnings lagged management's expectations as energy and freight costs were approximately $15 million higher
       than originally anticipated in Europe.
      Americas segment operating profit was down slightly from the prior-year period amid commercial headwinds.
      Europe segment operating profit was down significantly from the prior-year period, primarily due to net price
       pressure. The company made progress improving plant efficiency following first-half restructuring.
      Fit to Win delivered approximately $50 million of benefits during the quarter. O-I now targets approximately
       $180 million of benefits in 2026 and continues to expect at least $650 million of cumulative savings by 2027.
      The company now expects 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $925 to $950 million, below its prior outlook due to
       elevated energy and freight inflation, softer demand, and pace of recovery in Europe.

     “Third-quarter results demonstrated continued resilience in the Americas, while Europe
     remained well below our expectations. Commercial pressures and elevated energy and
     freight costs weighed on results, compounded by internal supply-chain challenges that we
     are actively addressing. We are beginning to see initial progress, with improving European
     production efficiency, better sequential shipment trends and continued Fit to Win cost
     reductions.
     Our focus now is converting that initial progress into stronger commercial performance,
     lower costs and improved margins. While near-term energy and trade conditions remain
     uncertain, the actions underway, together with continued resilience in the Americas, are
     expected to support higher adjusted earnings and free cash flow in 2027 and strengthen
     the foundation for long-term value creation.”
     GORDON HARDIE – CHIEF EXECUTIVE OFFICER

Net sales in the Americas were $970 million, up more than 3 percent from the prior-year period. A 1.6 percent
increase in selling prices and favorable currency translation more than offset a 1.3 percent decline in sales volume,
measured in tons. Strong shipment growth in Latin America largely offset headwinds in North America.

Segment operating profit was $137 million, down slightly from the prior-year period, as Fit to Win benefits and
favorable currency translation mostly offset lower volume and unfavorable net price. The prior year benefited from
a $6 million one-time insurance claim that did not repeat.

Net sales in Europe were $634 million, down 8 percent from the prior-year period. The decline reflected 3.8
percent lower selling prices, a 3.7 percent decrease in sales volume, measured in tons, and unfavorable currency
translation. Shipments increased in Northern Europe, while Southern Europe remained under pressure, particularly
in wine – a category that continues to suffer declining consumer and export volumes.

Segment operating profit was $2 million, compared with $95 million in the prior-year period. The decline was
primarily driven by unfavorable net price, reflecting competitive pricing pressure and elevated energy and freight
costs which were approximately $15 million above management’s expectations entering the quarter. Favorable
operating costs offset the impact of lower sales volume. One-time items provided a year-over-year net unfavorable
impact of $2 million.

Europe’s year-to-date segment operating profit is down significantly in 2026. Commercial pressures and elevated
energy and freight costs have weighed on results, with internal supply-chain challenges compounding these
headwinds. The company has established a detailed recovery plan focused on improving commercial performance,
managing energy costs, as well as driving plant and supply-chain efficiencies. These actions are expected to help
improve adjusted earnings and free cash flow in 2027.

FIT TO WIN
Fit to Win remains central to O-I’s strategy to strengthen competitiveness and cash generation. The program
delivered approximately $50 million of benefits during the third quarter ($135 million year-to-date) which more
than offset the impact of higher freight and maintenance costs, lower joint venture performance, and one-time
items. Compared to its prior outlook of at least $200 million in Fit to Win benefits, O-I now expects approximately
$180 million of net benefits this year given the pace of recovery in Europe. Those opportunities are expected to
shift to next year as the company continues to target at least $650 million of cumulative Fit to Win benefits by the
end of 2027.

O-I has updated its 2026 business outlook and now expects adjusted EBITDA of approximately $925 to $950 million
and a free cash flow use of approximately $300 million. The revised outlook primarily reflects continued soft demand
including the potential impact of U.S.-Canada trade disputes, further energy related cost inflation, slower pace of
recovery in Europe and higher inventory levels due to softer shipments. Liquidity was approximately $1.5 billion at
the end of the third quarter and O-I expects its net debt leverage ratio will approximate 4.5 to 5.0 times at year
end.
For 2027, the company expects higher net sales, adjusted EBITDA, and free cash flow, supported by the company’s
efforts to improve net price, maintain stable sales volumes, and reduce operating costs through Fit to Win. Given
evolving commercial, energy, and global trade conditions, among other factors, O-I is no longer maintaining its 2027
targets and plans to provide 2027 guidance on its 2026 year-end earnings call.
In late October 2026, the company decided to initiate a process with employee representatives regarding the
proposed permanent closure of a production facility in Europe. No final decision will be made until that process is
complete. As a result, the company currently estimates related charges of approximately $100 million to $120
million will be recorded in the fourth quarter of 2026, including approximately $40 million to $50 million of future
cash expenditures.
Updated 2026 guidance reflects the company’s current expectations for sales and production volumes, mix, and
working capital trends. However, the outlook for adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow remains subject to
macroeconomic uncertainty, including conflicts in the Middle East, currency movements, energy and raw material
costs, supply-chain disruptions, labor availability, changes in trade or immigration policies, and the execution of
global profitability improvement initiatives.


 “Our revised 2026 outlook reflects additional pressure from energy and freight cost inflation,
 continued soft demand and the slower pace of recovery in Europe. While these factors are
 weighing on near-term results and cash flow, our liquidity remains strong and we are
 committed to financial and operational discipline and execution.
 Looking ahead, we expect higher adjusted earnings and free cash flow in 2027, supported
 by improved commercial and operational performance in Europe, additional Fit to Win
 benefits, and continued resilience in the Americas. We remain focused on converting these
 actions into measurable financial improvement, strengthening free cash flow, and reducing
 leverage. We will provide specific 2027 guidance with our year-end results.”

 JOHN HAUDRICH – CHIEF FINANCIAL OFFICER

NON-GAAP FINANCIAL MEASURES
The company uses certain non-GAAP financial measures, which are measures of its historical or future financial
performance that are not calculated and presented in accordance with GAAP, within the meaning of applicable SEC
rules. Management believes that its presentation and use of certain non-GAAP financial measures, including adjusted
earnings, adjusted earnings per share, free cash flow, segment operating profit, segment operating profit margin,
EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA, net debt, leverage ratio and adjusted effective tax rate provide relevant and useful
supplemental financial information that is widely used by analysts and investors, as well as by management in assessing
both consolidated and business unit performance. These non-GAAP measures are reconciled to the most directly
comparable GAAP measures and should be considered supplemental in nature and should not be considered in
isolation or be construed as being more important than comparable GAAP measures.
Adjusted earnings relates to net earnings (loss) attributable to the company, exclusive of items management
considers not representative of ongoing operations and other adjustments because such items are not reflective of
the company’s principal business activity, which is glass container production. Adjusted earnings are divided by
weighted average shares outstanding (diluted) to derive adjusted earnings per share. Segment operating profit
relates to earnings (loss) before interest expense, net, and before income taxes and is also exclusive of items
management considers not representative of ongoing operations as well as certain retained corporate costs and
other adjustments. Segment operating profit margin is calculated as segment operating profit divided by segment
net sales. EBITDA refers to net earnings, excluding gains or losses from discontinued operations, interest expense,
net, provision for income taxes, depreciation and amortization of intangibles. Adjusted EBITDA refers to EBITDA,
exclusive of items management considers not representative of ongoing operations and other adjustments. Net

Further, free cash flow relates to cash provided by operating activities less cash payments for property, plant, and

performance because it believes these have provided useful supplemental measures related to its principal business
activity. It should not be inferred that the entire free cash flow amount is available for discretionary expenditures,
since the company has mandatory debt service requirements and other non-discretionary expenditures that are not
deducted from these measures. Management uses non-GAAP information principally for internal reporting,
forecasting, budgeting and calculating compensation payments.
