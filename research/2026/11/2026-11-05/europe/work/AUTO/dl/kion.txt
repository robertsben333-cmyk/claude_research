BUSINESS PERFORMANCE AND
ECONOMIC SITUATION

Financial position and financial performance
KION’s business performance varied across its two operating segments in the first nine months of
2026. Consolidated revenue and adjusted EBIT rose compared with the prior-year period, driven by
the strong growth of revenue and earnings in the Intelligent Automation Solutions (IAS)* segment.
By contrast, the financial performance of the Industrial Trucks & Services (ITS) segment remained
largely on a par with the first three quarters of 2025. The Group’s free cash flow was comfortably into
positive territory in the reporting period, despite increased M&A activities and the cash outflows
under the efficiency program aimed at strengthening long-term competitiveness and capacity to
carry out capital investment (‘efficiency program’).



Business situation and financial performance of the Group
Level of orders
The value of order intake at KION amounted to €8,445.0 million in the first three quarters of 2026
(Q1–Q3 2025: €8,882.0 million). Both operating segments recorded a year-on-year decline in their
order intake. The decrease in the Intelligent Automation Solutions segment was attributable to the
high figure for the comparative period, which had been boosted by the record level of order intake in
the second quarter of 2025. In the Industrial Trucks & Services segment, momentum declined over
the course of the year following a first quarter that had benefited from orders being brought forward.
The Group’s order book stood at €4,884.3 million as at the reporting date (December 31, 2025:
€4,892.3 million**). Currency effects had a positive impact on the order book, increasing it by
€110.0 million.


Revenue
Consolidated revenue went up by 3.9 percent to €8,522.0 million in the first nine months of 2026
(Q1–Q3 2025: €8,200.4 million). The increase in revenue stemmed from the strong growth of the
Intelligent Automation Solutions segment, whereas the Industrial Trucks & Services segment saw a
slight year-on-year decline.
In the Industrial Trucks & Services segment, revenue generated from external customers decreased
by 0.7 percent to €6,023.3 million (Q1–Q3 2025: €6,066.5 million) owing to the reduction in order
intake for new business. Conversely, revenue in the service business was up slightly overall.

Earnings and profitability
EBIT and EBITDA
KION generated earnings before interest and tax (EBIT) of €570.9 million in the first three quarters of
2026. The figure for the prior-year period of €325.0 million had been weighed down heavily by non-
recurring items in connection with the efficiency program aimed at strengthening long-term
competitiveness and capacity to carry out capital investment. This had materially affected the cost
of sales and other functional costs.
Against this backdrop, gross profit rose from €2,040.8 million in the prior-year period to
€2,143.9 million. At the same time, selling and administrative expenses and research and
development costs returned to a normal level, declining strongly by 7.4 percent and 16.2 percent
respectively during the reporting period.
The ‘other’ item amounted to income of €17.8 million overall (Q1–Q3 2025: income of €22.2 million).
This figure contained foreign currency exchange rate gains and losses, which, unlike in the prior-year
period, had a strong negative impact. It also included, in particular, the share of profit (loss) of equity-
accounted investments, along with income from the sale during the reporting period of individual
shareholdings that were not material to the Group.

Net financial expenses
Net financial expenses, representing the balance of financial income and financial expenses,

The income tax expenses incurred in the first nine months of 2026 amounted to €158.1 million.

earnings before tax. In addition, deferred tax income had been recognized in the third quarter of 2025
(€38.1 million on a net basis) as a result of the decision made in 2025 to gradually reduce the German
corporate income tax rate from 2028. The Group’s effective tax rate for the reporting period stood at
33.8 percent (Q1–Q3 2025: 21.9 percent).

Business situation and financial performance of the Industrial Trucks &
Services segment
Business performance and level of orders
The number of new trucks ordered in the Industrial Trucks & Services segment rose by 7.5 percent
to 210.1 thousand in the first nine months of 2026. Warehouse trucks saw a much stronger increase
than counterbalance trucks. Order numbers went up by 2.9 percent in the EMEA region and by
20.2 percent in the APAC region. In the Americas region, the number of new orders was much lower
than in the prior-year period (down by 10.1 percent).
The value of order intake fell by 1.2 percent to €5,897.5 million in the reporting period (Q1–Q3 2025:
€5,970.1 million). This was attributable to the value of order intake in new business, which declined
over the course of the year despite the overall rise in order numbers. The reasons for this were
twofold. Firstly, the disproportionately strong growth of the APAC region, which eclipsed that of the
EMEA and Americas regions, led to a shift in the country mix. Secondly, entry-level trucks made up
a higher proportion of the orders for warehouse technology. By contrast, order intake in the service
business was slightly higher than in the prior-year period.
The order book of the Industrial Trucks & Services segment amounted to €2,079.5 million as at
September 30, 2026 (December 31, 2025: €2,232.4 million*).

Revenue
Total revenue in the Industrial Trucks & Services segment fell to €6,034.2 million in the first three
quarters of 2026 (Q1–Q3 2025: €6,079.4 million). The decline in the order book for new business
resulted in a moderate reduction in revenue compared with the same period of the prior year. Despite
a higher number of new trucks being delivered, revenue was adversely affected by an increased
proportion of entry-level warehouse technology in the product mix. Overall, the service business
recorded a slight increase in revenue, with growth in the aftersales business and in the rental
business comfortably offsetting declines in used trucks and other service business. The proportion
of the segment’s revenue from external customers accounted for by the service business increased
to 52.6 percent in the period under review (Q1–Q3 2025: 51.3 percent).
Further details concerning revenue generated from external customers in the Industrial Trucks &

operating profit. The decline in revenue was partly offset by the higher gross margin in the service
business, the increasing impact of cost savings from the efficiency program, and the lower value of

20.5 percent).

first three quarters of 2026 (Q1–Q3 2025: net cash inflow of €676.9 million). This figure was weighed
down significantly by the repayment of liabilities from the lease business using funds obtained from
the corporate bond issued by KION GROUP AG in March 2026. Taking into account this inflow of
funds (less the issue discount and transaction costs) on an appropriate adjusted basis, cash flow
from operating activities amounted to a net cash inflow of €460.2 million > table ‘Free cash flow’.
There was also a material negative impact from the accumulation of net working capital during the
year as a result of milestone payments in the project business that are still outstanding. Most of
these payments are expected to be received in the fourth quarter of 2026. Cash flow from operating
activities also included cash payments in connection with the implementation of the efficiency
program, the bulk of which were not being made until the current year; such payments amounted to
€70.3 million in the reporting period.
There was an increase in net cash used for investing activities to minus €339.1 million in the first nine
months of 2026 (Q1–Q3 2025: minus €284.1 million). This rise was attributable to higher cash
payments of minus €97.7 million (Q1–Q3 2025: minus €29.4 million) for M&A activities, which are
primarily aimed at the strategic expansion of the portfolio of product solutions for warehouse
automation and supply chain robotics. By contrast, cash payments for capital expenditure on
property, plant and equipment and on intangible assets declined from the elevated level seen in prior
years to minus €249.2 million (Q1–Q3 2025: minus €264.2 million). Of this figure, minus
€73.5 million was attributable to capitalized development costs (Q1–Q3 2025: minus €98.6 million)
and minus €175.7 million to other non-current assets (Q1–Q3 2025: minus €165.6 million).
The Group’s free cash flow, the definition of which was changed at the start of 2026, came to
€121.1 million in the first three quarters of this year (Q1–Q3 2025: €392.8 million) > table ‘Free cash
flow’.
Free cash flow was previously defined as cash flow from operating activities plus cash flow from
investing activities. Starting in 2026, it also takes into account cash flow from additions to, and
repayments of, financial instruments (including related interest payments and borrowing costs) that
are used solely to finance the lease business and short-term rental business. The changed definition
was necessary because, for the first time, funds from the Company’s general funding are being used
to finance the lease business and short-term rental business. If this change were not made, cash
flows that, from an economic perspective, should be assigned to the lease business and short-term
rental business would not be recognized within free cash flow because of their inclusion in cash flow
from financing activities. Changing the definition ensures that these cash flows are assigned
correctly from an economic perspective and maintains this key figure’s comparability over time.
The changed definition increased free cash flow for the reporting period by €494.7 million as a result
of including the funds obtained from the corporate bond (less the issue discount and transaction
costs) to finance the lease business.

Analysis of capital structure
Net financial debt (non-current and current financial liabilities less cash and cash equivalents) rose
to €1,196.0 million as at September 30, 2026 due to the corporate bond issued during the reporting
period (December 31, 2025: €584.0 million). This equates to 0.6 times adjusted EBITDA on an
annualized basis (December 31, 2025: 0.3 times).
With effect from 2026, KION has changed the definition of its alternative performance measure
‘industrial net operating debt’ (INOD). Previously, INOD was defined as the total of net financial debt,
liabilities from the short-term rental business, and liabilities from procurement leases. Starting in
2026, funds are deducted from this total that, although they are used solely to finance the lease
business and short-term rental business, are recognized within financial liabilities in the statement
of financial position because they are part of the Company’s general funding. Changing the definition
was necessary because funds from a corporate bond without a specified purpose were used for the
first time in 2026 to finance the lease business. Whereas the lease business was previously financed
using specifically designated and directly assignable financial instruments, the funds obtained from
the corporate bond are recognized within financial liabilities in the statement of financial position.
If the definition were not changed, these funds, which should be assigned to the lease business from
an economic perspective, would be fully included in industrial net operating debt even though the
underlying lease business is not assigned to the industrial business. The changed definition therefore
helps to present the financing in accordance with its economic categorization and thus to ensure
that this key figure remains meaningful and provides comparability over time.
To reconcile the net financial debt with the industrial net operating debt (INOD) of €2,248.3 million
as at September 30, 2026 (December 31, 2025: €2,158.3 million), the liabilities from the short-term
rental business of €753.2 million (December 31, 2025: €810.2 million) and the liabilities from
procurement leases of €793.8 million (December 31, 2025: €764.1 million) are firstly added to net
financial debt. Secondly (in accordance with the changed definition), the funds of €494.7 million
obtained from the corporate bond (less the issue discount and transaction costs) to finance the
lease business are now deducted in order to calculate the industrial debt. It was not necessary to
restate the comparative figures for prior-year periods because the lease business was previously
financed solely from financial instruments that could be directly assigned to the lease business,
which meant there was no discrepancy between the presentation in the statement of financial
position and the assignment from an economic perspective. Leverage on industrial net operating
debt (INOD) stood at 1.2 times adjusted EBITDA on an annualized basis (December 31, 2025:
1.2 times).

Outlook
Expected sectoral conditions
Based on revised market expectations for the full year 2026, KION now expects order numbers in
the global industrial truck market to increase strongly year on year, supported by strong growth
across all regions. In the interim report for the second quarter of 2026, substantial growth was still
projected. In value terms, market growth is expected to remain below unit-based growth. This is due
to the continued shift in the product mix toward lower-priced warehouse trucks compared with
counterbalance trucks, as well as a general shift in customer demand toward lower-priced
segments.
Supported by market research from Interact Analysis (April 2026), KION continues to anticipate that
the global volume in the warehouse automation market (as measured by order intake) will rise
strongly in 2026.
The market outlook for 2026 remains subject to continued macroeconomic and geopolitical
uncertainties as well as heightened market volatility.
