Q. What is the quorum required in order to conduct business at the Meeting?

 

A. Under our amended and restated articles of association (the “A&R Articles”) a quorum is constituted when there are present, in person or by proxy, holders of at least a majority of the outstanding Shares of our Company. Shares that abstain from voting on any and all proposals or that represent “broker non-votes” (as described below under the answer to the question “How can I vote my Shares without attending the Meeting?”) are considered present for purposes of the quorum requirements. In accordance with the authority granted to the chairman of the Meeting under our A&R Articles, if a quorum is not present at the Meeting within half an hour from the time designated for the start of the Meeting, the chairman will adjourn the Meeting for one week, to December 23, 2026, at the same time and same place at which the Meeting is held, at which resolutions may be adopted with respect to any matter stated in the notice of the original Meeting. If a quorum is not present at the adjourned Meeting within half an hour from the time designated for it to begin, those shareholders present will be deemed to constitute a quorum and action may be taken on any and all matters on the agenda for the original Meeting despite the absence of a quorum at the adjourned meeting.

 

Q. What are the voting requirements to approve the proposals to be presented at the Meeting?

 

A. The affirmative vote of shareholders present in person or represented by proxy and holding Shares amounting in the aggregate to at least a majority of the Shares as, being entitled to do so, vote in person or by proxy at the Meeting with respect to each of Proposals 1 and 2 and Proposals 3 and 4 (Dissident Shareholder Proposals 1 and 2) is required for the approval of each such proposal. Because Proposal 5 (Dissident Shareholder Proposal 3) would amend our A&R Articles, it requires a special resolution. In other words, the affirmative vote of shareholders present in person or represented by proxy and holding Shares amounting in the aggregate to at least two-thirds of the Shares as, being entitled to do so, vote in person or by proxy at the Meeting with respect to such proposal is needed in order for Proposal 5 to pass. Abstentions and broker non-votes are not considered “actually cast” and are therefore not taken into consideration in determining whether the required majority has been achieved for any of the proposals (although they are considered present for purposes of the quorum requirements for the Meeting).

Statement of the Board of Directors in Opposition to Proposal 5 (Dissident Shareholder Proposal 3):

 

The Board of Directors has carefully considered Proposal 5 (Dissident Shareholder Proposal 3) and has unanimously determined that maintaining our classified board structure at this time is in the best interests of the Company and its shareholders, for the following primary reasons:

 

●Protection Against Opportunistic Takeovers and Hostile Disruption. In an environment where our ordinary shares trade at levels that the Board believes do not reflect the Company’s intrinsic long-term value, a classified board is a vital safeguard. It prevents a minority shareholder or opportunistic acquirer from seizing control of the Company or replacing the entire Board in a single election cycle without paying an appropriate control premium. Instead, a classified board structure ensures that any party seeking a change in control must engage and negotiate directly with the Board, empowering directors to protect the interests of all shareholders.

 

●Preservation of Multi-Year Strategic Execution. The Company became a public company in 2023. As a growth-stage technology company navigating a multi-year transformation of global freight, the Company benefits from the stability and continuity that a classified board provides. A classified board helps ensure that, at any given time, experienced directors oversee the Company’s complex technology roadmap and operational strategy. This stability allows management to resist short-term market pressures and enables uninterrupted focus on achieving key financial milestones, including disciplined execution toward sustainable profitability and Adjusted EBITDA breakeven.

 

●Commercial Partner Confidence and Platform Neutrality. The Company operates a neutral marketplace connecting global logistics providers, airlines, ocean carriers and freight forwarders. These enterprise commercial partners rely on the continuity of the Company’s long-term strategy and trust Freightos to remain an independent, neutral platform provider. Sudden or frequent changes to Board composition could create commercial uncertainty and jeopardize key partner relationships that are essential to driving transaction volume and platform network effects.

 

●Alignment with Growth-Stage Technology Peer Practice. Classified board structures remain standard market practice among high-growth technology companies in the years following their initial public offerings. While the Board recognizes that some shareholders and proxy advisory firms favor the annual election of all directors for mature large-cap issuers, market practice widely affirms that younger public companies require early-stage governance stability. The Board will continue to review the Company’s governance structure periodically as the Company’s business matures.
