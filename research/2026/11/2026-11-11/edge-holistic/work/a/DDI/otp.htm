Q: What happens to ADSs I do not tender?

A: If the Offer is completed, DoubleU Games will acquire the remaining Common Shares, at an amount per ADS equal to the Final Offer Price or such higher amount as may be required by applicable law or finally determined by a court, through a second-step transaction under the Korean Commercial Act (the “KCA”): (i) at or above the Small-Scale Threshold (currently estimated at approximately 97%), a small-scale share exchange (Article 360-10) on the DoubleU Games side plus a simplified share exchange (Article 360-9) on the DDI side; (ii) at 95% up to the Small-Scale Threshold, a controlling-shareholder statutory purchase under Article 360-24; or (iii) if the Minimum Tender Condition is reduced (to not below the 90% Waiver Floor) and DoubleU Games holds 90% to less than 95%, a simplified share exchange (Article 360-9, DDI side) plus a general comprehensive share exchange (Articles 360-2 and 360-3, DoubleU Games side, requiring a DoubleU Games shareholders’ meeting and giving rise to DoubleU Games appraisal rights). In the case of clause (iii), the second-step transaction cannot be completed unless DoubleU Games’ shareholders approve it by special resolution, and there can be no assurance that such approval will be obtained or as to when the second-step transaction will be completed. No merger is contemplated. See Section 12 — “Second-Step Transaction; Consideration.”

To exercise such rights, an ADS holder must, sufficiently in advance, cancel its ADSs, withdraw the underlying Common Shares and become a registered shareholder of DDI as of the applicable record date. See Section 15 — “Appraisal / Dissenters’ Rights” and Schedule II — “Appraisal / Dissenters’ Rights Under the Korean Commercial Act.”

2. Purposes and Reasons for the Offer

Purchaser’s purposes are to acquire all of the equity of the Company that it does not already own, to cause the ADSs to be delisted from Nasdaq and the Company to be deregistered under the Exchange Act, and to operate the Company as a wholly-owned private subsidiary. Purchaser believes the going-private transaction will: eliminate the substantial costs and management time associated with SEC/Nasdaq public-company and foreign-private-issuer compliance; remove the structural complexities, and the compliance and audit challenges, inherent in the Company’s current cross-border public-company status; afford greater strategic and operational flexibility; and provide the unaffiliated holders immediate, certain cash value at a premium in a security with limited trading liquidity.

Reasons for the tender-offer structure (rather than the previously proposed comprehensive share exchange) include the ability to deliver cash to tendering holders more quickly, reduced exposure to foreign-exchange movement between signing and a shareholder-meeting-based closing, and mitigation of KOSPI-related and timing considerations, while preserving robust procedural protections for unaffiliated holders (an empowered Special Committee and a non-waivable majority-of-the-minority tender condition).
