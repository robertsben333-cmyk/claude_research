Background of the Offer. Except as described in this Statement, during the two years before the date of this Statement, there were no negotiations or material contacts concerning a tender offer, merger, acquisition or other similar transaction involving the Company. On April 27, 2026 (U.S. time), DoubleU Games delivered to the Board a preliminary non-binding proposal (the “Proposal”) to acquire all of the outstanding Common Shares and ADSs not already owned by DoubleU Games in a going-private transaction for cash consideration of US$11.25 per ADS. In the Proposal, DoubleU Games stated that it was only interested in acquiring the publicly held securities of the Company and that it did not intend to sell its stake in the Company to a third party. DoubleU Games also filed a regulatory disclosure with the Korea Exchange in connection with the proposed transaction. On April 28, 2026, DoubleU Games filed Amendment No. 1 to its statement on Schedule 13D with the SEC disclosing the Proposal and filing the Proposal and the regulatory disclosure as exhibits. On April 29, 2026 (U.S. time), the Board held a meeting to consider the Proposal. At the invitation of the Board, representatives of Greenberg Traurig, LLP (“Greenberg Traurig”), legal counsel to the Company, attended the meeting. Following deliberation, the Board approved the formation of the Special Committee, consisting solely of independent and disinterested directors of the Company, to evaluate the Proposal. The resolutions establishing the Special Committee granted the Special Committee the full authority of the Board with respect to matters relating to the Proposal or any alternative transaction thereto. The resolutions further empowered the Special Committee to review, investigate, consider, evaluate, discuss and negotiate the Proposal, including the authority to determine not to recommend it and to reject it, and to retain, at the Company’s expense, its own independent legal and financial advisors. The initial members of the Special Committee were Whanlim Kim and Il Jin Park. On April 30, 2026 (U.S. time), DoubleU Games filed Amendment No. 2 to its statement on Schedule 13D, revising the description of the Proposal to reflect that the proposed transaction would be effected through a comprehensive share exchange under Korean law and would be subject to the affirmative vote of holders of at least 95% (rather than 80%) of the Company’s outstanding Common Shares, consistent with the communication previously made to the Board. Purposes and Reasons for the Offer Purchaser’s purposes are to acquire all of the equity of the Company that it does not already own, to cause the ADSs to be delisted from Nasdaq and the Company to be deregistered under the Exchange Act, and to operate the Company as a wholly-owned private subsidiary. Purchaser believes the going-private transaction will: eliminate the substantial costs and management time associated with SEC/Nasdaq public-company and foreign-private-issuer compliance; remove the structural complexities, and the compliance and audit challenges, inherent in the Company’s current cross-border public-company status; afford greater strategic and operational flexibility; and provide the unaffiliated holders immediate, certain cash value at a premium in a security with limited trading liquidity. Reasons for the tender-offer structure (rather than the previously proposed comprehensive share exchange) include the ability to deliver cash to tendering holders more quickly, reduced exposure to foreign-exchange movement between signing and a shareholder-meeting-based closing, and mitigation of KOSPI-related and timing considerations, while preserving robust procedural protections for unaffiliated holders (an empowered Special Committee and a non-waivable majority-of-the-minority tender condition). 3. In general, a non-U.S. corporation will be classified as a passive foreign investment company, or a PFIC, for any taxable year in which, after applying certain look-through rules, either: • at least 75% of its gross income is passive income (such as interest income); or • at least 50% of its gross assets (determined on the basis of a quarterly average) is attributable to assets that produce passive income or are held for the production of passive income. Gross income for this purpose generally includes all sales revenue less the cost of goods sold, plus income from investments and from incidental or outside operations or sources. Passive income for this purpose generally includes dividends, interest, royalties, rents and gains from commodities and securities transactions, and gains from assets that produce passive income. Cash is generally treated as an asset that produces passive income. For purposes of the PFIC income test and asset test described above, if the Company owns, directly or indirectly, 25% or more of the total value of the outstanding shares of another corporation, the Company will be treated as if it (i) held a proportionate share of the assets of such other corporation and (ii) received directly a proportionate share of the income of such other corporation. As indicated in the Company’s annual report in Form 20-F for the year ended December 31, 2025, the Company has not made a determination as to its PFIC status for the current or preceding taxable year. The determination of whether the Company is a PFIC is a fact-intensive determination made on an annual basis applying principles and methodologies that in some circumstances require subjective determination and, as a result, any such determination is subject to varying interpretation. The remainder of this discussion addresses certain U.S. federal income tax consequences that may apply to a U.S. Holder if the Company is a PFIC for its current taxable year or was a PFIC for any other taxable year with respect to which such U.S. Holder owns the ADSs. If the Company is classified as a PFIC in the current taxable year or was classified as a PFIC for any other year with respect to which a U.S. Holder owns the ADSs, U.S. Holders will be subject to special tax rules with respect to any gain such U.S. Holder recognizes from the Offer or the second-step transaction, unless (i) such U.S. Holder makes a “qualified electing fund” election, or QEF Election, with respect to all taxable years during such U.S. Holder’s holding period in which the Company was a PFIC or (ii) the ADSs constitute “marketable” securities, and such U.S. Holder makes a mark- to-market election as discussed below. Under these special tax rules: • the gain will be allocated ratably over a U.S. Holder’s holding period for the ADSs; • the amount allocated to the taxable year of disposition, and any taxable year prior to the first taxable year in which the Company became a PFIC, will be treated as ordinary income; and 40 • the amount allocated to each other year will be subject to the highest tax rate in effect for that year and the interest charge generally applicable to underpayments of tax will be imposed on the resulting tax attributable to each such year. The tax liability for amounts allocated to taxable years during which the Company was a PFIC prior to the taxable year in which a disposition of ADSs occurs pursuant to the Offer or the second-step transaction cannot be offset by any net operating losses for such taxable years, and gains (but not losses) realized on the disposition of the ADSs pursuant to the Offer or the second-step transaction cannot be treated as capital, even if a U.S. Holder holds the ADSs as capital assets. If a U.S. Holder makes an effective QEF Election with respect to a PFIC, it will be taxed currently on its pro rata share of the PFIC’s ordinary earnings and net capital gain (at ordinary income and capital gain rates, respectively) for each taxable year that the entity is a PFIC, even if no distributions were received. Such U.S. Holder’s tax basis in its ADSs would be increased by an amount equal to any income included under the QEF Election and decreased by any amount distributed on the ADSs that is not included in its income. On the disposition of the ADSs in connection with the Offer or the second-step transaction, a U.S. Holder that has a QEF Election in effect would generally recognize capital gain or loss in an amount equal to the difference between the amount realized and its adjusted tax basis in the ADSs. Republic of Korea SCHEDULE II — APPRAISAL / DISSENTERS’ RIGHTS UNDER THE KOREAN COMMERCIAL ACT This Schedule is the statement of security holders’ appraisal / dissenters’ rights and the procedures for exercising them referred to in the response to Item 4 of the Schedule 13E-3 (Item 1004(d) of Regulation M-A), and is filed as Exhibit (f)(1) to the Schedule TO/13E-3. A. No appraisal or dissenters’ rights in the Offer . A tender of ADSs pursuant to the Offer is a voluntary sale by the tendering holder. Holders of ADSs are not entitled to appraisal or dissenters’ rights, or to any comparable right of objection, in connection with the Offer itself, and ADSs accepted for payment in the Offer carry no residual appraisal or share-purchase rights. B. Other rights available to holders who do not tender . A holder who does not tender its ADSs in the Offer will remain a holder at the time of the second-step transaction described in Section 12— “ Second-Step Transaction; Consideration ” and may, depending on the structure of that transaction and subject to the procedures described below, have the rights described in Parts C and D of this Schedule. The second-step transaction will occur only if the Offer is completed. If the Offer is not completed, no second-step transaction will be effected, and none of the rights described in Parts C and D will arise. C. Share-purchase (appraisal) rights on a simplified share exchange — Articles 360-5(2) and 374-2(2) to (5) of the Korean Commercial Act . If the second-step transaction is effected as a simplified share exchange, DDI must, within two weeks after the date on which the share exchange agreement is prepared, give public notice or notify its shareholders that the share exchange will be effected without the approval of a shareholders’ meeting. A holder of Common Shares who, within two weeks after the date of that public notice or notification, notifies DDI in writing of its objection to the share exchange may, within 20 days after the expiry of that two-week period, demand in writing — stating the class and number of shares — that DDI purchase the shares it holds. DDI must purchase those shares within two months after the end of the demand period. The purchase price is determined by agreement between the shareholder and DDI; if no agreement is reached within 30 days after the end of the demand period, either DDI or the demanding shareholder may petition the court to determine the price, and the court determines a fair price taking into account DDI’s financial condition and other circumstances. Because the Common Shares are not listed on a Korean exchange, the market-price formula under the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act does not apply. D. Controlling shareholder’s demand for sale of shares — Article 360-24 of the Korean Commercial Act . A shareholder holding, for its own account (and together with its subsidiaries), at least 95% of DDI’s total issued and outstanding shares may, where necessary to achieve a business purpose, demand that the remaining shareholders sell the shares they hold. The demand requires the prior approval of a shareholders’ meeting of DDI, and the notice convening that meeting must state the controlling shareholder’s shareholding in DDI, the purpose of the demand, the basis for and the appropriateness of the purchase price as assessed by a certified appraiser, 56 and the guarantee of payment of the purchase price. The controlling shareholder must, at least one month before the date of the demand, give public notice and separately notify the shareholders and pledgees entered in DDI’s shareholder register that a minority shareholder must deliver its share certificates against receipt of the purchase price, and that if it does not do so the share certificates become void on the date the purchase price is received or is deposited by the controlling shareholder. A minority shareholder that receives the demand must sell its shares to the controlling shareholder within two months after receiving it. The purchase price is determined by agreement between the minority shareholder and the controlling shareholder; if no agreement is reached within 30 days after the demand is received, either of them may petition the court to determine the price, and the court determines a fair price taking into account DDI’s financial condition and other circumstances. A demand under Article 360-24 does not give rise to a right of objection or to a share-purchase demand under Article 360-5. E. Steps required of holders of ADSs . All Common Shares not owned by DoubleU Games are represented by ADSs, and Citibank, N.A., as depositary (or its custodian or nominee), is the holder of record of the underlying Common Shares. The public notices and notifications described in Parts C and D are given to, and the rights described in those Parts are exercisable by, holders of record of Common Shares. Citibank, N.A. will not deliver an objection, make a share-purchase demand, respond to a demand for sale, or petition a court for a determination of price on behalf of any holder of ADSs. A holder of ADSs that wishes to be in a position to exercise any of those rights must, before the applicable period expires: (i) surrender its ADSs to the depositary for cancellation and pay any applicable fees, charges and taxes; (ii) withdraw the underlying Common Shares; (iii) become a holder of record of those Common Shares on DDI’s shareholder register; and (iv) thereafter exercise the relevant right directly and in its own name. Those steps are governed by the deposit agreement and by Korean securities account and share registration requirements, and are carried out by the depositary, its custodian, the Korea Securities Depository and DDI’s transfer agent rather than by DoubleU Games or DDI. A holder that wishes to take them should consult the depositary and its own broker as to the applicable procedures, fees and account requirements. Neither DoubleU Games nor DDI gives any assurance as to the time required to complete them. A holder that takes those steps will hold Common Shares directly and will no longer be able to tender in the Offer, which is an offer to purchase ADSs only. The periods described in Parts C and D run from the date of DDI’s public notice or notification, or from the date on which the demand for sale is received, in each case at the second step and after the Offer has been completed; the specific dates will be stated in that public notice, notification or demand and are not determinable as of the date of this Offer to Purchase. A holder of ADSs that wishes to preserve the ability to exercise those rights should begin the cancellation and withdrawal process as early as possible and should consult its own legal, tax and financial advisers. Neither DoubleU Games nor DDI gives any assurance that a holder that begins that process after the relevant public notice, notification or demand is given will be able to complete the necessary steps within the applicable period. No action is required, and no deadline applies, in order to receive the consideration payable in the second-step transaction. If that transaction is effected as a comprehensive share exchange, all Common Shares not owned by DoubleU Games are transferred to DoubleU Games by operation of law on the share exchange date, whether those shares are held directly or through the depositary. If it is effected as a demand for sale under Article 360-24, those shares are transferred upon payment or deposit of the purchase price. In each case a holder becomes entitled to the consideration described in Section 12— “ Second-Step Transaction; Consideration ” without taking any action within any period. The summaries in Parts C and D do not purport to be complete and are qualified in their entirety by reference to the full text of those provisions. 57 Article 360-5 (Appraisal Rights of Dissenting Shareholders) . (1) A shareholder who dissents from a resolution of the board of directors on a matter subject to approval under Article 360-3(1) (including a shareholder without voting rights or whose voting rights are restricted; hereinafter the same applies in this Article) and who has notified the company in writing, before the shareholders’ meeting, of its intention to dissent from the resolution may, within 20 days from the date of the resolution of that meeting, demand in writing, stating the class and number of shares, that the company purchase the shares it holds. (2) A shareholder who has notified the company in writing, within two weeks from the date of the public notice or notification under Article 360-9(2), of its intention to dissent from the share exchange may, within 20 days from the date on which that period expires, demand in writing, stating the class and number of shares, that the company purchase the shares it holds. (3) Article 374-2(2) through (5) applies mutatis mutandis to a demand for purchase under paragraphs (1) and (2). Article 360-9 (Simplified Comprehensive Share Exchange) . (1) Where all the shareholders of the company that is to become a wholly owned subsidiary consent, or where the company that is to become the wholly owning parent company holds at least 90 per cent of the total number of issued and outstanding shares of the company that is to become a wholly owned subsidiary, the approval of the shareholders’ meeting of the company that is to become a wholly owned subsidiary may be replaced by the approval of its board of directors. (2) In the case of paragraph (1), the company that is to become a wholly owned subsidiary shall, within two weeks from the date on which the share exchange agreement is prepared, give public notice, or notify its shareholders, to the effect that the share exchange will be effected without the approval of a shareholders’ meeting; provided that this does not apply where all the shareholders have consented. Article 360-24 (Controlling Shareholder’s Right to Demand Sale of Shares) . (1) A shareholder holding, for its own account, at least 95 per cent of the total number of issued and outstanding shares of a company (hereinafter in this Subsection referred to as the “controlling shareholder”) may, where necessary to achieve a business purpose of the company, demand that the other shareholders of the company (hereinafter in this Subsection referred to as the “minority shareholders”) sell the shares they hold. (2) In calculating the number of shares held under paragraph (1), the shares held by a parent company and a subsidiary shall be aggregated. In such case, the shares held by a company in which a shareholder that is not a company holds shares exceeding 50 per cent of the total number of issued and outstanding shares shall also be aggregated with the shares held by that shareholder. (3) A demand for sale under paragraph (1) requires the prior approval of a shareholders’ meeting. (4) The notice convening the shareholders’ meeting under paragraph (3) shall state the following matters, and the controlling shareholder making the demand for sale shall explain them at the shareholders’ meeting: 1. the controlling shareholder’s holdings of shares of the company; 2. the purpose of the demand for sale; 3. an assessment by a certified appraiser of the basis for computing, and the appropriateness of, the purchase price; 4. the guarantee of payment of the purchase price. (5) The controlling shareholder shall, by no later than one month before the date of the demand for sale, give public notice of the following matters and separately notify them to the shareholders and pledgees entered in the shareholder register: 1. that a minority shareholder shall deliver its share certificates to the controlling shareholder simultaneously with receipt of the purchase price; 2. that, if it fails to deliver them, the share certificates shall become void on the date on which the purchase price is received or on which the controlling shareholder deposits the purchase price. (6) A minority shareholder that has received a demand for sale under paragraph (1) shall sell its shares to the controlling shareholder within two months from the date of receipt of the demand. (7) In the case of paragraph (6), the purchase price shall be determined by agreement between the minority shareholder that received the demand for sale and the controlling shareholder that made it. (8) Where no agreement on the purchase price under paragraph (7) is reached within 30 days from the date of receipt of the demand for sale under paragraph (1), the minority shareholder that received the demand or the controlling shareholder that made it may petition the court to determine the purchase price. (9) Where the court determines the purchase price of shares under paragraph (8), it shall compute a fair price taking into consideration the financial condition of the company and other circumstances. Article 374-2 (Appraisal Rights of Dissenting Shareholders) . (1) A shareholder who dissents from a matter resolved under Article 374 (including a shareholder without voting rights or whose voting rights are restricted; 58 hereinafter the same applies in this Article) and who has notified the company in writing, before the shareholders’ meeting, of its intention to dissent from the resolution may, within 20 days from the date of the resolution of that meeting, demand in writing, stating the class and number of shares, that the company purchase the shares it holds. (2) Upon receipt of a demand under paragraph (1), the company shall purchase the shares within two months from the date on which the period for the demand for purchase under that paragraph (hereinafter in this Article referred to as the “demand period”) ends. (3) The purchase price of the shares under paragraph (2) shall be determined by agreement between the shareholder and the company. (4) Where no agreement under paragraph (3) is reached within 30 days from the date on which the demand period ends, the company or the shareholder that demanded the purchase of shares may petition the court to determine the purchase price. (5) Where the court determines the purchase price of shares under paragraph (4), it shall compute a fair price taking into consideration the financial condition of the company and other circumstances. 59
