## hp.v2 (from hp.v1), 2026-11-11T19:37:34+00:00

hp.v1 has 35 resolved names over 3 run days (11-06, 11-09, 11-10). Brier is 0.2492, a skill of 0.003 over a coin. Direction is 16/32 and there are 0 recommendations. The control (also hp.v1, frozen) has 12 names at Brier 0.2416. The paired difference on those 12 is -0.004 (sd 0.031, t -0.45), and because both arms ran the same text, that figure is the stage's reproducibility noise, not an effect. The views carry no information yet, and the reason is not miscalibration: every evolving p_up sits between 38 and 58, and only 3 of 35 are 10 or more points from 50. That spread is honest for a researcher with no edge. Two mechanisms in the playbook keep it there. (1) The researchers read P7's mechanism clause together with P4 and P8 as 'the deciding line must be visible before the release'. It never is, so on name after name they gathered dated evidence on the inputs to that line and then sat near 50 with 'no public trace before the release' (QBTS, HSAI, CRWV, RKLB, STUB, HIMS, LQDA). Where the inputs pointed, the facts often followed. RKLB's guide-beat record was right: record revenue, backlog +52% q/q, Q4 guided to record revenue and margin expansion. CRWV's counterparty capacity was on time and the stock rose 14.9%. The recommendation channel produced nothing in 47 views, so it cannot even be evaluated. (2) Five researchers wrote, without prompting, that taking the run-in apart against peers and the tape was their most useful step and that the playbook does not name it (HSAI, QBTS, RKLB, TTD, LPTH). Separately, P6's positioning sentence is wrong as written: it says a crowded short makes a good print move less. Researchers instead pulled p_up toward 50 for squeeze risk (ASTS, LPTH, FRNM); direction against crowded shorts was 3 of 6, which says positioning is size, not sign. Stage E-P's selected calls on the same names went 5 of 6 (+8.2%, t 1.91), and its sign over all 34 names with a move was 21 of 34. Stage H agreed in sign on all 6 selected names but stayed at 38-58, so the evidence was not missing; the playbook would not let it count. Nothing here rests on returns. The 11-06 day ran without hunt files, so it is cited nowhere. The run-in names that fell (QBTS, HSAI, RKLB, TTD, LPTH, ASTS) mostly exited on one market morning, so they are correlated, and no direction rule is drawn from them.

The old positioning sentence was garbled: it lumped a crowded short with a big run-up, and the researchers worked around it by pulling p_up toward 50 for squeeze risk. Positioning direction was 3 of 6 against crowded shorts (ASTS, LPTH, TTD down; CRWV, CAPR, FRNM up), which fits 'size, not sign'. The edit does not prescribe fading rallies.
